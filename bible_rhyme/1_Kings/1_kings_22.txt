Three years of peace, no war in sight,  
Jehoshaphat came down, ready to unite.  
Israel’s king said, "Ramoth Gilead's ours,  
But we do nothing, let’s reclaim our powers."  

He asked Jehoshaphat, "Will you fight by my side?"  
Jehoshaphat replied, "Our forces will ride."  
"But first," he said, "Let's seek Yahweh’s command,"  
Prophets gathered, four hundred strong, in the land.  

"Go to battle," they said, "It’s yours to take,"  
Jehoshaphat asked, "Is there a true voice for Yahweh’s sake?"  
"There’s Micaiah," Israel's king confessed,  
"But his words bring me distress, not blessed."  

"Bring him here," said the king with demand,  
While the prophets prophesied, iron horns in hand.  
Zedekiah shouted, "With these you'll win,"  
The messenger nudged Micaiah, "Speak like them, begin."  

Micaiah declared, "Yahweh’s words I’ll speak,"  
The king asked, "From truth don’t you dare sneak."  
Micaiah said, "Israel’s scattered like sheep,  
No master, back to their homes, in peace they seep."  

The king groaned, "See, never good for me,  
Always evil, never setting me free."  
Yahweh's vision Micaiah shared loud,  
"Heaven's army, Yahweh’s questions, proud."  

"Who'll entice Ahab?" Yahweh asked the throng,  
A spirit stepped up, "I’ll make it wrong."  
"Go," Yahweh said, "deceive with might,"  
Now the prophets’ words fall from Yahweh’s sight.  

Zedekiah struck, "Yahweh, where'd you go?"  
"Hidden you'll see," Micaiah replied slow.  
The king ordered, "Take him, locked away,  
Bread and water of affliction, till I return day."  

"If you come back," Micaiah's words rang,  
"Yahweh didn’t speak, hear the clang."  
Into battle they marched, kings side by side,  
Israel's king disguised, in robes Jehoshaphat complied.  

Syria’s orders: "Only the king to fight,"  
They chased Jehoshaphat, then turned in flight.  
A bow at random, hit Israel's king,  
"Wounded, take me away," his voice did sing.  

Propped in his chariot, the king met his fate,  
Blood pooled as the sun set late.  
"Return to your homes," the cry did say,  
In Samaria, Ahab was laid that day.  

Chariot washed, dogs licked his blood,  
As Yahweh spoke, there was no flood.  
Ahab's deeds in chronicles written down,  
Ahaziah reigned next, taking the crown.  

Jehoshaphat walked in the path of his kin,  
Doing right in Yahweh's eyes, but high places didn’t dim.  
Ships to Ophir wrecked on the way,  
Ahaziah asked to join, but Jehoshaphat said nay.  

Jehoshaphat slept, legacy sustained,  
Jehoram his son, now reigned and remained.  
Ahaziah did evil, Baal he served,  
Provoking Yahweh's wrath, justice reserved.
