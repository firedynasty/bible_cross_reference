Ahab told Jezebel, "Elijah's done a feat,  
He struck down prophets, made their end complete."  
Jezebel sent a threat, hot as a flame,  
"By tomorrow, Elijah, your fate will be the same."  

Elijah heard this and ran for his life,  
To Beersheba he went, escaping the strife.  
Alone in the wilderness, under the tree,  
He begged the Lord, "Take my life, let me be."  

He lay down in despair but wasn't alone,  
An angel tapped him, with a voice like a tone,  
"Arise and eat," the angel softly said,  
A cake and water waited by his head.  

He ate and drank, then lay down again,  
The angel returned, "The journey's not plain.  
Arise and eat, for the road is long,  
In this strength, you must be strong."  

Forty days, forty nights, to Horeb’s height,  
Elijah walked with God's might.  
In a cave he found rest, in the mountain deep,  
Then Yahweh spoke to him, waking him from sleep,  

"What are you doing here, Elijah, my man?"  
"I've been zealous, but they break your plan.  
Israel's lost, your altars they razed,  
I'm the last, and they're seeking my days."  

"Stand on the mountain, see Yahweh pass by."  
Wind tore the mountains, rocks broke and flew high.  
But Yahweh wasn't in the wind or in the quake,  
Nor in the fire did His voice awake.  

A still small voice in the silence came,  
Elijah heard it, wrapped his face in shame.  
"What are you doing here?" the voice asked again,  
Elijah poured out his heart, his pain.  

"Return to Damascus, the wilderness way,  
Anoint Hazael and Jehu without delay.  
Elisha shall follow, in your prophetic place,  
Seven thousand I've kept who have not turned their face."  

Elijah moved on, found Elisha at the plow,  
Placed his mantle on him, made the sacred vow.  
Elisha said, "Let me bid my family goodbye."  
Elijah nodded, "Go on, don't ask why."  

Elisha returned, the oxen were slain,  
He fed the people, broke every chain.  
Then he rose up, followed Elijah's track,  
Together they went, not looking back.
