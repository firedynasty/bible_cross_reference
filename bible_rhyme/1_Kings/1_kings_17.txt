Elijah the Tishbite, bold and brave,  
Spoke to Ahab, no rain, no dew to save.  
"As Yahweh lives, before whom I stand,  
No rain till I command it in this land."

God's word came, "Head east, don't stay,  
Hide by Cherith brook, that's the way.  
Drink from its flow, the ravens will feed,  
Bread and meat twice daily, that's your need."

Elijah obeyed, lived by the brook's side,  
Ravens brought food, his needs supplied.  
But soon the brook ran dry, no rain to fall,  
Then Yahweh's word came with a new call.

"Arise, go to Zarephath, Sidon's place,  
A widow I've chosen, to sustain you with grace."  
He went to the city, saw her gathering there,  
"Bring me some water," he asked with care.

"And a morsel of bread," he added on,  
But she said, "I have barely any, it's almost gone.  
Just a handful of meal, a little oil in a jar,  
After this meal, death won't be far."

Elijah reassured her, "Don't be afraid,  
First make me a cake, as I have said.  
For Yahweh says, your supplies won't deplete,  
Until rain falls again, your kitchen's replete."

She obeyed his words, and many days they ate,  
Meal and oil didn't run out, it was fate.  
But her son grew ill, breath ceased in his chest,  
She cried to Elijah, her heart in distress.

Elijah said, "Give me your son," took him up high,  
Laid him on his bed, pleaded with God on high.  
"Yahweh, bring back this child's soul," he cried,  
Three times he stretched, no breath denied.

God heard Elijah, brought life back anew,  
Elijah brought him down, "Behold, he lives, it's true!"  
The woman exclaimed, her faith now strong and clear,  
"You're a man of God, your words I hold dear."
