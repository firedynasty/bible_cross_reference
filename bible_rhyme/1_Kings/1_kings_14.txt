Abijah got sick, Jeroboam felt the heat,  
Told his wife, "Disguise, don’t let them see your feet.  
Head to Shiloh, where Ahijah stays,  
Take loaves, cakes, honey, listen to what he says.”

She did as told, journeyed with care,  
Ahijah, blind but knew she'd be there.  
God whispered to him, “Jeroboam’s wife will come,  
Pretending she's another, but let’s not play dumb.”

She walked through the door, he didn’t miss a beat,  
“Come in, Jeroboam’s wife, take a seat.  
Heavy news I bring, straight from the Lord,  
‘Cause you turned from the path, ignored His accord.”

"You were lifted up, made king of this land,  
But turned to idols, provoked by your hand.  
So your house will fall, swept away like waste,  
Dogs and birds will feast, such is your fate.”

“When you step back home, your child will die,  
Israel will mourn, tears they won’t deny.  
A new king will rise, Jeroboam’s line will fail,  
Israel shaken, scattered like a gale.”

She left Ahijah, with a heavy heart,  
Reached home’s threshold, her son did depart.  
Israel mourned, just as the word had been,  
Written in chronicles, of what had been seen.

Jeroboam reigned, twenty-two years long,  
Then he rested, his son carried on.  
Rehoboam ruled Judah, with sin in its sight,  
Idols and high places, under every tree’s light.

In his fifth year, Egypt’s king came,  
Took treasures of gold, left Judah in shame.  
Bronze shields replaced what was once gold’s gleam,  
Wars raged on, like a recurring theme.

Rehoboam passed, laid with his own,  
Abijam took the throne, his legacy shown.  
The tales of kings, their deeds and their fate,  
Written in chronicles, a history's weight.
