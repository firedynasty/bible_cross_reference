Yo, after many days, Yahweh's word came through,  
Told Elijah, "Show yourself to Ahab, rain's due."  
The famine was fierce, Samaria was dry,  
Elijah set his sights on Ahab, said bye-bye.  

Ahab called Obadiah, his right-hand man,  
Obadiah feared Yahweh, had a prophet-saving plan.  
Jezebel cut prophets like a villain in the night,  
Obadiah hid a hundred, kept 'em out of sight.  

Ahab said, “Search the land, find us some grass,  
Save the horses and mules, make our resources last.”  
Dividing paths, Ahab and Obadiah split ways,  
Obadiah met Elijah, and was amazed.  

"Is it you, my lord Elijah?" he bowed down low,  
Elijah said, "It is I, go and let Ahab know."  
Obadiah shook his head, fear in his eyes,  
"If I say you're here, and then you fly, I might die."  

But Elijah stood firm, "As Yahweh lives," he said,  
"I'll show myself to Ahab, no need for dread."  
Obadiah told Ahab, who met Elijah quick,  
Ahab called him trouble, but Elijah came back slick.  

"I ain't troubled Israel, it's you and your kin,  
Forsaken Yahweh, let Baal's worship begin."  
"Gather all Israel to Mount Carmel's peak,  
Four fifty prophets of Baal, and more from Jezebel's clique."  

Ahab gathered them all, the prophets in line,  
Elijah stepped up, his voice like a chime.  
"How long will you waver, between two paths?  
If Yahweh's God, follow Him, or face His wrath."  

Elijah took two bulls, laid the challenge down,  
"Call your god’s name, I'll call Yahweh’s renown.  
Let the one who sends fire be the true divine,  
People agreed, "This idea's mighty fine."  

Baal’s prophets danced, called from morning till noon,  
But the heavens stayed silent, no voice in tune.  
Elijah teased, "Maybe Baal's on a trip,  
Cry louder, wake him up, get him to flip."  

No answer came, even as blood spilled,  
Prophets of Baal stood there, hopes unfulfilled.  
Elijah called the people, gathered them near,  
Repaired Yahweh's altar, said, "Come here."  

Twelve stones, one for each tribe, he took with care,  
Built an altar, Yahweh's name in the air.  
Filled jars with water, soaked the altar all through,  
"Do it again," then a third time too.  

Water flowed, filling trenches wide,  
Elijah stepped forth, no fear to hide.  
"Yahweh, God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel," he prayed,  
"Show them your power, let their hearts be swayed."  

Fire fell from heaven, a consuming blaze,  
Burned offering, wood, stones, in a mighty craze.  
People fell on faces, shouted loud and broad,  
"Yahweh, He is God! Yahweh, He is God!"  

Elijah called, "Seize Baal's prophets, not one to flee,"  
Took them to Kishon, ended them decisively.  
To Ahab, Elijah said, "Get up, eat, and drink,  
For the sound of rain approaches, making you think."  

Ahab dined, while Elijah climbed to the top,  
Prayed with face to knees, no plan to stop.  
Sent his servant to look toward the sea's rim,  
On the seventh try, a cloud appeared slim.  

"Tell Ahab, 'Get ready, rain is on the way,'"  
Sky turned black, clouds gathered, with no delay.  
Elijah tucked his cloak, with Yahweh's strength he ran,  
Before Ahab to Jezreel, speed like a whirlwind fan.
