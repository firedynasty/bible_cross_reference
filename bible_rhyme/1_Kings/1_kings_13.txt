A man of God from Judah came to Bethel land,  
Jeroboam at the altar, incense in his hand.  
He cried out loud, “Oh altar, hear Yahweh’s decree,  
A son named Josiah will bring priests to their knees."  

He gave a sign, the altar would split wide,  
Ashes pouring out, with nowhere to hide.  
The king heard the word, and stretched his hand,  
“Seize him!” he commanded, a fractured demand.  

But his hand shriveled up, frozen in place,  
Couldn't pull it back, left in disgrace.  
The altar split, ashes poured on the ground,  
The sign of Yahweh, His power renowned.  

The king pleaded, “Pray for me, let my hand be restored,”  
The man of God prayed, and Yahweh’s grace poured.  
The hand was healed, as good as new,  
The king offered gifts, “Come, refresh with my crew.”  

But the man of God said, “No way, no bread,  
Yahweh told me ‘fast,’ I’ll obey what He said.”  
He took a different path, obeying the route,  
Not returning the way he came, no doubt.  

An old prophet in Bethel heard the tale,  
His sons told him all, they didn’t fail.  
“Which way did he go?” the prophet did ask,  
On a donkey he rode, a determined task.  

He found the man under an oak tree's shade,  
“Come home with me, let’s feast,” he bade.  
But the man of God stayed firm to his word,  
“Yahweh’s command is all I’ve heard.”  

The old prophet lied, “An angel did speak,  
‘Bring him back, let him eat, let him drink.’”  
So he went back, breaking the vow,  
In the prophet's home, he ate bread now.  

At the table, Yahweh’s voice came to the liar,  
Spoke to the man, words of dire fire,  
“For breaking command, you’ll die on the road,  
Your body alone, not in father’s abode.”  

After the meal, he saddled his ride,  
A lion met him, and there he died.  
The donkey and lion stood by the slain,  
Men passed by, saw the body, felt the pain.  

The old prophet heard, “It’s the man, disobeyed,  
Yahweh's word, now he's slayed.”  
He found the body, lion still on guard,  
Took him home, the prophet's heart scarred.  

Laid him in his own grave, mournful cries,  
“Alas, my brother,” with teary eyes.  
He told his sons, “When my life is done,  
Lay me beside him, our bones as one.”  

The king didn't change, kept walking in sin,  
Made high place priests, the trouble within.  
This sin cut off Jeroboam's line,  
Destroyed his house, a judgment divine.
