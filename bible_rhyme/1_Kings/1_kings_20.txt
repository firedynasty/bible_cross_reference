Ben Hadad gathered his force, thirty-two kings by his side,  
Chariots and horses, ready for a fierce ride.  
Samaria was besieged, Ahab faced the threat,  
A message was sent, "Your silver, gold, don't forget.  

"Your wives, your children, the best you possess,  
Hand them over," was Ben Hadad's request.  
Ahab replied, "All I have, it's yours, my king,"  
But Ben Hadad's demands, they had a nasty ring.  

"You'll hand it all over, tomorrow they'll take,  
Search your houses, whatever they like, they'll rake."  
Ahab called his elders, "This man craves more,  
Our silver and gold, he seeks to score."  

Elders and people, they all took a stand,  
"Don't listen, don't consent to his demand."  
Ahab sent word, "The first I will do,  
But to all your demands, I can't be true."  

Ben Hadad replied, "The gods curse me more,  
If Samaria's dust isn't enough for my war."  
Ahab said, "Don't boast as you put armor on,  
Like you would when the battle's been won."  

Ben Hadad drank, prepared to attack,  
While a prophet approached Ahab, ready to crack.  
"Yahweh says, 'This multitude, in your hand I'll place,  
So you'll know I'm Yahweh, giver of grace.'"  

"By whom?" Ahab asked, and the prophet said,  
"By the young princes, they'll take the lead ahead."  
Two hundred thirty-two young men stood tall,  
And all of Israel answered the call.  

At noon they marched, while Ben Hadad drank,  
The princes went first, Ben Hadad's confidence sank.  
"If for peace or war, take them alive,"  
Yet Israel's youth began to strive.  

Each struck his man, Syrians fled and ran,  
Ben Hadad escaped, his royal plan in sand.  
Israel struck hard, horses and chariots fell,  
With a great slaughter, they made Syrians dwell.  

The prophet returned, said to Ahab, "Get strong,  
For Syria again will come before long."  
Syria's servants plotted, "We'll fight on the plain,  
Their god rules hills, but here we'll reign."  

New captains, new ranks, horses and chariots amassed,  
They faced Israel, the countryside was vast.  
Yet like young goats, Israel stood small,  
Against Syrian might, they'd answer the call.  

A man of God spoke, "Yahweh says to you,  
'Though they think I'm a hill god, I'll prove it's not true.'"  
For seven days they camped, then war they did wage,  
Israel killed a hundred thousand, set the stage.  

The rest fled, walls fell, twenty-seven thousand crushed,  
Ben Hadad hid, his escape was hushed.  
His servants said, "Beg for mercy, for Israel's kings show grace,"  
Wrapped in sackcloth, they sought Ahab's face.  

"Let me live," Ben Hadad pleaded with plea,  
Ahab called him "brother," set him free.  
Cities restored, treaties made anew,  
Ahab let him go, the deal bid adieu.  

A prophet disguised, a test for the king,  
In the battlefield's midst, a tale he did bring.  
"He was gone," the servant said, busy he had been,  
The king passed judgment, his own words came in.  

The prophet revealed, his disguise cast away,  
"Yahweh's word, your life now must pay."  
Ahab went home, sullen and mad,  
From victory's joy, his ending was sad.
