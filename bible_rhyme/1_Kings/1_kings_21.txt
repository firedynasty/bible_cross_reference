Naboth had a vineyard, right next to the king,  
Ahab saw it, thought, "A garden it could bring."  

"Let me have your vines," the king did plea,  
"I'll pay or trade, whichever suits thee."  

Naboth said, "No, it's my father's land,"  
Yahweh forbids the deal you demand."  

Ahab went home, sour and alone,  
Lay on his bed, face to the stone.  

Jezebel came, asked, "Why the long face?"  
Ahab replied, "Naboth's put me in place."  

Jezebel said, "Aren't you the king? Rise and dine,  
I'll make that vineyard yours, just give it time."  

She wrote letters, sealed with the king's name,  
To elders and nobles, playing a cruel game.  

"Proclaim a fast, set Naboth high,  
Have two scoundrels say he cursed the Most High."  

The city elders did what she planned,  
They stoned Naboth by Jezebel's hand.  

"He's dead," they said, "now claim your prize,"  
Jezebel told Ahab, "Open your eyes."  

Ahab rose up, to the vineyard he'd tread,  
But Elijah came with words to dread.  

Yahweh's voice spoke through the prophet's shout,  
"You've killed and taken, so here's your route."  

"Dogs licked Naboth’s blood, they'll lick yours too,  
For the evil things that you pursue."  

Ahab cried, "My enemy, you've found me now!"  
Elijah said, "Yes, for evil you allow."  

"I'll cut you off, your house will fall,  
Like Jeroboam's, no one left at all."  

"Jezebel's fate? Dogs will feast,  
In Jezreel, they'll eat her piece by piece."  

Ahab repented, tore his clothes in despair,  
Put on sackcloth, walked with care.  

Yahweh saw him humble, and said with grace,  
"No evil now, but in your son’s place."
