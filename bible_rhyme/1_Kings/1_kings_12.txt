Rehoboam came to Shechem, aiming to be crowned king,  
Israel gathered round, ready for the offering.  

Jeroboam heard the news while in Egypt, he had fled,  
Came back with the people, this is what he said:  

“Your father was harsh, gave us a heavy load,  
Lighten our burden, and our loyalty will be bestowed.”  

Rehoboam said, “Give me three days, then return,”  
He sought counsel, wisdom to discern.  

Old men advised, “Serve the people, speak with care,  
Do this today, and they'll always be there.”  

But he turned to the young, who grew by his side,  
“What should I say?” he asked, setting wisdom aside.  

The young men replied, “Tell them this tale,  
‘My finger’s thicker than my father’s scale.’  

‘My father whipped you, but with scorpions I'll reign,  
I'll add to your burdens, feel my disdain.’”  

On the third day, the people returned to the king’s court,  
He spoke roughly, ignoring wisdom's report.  

“My father was harsh, but I’ll be more severe,  
He used whips, but scorpions you'll fear.”  

The people saw the king wouldn’t heed their plea,  
Broke from David’s line, shouting, “Israel, be free!”  

Rehoboam ruled Judah, but the rest split away,  
Sent Adoram, but stoning marked the fray.  

Rehoboam fled, rushed to Jerusalem's gate,  
Israel rebelled, dividing the state.  

Jeroboam became king, Israel’s new pride,  
Only Judah remained by Rehoboam’s side.  

God spoke through Shemaiah, His command clear,  
“Don’t fight your brothers, this division must adhere.”  

Jeroboam built altars, calves of gold to bow,  
“Don’t go up to Jerusalem, worship here now.”  

In Bethel and Dan, idols stood tall,  
People sinned, heedless to God’s call.  

Priests from the people, not Levi’s line,  
Jeroboam’s feast, his own design.  

Sacrifices made in his crafted place,  
Worshiping idols, forgetting God’s grace.
