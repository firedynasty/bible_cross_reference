Joab saw the king's heart was on Absalom's track,  
He sent for a wise woman, to bring his son back.  

"Act like you're mourning," he whispered low,  
"Put on your mourning clothes, and let no oil flow."  

She went to the king, fell face to the floor,  
"Help me, O king!" she cried, wanting more.  

"What ails you?" he asked, with a curious eye,  
"I'm a widow," she said, "my husband's gone by."  

"I had two sons; in the field, they fought,  
One struck the other, and now he's caught."  

"The family wants blood, to kill the heir too,  
Quench my last coal, and what will I do?"  

The king said, "Go home; I'll give a command,  
No one will bother, just understand."  

She pleaded, "O king, let guilt be on me,  
Don't let the avenger my son seize."  

"Not one hair will fall," he swore with might,  
The woman then paused, ready to ignite.  

"Why don't you bring home your own son, banished away?  
We die, like spilled water, at the end of the day."  

The king caught the scheme, asked if Joab's hand,  
The woman replied, "Your wisdom is grand."  

So Joab was called, and the king decreed,  
"Bring Absalom back, fulfill the need."  

Joab bowed low, his thankfulness clear,  
Absalom returned, but kept the king's face near.  

Handsome Absalom, praised in the land,  
Cut his hair yearly, and grew as planned.  

Two years passed in a distant space,  
Still, Absalom did not see the king's face.  

He called Joab, twice with no heed,  
Set Joab's field ablaze, pushing his need.  

Joab confronted, "Why burn my field's share?"  
Absalom spoke out, "I want to clear the air."  

"Let me speak to the king, or let my life cease,"  
Joab took his word, sought the king's peace.  

Absalom bowed as he entered the room,  
The king kissed his son, resolving the gloom.
