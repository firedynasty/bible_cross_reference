In the springtime, when kings go to fight,  
David sent Joab, his army, a fearsome sight.  
They crushed Ammon and laid Rabbah under siege,  
But David stayed back, in Jerusalem’s peace.  

One evening, restlessly he roamed his roof high,  
Saw a woman bathing, caught his eye.  
"Who is this beauty?" he asked with intent,  
"Bathsheba," they told him, "Uriah's wife, heaven-sent."  

So David summoned her, and she came near,  
He knew her well, then she disappeared.  
Later she sent word, "I'm with child,"  
David’s heart raced, his thoughts ran wild.  

"Bring me Uriah," David decreed,  
Joab sent him back, fulfilling the need.  
David inquired of how Joab fared,  
"Go home, rest, let your worries be spared."  

But loyal Uriah stayed by the gate,  
With servants he slept, his duty innate.  
David was puzzled, called him back too,  
"Why not go home, enjoy what’s due?"  

"The ark, the troops, they’re in the fields," he pled,  
"I can’t enjoy comforts, while they shed."  
David persisted, "Stay one more day,"  
Made Uriah drink, but firm he'd stay.  

Next morning, David wrote with a heavy heart,  
Sent Uriah to Joab, played his part.  
"Put him upfront where the battle's hot,  
Then step back, leave him in that spot."  

Joab obeyed, at the city’s edge,  
Uriah fell—war’s tragic pledge.  
News came to David, the report was grim,  
"Uriah's gone, others too, fallen victims."  

David replied, "Let Joab press on,  
Strengthen the fight, until the city's gone."  
Bathsheba mourned, her tears were real,  
But soon joined David, a new life to feel.  

Yet in this tale, God's displeasure reigned,  
David’s choices marked, consequences gained.
