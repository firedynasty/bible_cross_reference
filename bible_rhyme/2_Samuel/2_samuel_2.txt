David asked the Lord, "Should I head to Judah's land?"  
God replied, "Yes, go up as planned."  

"Where to?" David asked, seeking the zone.  
God said, "To Hebron, make it your throne."  

David packed up with his wives by his side,  
Ahinoam and Abigail, off for the ride.  

His men came too, each with his kin,  
They settled in Hebron, their new life to begin.  

Judah's men came, crowned David their king,  
They spoke of Saul's burial, a noble thing.  

David sent word to Jabesh Gilead, blessed them so,  
"Yahweh's kindness and truth, may you know.  

For this great act, I reward your grace,  
Be strong, be brave, in this new place."  

Meanwhile, Abner brought Ishbosheth to reign,  
Over tribes of Israel, a kingdom to gain.  

But Judah held firm, David was their choice,  
Seven years and six months, they raised their voice.  

Abner and Joab by Gibeon's pool,  
Two armies sat, tensions cool.  

"Let young men duel," Abner did suggest,  
"Let them arise," Joab agreed to the test.  

Twelve from each side took the stand,  
Swords clashed, fell together, blood stained the land.  

Battle raged on, fierce was the day,  
David's servants drove Abner away.  

Asahel ran swift, like a gazelle on the chase,  
Abner turned, saw him, "Is that you?" he faced.  

"Turn aside," Abner urged, but Asahel pressed,  
"Why must I harm you, give Joab no rest?"  

But Asahel refused, so Abner struck hard,  
The spear went through, Asahel lay scarred.  

Joab and Abishai chased through the night,  
Abner called, "End this endless fight!"  

Joab replied, "Had you not spoken,  
The dawn would see no brother broken."  

The trumpet was blown, the fighting ceased,  
Each side retreated, seeking peace.  

Abner crossed Jordan, back to his camp,  
Joab paused, counted, nineteen fell in the stamp.  

Three hundred sixty of Abner's men lay,  
Asahel was buried at Bethlehem next day.  

Joab's men marched through the night,  
Back to Hebron, with dawn's first light.
