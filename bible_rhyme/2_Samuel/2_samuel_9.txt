David wondered, "Is there anyone left from Saul's line?  
Jonathan's sake, I'm showing kindness, it's time to shine."  

A servant named Ziba, from Saul's house, came near.  
"Are you Ziba?" the king asked. "Yes, I'm here."  

"Is there anyone left from Saul's family, I pray?  
To show God's kindness, in a generous way?"  

"Jonathan has a son," Ziba said, "though he's lame."  
"In Machir's house, Lo Debar, that's his name."  

So King David sent for him, brought him from afar,  
From Machir's house, Lo Debar, under David's star.  

Mephibosheth bowed low, respect on his face.  
David called, "Mephibosheth," in the king's place.  

"Don't be afraid," David said, "kindness you'll see,  
For Jonathan's sake, your father's land, I decree.  

At my table, you'll eat, bread without strife,  
Restored lands to you, a secure, honored life."  

"What am I," he asked, "a dead dog you'd seek?"  
Yet David showed kindness to the humble and meek.  

To Ziba, the king said, "All Saul had, it's done,  
For Mephibosheth, your master's son."  

"Work the land, bring the harvest in hand,  
But Mephibosheth eats at my table, understand?"  

Ziba agreed, with fifteen sons, twenty men,  
"All you command, I'll fulfill, again and again."  

Mephibosheth dined, like a king's son, with pride,  
In Jerusalem, at the table, he'd abide.  

His son Mica watched his father, glad to see,  
How kindness and grace from the king set him free.  

Though lame in his feet, Mephibosheth's heart soared,  
For at David's table, he was always restored.
