Nathan came to David, with a tale to unfold,  
About two men in a city, one rich, one with pockets cold.  
The rich man had flocks, more than he could ever need,  
The poor man just one lamb, his heart's only feed.  

The lamb grew with him, like a daughter it stayed,  
A traveler came, and the rich man betrayed.  
He took the poor man's lamb, prepared it for the guest,  
David burned with anger, his fury didn't rest.  

"As Yahweh lives," David shouted out,  
"The man who did this deserves to be knocked out.  
Restore the lamb four times, for this heartless act,  
No pity in his heart, that's a matter of fact."  

Nathan pointed to David, "You are the man," he said,  
"Yahweh's words to you, listen and dread.  
I made you king, took you from Saul's hand,  
Gave you your master's house, and all the land."  

"Yet you struck down Uriah, took his wife as your own,  
Evil in Yahweh's sight, now seeds of trouble are sown.  
The sword won’t leave your house, for the wrongs you've done,  
Your wives given to your neighbor, in the sight of the sun."  

David confessed, "I have sinned against Yahweh's name,"  
Nathan replied, "Your sin’s put away, but sorrow still came.  
The child shall die, for bringing shame to Yahweh's fame."  

David mourned and fasted, pleading on the ground,  
But on the seventh day, silence was the sound.  
His servants feared the truth, their whispers caught his ear,  
"Is the child dead?" David asked, his voice sincere.  

"He is dead," they replied, sorrow in their tone,  
David rose, washed, worshiped, on Yahweh’s throne.  
He ate and explained, "Who knows if grace would save,  
But now he’s gone, I shall join him in the grave."  

Comforted Bathsheba, and they bore a son,  
Named Solomon, Yahweh's love to him was won.  
Nathan came again, and Jedidiah was his name,  
For Yahweh's sake, a child of great fame.  

Meanwhile, Joab fought and Rabbah fell,  
He sent for David, with victories to tell.  
David gathered troops, took the city’s crown,  
Placed it on his head, wore Ammon’s renown.  

He brought back the spoils, and with justice it was done,  
Returned to Jerusalem, battles won.  
David’s story marked by sorrow and fight,  
A tale of power, repentance, and the quest for what's right.
