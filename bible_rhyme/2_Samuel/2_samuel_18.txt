David lined his troops, counting each man,  
Captains of thousands, hundreds, ready to stand.  

Divided the forces, Joab took his third,  
Abishai and Ittai, each got a word.  

David said, "I'll join you, I'll lead the fight,"  
But the people replied, "Your worth's a greater height."  

"If we flee or fall, they won't pursue,  
Stay back in the city, and we'll fight for you."  

David agreed, "I'll do what you say,"  
Stood by the gate as his troops marched away.  

"Deal gently with Absalom," the king's command,  
Echoed to captains, across the land.  

The battle unfolded in Ephraim's woods,  
Israel's forces fell, David's men withstood.  

The trees took more lives than swords in hand,  
Absalom caught, between earth and sky, he'd stand.  

A mule beneath an oak, his hair caught tight,  
A soldier saw this and reported the sight.  

Joab's anger flared, "Why didn't you strike?"  
"Ten silver coins, a sash, your prize alike."  

The man replied, "Even a thousand's no pay,  
Would make me betray what the king did say."  

Joab, impatient, took three darts,  
Pierced Absalom's chest, stopped his heart.  

Joab blew the trumpet, called the troops back,  
Absalom's end was a pit, stones stacked.  

Ahimaaz wanted to run with the word,  
Joab held him back, "Today’s not preferred."  

Sent the Cushite instead, to bring the tale,  
But Ahimaaz insisted, fast on the trail.  

David waited, between two gates,  
Two runners came, sealing Absalom's fate.  

Ahimaaz first, face to ground, he cried,  
"Yahweh’s delivered those who defied."  

"But what of Absalom?" David pressed,  
"I saw some chaos, but know not the rest."  

The Cushite arrived, spoke with haste,  
"Your enemies are gone, Absalom faced."  

The king's sorrow deep, he climbed alone,  
Wept for his son, "Absalom, my own!"
