Yahweh's anger burned, David felt the call,  
"Go count Israel and Judah, count them all."  

The king turned to Joab, his captain bold,  
"Survey the tribes, let the numbers be told."  

Joab questioned, "Why delight in this task?"  
But David's command was firm, Joab couldn't ask.  

Crossing Jordan, they began the quest,  
Through valleys and cities, they wouldn't rest.  

From Aroer to Jazer, through Gilead they roamed,  
To Tyre and Sidon, then back home they combed.  

Nine months and twenty days it took to see,  
Joab returned with numbers, as many as could be.  

Israel held eight hundred thousand strong,  
While Judah had five hundred thousand all along.  

David's heart struck with regret and remorse,  
"I've sinned greatly, Yahweh, forgive my course."  

Yahweh sent Gad with a choice so grim,  
"Three things I offer, choose your fate within."  

"Seven years of famine, or flee from foes?  
Or three days' pestilence, which path do you chose?"  

David in distress cried, "Let me fall into Yahweh's hand,  
For His mercy is great, greater than any land."  

A pestilence struck, seventy thousand fell,  
From Dan to Beersheba, the toll did tell.  

The angel poised over Jerusalem's height,  
But Yahweh's compassion halted the fight.  

"It is enough," said Yahweh, "withdraw your might,"  
The angel stayed his hand, spared the city's sight.  

David saw the angel, his heart filled with dread,  
"I'm the sinner, not them, let me pay instead."  

Gad came with news, "Build an altar to Yahweh,  
On Araunah's floor, make the plague go away."  

Araunah offered all, but David said, "No,  
I must buy it, for offerings must cost me, so."  

Fifty shekels paid, the altar was raised,  
And Yahweh heard, the land was saved.  

David's sacrifice brought peace, the plague was ceased,  
Israel's suffering ended, and their fears released.
