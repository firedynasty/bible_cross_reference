The king was weepin', Absalom's gone,  
Joab heard, said, "The mourning's on."  
Victory turned sour, the people sneaked away,  
Like soldiers ashamed at the end of the day.  

David cried, "My son, Absalom, my son!"  
Grief in his voice, a sorrowful tone.  
Joab stepped in with a hard truth to share,  
"Your people saved you, show them you care."  

"Those who love you feel betrayed," Joab said,  
"Rise up, speak, or they'll leave you for dead."  
David listened, went to the gate,  
Sat where folks could see him, not too late.  

Israel divided, tribes in dispute,  
"David saved us, now he’s mute."  
Judah lagged, but David's call rang,  
"Bring me home," to the elders he sang.  

"Brothers, bone of my bone," he cried,  
"Why are you last?" he questioned with pride.  
Amasa's named captain, Joab's set aside,  
Judah united, standing with their guide.  

David crossed the Jordan, Judah by his side,  
Shimei begged, for past sins he tried.  
Abishai said, "Shouldn't he pay?"  
David replied, "No deaths today."  

Mephibosheth came, unkempt and sincere,  
Explained his absence, his loyalty clear.  
"Divide the land," said David, "it’s fair."  
But Mephibosheth, for peace, didn't care.  

Barzillai, aged, served the king in need,  
David offered him a reward indeed.  
Barzillai declined, but Chimham went,  
With blessings and thanks, David was sent.  

Judah had brought the king across,  
Israel questioned, felt at a loss.  
"Why did Judah take him, what’s fair?"  
But fierce words flew, tension in the air.
