In the house of David, tension's brewing in the air,  
Absalom had a sis, Tamar, beauty rare.  
Amnon, her half-brother, loved her deep and true,  
But the love made him sick, didn't know what to do.  

His friend Jonadab, sly and wise with a plan,  
Asked, "Why so sad, son of the king, my man?"  
Amnon confessed, "I love Tamar, Absalom's kin,"  
Jonadab said, "Here's how you draw her in."  

"Lie down, act sick, let the king come and see,  
Ask for Tamar to bake you some food personally."  
Amnon played his part, the king was deceived,  
Tamar came to bake, and Amnon's plot achieved.  

She kneaded the dough, made cakes with grace,  
But Amnon had plans that would bring disgrace.  
He sent the others out, asked her to stay,  
But when he spoke his mind, Tamar said, "No way!"  

"Don't force me, brother, this shouldn't be,  
Talk to the king, he'll not withhold me," she plea.  
But Amnon ignored, his strength he abused,  
A tragic act, Tamar was bruised.  

After his crime, his love turned to hate,  
He threw her out, sealing her tragic fate.  
Tamar tore her robe, ashes on her head,  
With anguish and sorrow, her tears she shed.  

Absalom saw her pain, his anger grew,  
But told her, "Sister, hold your peace, let it stew."  
King David heard, his anger burned,  
But silent he stayed, no justice discerned.  

Two years passed, Absalom plotted his scheme,  
Invited all David's sons to a sheep-shearing scene.  
He asked the king to let Amnon attend,  
Pressing for a chance to bring Amnon's end.  

Absalom's men struck when Amnon's heart was merry,  
The king's sons fled, the news they did carry.  
David mourned, his garments torn in despair,  
But Jonadab assured him, only Amnon's not there.  

Absalom fled to Geshur, seeking refuge and space,  
While David longed for him, in a sorrowful embrace.  
Three years passed, David's heart began to mend,  
His grief for Amnon faded, but his longing for Absalom never did end.
