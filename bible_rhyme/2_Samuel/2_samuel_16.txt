David walked just past the hill, what did he see?  
Ziba with donkeys, saddled, waiting patiently.  

Two hundred loaves, and raisins in a heap,  
Summer fruits, wine, provisions to keep.  

The king asked Ziba, "What’s all this for?"  
Ziba replied, “It’s for the king’s own, evermore.  

Donkeys to ride, bread and fruit to eat,  
Wine for the faint, a wilderness treat.”  

“Where’s your master’s son?” David sought to know.  
“In Jerusalem,” said Ziba, “thinking he’ll regrow.”  

David then declared, “All his goods are yours.”  
Ziba bowed low, “Favor in your sight, restores.”  

On to Bahurim, David went, eyes on the scene,  
Shimei from Saul’s house, cursing, growing mean.  

Stones he cast, cursing with spite,  
David’s men to the left and right.  

“You man of blood, wicked and foul!”  
Yahweh’s justice, for Saul’s house you prowl.”  

Abishai pleaded, “Let me strike him down.”  
But David, wise, said, “God’s words are profound.”  

“My own son seeks my life, let him be,  
Perhaps Yahweh’s eyes will see this misery.”  

David moved on, Shimei cursed and threw,  
Dust and stones, as the weary troop passed through.  

To Jerusalem, Absalom came with might,  
Hushai greeted, “Long live the king!” in sight.  

Absalom asked, “Is this how you treat a friend?”  
Hushai replied, “I serve Yahweh's chosen to the end.”  

“Give advice,” Absalom asked Ahithophel,  
“Take your father’s concubines, make Israel tell.”  

So a tent was spread, in sight of all,  
Ahithophel’s counsel, like divine call.  

Momentum built, plans in play,  
David’s world shifted, a heavy day.
