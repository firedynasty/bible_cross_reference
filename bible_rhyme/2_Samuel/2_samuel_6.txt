David gathered thirty thousand, a chosen mighty band,  
To bring God's ark from Judah's land, a task so grand.  

They set the ark on a cart, new and bright,  
Uzzah and Ahio drove it, guiding it right.  

Instruments played, cypress wood, harps in hand,  
Tambourines, cymbals—music filled the land.  

But at Nacon's floor, Uzzah reached out bold,  
The cattle stumbled, the ark's touch was cold.  

God's anger burned, Uzzah fell, struck down,  
David, displeased, named the place with a frown.  

"How can Yahweh's ark come to me?" David cried,  
Afraid, he left it, in Obed-Edom's house to abide.  

For three months it stayed, blessings poured down,  
David heard the news, joy spread around the town.  

He went to bring the ark, joyfully to his city,  
Six paces in, they sacrificed with heartfelt pity.  

David danced with might, in linen ephod he spun,  
Shouting, trumpets blared, a celebration begun.  

Michal watched from the window, despising the scene,  
As David danced before Yahweh, heart open and keen.  

The ark found its place in a tent set with care,  
Burnt offerings, peace offerings, David's prayer.  

To all the people, bread, dates, raisins as a feast,  
A blessing in Yahweh's name, joy increased.  

Michal scorned, "How glorious today, your display!"  
But David replied, "Before Yahweh, I'd dance this way."  

"I'll be more vile, in my own sight less,  
Yet the maids will honor me, my joy confess."  

And Michal, childless, lived on through her days,  
As David danced on, giving Yahweh his praise.
