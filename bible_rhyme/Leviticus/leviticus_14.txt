Yahweh spoke to Moses, laying it down clear,  
"Here's the law for a leper when cleansing is near.  
Bring him to the priest, out from the camp's hold,  
The priest checks him out, sees if the plague's been controlled.  

If leprosy's healed, then here's what you'll need:  
Two clean birds, wood of cedar, scarlet, and hyssop proceed.  
One bird gets killed in an earthen jar, under running stream,  
The living bird's dipped with scarlet and hyssop, to seal the dream.  

The priest sprinkles the leper seven times to proclaim,  
"You're clean now," he says, and lets the living bird take aim,  
Flying into the open field, free as a breeze,  
The leper washes clothes, shaves, bathes, and feels the peace.  

Seven days outside his tent, then on the seventh day,  
Shaves off all his hair, washes, cleans, he's okay.  
By day eight, it's time for lambs and flour untold,  
As offerings mixed with oil, a ritual bold.  

The priest sets the scene at the Tent of Meeting's door,  
Waves the lamb for trespass, it's all part of the core.  
Kill the lamb for trespass, in the holy place it goes,  
Blood on ear, thumb, and toe, that's how the ritual flows.  

Oil's poured in the palm, sprinkled seven times before,  
Some on the ear, thumb, toe, mixing blood and oil more.  
Rest of the oil anoints his head, a holy sign,  
Atonement made, cleansed and clean by divine design.  

If he's short on cash, brings what he can afford,  
Two doves or pigeons, offerings to the Lord.  
The priest makes atonement, with oil and birds in flight,  
The cleansing ritual's done, everything's set right.  

Yahweh spoke again, laying it down plain,  
"When you enter Canaan, and mildew brings pain,  
The house owner tells the priest, 'There's a plague in sight,'  
Empty the house, inspect it by daylight.  

If greenish-red streaks deeper than the stone,  
Seal the house seven days, leave it alone.  
Return on the seventh, see if the plague has grown,  
Stones are pulled, and the house is overthrown.  

Scrape inside, mortar out to an unclean place,  
New stones, fresh plaster, give it a new face.  
If mildew returns, breaking out full force,  
The house comes down, timber and stones, no remorse.  

But if the house stays clean, with no plague in the air,  
Pronounce it clean, for the plague’s no longer there.  
Two birds, cedar, scarlet, cleanse the place,  
Blood and water, set the house in grace.  

The living bird flies free, into the open field,  
Atonement made, the house is healed.  
These are the laws for leprosy and itch,  
For garments and houses, whether clean or rich.  

Swelling, scabs, spots, this is how you know,  
When it's unclean or clean, let the law show."
