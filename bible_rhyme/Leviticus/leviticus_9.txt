On the eighth day, Moses set the scene,  
Called Aaron, sons, elders — a holy routine.  
"Bring a calf and a ram, pure as can be,  
Lay them before Yahweh, as an offering decree.  

Tell Israel, 'A goat for your sins,  
A calf and a lamb, pure, without sins.  
A bull and a ram, for peace they shall bring,  
Yahweh appears, His glory will swing.'"  

They gathered 'round the Tent, congregation was tight,  
Moses spoke, "Do this, Yahweh's glory will ignite."  
He turned to Aaron, said, "Near to the altar step,  
Make atonement for you and the people's debt."  

Aaron approached, the calf he did slay,  
His sons gave him blood, by the altar it lay.  
Finger dipped, blood on horns, base soaked deep,  
Fat and organs burned, Yahweh's command he'd keep.  

Meat and skin sent outside, with fire they'd meet,  
Burnt offering slain, blood on the altar complete.  
Piece by piece, head to flame,  
Washed the parts, the altar they claimed.  

Goat for the people, sin offering's call,  
Just like the first, offered for all.  
Burnt offering up, ordinance set,  
Meal offering burned, morning's duet.  

Bull and ram slaughtered for peace,  
Blood sprinkled 'round, offerings didn't cease.  
Fat tails, kidneys, all the rest,  
On the altar, the fat did rest.  

Wave that breast and thigh high,  
Before Yahweh, watched sky.  
Aaron lifted hands, blessings did flow,  
From sin, burnt, and peace, blessings did grow.  

Into the Tent, Moses and Aaron both went,  
Came out, blessed the crowd, in Yahweh's glory they were sent.  
Fire from Yahweh, offering consumed,  
The people shouted, faces to the ground they were doomed.
