Yahweh spoke to Moses and Aaron with might,  
"If a man has a spot or a scab that's bright,  
Bring him to Aaron or one of his sons,  
Let the priest decide what must be done.  

Is the hair white? Is the plague deep?  
Pronounce him unclean, don’t let him keep.  
But if the spot isn't deeper and hair stays the same,  
Seven days alone, the priest will proclaim.  

On the seventh day, check him again,  
Is it stable, or does it remain?  
If it rests, another seven days,  
Watch it close, in all its ways.  

If it fades, then he's clean, wash his gear,  
But if it spreads, the priest must steer.  
Look again, if it's white, but flesh is raw,  
Chronic leprosy, beyond nature's law.  

When the skin's all white, from head to feet,  
If it's all covered, he's clean in defeat.  
Yet raw flesh says uncleanness stays,  
The priest will judge in all these ways.  

Boils, burns, and spots so white,  
Priest examines by day or night.  
If the hair turns or the plague digs deep,  
Declare unclean, let him not weep.  

On the skin, if spreads it might,  
Unclean he is, until it's right.  
But if it stays or fades away,  
He is clean, the priest will say.  

In the beard or on the head,  
Yellow thin hair, by priest is read.  
If the itch spreads not, and black hairs grow,  
He's clean once more, let him know.  

If it's just a harmless rash,  
He is clean, no need to dash.  
Bald or forehead without a scare,  
Clean he is, let him wear.  

But red-white plague on the bald head found,  
Unclean he is, priest will pronounce around.  
The leper cries, ‘Unclean! Unclean!’  
Dwelling alone, unseen, unseen.  

Garments too, with plague so green,  
Priest examines, seven days intervene.  
If it spreads, burn the clothes in fire,  
If it fades, wash—higher and higher.  

Mildew on threads, it's a test,  
Priest decides, clean or unrest.  
This is the law for spots and mold,  
The priest determines, truth be told."
