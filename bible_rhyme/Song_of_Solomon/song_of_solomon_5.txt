I stepped into my garden, with my bride by my side,  
Gathered my myrrh, spices, honey to abide.  
Drank my wine and milk, feeling satisfied,  
"Eat, friends! Drink deeply, be unified."

While I slept, my heart stayed awake,  
Heard my beloved, a soft knock to make.  
“Open, my love, my dove, in this night’s mist,  
For my head’s soaked from the dew’s gentle kiss.”

I’d shed my robe, should I wear it again?  
Washed my feet, should I soil them in vain?  
He reached through the latch, my heart gave chase,  
I rose to him, my hands with myrrh did grace.

Opened the door, found him gone away,  
My heart sank deep, heard his voice sway.  
I searched, no sight, my calls met air,  
The city’s watchmen found me in despair.

They struck me down, bruised my plea,  
Took my cloak, left me empty.  
Daughters of Jerusalem, hear my call,  
Tell him I’m faint with love, that’s all.

“What makes your beloved greater than the rest,  
Fairest of women, say why he’s the best?”  
He stands out in ten thousand, pure gold his head,  
Hair dark like a raven, where dreams are fed.

Eyes like doves, by waters they gleam,  
Cheeks spice-laden, perfumed in a dream.  
Lips like lilies, dripping myrrh,  
Hands are golden, beryl they stir.

Body ivory, sapphire inlay,  
Legs of marble, gold in display.  
He’s like Lebanon, cedars tall,  
Mouth sweet as honey, he’s my all.

This is my beloved, my friend, my song,  
Daughters of Jerusalem, to him I belong.
