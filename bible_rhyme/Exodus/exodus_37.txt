Bezalel got to work, with acacia wood in hand,  
Building the ark, the finest in the land.  
Two and a half cubits long, a cubit and a half wide,  
Pure gold inside and out, molding gleaming with pride.  

Four golden rings he cast, at the ark's four feet,  
Poles of acacia wood, with gold complete.  
In those rings, he slid the poles to bear,  
A mercy seat of gold, shining fair.  

Two cherubim of gold, wings spread wide,  
Faces toward each other, by the mercy seat they abide.  
Crafted from one piece, at each end they stand,  
Guardians of glory, crafted by skilled hand.  

Next, he made a table, acacia wood its base,  
Two cubits long, a cubit wide, a cubit and a half in space.  
Overlaid with gold, a border around,  
Four rings on the corners, to carry it when bound.  

Dishes, spoons, bowls, pitchers, all gold shining bright,  
Golden vessels for the table, a marvelous sight.  
The lamp stand next, of gold so pure,  
Beaten work of beauty, every detail sure.  

Six branches out each side, cups like almond blooms,  
Buds and flowers crafted, filling the room.  
Four cups on the stand, buds beneath each pair,  
A masterpiece of gold, crafted with such care.  

Seven lamps, snuffers, all of gold's embrace,  
A talent's worth of pure gold, in every place.  
The altar of incense, acacia wood and gold,  
Square and splendid, a sight to behold.  

Rings on its sides, poles to carry there,  
Anointing oil and incense, scents fill the air.  
Bezalel made it all, with hands skilled and true,  
Every piece was crafted, in gold's radiant hue.
