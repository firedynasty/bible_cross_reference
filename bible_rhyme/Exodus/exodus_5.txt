Moses and Aaron stepped to Pharaoh's throne,  
Saying, "Yahweh says, 'Let my people go alone.'"  
Pharaoh snapped back, "Who's this Yahweh you bring?  
I don't know his voice, I won't change a thing."  

They pleaded, "It's the God of the Hebrews we meet,  
Let us journey three days, make sacrifices complete."  
But the king scoffed, "Moses, Aaron, stop this charade,  
Get back to your burdens, this work can't be delayed."  

Pharaoh saw the multitude, said, "So many in sight,  
Yet you want them resting? You'll work day and night!"  
That same day, a command, harsh and clear,  
"No more straw for bricks, let them find it far and near."  

"Keep the quota high, don't let them retreat,  
They're just lazy, crying out to Yahweh, a false beat."  
"Pile the work high, ignore their lying call,  
Let them sweat hard, let the heavy burdens fall."  

The taskmasters went, their orders precise,  
"Pharaoh says no straw, seek it, pay the price."  
Scattered and frantic, they searched the land,  
Yet the quota remained, a harsh demand.  

The officers beaten, questioned with might,  
"Why's the quota unmet, both day and night?"  
They cried to Pharaoh, "This isn't fair,  
No straw, yet bricks, we're in despair."  

Pharaoh replied, "Lazy, that's what you are,  
Talking 'bout Yahweh, aiming too far."  
"Get back to work, no straw, same load,  
Bricks won't lessen, this is your road."  

The children of Israel saw their plight,  
Meeting Moses and Aaron in the dim light.  
"You made us stink, a curse to bear,  
Pharaoh's wrath, now our nightmare."  

Moses turned back, cried out to the Lord,  
"Why this trouble, why this discord?  
I spoke in your name, now trouble's grown,  
Your rescue's delayed, we're all alone."
