Make the altar of acacia wood, precise and square,  
Five by five cubits, three cubits in the air.  

Four corners with horns, one piece it's got,  
Overlay it all with bronze, make it hot.  

Pots for ashes, and shovels too,  
Basins, hooks, fire pans, all in bronze hue.  

A bronze net grating, with rings on the four,  
Underneath the ledge, halfway up, but no more.  

Acacia poles, overlaid with bronze sheen,  
Slide them in the rings, keep it mobile and clean.  

Hollow with planks as on the mountain shown,  
Crafted with care, the details are known.  

The tabernacle court, stretch wide and long,  
South side hangs linen, hundred cubits strong.  

Twenty pillars stand, sockets bronze and bright,  
Hooks and fillets silver, gleaming in the light.  

North side matches, hundred cubits grand,  
Bronze holds the pillars, silver hooks in hand.  

West side's fifty, pillars ten will stand,  
East mirrors west, a courtyard well spanned.  

Gate hangings fifteen, three pillars in line,  
On each side they stand, in sockets they shine.  

Gate screen of twenty, blue, purple, scarlet blend,  
Embroidered work, four pillars at each end.  

Silver-filleted pillars, bronze sockets all round,  
Court's a hundred long, fifty wide, tightly bound.  

All instruments and pins, bronze-crafted to serve,  
Every detail precise, nothing left to swerve.  

Command the children, bring oil pure and bright,  
Keep the lamp burning, through the long night.  

In the Tent of Meeting, Aaron stands tall,  
Evening till morning, he serves the call.  

A lasting statute, generations to hold,  
For Israel's children, the story unfolds.
