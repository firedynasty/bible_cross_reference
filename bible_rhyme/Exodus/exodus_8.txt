Yahweh spoke to Moses, "Head to Pharaoh's place,  
Tell him, 'Yahweh says, let my people embrace.'  
If he refuses, frogs will invade,  
They'll swarm his land, no place unscathed.  

Rivers will teem, frogs in his bed,  
In the ovens, the kneading troughs, where servants tread.  
Moses, to Aaron, "Stretch your rod high,  
Frogs will rise, cover Egypt, low and sky."  

Aaron's hand stretched, frogs covered the land,  
The magicians matched with a similar stand.  
Pharaoh called out, "Plead with Yahweh for relief,  
Take these frogs away, end this grief."  

Moses replied, "You choose the time,  
For frogs to leave, keep the rivers in line."  
Pharaoh said, "Tomorrow," Moses agreed,  
"So you know there's none like Yahweh indeed."  

Frogs retreated, houses cleared,  
Gathered in heaps, the land was smeared.  
But Pharaoh's heart hardened, he wouldn't relent,  
As Yahweh foretold, his stubbornness spent.  

"Tell Aaron," Yahweh said, "Dust to lice,  
Strike the earth, make Egypt pay the price."  
Aaron's rod struck, lice filled the air,  
Magicians failed, for this they couldn't prepare.  

"This is God's finger," the magicians declared,  
But Pharaoh's heart hardened, he wasn't scared.  
"Go to Pharaoh," Yahweh called,  
"Let my people go, or flies will befall."  

If you refuse, flies will swarm,  
But Goshen's land will remain warm.  
Separate my people, show my might,  
Tomorrow this sign, bring day from night."  

Flies invaded Pharaoh's domain,  
Land corrupted, unbearable pain.  
"Go and sacrifice," Pharaoh said,  
"In this land, fulfill your dread."  

Moses replied, "Not here, not right,  
Three days' journey, out of sight."  
Pharaoh allowed, but with a plea,  
"Pray for me, don't venture too free."  

Moses said, "I'll pray, flies will cease,  
But no deceit, let my people find peace."  
Moses prayed, Yahweh complied,  
Flies vanished, air purified.  

Yet Pharaoh's heart, once more turned cold,  
He wouldn't let them go, as the story's told.
