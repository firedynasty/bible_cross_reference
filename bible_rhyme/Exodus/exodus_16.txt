In the wilderness of Sin, the Israelites roam,  
Murmurs rise, hunger's their new home.  

"We wish we had died with full pots in Egypt's land,  
Brought here to starve, this isn't what we planned."  

God spoke to Moses, "Bread from the sky I'll send,  
Gather it daily, see if they follow my command."  

On day six, gather double, prepare it right,  
In the evening, meat, in the morning, bread's in sight.  

Moses and Aaron to the people said,  
"Tonight you'll know, God brought you out, not dead.  

In the morning, Yahweh's glory you'll see,  
Your complaints aren't against us, but against the Almighty."  

To Yahweh's glory, they turned their eyes,  
A cloud appeared, a divine surprise.  

God to Moses, "I've heard their cries,  
Meat in the evening, bread 'neath morning skies.  

Then they'll know, I am their God indeed."  
Quail covered the camp, satisfied their need.  

Morning dew vanished, left a mystery thing,  
Small, round, like frost, a new blessing to bring.  

"What is it?" they asked, not knowing what they see,  
"Manna," said Moses, "God's gift it shall be."  

"Gather what you need, an omer per head,  
Take it to your tents, by God's law be led."  

Some gathered more, some gathered less,  
But all had enough, God's provision no guess.  

"Don't leave it till morning," Moses warned the crowd,  
Some didn't listen, worms and stench spoke loud.  

Six days you gather, rest on day seven,  
A holy Sabbath, a gift from heaven.  

Some went out on the seventh, found none,  
God said, "Obey, the command can't be undone."  

The name was Manna, white like coriander seed,  
Tasting sweet as honey, fulfilling every need.  

"Keep an omer for generations to see,  
The bread with which in the desert I fed thee."  

Aaron took a pot, filled it with Manna,  
Kept it as a testimony, a divine banner.  

Forty years of Manna till Canaan land,  
From Egypt's clutches, held by God's hand.
