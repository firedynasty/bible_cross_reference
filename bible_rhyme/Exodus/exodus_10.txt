Yahweh spoke to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh's place,  
I've hardened his heart, for a sign of my grace.  

Tell your sons and their sons about what I’ve done,  
In Egypt, the signs that have only begun."  

Moses and Aaron, they faced Pharaoh bold,  
"This is what Yahweh says, the God of old:  

'How long will you refuse to humble down?  
Let my people go, stop playing the clown.  

If not, tomorrow locusts will arrive,  
Cover your land, nothing will survive.  

They'll eat up what's left from the hail's harsh blow,  
Fill every house, the despair will grow.'"  

He turned on his heel, left Pharaoh alone,  
His servants cried, "Let them go, let them roam!  

Don’t you see Egypt's in ruins, in tears?"  
Pharaoh brought them back, "Tell me, who goes and who stays here?"  

Moses said, "The young and old, flocks and herds,  
Everyone goes, for a feast with Yahweh's words."  

Pharaoh sneered, "Yahweh be with you, but no,  
Just the men can go, the rest won’t show."  

Out from his presence, they were driven away,  
Yahweh then spoke, "Stretch your hand, don’t delay."  

Moses raised his rod, the wind blew east,  
Locusts swarmed, a darkening feast.  

They covered the earth, left nothing green,  
A sight Egypt had never seen.  

Pharaoh pleaded, "Forgive my sin,  
Pray to Yahweh, bring light back in."  

Moses prayed, the west wind blew,  
The locusts vanished, a cleansing through.  

But Pharaoh's heart was hard as stone,  
Still, he wouldn't let them roam.  

Yahweh said, "Stretch to the sky,  
Darkness shall fall, the light will die."  

Three days of black, no one could see,  
But Israel's lights shone, they were free.  

Pharaoh called out, "Take your kids and go,  
But leave your herds, and let them show."  

Moses replied, "Every hoof must come,  
For Yahweh's call, we might need some."  

Pharaoh's heart stayed hard, he wouldn't bend,  
"See my face again, it’s the end."  

Moses said, "Alright, that's your decree,  
I won't see your face, let it be."
