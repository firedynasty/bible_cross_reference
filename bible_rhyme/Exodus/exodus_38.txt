Acacia wood crafted into an altar square,  
Five by five by three, standing tall with care.  

Horns at the corners, bronzed and bold,  
With vessels of bronze, the story's retold.  

A grating of bronze, network designed,  
Four rings cast, perfectly aligned.  

Poles of wood, bronzed in display,  
Fit in the rings to carry away.  

Bronze basin from mirrors, polished and clean,  
Where ministering women once were seen.  

Court of linen, south side's long,  
Twenty pillars standing strong.  

North side matches, a hundred feet wide,  
Pillars and sockets, harmony abide.  

West side, fifty cubits of grace,  
Silver hooks on pillars in place.  

East side's entry, fifty cubits long,  
Each gate adorned, a beautiful song.  

Screen embroidered with colors so grand,  
Blue, purple, and scarlet, all at hand.  

Materials counted, the Tabernacle's weight,  
Moses directed, Ithamar's state.  

Bezalel and Oholiab, skillful and true,  
Crafting all that the Lord had them do.  

Gold, silver, and bronze measured right,  
Sanctuary's beauty, a glorious sight.  

Sockets and hooks, each in their spot,  
Under the Tabernacle, every lot.  

Bronze altar, grating, and vessels all,  
Pins of the Tabernacle, standing tall.
