Bezalel and Oholiab, their skills divine,  
Crafting the sanctuary, every line.  
God gave wisdom, hearts stirred to create,  
Each wise worker, their hands wouldn't wait.  

Morning offerings, the people did bring,  
More than enough, so the craftsmen could sing.  
Moses said, “Stop! No more needs to come,  
The work is complete, and then some.”  

Fine twined linens, blue, purple, and red,  
Curtains with cherubim skillfully spread.  
Twenty-eight cubits long, four cubits wide,  
Five curtains together, seamlessly tied.  

Loops of blue, fifty each side,  
Golden clasps, the curtains unify and abide.  
Curtains of goat hair to cover the tent,  
Eleven pieces with purpose bent.  

Covers of ram skins, dyed deep red,  
Sea cow hides placed up overhead.  
Boards of acacia, tall and grand,  
Standing upright, by wisdom's hand.  

Sockets of silver, holding strong,  
Every board in place, where they belong.  
Bars of acacia, overlaid with gold,  
Holding the structure, mighty and bold.  

Veil of blue, purple, scarlet hue,  
Cherubim dancing, skillful and true.  
Pillars of acacia, overlaid with gold,  
Silver sockets, the structure controlled.  

Screen for the entrance, colors displayed,  
Embroidered with care, each stitch carefully laid.  
Five golden pillars stand in a row,  
Bronze sockets anchoring, steady and slow.  

The work complete, each detail precise,  
A tabernacle worthy, a holy device.
