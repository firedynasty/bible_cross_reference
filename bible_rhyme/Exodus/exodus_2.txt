A man from Levi's clan took a Levi wife,  
She bore a son, hid him to save his life.  
Three months on, she could no longer hide,  
A basket with tar and pitch, she set him inside.  

Placed among reeds by the river's flow,  
His sister watched from afar, what would show.  
Pharaoh's daughter came to bathe at the tide,  
Spotted the basket, had it brought to her side.  

She opened it up, the baby cried out,  
"This is a Hebrew child," she had to shout.  
His sister approached, quick with a plan,  
"Shall I fetch a nurse, a Hebrew woman?"  

"Go," she replied, and so it was done,  
The baby’s own mother became the chosen one.  
"Take this child, nurse him, pay you I'll see,"  
The child grew strong, Pharaoh’s daughter agreed.  

She named him Moses, "From water I drew,"  
His life a journey, beginnings anew.  
When grown, he saw his brothers' pain,  
An Egyptian struck one, causing disdain.  

He looked around, no one in sight,  
Struck down the Egyptian, hid him from light.  
The very next day, saw Hebrews in fight,  
"Why harm your fellow?" he asked outright.  

"Who made you prince, judge in this land?  
Will you kill me too, as you had planned?"  
Moses feared, said, "It's known what I've done,"  
Pharaoh sought his life, Moses had to run.  

To Midian he fled, sat by a well,  
Seven daughters came, their story to tell.  
Shepherds pushed them, but Moses was strong,  
He helped them draw water, righted the wrong.  

Their father asked, "Why back so fast?"  
"An Egyptian helped us, his strength was vast."  
"Invite him to eat," the father then pled,  
Moses stayed with him, a family was bred.  

Zipporah, his wife, a son she bore,  
Named Gershom, a foreigner, no more.  
Meanwhile in Egypt, oppression ran deep,  
Israel cried out, their anguish to keep.  

God heard their cries, remembered His vow,  
With Abraham, Isaac, Jacob somehow.  
He saw their plight, and with concern,  
The time would come, for freedom to earn.
