Jacob's sons rolled into Egypt's land,  
Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah hand in hand.  

Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin,  
Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, all kin.  

Seventy souls came from Jacob's bone,  
Joseph's in Egypt, already known.  

Generations passed, brothers all died,  
Yet Israel's children multiplied, worldwide.  

A new king rose, didn’t know Joseph's tale,  
Feared Israel's strength, worried they'd prevail.  

"Look," he said, "they're mightier than we,  
Let's act wisely, keep our land free."  

Taskmasters set to burden and grind,  
Pithom and Raamses, cities they designed.  

But affliction couldn't keep them down,  
The more they spread, the more they'd astound.  

Egyptians feared their growing might,  
Made them serve with all their might.  

Pharaoh spoke to the midwives, Shiphrah, Puah by name,  
“When sons are born, put an end to their game.”  

But these women feared God above,  
Saved the boys, for they knew His love.  

Pharaoh asked, “Why spare the boys' cries?”  
Midwives replied, “Hebrew birth too quick for our eyes.”  

God blessed them for their fear of Him,  
Israel grew, their might didn't dim.  

Pharaoh's decree, harsh and severe,  
“Every son, cast into the river, crystal clear.”
