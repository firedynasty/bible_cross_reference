Moses said, "They won't hear my voice, no way,  
They'll say, 'Yahweh never came your way.'"  

Yahweh asked, "What's in your hand, my friend?"  
"A rod," Moses replied, not hard to comprehend.  

"Throw it down," Yahweh said, "let it hit the ground."  
It turned into a snake, Moses' heart was bound.  

"Stretch out your hand, grab its tail fast,"  
It turned back to a rod, like it was in the past.  

"Now they’ll believe Yahweh’s at your side,  
God of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, your guide."  

"Put your hand in your cloak," Yahweh had him try,  
Moses pulled it out, white as snow, oh my.  

"Now put it back," Yahweh did command,  
It turned normal again, like his other hand.  

"If they doubt one sign, they'll believe the next,  
Pour river water on dry land, watch it transform, complex."  

Moses said, "O Lord, I’m not quick of tongue,  
Words don’t flow smooth, I’m not that strong."  

Yahweh replied, "Who makes mouths to speak?  
Isn’t it I, Yahweh, the one you seek?"  

"Go now, I’ll teach you the words you'll need."  
Moses begged, "Please, send another to lead."  

Yahweh’s fire burned, yet He gave a plan instead,  
"Let Aaron, your brother, help with what must be said.  

He speaks well, he’s on his way.  
Together, you’ll lead, without delay."  

"You’ll speak to Aaron, put words in his mouth,  
I’ll guide you both, north, east, west, south."  

"Take this rod in hand, it’ll do the signs,  
Lead the people, follow these lines."  

Moses went to Jethro, "Let me see my kin,"  
Jethro said, "Go in peace, let your journey begin."  

Yahweh told Moses, "Back to Egypt, take that track,  
For those who sought your life won’t be coming back."  

With wife and sons on a donkey’s back,  
Moses took the rod, ready for the attack.  

Yahweh warned, "In Egypt, do the wonders, don’t be slow,  
But Pharaoh’s heart’s hard; he won’t let them go."  

"Tell Pharaoh, 'Israel's my firstborn, not your own,  
Let my son go, or I'll take your firstborn.'"  

On the road, Yahweh came, ready to fight,  
But Zipporah acted, set things right.  

She took a flint, cut, and said with a shove,  
"You’re a bridegroom of blood, my love."  

Yahweh left Moses alone, the deed was done,  
In blood and foreskin, the battle was won.  

Yahweh told Aaron, "Go meet your brother,"  
On God's mountain, they embraced one another.  

Moses shared God’s words, every sign, every act,  
Together they moved, no turning back.  

They gathered the elders, Aaron spoke the plan,  
Signs were shown, faith spread through the land.  

The people believed, seeing Yahweh's care,  
They bowed and worshiped, knowing He was there.
