In the third year of Hoshea, a new king's tale begins,  
Hezekiah of Judah, where righteousness wins.  
At twenty-five he rose, reigned in Jerusalem bold,  
For twenty-nine years, this story's told.

He did right in Yahweh's sight, like David before,  
Tearing down altars, idols, and more.  
Broke the serpent of bronze, turned it to dust,  
Called it Nehushtan, for in God he did trust.

He held fast to Yahweh, never turned aside,  
Among Judah's kings, none could match his stride.  
Yahweh was with him, success in his hand,  
He rebelled against Assyria, taking a stand.

Struck Philistines down, from tower to city walls,  
In his fourth year, Samaria falls.  
Assyria took Israel, scattered them wide,  
For they broke God's covenant, turned aside.

In Hezekiah's fourteenth year, the tension peak,  
Sennacherib of Assyria, Judah he seeks.  
Hezekiah reached out, said, "I’ll bear the load,"  
Paid gold and silver, down a heavy road.

But Assyria sent Rabshakeh with words to declare,  
"Who do you trust, that you dare not despair?  
Don't lean on Egypt, a reed that will pierce,  
Or on Yahweh, whom Hezekiah has dismissed."

"Give pledges, I'll grant you horses to ride,  
Yet can you face even one captain with pride?  
Yahweh told me, 'Destroy this land,' so I came,  
To break you down, to make my name."

The people stayed silent, obeyed the king's call,  
Hezekiah's men returned, their clothes did fall.  
They told the king of Rabshakeh's loud boast,  
But Hezekiah trusted Yahweh, more than most.
