Elisha spoke with a prophetic vibe,  
"Tomorrow flour’s gonna be real cheap, no jive.  

The captain scoffed, doubting the divine,  
"If Heaven had windows, could it align?"  

Four lepers sat at the city’s gate,  
"Why sit here, just accept our fate?  

In the city, famine's got its grip,  
If we stay, we die, let’s take a trip.  

Let’s head to the Syrians, see if they’re kind,  
If they save us, great, if not, we’re resigned."  

At twilight they walked, hope on their side,  
To the Syrians’ camp, no soldiers to hide.  

God had made sounds like a mighty, fierce raid,  
The Syrians fled fast, no time to parade.  

Lepers found tents, gold, and silver to spare,  
They ate, drank, and hid treasures with care.  

Then guilt struck their hearts, "This news is grand,  
Let’s tell the king, he'll understand."  

They ran to the gatekeepers, shared what they saw,  
Syrians gone, but treasures and awe.  

The king was cautious, "It’s a trap, I'm sure,  
The Syrians are hiding, they’ll lure us for sure."  

A servant suggested, "Send scouts to see,  
What truth lies beyond, what reality?"  

Chariots raced, found garments strewn,  
Syrians had fled, under the moon.  

People rushed out, plundering the site,  
Flour and barley sold for a shekel, just right.  

The captain, at the gate, trampled and gone,  
God’s word fulfilled, like Elisha had drawn.  

The prophecy stood, every word was true,  
He saw the promise, but never tasted the view.
