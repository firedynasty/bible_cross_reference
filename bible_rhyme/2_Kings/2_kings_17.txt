In the twelfth year of Ahaz's reign, Hoshea took the stage,  
Ruling Israel from Samaria, in a nine-year engage.  

He did evil in God's sight, not as bad as before,  
Yet still, Shalmaneser came knocking at his door.  

Hoshea bowed to Assyria, paying tribute as the norm,  
But when he conspired with Egypt, it brought on a storm.  

The Assyrian king found out and threw Hoshea in chains,  
Marched through the land, to Samaria he came.  

Three years of siege, till Samaria was taken,  
Israel led to Assyria, a new path forsaken.  

Why? God had freed them from Pharaoh’s cruel hand,  
But they feared other gods, forgot His command.  

Built high places in secret, from watchtower to town,  
Pillars and Asherah poles, defying God’s crown.  

They burned incense, served idols, made God frown,  
Yahweh said, “You shall not do this,” but they did it anyhow.  

Prophets warned them, “Turn back, keep the law,”  
But they hardened their necks, ignored what they saw.  

Rejected His covenant, followed the crowd,  
Made idols, served Baal, ways of sin they allowed.  

Sacrificed sons and daughters, provoked God’s wrath,  
So He removed Israel, left Judah on a narrow path.  

Judah too strayed, didn’t heed God’s way,  
Yahweh afflicted them, cast them away.  

Carried off by Assyria, new folks took their place,  
They didn’t know Yahweh, lions set the pace.  

They called on a priest to teach God’s law,  
But each nation, their own gods they still saw.  

They feared Yahweh, yet served idols in stride,  
Passed down from fathers, a divided pride.  

God said, “Fear Me, remember the covenant made,  
Don’t bow to others, let My commands be obeyed.”  

Yet they did as before, mixed worship and sin,  
Fear of Yahweh and idols, from father to kin.
