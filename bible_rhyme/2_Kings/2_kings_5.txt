Naaman, a captain, mighty and grand,  
But leprosy marked him with a heavy hand.  
An Israelite girl, a captive, a maid,  
Said, "A prophet in Samaria could have him saved."

The news reached the king, who wrote with pride,  
Sent Naaman with gifts to the Israelite side.  
The king of Israel read, tore his robe in fear,  
"Am I God to heal? Why send Naaman here?"

Elisha, the prophet, heard the king's distress,  
"Send him to me, and I'll handle the rest."  
Naaman with chariots stood at Elisha's door,  
But a messenger came with instructions, nothing more.

"Go wash in the Jordan, seven times flow,  
Then you'll be clean; let your true colors show."  
Naaman was angry, "This can't be right!  
The rivers of Damascus put this Jordan to flight."

His servants spoke softly, "If he'd asked for more,  
Wouldn't you do it? Just try—there's hope in store."  
Down to the Jordan, Naaman dipped seven round,  
His skin renewed, like a child's, glory found.

Back to Elisha, with gratitude and awe,  
"There's no God but in Israel, so pure and raw.  
Take a gift from me, for the work you've done."  
But Elisha refused, "I take gifts from none."

Naaman asked for earth, two mules' worth,  
To worship Yahweh, the God of all earth.  
"Grant me pardon when my king bows in Rimmon's place,"  
Elisha said, "Go in peace, and walk in grace."

But Gehazi, Elisha's servant, sought a prize,  
Chased after Naaman, with deceitful lies.  
"Give silver and garments," he asked with a grin,  
Naaman, generous, gave without chagrin.

Gehazi hid the goods, then faced Elisha's truth,  
"Where have you been?" Elisha asked, without sooth.  
"My heart went with you, saw the deceit you spun,  
Now Naaman's leprosy to you and your sons."

Gehazi left, his skin white as snow,  
Bearing the curse, reaping what he'd sow.  
Thus the tale of Naaman, in Israel's heart it stays,  
Of healing, deceit, and Elisha's unyielding ways.
