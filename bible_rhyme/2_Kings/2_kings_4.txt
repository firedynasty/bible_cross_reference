A wife cried to Elisha, "My husband is gone, you see,  
Feared the Lord, but now a creditor threatens my family."  
Elisha asked, "Tell me, what do you possess?"  
"Only a pot of oil," she confessed.  

"Go, borrow vessels, every neighbor you can reach,  
Not just a few; let that be my teach.  
Close the door, pour the oil, don't hesitate,  
Fill each one, set aside, don't be late."  

She did as he said, doors shut tight,  
Poured oil till every vessel was full, a miraculous sight.  
"No more containers," her son declared,  
And the flow ceased, just as she feared.  

She told Elisha, the man of God spoke,  
"Sell the oil, pay your debt, live on the stroke."  

Then Elisha journeyed to Shunem's place,  
A woman gave him bread, a warm embrace.  
She said to her man, "Let's build him a room,  
With a bed, a chair, and a lamp to light the gloom."  

Elisha asked, "What can be done for your care?"  
Gehazi said, "She has no son, her husband's old, a rare affair."  
Elisha called her, "Next year, a son you'll hold,"  
She replied, "Don't lie, don't be bold."  

But true were the words, a son she bore,  
Years passed, the child grew, loved even more.  
One day, "My head!" cried the boy in pain,  
Carried to his mother, where he lay till life wane.  

She placed him on the man of God's bed,  
Mounted a donkey, "To Elisha," she said.  
"Why go today?" her husband inquired,  
"It's all right," she assured, not tired.  

She reached Elisha on Mount Carmel high,  
"Is all well?" he asked, she answered nigh.  
Fell at his feet, "Don't deceive, did I plead?"  
Gehazi couldn't wake the child, he failed in the deed.  

Elisha entered, shut the door, and prayed,  
Stretched on the child, the miracle displayed.  
The boy sneezed seven times, opened his eyes,  
Elisha called, "Now take your prize."  

The mother bowed, thanked with tears and grace,  
She picked up her son, left the sacred space.  

Famine struck, in Gilgal's land,  
Elisha ordered stew, no other demand.  
A wild vine’s gourds were gathered in haste,  
"Death in the pot!" the prophets cried, distaste.  

"Bring meal!" Elisha threw it in,  
"Now serve the stew," the end of their din.  
Nothing harmful remained, God’s word held true,  
All ate and were filled, with plenty of stew.  

A man came with barley bread, first fruits at hand,  
"Give it to the people," was Elisha’s command.  
"What, for a hundred?" the servant did protest,  
"They'll eat and have leftovers," Yahweh’s promise blessed.  

The people ate their fill, with food left for later,  
In God’s provision, no one was a debater.
