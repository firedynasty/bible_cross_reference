In Jehu's seventh year, Jehoash took the throne,  
Forty years in Jerusalem, his reign was well-known.  
His mother Zibiah from Beersheba hailed,  
Jehoash did right, in Yahweh's ways he never failed.  

Yet high places stood, incense still burned,  
Jehoash ordered repairs, Yahweh's house was spurned.  
"Take the money," he said, "for the house repair,"  
But twenty-three years in, no progress to declare.  

"Why no repairs?" Jehoash asked with might,  
"Stop taking funds, and fix it right."  
Priests agreed, no more cash to be claimed,  
Jehoiada took charge, his plan now proclaimed.  

A chest by the altar, a hole in the lid,  
Money flowed in, as the priests bid.  
The king's scribe and high priest counted it all,  
To workers' hands, the funds would fall.  

Carpenters, builders, they set to restore,  
Masons, stone cutters, and timber galore.  
No silver cups or trumpets made,  
All went to repair, where damages laid.  

Faithful they worked, no need for account,  
Trust in their honesty, a fine amount.  
Trespass and sin offerings, priests kept aside,  
Hazael of Syria, to Gath took stride.  

Jehoash sent gold, treasures too,  
To keep Jerusalem safe, his fears did strew.  
The chronicles hold his story tight,  
But his end came in the night.  

Servants conspired, struck him down,  
In Millo's house, his life unbound.  
With fathers he lay, buried with care,  
Amaziah, his son, the crown would wear.
