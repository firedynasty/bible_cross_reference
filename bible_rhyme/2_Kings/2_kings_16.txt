In the seventeenth year of trouble and fame,  
Ahaz the young king of Judah came to reign.  

Just twenty years old, sixteen years he'd rule,  
But in God's eyes, he played the fool.  

Walked in Israel's kings' old ways,  
Made his son pass through the fires ablaze.  

Sacrificed on hills, incense burned high,  
Under green trees reaching for the sky.  

Rezin of Syria and Pekah from Israel,  
Besieged his city but couldn't make him fail.  

Rezin took Elath, drove the Jews away,  
Syrians there lived to this very day.  

Ahaz sent word to Assyria's land,  
"I'm your servant, need a helping hand."  

Silver and gold from the temple he sent,  
To Tiglath Pileser, a desperate present.  

The Assyrian king listened well,  
Took Damascus, Rezin fell.  

Ahaz traveled, saw an altar grand,  
Sent plans to Urijah with a command.  

Urijah built it, just like the design,  
For when the king returned, it was time.  

The king approached, offerings ablaze,  
Burnt, meal, drink, in a ritual haze.  

Bronze altar moved to the side,  
North of his altar, a prideful stride.  

"Burn on the great altar," the king did say,  
All offerings, blood, all in one display.  

Urijah obeyed, did what was planned,  
Ahaz's words, his direct command.  

Bases cut, the basin removed,  
The sea on stone, his power proved.  

Covered way and entry changed for gain,  
To please Assyria, Ahaz did reign.  

His acts recorded in the chronicles' line,  
Then he slept with fathers, his time to resign.  

Hezekiah his son, the throne did grace,  
Ahaz's reign, in history's embrace.
