Woe to Ariel, city where David camps tight,  
Year by year, feasts roll, but comes the night.  

"I'll distress Ariel," Yahweh's words burning,  
Mourning, lamentation, altars, yearning.  

I'll encamp around, siege your very ground,  
Your voice whispers low, dust-bound sound.  

Foes like dust, scattered chaff in flight,  
In an instant—sudden, out of sight.  

Yahweh's wrath thunders, quakes, storms, and fire,  
A vision like a dream, faded desire.  

Hungry man dreams he's had his fill,  
Wakes up empty, craving still.  

Thirsty man drinks in dream's own light,  
Awakens faint, thirst's not in sight.  

Pause, wonder, blind in deep sleep’s keep,  
Drunk, not from wine, eyes closed, seers weep.  

A sealed book’s words no wise can read,  
"The Lord says, 'This people honors in word, no deed.'"  

"Marvelous work," Yahweh plans to unfold,  
Wise men’s wisdom, hidden and cold.  

Woe to those hiding, deeds in the night,  
"Who sees us?" they whisper, out of sight.  

The potter's hand, clay can't ignore,  
"You didn't make me," clay cries more.  

Lebanon soon a fruitful field to show,  
Deaf will hear, blind eyes will glow.  

Humble rejoice, poor men sing,  
Ruthless brought low, justice they bring.  

Yahweh speaks of Jacob's light,  
"No more shame," faces bright.  

Children, the work of His hands, they'll see,  
"Sanctify My name," in awe they'll be.  

Those who err will find their way,  
Grumblers receive instruction, a new day.
