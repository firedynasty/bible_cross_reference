Damascus falls, hear the city's cry,  
Laid to ruin, under a darkened sky.  

Aroer's towns left alone,  
Flocks lie down, no fear shown.  

Ephraim's fortress, gone away,  
Damascus' kingdom, in dismay.  

Syria's remnant fades like a dream,  
Like Israel's glory, or so it seems.  

"On that day," says the Lord's decree,  
Jacob's glory will thin, lean he'll be.  

Like grain that's reaped with a strong arm's sweep,  
Or olives left on branches, no longer to keep.  

Yet remnants reside, in branches they stay,  
Two or three clinging, still holding sway.  

Eyes turn to their Maker on high,  
Respect for Israel's Holy One, they won't deny.  

No more altars made by their hands,  
No more creation by their commands.  

Strong cities like desolate woods,  
Forsaken places, where silence broods.  

"You forgot your salvation’s God,  
Ignored the strength of your rock, how odd.  

You plant your seeds, foreign and new,  
Yet the harvest flees, sorrow comes due."  

Nations roar like seas in a storm,  
Rushing waters, a mighty form.  

But He rebukes, they scatter wide,  
Chased like chaff, with nowhere to hide.  

Evening terror, morning's calm,  
Gone are those who brought us harm.
