Beyond Ethiopian streams, wings rustling in flight,  
Ambassadors sail on papyrus boats, out into the night.  

"Go, swift messengers, to a nation tall and sleek,  
Where the rivers divide, a people mighty and unique."  

World inhabitants, hear! When banners rise on the hill,  
A trumpet blast echoes — be silent, be still.  

Yahweh says, "I'll be still, watchful from my place,  
Like sunshine's heat or dew's gentle embrace."  

Before the harvest, when blossoms fade away,  
He'll snip with pruning hooks, the spreading boughs he'll sway.  

Left for ravenous birds, beasts roam and feast,  
Birds in the summer, animals in winter's least.  

In time, gifts to Yahweh from this tall, smooth nation,  
Treaders of land, with rivers' separation.  

To Mount Zion, Yahweh's name presides,  
The mighty offering, from the rivers' divide.
