In the year King Uzziah took his last breath,  
I saw the Lord on a throne, high above death.  

His robe filled the temple, mighty and grand,  
Seraphim hovered with six wings at hand.  

"Cover the face, cover the feet, and fly,"  
They sang out a chorus that reached the sky.  

"Holy, holy, holy!" they cried out loud,  
"Yahweh's glory fills this earth, like a shroud."  

Foundations trembled, the thresholds shook,  
The house filled with smoke, like a storybook.  

"Woe is me!" I declared in fright,  
"Unclean lips, unclean world, I’ve seen the light!"  

A seraph flew swift with a coal from the fire,  
Touched my lips, took away my heart's mire.  

"Your sin's forgiven, your iniquity's gone,"  
I heard the words like a new dawn.  

The Lord asked aloud, a voice like thunder,  
“Who shall I send?” a call to ponder.  

"Here I am, send me!" I boldly proclaimed,  
He commissioned me, my purpose untamed.  

“Go to the people, let them hear,  
But not understand, let them see unclear.  

Hearts will grow heavy, ears will be blocked,  
Eyes will be shut, for the truth they'll have mocked."  

“How long, Lord?” I asked with a sigh,  
“Till cities lie empty, beneath the sky.  

Homes without people, lands laid waste,  
Till far away men have been displaced.  

A tenth remains, but even that will fall,  
Yet from the stump, a holy seed will call.”
