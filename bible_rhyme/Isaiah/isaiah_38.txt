Hezekiah lay sick, facing death's call,  
Isaiah the prophet spoke, "Set your house, y'all.  

Yahweh says, ‘Your time's up, make amends,  
For soon your life's journey comes to an end.’"  

Hezekiah turned, eyes fixed on the wall,  
Prayed to Yahweh with a heart sincere and small.  

"Remember, Yahweh, how I've walked in Your truth,  
Done what’s good since the days of my youth."  

Tears flowed bitterly down the king's face,  
Pleading for mercy, for a little more grace.  

Yahweh's word then came swift and clear,  
"Isaiah, tell Hezekiah, have no fear.  

‘God of David says, ‘I’ve heard your prayer,  
Seen your tears, fifteen more years I'll spare.  

I'll rescue this city from Assyria's might,  
Defend it with power, defend it with light.  

Here's the sign, a miracle I’ll make,  
The shadow on the sundial, ten steps back it'll take.’"  

The sun reversed its course, a wonder to see,  
Hezekiah's life extended, his spirit set free.  

In his writing, the king expressed his despair,  
"I was heading to Sheol, no life left to spare.  

No more to see Yah in the land of the living,  
Cut off like a tent, my life unraveling.  

Morning to night, my bones felt the break,  
Like a weaver's loom, my end You’d make.  

I waited like a swallow, moaned like a dove,  
Eyes weak, I cried, ‘Lord, send Your love.’"  

Then he spoke, "What can I say of this grace?  
He spoke to me, saved me from this place.  

In His love, my soul from corruption He freed,  
Cast my sins behind, fulfilled every need.  

The dead can't praise, but the living, we sing,  
Declare Your truth, like a father's gift to bring.  

Yahweh saves, so let every string resound,  
In His house, with songs, His praises abound."  

Isaiah said, "Take figs for a healing balm,  
Lay it on the boil, restore the king’s calm."  

Hezekiah asked, "What's the sign, I must know,  
That to Yahweh's house my steps will go?"
