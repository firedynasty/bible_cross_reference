Hezekiah hears the news, tears his clothes, sackcloth worn,  
Heads to the house of Yahweh, weary and worn.  

Eliakim and Shebna, priests in tow, sent on a ride,  
To Isaiah the prophet, in whom they confide.  

Hezekiah says, "It's a day of trouble, rebuke, and rejection,  
The birth pains come, yet there's no strength for this connection.  

Pray, maybe Yahweh will hear Rabshakeh's defiance,  
And rebuke his words with divine reliance.  

Pray for the remnant, the few who remain,  
Lift up your voice and help us sustain."  

Isaiah speaks, "To your king, this word convey,  
Yahweh says, ‘Fear not the words that came your way.  

I'll put a spirit in him, news he will hear,  
Return to his land, his end draws near.’"  

Rabshakeh returns, finds Assyria in fight,  
The king's left Lachish, gone to Libnah's light.  

News of Tirhakah, Ethiopia's king,  
"He's come to war," the news does ring.  

Messengers sent with words of disdain,  
"Don't trust your God, you'll be in pain.  

Look at Assyria's wrath on all lands,  
No gods saved them from our hands."  

Hezekiah reads the words in despair,  
Spreads it before Yahweh, in earnest prayer.  

"Yahweh of Armies, enthroned on high,  
You alone made earth and sky.  

Hear Sennacherib's challenge, see his game,  
Save us, prove your holy name."  

Isaiah sends word, "Yahweh heard your plea,  
The daughter of Zion mocks with glee.  

Who do you defy with your boastful tone?  
Against the Holy One, you've raised your throne.  

With chariots and pride, you claim this land,  
But Yahweh's might, you can't withstand.  

I know your moves and your rage against me,  
Back you’ll go, to where you should be.  

A sign awaits, the remnant will thrive,  
Eating from the land, they'll stay alive.  

Out of Jerusalem, survivors will rise,  
Yahweh's zeal will break these ties."  

"No arrow shall hit, no siege will stand,  
I protect this city by my own command.  

For David’s sake, this city I'll save,  
Assyria's king won’t dig a grave."  

Yahweh's angel strikes in the dark of night,  
185,000 fall, dawn brings the sight.  

Sennacherib, humbled, to Nineveh he returns,  
In his own god's house, his fate he learns.  

His sons with swords end his reign,  
Esar Haddon takes the king's chain.
