In the fourteenth year of Hezekiah's reign,  
Assyria's king brought Judah pain,  

Sennacherib attacked with force and might,  
Took every fortified city, day or night.  

Rabshakeh came from Lachish with an army bold,  
Stood by the aqueduct, stories to be told.  

Eliakim, Shebna, Joah stepped out to meet,  
Rabshakeh's words, they couldn't beat.  

"Tell Hezekiah," Rabshakeh did say,  
"The great king speaks, hear me today.  

What’s this trust, this confidence in you?  
Your counsel's vain, your strength too.  

You lean on Egypt, that broken reed,  
Pharaoh will pierce, he won’t meet your need.  

You say, 'We trust in Yahweh, our God,’  
Yet Hezekiah removed altars, odd.  

Make a pledge, join Assyria's way,  
Two thousand horses, if you ride today.  

Turn not to Egypt's chariots here,  
My master's weakest you should fear.  

Did Yahweh not send me to destroy?  
This land's mine to break, not to enjoy."  

Eliakim and crew said, "Speak Aramaic please,  
Not in our tongue, let the people be at ease."  

Rabshakeh shouted out in Hebrew loud,  
"Listen, people, gather 'round.  

Don't let Hezekiah lead you astray,  
He can't save you, hear what I say.  

‘Make peace with Assyria, come out, be free,  
Eat from your vine, drink from your tree.  

Until we take you to lands anew,  
There’s grain, wine, just like you knew.  

Don’t be fooled by Hezekiah's call,  
No god has saved their land at all.  

Where are Hamath's gods, Arpad's divine?  
Samaria fell, and they'll align.  

No god saved them from my king’s hand,  
Why should Yahweh save this land?’”  

Silence fell, no reply was made,  
For the king's command, they obeyed.  

Eliakim, Shebna, Joah, clothes torn in grief,  
Told Hezekiah Rabshakeh’s disbelief.
