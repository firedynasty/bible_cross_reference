From the temple, a voice boomed loud and clear,  
"Pour the seven bowls; let the wrath appear!"  

The first angel stepped up, pouring his bowl to the ground,  
Sores erupted on those with the beast's mark, profound.  

The second hit the sea, turned it red like blood,  
Every creature in the ocean felt the flood.  

The third bowl poured where rivers flow,  
Waters turned crimson, nothing could grow.  

An angel declared, "You're righteous and just,  
For they spilled the blood, now drink it they must."  

The altar agreed, "True are your ways,  
Almighty God, in all your displays."  

The fourth bowl unleashed the sun's burning might,  
Scorching men, yet they cursed the light.  

Blazing heat, but no hearts turned,  
No repentance, though they burned.  

The fifth brought darkness over the beast's domain,  
Tongues gnawed in agony, cursing in vain.  

Still, no change, no remorse shown,  
They clung to their ways, to the bone.  

The sixth dried up the Euphrates wide,  
Clearing paths for kings, with armies at their side.  

Out came spirits like frogs, sneaky and sly,  
Deceiving the leaders under the sky.  

"Behold, I come like a thief in the night,  
Blessed is he who's ready, clothed in the light."  

To Megiddo, he gathered them all,  
For the great day, when kingdoms would fall.  

The seventh poured his bowl into the air,  
"It is done!" echoed from the heavenly chair.  

Lightning flashed, thunder roared so fierce,  
An earthquake split the ground, humanity pierced.  

Cities crumbled, Babylon's fate was sealed,  
Every island vanished, mountains no longer revealed.  

Hailstones fell, heavy as a talent's weight,  
Men cursed God's name under the crushing slate.
