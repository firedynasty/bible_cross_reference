I stood on the sand by the sea's wide expanse,  
Saw a beast rise up, as if in a trance.  
Ten horns, seven heads, each crown shining bright,  
With names that blaspheme, a fearsome sight.  

Looks like a leopard, feet like a bear,  
Mouth of a lion, a beast beyond compare.  
The dragon gave power, throne, and authority,  
A ruler of chaos, a sight of ferocity.  

One head was wounded, seemed fatally scarred,  
But healed it rose, the world caught off guard.  
They worshiped the dragon, power he bestowed,  
Said, "Who can compare? Who can break his code?"  

A mouth filled with blasphemy, speaking grand things,  
Forty-two months of war, the chaos it brings.  
Against God he spoke, defying the divine,  
Blaspheming heaven, crossing the line.  

Given power over nations, tribes, and speech,  
To conquer the saints, their faith he would breach.  
The earth in awe, their names not in the book,  
Worshiped the beast, at the world they shook.  

"If you hear, then listen, let truth draw near.  
Destined for captivity, let that be clear."  
Endurance and faith, the saints' steady way,  
Through trials and fire, they stand, they stay.  

Another beast rose, from the earth it came,  
Two horns like a lamb, a voice of dragon flame.  
With the first beast's power, it made them bow down,  
To the healed beast, holding its crown.  

Signs and wonders, fire from the sky,  
Deception and awe, the people comply.  
“Make an image,” it said, “to the one who survived,  
The sword-struck beast, now revived.”  

It gave breath to the image, made it speak loud,  
And those who refused, laid under a shroud.  
Marks on their hands, or on their heads,  
Without it no trade, on this path they tread.  

Wisdom calls out, hear the number well,  
Six hundred sixty-six, a tale to tell.  
The number of man, a mystery deep,  
In this prophecy, truths and warnings seep.
