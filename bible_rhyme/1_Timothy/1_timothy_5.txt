Respect an older man, treat him like your dad,  
Younger men as brothers, never make them mad.  

Older women as mothers, younger as sisters with care,  
In purity, approach them, show love, and be fair.  

Honor the widows, those truly alone,  
If they've got family, let respect be shown.  

Widows with hope in God, praying night and day,  
But those who seek pleasure are lost along the way.  

Command these things, keep the reputation clean,  
Neglecting your family is a faithless scene.  

Widows under sixty, one man's wife, no more,  
Good works define her, as she serves and restores.  

Younger widows, passions may lead them astray,  
They break their pledge, idle words they display.  

Encourage them to marry, lead their own home,  
Stay clear from gossip, where troubles may roam.  

Help the true widows, support them with care,  
Let others step in, let burdens be shared.  

Elders who lead well, give them double honor,  
They work in the word, their teachings grow fonder.  

"Don't muzzle the ox," the Scripture makes plain,  
"The laborer’s worth his wage" again and again.  

Don’t accuse an elder without two or three,  
Reprove them public, let their sins be seen.  

God and Christ command, no bias allowed,  
Be just and pure, stand tall and proud.  

Don't rush to lay hands, don't share others' sin,  
Keep yourself clean, let purity win.  

A drink of wine, your stomach will mend,  
A little comfort, a medicine friend.  

Some sins are clear, right before your eyes,  
Others hidden, in time they will rise.  

Good works shine bright, some hidden from view,  
But in time, they'll surface, as truth breaks through.
