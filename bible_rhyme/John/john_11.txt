In Bethany, a man named Lazarus lay sick,  
Mary and Martha worried, the clock ticked quick.  

They sent word to Jesus, “The one you love is ill,”  
But Jesus said, “This won't end in death; it's God's will.”  

He stayed two days more, love for them in his heart,  
Then told his disciples, “It's time for us to depart.”  

“Back to Judea?” they asked, “The threat is real!”  
He spoke of daylight, and light's power to reveal.  

“Lazarus sleeps,” he said, “but I’ll wake him in time.”  
The disciples, puzzled, missed the death in his rhyme.  

“Lazarus is dead,” he declared straight and clear,  
“I'm glad for your belief, now let's go near.”  

Thomas, called Didymus, turned to the crew,  
“Let's join him, even if it’s death we'll pursue.”  

When Jesus showed up, four days in the grave,  
Bethany near, a short walk away; none could save.  

Martha ran out, “Lord, if you’d been here, he'd live,”  
Yet her faith in his power, she still did give.  

“Your brother will rise,” Jesus assured with grace,  
“I am the life, the resurrection, in this place.”  

“Do you believe?” he asked Martha with intent,  
“Yes, Lord,” she affirmed, on his mission she was bent.  

Martha fetched Mary, whispered secret and fast,  
“The Teacher's here, calling,” to Jesus, she dashed.  

The mourners followed, thinking she’d weep at the tomb,  
But she fell at his feet, grief in the gloom.  

“Lord, if you’d been here, my brother would survive,”  
Seeing her sorrow, Jesus' heart felt alive.  

“Where is he laid?” he asked with a sigh,  
“Come see,” they replied, tears in their eye.  

Jesus wept, and the crowd saw his love,  
Could this healer not save the one he thought of?  

To the tomb he walked, a cave sealed with stone,  
“Take it away,” he said, his voice strong, not alone.  

“Four days dead!” Martha protested, fearing the stench,  
But Jesus reminded, “Believe and God’s glory will quench.”  

The stone was moved, he looked up in prayer,  
“Father, I thank you; I know you're always there.”  

Then he shouted loud, “Lazarus, come out!”  
Bound in grave clothes, Lazarus walked about.  

“Free him and let him go,” Jesus declared,  
Many believed, in awe they stared.  

Some told the Pharisees of the signs so grand,  
Fear and plots brewed in the high priest's hand.  

Caiaphas spoke, seeing the danger at hand,  
“One for the nation’s sake, lest we lose the land.”  

The plot thickened, the council sought his breath,  
Jesus moved quietly, away from death.  

Passover approached, hearts filled with suspense,  
The priests sought Jesus, the tension immense.  

In the temple they wondered, “Will he appear?”  
Quiet whispers spread, all ears tuned to hear.
