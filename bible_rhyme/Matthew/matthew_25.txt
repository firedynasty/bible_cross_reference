Ten lamps lit in the night, waiting for the groom,  
Five girls prepared, the others faced doom.  
Foolish with no oil, wise with a stash,  
When the groom was delayed, they all took a nap.  

"Behold, he's coming!" the midnight cry rang,  
Virgins trimmed their lamps as the bridegroom's name sang.  
"Share your oil," the foolish ones plead,  
"Not enough for both," wise ones decreed.  

Off to the shop, the unready ones fled,  
The groom arrived, the wise ones led.  
Locked out, the foolish cried, "Open, please!"  
But the groom replied, "I don't know you, cease."  

Be alert, he warned, for the day you don't know,  
The Son of Man's coming, be ready to go.  

A man gave talents, five, two, and one,  
To his servants, each by what could be done.  
The five made ten, the two made four,  
But the one hid his, fearing a score.  

The master returned and praised the wise,  
"Well done, enter joy, with more to devise."  
The one who buried? "Wicked and slow,  
You knew my ways, yet nothing to show."  

His talent was taken, given to the most,  
Thrown to darkness, where regrets engross.  
For those who have, abundance will grow,  
To those who lack, even less will they stow.  

When the Son returns, his glory displayed,  
Nations gathered, a verdict conveyed.  
Sheep to the right, goats to the left,  
Blessed for kindness, others bereft.  

"I was hungry, you gave food,  
Thirsty, you gave drink, you were good.  
A stranger welcomed, naked clothed,  
Sick and in prison, by your love enclosed."  

"Lord, when did we see these things?" they ask,  
"When you served my brethren," he reveals the task.  
To the left, "You turned away,  
Now eternal fire, you'll repay."  

"Lord, when did we see you and not care?"  
"When you ignored my brethren, I was there."  
The righteous enter life, the others, strife,  
With acts of love, they gain eternal life.
