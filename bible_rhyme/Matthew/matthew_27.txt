Morning light, chief priests plot, taking counsel for the spot  
Against the man named Jesus, plotting death, so they thought.  

Bound him up, took his fate to Pilate, the Roman state,  
Judas, filled with regret, tried to change his ill-set fate.  

Thirty silver coins, he tossed them back, "I’ve sinned," he declared,  
Chief priests shrugged, "What's that to us?" No sympathy was spared.  

Judas left, heart heavy, found a tree to end his strife,  
Priests, with silver in their hands, bought a field with price of life.  

Potter's Field, Field of Blood, Jeremiah's words fulfill,  
Thirty coins for innocent blood, prophecy stood still.  

Jesus stood before Pilate, "King of Jews?" he asked with doubt,  
"So you say," Jesus replied, silent with no shout.  

Accused by many voices, yet not a single word he gave,  
Pilate marveled at his silence, how Jesus behaved.  

Feast time custom, one prisoner released to the crowd,  
Barabbas or Jesus, Pilate's question was loud.  

Priests stirred up the people, "Barabbas!" they all cried,  
Pilate questioned, "And Jesus?" "Crucify!" they replied.  

Pilate washed his hands, "This blood's not on me, you see,"  
"On us and our children," the crowd shouted in glee.  

Barabbas freed, Jesus flogged, soldiers led him away,  
Mocked with thorns, and robe of scarlet, they made him their prey.  

"King of the Jews," they taunted, as they knelt with cruel jest,  
Struck him, spat, and led him on, to crucify with the rest.  

On the road, Simon of Cyrene, forced to bear the cross,  
To Golgotha, place of skulls, where life would meet its loss.  

Sour wine and gall, he tasted, but he would not drink,  
Divided clothes, casting lots, beneath the cross they’d sink.  

Accusation hung above, "This is Jesus, King of Jews,"  
Two robbers on each side, shared the same damnable news.  

Passersby mocked, priests joined in, "Save yourself, Son of God!"  
Darkness fell at noon, till ninth hour, creation seemed flawed.  

Jesus cried aloud, "My God, why have you left me alone?"  
Some thought he called Elijah, but the truth was his own.  

A sponge of vinegar raised up, "Let's see if help will come,"  
With a loud cry, spirit gone, death's victory won.  

Temple veil, from top to bottom, tore with earth's great quake,  
Tombs were opened, saints arose, as creation seemed to shake.  

Centurion and guards looked on, "Truly God's Son," they feared,  
Women wept from afar, to Jesus their hearts adhered.  

Joseph of Arimathea, disciple hidden, now bold,  
Asked for Jesus' body, in linen, gently rolled.  

Placed him in a new tomb, sealed with a stone so round,  
Mary Magdalene watched on, hearts heavy, thoughts profound.  

Next day, priests sought Pilate, "His words could deceive,"  
"Seal the tomb, guard it well, lest disciples make believe."  

Pilate granted them a guard, "Make it secure and tight,"  
So they sealed the stone, keeping watch through the night.
