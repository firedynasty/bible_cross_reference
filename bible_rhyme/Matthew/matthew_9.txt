Stepped in a boat, crossed the sea, hit his city,  
Paralyzed man brought, friends showed faith gritty.  
“Son, cheer up! Sins forgiven,” Jesus declared,  
Scribes whispered "blasphemy," but Jesus wasn't scared.  

He read their minds, asked, "Why doubt in your heart?  
Is it easier to say sins forgiven or make this man start?"  
Then he turned to the man, “Get up and walk,  
Take your mat, go home,” no more talk.  

Crowds marveled, glorifying God's power,  
Authority on Earth was the talk of the hour.  
Jesus saw Matthew, at the tax booth he sat,  
“Follow me,” he said, Matthew was down with that.  

At dinner with sinners, Pharisees complained,  
"Why eat with these folks?" they disdainfully exclaimed.  
Jesus replied, “Healthy need no doctor, just the sick,  
Learn mercy, not sacrifice, that's my pick.”  

John’s crew questioned, “Why don’t your guys fast?”  
“Bridegroom's here now, but this won't last.  
New cloth on old, new wine in fresh skin,  
Both are preserved, no bursting within.”  

A ruler approached, “My daughter just died,  
But your touch will revive her,” he cried.  
On his way with disciples, a woman snuck around,  
Touched his clothes, her healing was profound.  

“Daughter, cheer up! Your faith made you whole,”  
In that moment, sickness lost control.  
At the ruler’s house, musicians made a noise,  
“She's just asleep,” he said, dismissing the ploys.  

Crowd laughed, but Jesus took her hand,  
The girl arose, spread news across the land.  
Two blind men followed, “Son of David, hear our plea!”  
“Believe I can do this?” asked He.  

“Yes, Lord,” they answered, their faith was true,  
Touched their eyes, vision renewed.  
“Don't spread the word,” his command tight,  
But they shared his fame, couldn't keep it light.  

A mute controlled by demons was brought,  
Jesus cast it out, man spoke, crowd was taut.  
“Nothing like this in Israel,” they yelled,  
“By demons’ prince,” Pharisees' accusations swelled.  

Jesus traveled, teaching, preaching good news,  
Healing sickness, mending life's bruise.  
Seeing crowds, compassion was his view,  
“Harvest is plenty, laborers are few.  

Pray to the Lord, send workers to the field,  
Gather the harvest, the lost must be healed.”
