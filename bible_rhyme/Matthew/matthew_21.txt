Rolling to Jerusalem, that city so fine,  
Jesus sent two disciples, with a clear sign:  

"Go to the village, find a donkey tied tight,  
Bring her and her colt, make it right."  

"If they ask, say, 'The Lord needs these,'  
And they'll send them along with ease."  

A prophet's words fulfilled, proclaiming the scene,  
"Your King rides in humble, gentle and clean."  

The disciples obeyed, brought the donkey pair,  
Clothes laid on their backs, Jesus sat there.  

Crowds spread garments, branches on the ground,  
"Hosanna!" they shouted, the joyful sound.  

In Jerusalem's heart, the city did shake,  
"Who is this man?" the people all spake.  

"This is Jesus, the prophet," the crowd proclaimed,  
From Nazareth of Galilee, He came unashamed.  

The temple he entered, and chaos he'd unwind,  
Tables overturned, doves sellers left behind.  

"My house is for prayer," He said, in a tone,  
"But you've turned it to a den, a robber's home."  

The blind and the lame, to Him they came,  
He healed them all, to the priests' disdain.  

"Hosanna!" the children cried, pure and bright,  
Priests questioned, Jesus replied, "They're right."  

He left the city, off to Bethany's rest,  
In the morning, hungry, a fig tree test.  

No fruit on the tree, only leaves in sight,  
"Wither forever!" spoken with might.  

The disciples marveled, how quick it died,  
"Faith can move mountains," Jesus replied.  

In the temple again, elders questioned His say,  
“By what authority do you act this way?”  

Jesus countered, "John's baptism, from where did it stem?"  
The elders pondered, feared the crowd's hem.  

Not knowing, they answered, Jesus withheld His own,  
Parables spoken, truth brightly shown.  

A man with two sons, the vineyard's call,  
One said no, but went after all.  

The second agreed, yet did not show,  
Which did the will? The first, they know.  

The tax collectors, they’re coming in first,  
You saw, you failed to quench your thirst.  

Another tale told, a vineyard and its fate,  
Servants beaten, the son's dire state.  

Farmers killed him, seeking greed's allure,  
"What will the owner do?" they were sure.  

"He’ll destroy those men, lease to farmers true,"  
Jesus confirmed, "God's Kingdom is due."  

"The stone rejected, now the cornerstone bright,  
God's work is marvelous, shining in sight."  

"The Kingdom's shift, to those bearing fruit,  
The stone will crush all, none will refute."  

The priests and Pharisees felt the parable's sting,  
Wanted to seize Him, but feared the crowd’s ring.
