Jesus wrapped up his words, left Galilee's land,  
To Judea's borders, where the Jordan sands stand.  

Crowds flocked around, healing flowed like a stream,  
Pharisees tested him with their divorce scheme.  

"Is it lawful?" they asked, trying to trip him in speech,  
He replied, “From the start, male and female, God did teach.  

‘Leave mother and father, be one flesh, not two,’  
What God joined, let no man undo.”  

“But Moses,” they said, “gave divorce command,”  
“'Cause your hearts were hard, not by God's demand.  

From the start, it was never so,” he declared,  
“Divorce leads to sin, unless infidelity's been shared.”  

Disciples, puzzled, questioned his plan,  
“If marriage is tough, is it better for man?”  

“Not all can accept this, only those to whom it’s given,  
Some choose celibacy for the Kingdom of Heaven.”  

Children came near, disciples turned them away,  
But Jesus said, “Let them come, don't you sway.  

The Kingdom belongs to hearts pure as these,”  
He blessed them, moved on with ease.  

A rich man asked, “What must I do for life eternal?”  
“Keep the commandments, love’s infernal.  

Don’t murder, steal, or lie, respect parents true,  
Love your neighbor—this is your cue.”  

“All this I've done, what do I lack?”  
“Sell all, help the poor, then come back.”  

Sad, the man left, for his riches were vast,  
“It’s hard for the wealthy to reach Heaven’s vast.”  

A camel through a needle, a rich man's plight,  
Disciples wondered, “Who can be saved outright?”  

“With men, impossible, but God makes a way,”  
Peter asked, “We left all, what’s our pay?”  

“In rebirth, you’ll sit on thrones high,  
Judging tribes in glory’s sky.  

Leave all for my name, gain a hundredfold,  
Eternal life, for the bold.  

The first will be last, the last will ascend,  
In God’s Kingdom, new paths will blend.”
