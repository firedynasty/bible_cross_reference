Pharisees and Sadducees, they came with a test,  
Asked for a sign from heaven, thinking they're the best.  
Jesus replied, "You read the sky, know red from blue,  
But you miss the signs, can't see what's true.  

This wicked generation wants a sign, but in vain,  
Only Jonah's sign you'll get, let me explain."  
Then He left them standing, no sign to show,  
Crossed to the other side, where the disciples go.  

They forgot the bread, minds in a twist,  
"Beware the yeast of the Pharisees," Jesus insists.  
"We forgot the bread," they reasoned, confused,  
Jesus perceived, "Why's your faith so bruised?  

Remember the five loaves, fed five thousand, no lack,  
And the seven loaves, more baskets to pack.  
I'm not talking 'bout bread, but beware instead,  
Of the Pharisees' teachings, the things they've said."  

Then they got it, understood the plot,  
It wasn't about bread, but the teachings they bought.  
In Caesarea Philippi, the question arose,  
"Who do people say I am?" Jesus chose.  

"John, Elijah, Jeremiah," the guesses flew,  
"But who do you say I am?" — a question for the few.  
Peter declared, "You're the Christ, God's Son!"  
"Blessed are you, Peter, for what's begun.  

On this rock, I'll build, Hades won't prevail,  
Heaven's keys I give, in binding, you'll not fail."  
But "Tell no one," He said, "keep it discreet,  
For soon I'll face death, but death I'll defeat."  

Peter, in fear, "Lord, never should you die,"  
But Jesus said, "Satan, step aside, don't try.  
Your mind's not on God's, but on man's way,  
Take up your cross, follow me, day by day.  

Gain the world, lose your soul, what's the gain?  
For the Son of Man will come to reign.  
Some here will see the Kingdom unfold,  
Before death takes its toll, the story told."
