Yo, they came with tales of Galilean pain,  
Pilate mixed their blood with the sacrifice, insane.  

Jesus spoke, “You think they were worse than the rest?  
I tell you, no way, you gotta repent, that’s the test.  

Remember the tower, eighteen crushed at Siloam’s gate,  
Were they worse offenders? Don’t fall for that bait.  

Repent now, or face a fate the same,  
It’s your choice, your life, this ain’t a game.”  

He told a story of a fig tree bare,  
The owner searched for fruit, none was there.  

“Cut it down,” he said, “why waste the ground?”  
The vine dresser pleaded, “Just one more round.  

Let me dig, feed it, give it one last chance,  
If it’s barren still, we’ll end this dance.”  

In the synagogue, a woman bent low,  
Eighteen years of pain, just wouldn’t let go.  

Jesus saw her, called her near,  
“Woman, you’re freed,” his voice was clear.  

His hands touched her, she stood tall, glorifying the Lord,  
But the ruler was mad, “Not today,” he roared.  

Jesus fired back, “You hypocrite, don’t you free your beast?  
This daughter of Abraham deserves her peace.”  

The crowd was hyped, his words were fire,  
They cheered his deeds, lifted higher and higher.  

Then he asked, “What’s God’s Kingdom like?  
A mustard seed growing, a tree taking flight.  

Or like yeast in the dough, it spreads and expands,  
God’s Kingdom’s alive, moves through all lands.”  

Teaching town to town, heading to Jerusalem,  
Someone asked, “Few saved?” He turned to them:  

“Strive for the narrow door, many will seek,  
When it’s closed, don’t knock, don’t beg or speak.  

‘We knew you,’ you’ll say, but I’ll reply,  
‘I don’t know you, away, don’t even try.’  

Outside’s weeping, while Abraham and crew,  
Sit in God’s Kingdom, and you’re left out, too.  

From all directions, they’ll gather and feast,  
Last’ll be first, and first might cease.”  

Pharisees warned, “Herod’s out to get you,”  
Jesus said, “Tell that fox what I do.  

Casting demons, healing, today and tomorrow,  
On the third day, my mission ends, no sorrow.  

Jerusalem, killer of prophets, I longed to gather you,  
Like a hen with her chicks, but you wouldn’t let me through.  

Your house, now empty, you will see me again,  
When you say, ‘Blessed is he who comes in the Lord’s name.’”
