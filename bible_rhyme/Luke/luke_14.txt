He rolled into the house of a Pharisee,  
On a Sabbath, they watched him, curiosity.  

A man was there with dropsy, right in view,  
Jesus asked, "Is healing on Sabbath lawful, too?"  

But silence fell, no words were caught,  
He healed the man and let him trot.  

He asked, "If your son or ox, a well would find,  
Wouldn’t you help them out, not stay behind?"  

Their silence continued, no answer to give,  
He saw the seats, where they chose to live.  

"When invited to a wedding, take the lowest seat,  
Or risk the shame, when someone better you meet.  

Sit low, be called up, higher you’ll advance,  
For the humble are lifted, the exalted lose their chance."  

He turned to his host, with a lesson to share,  
"When you feast, invite those with less to spare.  

Not friends or rich neighbors, who might repay,  
But the poor, blind, and maimed, bring them your way.  

You’ll be blessed, they cannot return what you give,  
But in the resurrection, your reward will live."  

One at the table said, "Blessed are those who will dine,  
In God's Kingdom, where the light will always shine."  

Jesus replied with a story, a great feast begun,  
Invitations sent, but excuses were the run.  

One had a field, another oxen to try,  
A third had married, said no with a sigh.  

The master, angered, sent his servant to fetch,  
The poor and the blind, in an urgent stretch.  

"Bring them in, fill my house, there's room for more,  
Forget those invited, who chose to ignore."  

Crowds followed him, so he turned to say,  
"If you cling to life, you can't follow my way.  

Bear your cross, and weigh the cost,  
Or your plans will crumble, foundation lost."  

A builder plans, before the tower's raised,  
A king counts his troops, his strength appraised.  

"Renounce what you have, if you follow my call,  
If salt loses taste, it’s no good at all."  

"Listen well, if you have ears," he spoke clear and loud,  
For those who seek truth, stand out from the crowd.
