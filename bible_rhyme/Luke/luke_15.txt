Tax collectors and sinners drew near,  
Pharisees shocked, their disapproval clear.  
"This man welcomes and dines with such folk!"  
Through a parable, the Teacher spoke:  

"If you had a hundred sheep, one lost its way,  
Would you not leave ninety-nine, come what may?  
In the wild you'd search, seek until it's found,  
And when you find it, joy would abound.  

You'd hoist it high, shoulders strong,  
Call your friends, celebrate with song.  
'Rejoice with me, my lost sheep's here!'  
In heaven, one repentant brings more cheer.  

Or a woman with ten coins so bright,  
Loses one, she searches day and night.  
Lamp in hand, she sweeps, digs around,  
And when she finds it, joy resounds.  

She calls her friends, 'Come, let's rejoice!  
The lost coin's found, hear my voice!'  
So too, angels sing from above,  
When one sinner returns to love."  

Then He told of a father, two sons had he,  
The younger asked for his inheritance free.  
He left for a distant land, wild and free,  
Spent it all in reckless spree.  

A famine hit, his pockets dry,  
Fed pigs, while his stomach cried.  
"I'll return to my father's place,  
And ask to be a servant in disgrace."  

Returning home, father saw him near,  
Ran with love, wiped away his fear.  
"Father, I've sinned, let me serve," he said,  
But his father hugged him, joy widespread.  

"Bring the best robe, dress him fine,  
Put a ring on his hand, sandals aligned.  
Slay the calf, let's feast and sing,  
My son was lost, but now he's living."  

The elder son heard music, curious sound,  
Asked the servant what joy he'd found.  
"Your brother's back, safe and sound,"  
Anger flared, his joy unbound.  

"Father, I've served, never gone astray,  
Yet no feast for me, no special day.  
But for him, who squandered, you celebrate?"  
The father spoke clear, no room for hate:  

"Son, you're with me, all I have is yours,  
But joy for the lost who find the doors.  
Your brother was dead, now alive, see,  
Lost and found, let love be free."
