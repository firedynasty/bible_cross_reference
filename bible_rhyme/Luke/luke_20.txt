In the temple teachin', preachin' that Good News,  
Priests and scribes approached, lookin' for clues.  

"By what power do you act? Who gave the okay?"  
Jesus flipped it back, "I got a question today.  

John's baptism, was it heaven's design?  
Or was it just man's, like a grape on the vine?"  

They huddled and whispered, caught in a bind,  
"If we say heaven, he'll ask why we're blind.  

But call it man's, and the crowd will stone,  
For they see John as prophet, a truth well-known."  

So they shrugged, "We can't say where it's from,"  
Jesus replied, "Then my answer's none."  

He spun a tale, to the crowd he began,  
"A vineyard owner rented land to some men.  

He sent a servant for a share of the grape,  
But they beat him down, and let him escape.  

Another was beaten, treated with shame,  
The third, tossed out, more of the same.  

The lord of the vineyard thought, ‘What's my move?  
I'll send my son; they'll respect his groove.’  

The tenants saw the heir, and plotted with glee,  
'Let's kill him now, and the vineyard's free.'  

They cast him out and took his life,  
What will the lord do with this strife?  

He'll destroy those tenants, give the land away,"  
The crowd gasped, "May it never be that day!"  

Jesus looked, said, "Read the words you reject,  
‘The stone rejected, the cornerstone perfect.’  

Fall on this stone, you're shattered, not whole,  
If it falls on you, it'll crush your soul."  

The priests and scribes plotted, fear in their eyes,  
Afraid of the people, they sent out spies.  

They asked, “Teacher, we know your righteous way,  
Should we pay Caesar, or no tax today?”  

Jesus saw their trick, asked with flair,  
"Whose image is this coin? Tell me, be fair."  

"Caesar's," they said, trying to stall,  
Jesus hit back, "Give Caesar his due, give God your all."  

They marveled, in silence, their trap undone,  
Sadducees then came, denying resurrection’s run.  

"Teacher, if seven brothers married one bride,  
In resurrection whose wife does she abide?"  

Jesus replied, "In the coming age, no marriage or wed,  
They're like angels, living, not dead.  

Moses showed with the bush aflame,  
God of the living, Abraham's name."  

Some scribes praised, "Teacher, you speak right,"  
Nobody dared to question his might.  

Jesus asked, "Why is Christ David's son?  
Yet David calls him Lord, how is this spun?"  

To the crowd, he warned of scribes' pretense,  
"Long robes, best seats, their prayers immense.  

They devour widows, with a show of grace,  
For them, condemnation has its place."
