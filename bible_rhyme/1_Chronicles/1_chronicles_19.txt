Nahash passed, and his son took the throne,  
David said, “Kindness, I won’t leave alone.”  
He sent messengers, their mission was clear,  
To comfort Hanun, for his father dear.  

But Ammon's princes whispered deceit,  
“David’s not here just to comfort or greet.  
Spies in disguise, to search and to pry,  
To overthrow, to see where you lie.”  

Hanun believed, distrust took the lead,  
Shaved David’s men, made them flee in need.  
Garments were cut, humiliation was vast,  
David was told; his anger was cast.  

“Stay in Jericho,” David advised,  
“Till your beards grow back, regain your pride.”  
Ammon knew they were odious now,  
Sent silver for chariots, to make a vow.  

Thirty-two thousand strong, from far and wide,  
Encamped at Medeba, ready to collide.  
David heard, and Joab led the charge,  
With mighty men, their army was large.  

Ammon and allies prepared for the fight,  
Joab saw the battle, surrounded left and right.  
“Abishai, my brother, stand strong beside me,  
If they’re too strong, help each other, be free.  

“Be brave for the cities and our land,  
Yahweh will guide us, by His hand.”  
Joab moved forward, Syrians fled,  
Ammon saw, and in fear they sped.  

Joab returned to Jerusalem’s gate,  
Syria regrouped, but it was too late.  
David crossed Jordan, battle aligned,  
Syrians fought, but defeat was designed.  

Seven thousand chariots fell to the ground,  
Forty thousand footmen, silence profound.  
Shophach was slain, Syria knew peace,  
David’s might, their wars would cease.  

Peace was made, servitude began,  
Ammon stood alone, without Syrian's plan.
