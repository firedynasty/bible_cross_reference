Yahweh spoke out to Moses and Aaron, no delay,  
"Here's the law, listen up, hear what I say.  

Bring a red heifer, perfect, no defect or yoke,  
Hand her to Eleazar, in camp she'll be broke.  

Eleazar takes her blood, with a finger, a sweep,  
Sprinkles it seven times, before the Tent, keep it neat.  

Burn the heifer in sight, skin, meat, blood ignite,  
Add cedar, hyssop, scarlet, in the fire so bright.  

Priest washes his clothes, bathes in water's embrace,  
Returns to the camp, unclean till night takes its place.  

The one who burns her, too, wash and bathe,  
Unclean till the evening, in the water they're saved.  

A clean one collects ashes, stores them outside,  
Use it for cleansing, a law to abide.  

Touch a dead body, unclean seven days,  
Purify on third and seventh, or be cut away.  

In a tent where a man dies, unclean all who enter,  
Every open vessel, unclean at the center.  

Touch the slain in the field, a grave or a bone,  
You're unclean seven days, don't stand alone.  

Take ashes, pour water, let it mix in the vessel,  
Hyssop and water, all impurities wrestle.  

Third day, seventh day, sprinkling's done,  
Wash clothes, bathe, by evening, it's a clean run.  

But fail to purify, and be cut from the crowd,  
Defiles Yahweh’s sanctuary, no impurities allowed.  

Perpetual statute, wash after the sprinkle,  
Touch impurity, unclean till stars twinkle.  

Whatever the unclean touch, it spreads their stain,  
Unclean till evening, till night's calm reign."
