After the plague, God called to Moses, said with might,  
“Count my people, those ready to fight.”  
In Moab's plains, by Jordan's flow,  
Moses and Eleazar made sure they'd know.  

Reuben's sons, the count began,  
Forty-three thousand, in a warrior clan.  
From Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi's line,  
Eliab's sons rebelled, crossed the divine.  

When Korah’s crew faced their doom,  
The earth opened wide, swallowed up in gloom.  
Yet Korah's sons lived; they didn’t die,  
Their numbers stayed, while others said goodbye.  

Simeon’s tally, twenty-two thousand strong,  
Counting Nemuel, Jamin, Zerah, where they belong.  
Gad's tribe next, with a roll call too,  
Forty thousand five hundred, the dream they pursue.  

Judah's sons listed, Er and Onan gone,  
Seventy-six thousand with Perez marching on.  
Issachar’s group, sixty-four thousand plus,  
From Tola to Shimron, they made a fuss.  

Zebulun's families counted, sixty thousand and more,  
From Sered, Elon, Jahleel, they marched forth to explore.  
Joseph's sons, Manasseh and Ephraim,  
With fifty-two thousand, their ranks were a grand hymn.  

Benjamin's numbers, forty-five thousand six,  
With Ard and Naaman, they joined the mix.  
Dan and Asher, with families vast,  
Naphtali's forty-five thousand, marching fast.  

“Grant the land by number,” God commanded, with a plan,  
“To the many, more land; to fewer, less span.”  
The Levites special, no land for their claim,  
But counted they were, in honor, not in name.  

From Gershon to Kohath, Levi’s sons stand,  
Amram’s line through Moses, Aaron, a righteous band.  
With strange fire, Nadab and Abihu fell,  
Their tale a warning, a story to tell.  

Moses and Eleazar counted anew,  
In Moab's plains, Israel's strength they knew.  
But none remained from Sinai's old count,  
Except Caleb and Joshua, whose faith did surmount.
