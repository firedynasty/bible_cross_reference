In the wilderness of Sinai, the story's told,  
Aaron and Moses, generations unfold.  

Sons of Aaron, Nadab, Abihu came first,  
Then Eleazar and Ithamar were rehearsed.  

Priests anointed, set to serve with pride,  
But Nadab and Abihu, strange fire, they died.  

No heirs left behind, so Eleazar and Ithamar remain,  
Serving with Aaron, their roles remain plain.  

God spoke to Moses, "Bring Levi's tribe near,  
Set them before Aaron, let their purpose be clear.  

Keep the congregation and Tent requirements tight,  
Serve in the tabernacle, do everything right.  

Give the Levites to Aaron, wholly given for the land,  
Strangers who approach, in death they will stand."  

God claimed the Levites instead of firstborn,  
"When Egypt's firstborn died, a new calling was born.  

Count Levi's sons, from a month old and up,  
Families numbered, filling the camp's cup."  

Gershon, Kohath, Merari named,  
Families counted, roles proclaimed.  

Gershonites westward, behind the tent they lie,  
Libni and Shimei, their numbers reach the sky.  

Caring for the tabernacle, tent, and covering too,  
Screens and hangings, their duties to pursue.  

Kohathites southside, with sacred tasks at hand,  
Ark, table, lampstand, altars in their command.  

Elizaphan leads, Eleazar oversees,  
Sanctuary's requirements are handled with ease.  

Merari northside, Zuriel guides their way,  
Boards, bars, pillars, sockets, where they stay.  

Aaron, Moses east, by the sunrise they stand,  
Sanctuary's duty, by God's command.  

Twenty-two thousand Levites were numbered that day,  
God's words to Moses kept enemies at bay.  

"Count the firstborn males," Yahweh decreed,  
"Levites for Me, instead of firstborn seed."  

Moses obeyed, counted each name,  
Twenty-two thousand two hundred seventy-three became.  

For those exceeding Levites, a price to redeem,  
Five shekels a head, as the sanctuary's theme.  

Moses took the silver, redemption paid,  
To Aaron and sons, God's command obeyed.  

The Levites stood, a chosen crowd,  
God's purpose fulfilled, clear and loud.
