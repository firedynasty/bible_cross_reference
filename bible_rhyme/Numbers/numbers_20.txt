In Zin, the people stayed, where Miriam laid,  
Her life came to an end, in Kadesh she was laid.  

No water in the land, the people were upset,  
Against Moses and Aaron, their complaints were set.  

"Wish we’d died with kin, why lead us here to fall?  
This place is barren, no crops, no fruits at all!"  

From the crowd they fled, to the Tent's gate,  
Fell on their faces, awaiting Yahweh’s fate.  

"Take the rod," said Yahweh, "speak to the rock's face,  
Water it will flow, to quench the people's chase."  

Moses took the rod, as Yahweh had decreed,  
Gathered all the people, their thirst he would heed.  

"Hear me now, you rebels, shall water come from stone?"  
Struck it twice with the rod, the water made its own.  

Waters rushed out, the people and beasts drank well,  
But Yahweh's voice rose, with a truth to tell.  

"Because you lacked trust, my name you did not raise,  
You won't lead them into the promised place."  

Meribah’s waters, a story of strife and test,  
Where Israel quarreled, and Yahweh was blessed.  

Moses sent word, to Edom’s king with hope,  
"Let us pass through, we won’t stray or grope."  

But Edom’s response was cold as stone,  
"You shall not pass, leave my land alone."  

Israel begged once more, offering peace in their plea,  
But Edom stood firm, with a threat and decree.  

So Israel turned, from Edom’s harsh command,  
Journeyed on to Hor, as Yahweh planned.  

At Mount Hor, Yahweh’s voice did proclaim,  
"Aaron will be gathered, his time has come to claim."  

Moses took Aaron, Eleazar by side,  
On top of Mount Hor, where Aaron would abide.  

Striped of his garments, Eleazar was dressed,  
Aaron died there, in his eternal rest.  

The whole congregation, saw Aaron’s end,  
Wept for thirty days, for their priest and friend.
