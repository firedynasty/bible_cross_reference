Balaam said to Balak, "Seven altars, make them grand,  
Prepare seven bulls, seven rams in this land."  
Balak followed through, just as Balaam had planned,  
A bull and a ram on each altar did stand.  

Balaam spoke to Balak, "Stay by your offering's flame,  
I'll seek Yahweh's word, see what He'll proclaim."  
To the heights he ascended, seeking divine acclaim,  
God met him there, and spoke to his name.  

Balaam reported, "Seven altars I've built,  
A bull and a ram on each, the incense spilt."  
Yahweh put His word in Balaam's mouth,  
"Return to Balak, let the truth go south."  

He came back to Balak, who stood by the fire,  
Princes of Moab with him, their eyes filled with desire.  
Balaam began his chant, from Aram he'd come,  
"Curse Jacob, defy Israel," Balak's beating drum.  

"How can I curse whom God has blessed?  
This nation stands tall, unoppressed.  
From the rocky heights, their strength I can see,  
A people alone, bound to be free."  

"Count their dust? Who can even endeavor?  
May I die as they, blessed forever."  
Balak cried out, "What have you done?  
I sought their curse, but blessings you've spun!"  

Balaam replied, "I speak what Yahweh imparts,  
His words in my mouth, direct to your hearts."  
Balak pleaded, "Try another place,  
See just a part, let curses trace."  

To Zophim's field, atop Pisgah they went,  
Seven altars again, their offerings sent.  
"Stand by your flames," Balaam said, taking leave,  
To meet with God, His word to receive.  

Yahweh spoke again, placed words in his mouth,  
Balaam returned, the plan heading south.  
Balak asked, "What did Yahweh reveal?"  
Balaam spoke, "Listen, here's the deal:  

God's no man, no lies, no mistaken vow,  
He blesses, who can reverse it now?  
In Jacob, no fault, no sin in His sight,  
Yahweh's with them, their king's delight."  

"Out of Egypt God brought them strong,  
Like a wild ox, they cannot go wrong.  
No enchantment can hold them, no diviner's spell,  
'What has God done?' the world will tell."  

"A lioness rising, ready to strike,  
Till prey is devoured, their strength's a spike."  
Balak sighed, "Neither curse nor bless,  
Your words bring me no rest."  

Balaam replied, "Didn't I say it true?  
Yahweh's words command what I must do."  
Balak, persistent, "Another place we'll find,  
Perhaps God will permit your curse this time."  

On Peor's peak, looking into the sands,  
Balaam instructed, "Seven altars, our final stands."  
Balak complied, once more with the plan,  
A bull and a ram on each altar grand.
