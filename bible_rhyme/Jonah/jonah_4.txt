Jonah sat seething, frustration on fire,  
His anger with God rising higher and higher.  

He prayed, "Oh Yahweh, wasn’t this my fear,  
Back home I knew, that's why I'm here.  

You're gracious and kind, mercy your trade,  
Slow to anger, your love never fades.  

Now take my life, I beg, let me be,  
Better to die than live in this plea!"  

God asked, "Is it right to rage and burn?"  
Jonah left the city, hoping to learn.  

He built a booth, sat in its shade,  
Waiting to see what judgement was made.  

God grew a vine, shade over his head,  
Jonah felt joy, his anger fled.  

But dawn came, a worm brought doom,  
The vine withered, sealing its gloom.  

The sun beat down, the wind did sway,  
Jonah wished again for his life to decay.  

God asked, "That vine, should anger confine?"  
Jonah cried, "I'm right, even to die this time."  

God said, "You weep for vines you didn't seed,  
Yet care for a city is not my deed?  

A place of thousands, lost in their plight,  
Shouldn’t I show concern, bring them light?"
