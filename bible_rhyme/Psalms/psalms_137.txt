By Babylon's riverside, there we sat, tears in our eyes,  
Thoughts of Zion filled us, under foreign skies.  

Harps hung high on willow trees, silent and still,  
Captors demanded joy, but we had no will.  

“Sing Zion's songs!” they taunted, voices loud,  
How could we sing Yahweh's song, feeling unbowed?  

“If I forget Jerusalem,” I swore in pain,  
“May my right hand fail, may my skills wane.”  

“Tongue to the roof, if I don't recall,  
Jerusalem above all, my greatest call.”  

"Remember Edom's cry," when they cheered our fall,  
"Raze it to the ground!" their heartless call.  

Babylon, oh Babylon, your time will come,  
Happy is he who repays, righting what's been done.  

Joy for those who strike back, where pain had once been,  
In a world of harsh justice, against the unseen.
