Saul at thirty took the throne, Israel's king,  
Forty-two years, he'd let his reign swing.  
Three thousand troops, he chose his own,  
Two thousand with him, where Michmash was shown.  

Jonathan in Gibeah, a thousand by his side,  
Struck the Philistines, their presence defied.  
Saul blew the trumpet, a rallying sound,  
“Let the Hebrews hear!” he declared all around.  

Israel heard, the Philistines were vexed,  
With hatred, they gathered, ready and perplexed.  
Thirty thousand chariots, horsemen galore,  
Like sands by the sea, they prepared for war.  

Seeing danger, Israel's men in distress,  
Hid in caves, thickets, rocks, in their stress.  
Some crossed to Gad, while Saul stayed in place,  
In Gilgal, trembling, they gathered with haste.  

Seven days waited, but Samuel was late,  
People scattered, fearing their fate.  
Saul said, “Bring the offerings near,”  
He offered burnt offerings, trembling with fear.  

Just as he finished, Samuel appeared,  
Saul went to greet him, but what he feared neared.  
“What have you done?” Samuel asked with a frown,  
Saul explained why he laid the offering down.  

“The people were leaving, you didn't arrive,  
Philistines gathered, I couldn't survive.  
So I made the offering, to seek Yahweh's grace.”  
Samuel replied, “You’ve left Yahweh’s embrace.  

You acted foolishly, ignored God’s call,  
Your kingdom now destined to falter and fall.  
Yahweh has sought a heart after His own,  
A new prince appointed, for obedience shown.”  

Samuel left, Saul counted men on hand,  
Six hundred stood firm, ready to withstand.  
In Geba, they camped, as Philistines closed,  
Three raider companies, their menace exposed.  

No blacksmiths in Israel, Philistines' control,  
No swords or spears, only tools to console.  
Each man to the Philistines, to sharpen their gear,  
Plowshares and axes, all in disrepair.  

On battle day, no weapons in sight,  
Only Saul and Jonathan prepared for the fight.  
Philistines at Michmash, their garrison set,  
The tension was high, the stage firmly met.
