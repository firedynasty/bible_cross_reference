Philistines gathered, armies in the fray,  
Achish told David, "You'll fight with me today."  
David replied, "You'll see what I can do,"  
Achish said, "Bodyguard, forever, that's you."  

Now Samuel's gone, all Israel mourned deep,  
Buried in Ramah, where he'd forever sleep.  
Saul sent away spirits, wizards too,  
Philistines camped in Shunem, with a view.  

Saul saw the foe, his heart trembled with fear,  
He called to Yahweh, but no answer was near.  
"Find me a woman, a spirit she'll bring,"  
"There's one in Endor," was their offering.  

Saul in disguise, with two men in the night,  
To the woman he said, "Bring a spirit to light."  
The woman replied, "Saul's cut us out,"  
"Why lay a snare, put my life in doubt?"  

Saul swore, "No harm will come by Yahweh's breath,"  
"Who'll I bring?" "Bring Samuel from death."  
She saw Samuel, cried out in fright,  
"Why deceive me? You're Saul in the night!"  

"Don't be scared," Saul assured, "What do you see?"  
"A god from the earth, coming to me."  
"Describe him," Saul asked, "What's his attire?"  
"An old man, robed," she spoke of the sire.  

Saul knew it was Samuel, bowed low,  
Samuel said, "Why disturb me so?"  
Saul answered, "I'm distressed, foes at my gate,  
God's gone, no word, tell me my fate."  

"Why ask me?" Samuel's voice thundered loud,  
"Yahweh's left you, and no longer proud.  
The kingdom's torn, given to David's hand,  
For disobeying Yahweh's command.  

Philistines shall seize, you and sons won't be,  
With me tomorrow, in death you'll greet me."  
Saul fell flat, terrified by the news,  
Weak from hunger, lost and confused.  

The woman saw Saul, troubled and frail,  
"Listen to me, let me end this tale.  
Eat for strength, go on your way,"  
He refused but they urged him to stay.  

With a calf she cooked, bread made quick,  
Saul and his men ate, feeling sick.  
Then they rose, went away into the night,  
Heavy with the words, and their future's plight.
