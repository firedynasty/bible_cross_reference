Samuel died, the nation cried, in Ramah he was laid,  
David hit the wilderness, to Paran he made his way.  

In Maon lived a man, Nabal was his name,  
Rich in sheep and goats, but surly in his game.  

Abigail, his wife, she was wise and fair,  
David heard of Nabal's shearing, and sent his men there.  

“Go to Nabal,” David said, “tell him these words from me,  
Peace on your house, and all your prosperity.  

Your men were with us, and we kept them safe and sound,  
So please, on this good day, spread some bounty around.”  

But Nabal sneered, “Who’s David? Who's this son of Jesse?  
Should I share my feast with strangers? No, I won't be messy.”  

David’s men returned, told him the tale,  
David shouted, “Grab your swords, our justice won't fail!”  

But a servant warned Abigail, “Trouble’s on the way,  
Nabal’s insulted David, now we’ll have to pay.”  

Quickly, Abigail packed bread, wine, and sheep,  
Loaded her donkeys, and into the night did creep.  

She met David on the path, bowed down at his feet,  
“Blame me, my lord, let my words be sweet.  

Nabal’s a fool, true to his name,  
But you don’t need this blood on your fame.  

God will make you great, don’t let this weigh your mind,  
Let your enemies be like Nabal, left far behind.”  

David replied, “Blessed be God who sent you here,  
You stopped my hand, now no blood will appear.”  

Abigail went home, found Nabal in his feast,  
Too drunk to hear her, she let the night cease.  

In the morning light, she told him all the scene,  
His heart froze within him, his face turned green.  

Ten days later, God struck him down,  
David heard and praised God, who kept him sound.  

He sent for Abigail, to make her his bride,  
She bowed low, and joined his side.
