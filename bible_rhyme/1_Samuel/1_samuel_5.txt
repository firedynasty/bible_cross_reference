The Philistines took the ark, brought it to Ashdod's land,  
Placed it next to Dagon, their idol, grand.  

Morning came, Ashdod folks rose to see,  
Dagon facedown, a sight of mystery.  

They picked him up, set him right once more,  
But next morning, same fate as before.  

Dagon lay broken, head and hands on the floor,  
Only his body left by the door.  

Priests of Dagon feared, the threshold they'd shun,  
Yahweh's power on Ashdod—none could outrun.  

Yahweh’s hand was heavy, tumors struck them down,  
Ashdod’s borders trembled, fear spread through the town.  

Men of Ashdod said, “This ark can't stay,  
His hand is fierce, we must send it away.”  

Philistine lords gathered, seeking what to do,  
“Send it to Gath,” they decided, “Let it pass through.”  

In Gath it went, but trouble followed still,  
Yahweh’s hand struck again, with a painful chill.  

Tumors on the people, small and great,  
Confusion and cries, sealing their fate.  

They sent the ark to Ekron, but the cries began,  
“They’ve brought the ark to kill us, to kill every man!”  

Philistine lords convened, troubled with dread,  
“Send it back home, or we’ll all end up dead.”  

Panic in the city, God’s hand heavy and grim,  
Those left alive, prayed and cried out to Him.
