In Ramathaim Zophim, a man named Elkanah,  
Two wives he had, Hannah and Peninnah.  
Peninnah bore children, but Hannah was bare,  
Yearly they'd worship in Shiloh, offering each share.

Elkanah to Peninnah and her kids did provide,  
But to Hannah, his love, a double portion beside.  
Though Yahweh closed her womb, Peninnah would tease,  
Hannah wept bitterly, her heart never at ease.

Elkanah asked, "Hannah, why is your heart sore?  
Am I not better than ten sons and more?"  
But Hannah prayed deeply, tears flowed like a stream,  
Promised a son to God, if He'd fulfill her dream.

Eli the priest saw her lips move without sound,  
Thought she was drunk, but no drink was around.  
She pleaded, "I'm pouring my soul, not filled with wine,  
Out of pain and sorrow, I utter each line."

Eli said, "Go in peace; may God grant your choice."  
Her heart felt lighter, hope found its voice.  
They worshiped at dawn, to Ramah they trod,  
Elkanah and Hannah, blessed by God.

Time passed, Hannah conceived, a son she bore,  
Named him Samuel, "Asked of Yahweh," she swore.  
Elkanah went to make his vow, true and sincere,  
But Hannah said, "I'll wait till our child is clear."

Elkanah agreed, "Do what you deem right,  
May Yahweh's word stand firm in His sight."  
When Samuel was weaned, to Shiloh they came,  
With offerings in hand, to honor God's name.

Hannah spoke to Eli, "Remember me here?  
I prayed for this child, my words sincere.  
Now I give him to Yahweh, for all his days,  
To serve and worship, in all of his ways."
