When I talk to you, Yahweh, you're always right,  
But I've got questions under this night:  
Why do the wicked thrive and get ahead?  
Why are the tricky ones so well-fed?  

You planted them, they've taken root,  
They're growing tall, producing fruit.  
Your name's on their lips, but hearts are cold,  
Near in word, far in soul, that's bold.  

But you, Yahweh, you know my name,  
You see my heart, you test my frame.  
"Pull them out like sheep to the slaughter,  
Prepare their end, that's what they oughta."  

How long will the land weep and mourn,  
While plants and beasts are wrecked and torn?  
The wicked say, "He won't see our fate,"  
But their time's coming, it ain't too late.  

"If footmen tire you out, what then with horse?  
In peace you're safe, but what about the Jordan's force?  
Even your own kin, your family line,  
Acted false, don't trust their kind words, fine."  

"I've left my house, my heritage I cast,  
Handed over to foes, it's gone at last.  
My land's a lion, roaring against me,  
Turned away from her, let it be."  

Is my land a prey, surrounded by birds?  
Gather the beasts, let actions match words.  
Shepherds have wrecked my cherished place,  
Made it mourn, a desolate space.  

Destruction's come from high to low,  
Yahweh's sword cuts to and fro.  
Wheat they planted, but thorns they got,  
Worked so hard, yet profit they did not.  

Yahweh says, "To those who steal my land,  
I'll pluck you up, with my own hand.  
But after wrath, comes compassion’s call,  
Back they'll come, each one and all."  

"If they learn my ways and speak my name,  
Like they once spoke Baal's in shame,  
They'll stand with my people, strong and tall,  
But if not, I'll pluck and end it all," says Yahweh, echoing the hall.
