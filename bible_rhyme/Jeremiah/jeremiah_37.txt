Zedekiah ruled, but not by his own hand,  
Nebuchadnezzar's choice for Judah's land.  

Yet he and his servants shut their ears tight,  
Ignoring Yahweh's words, no prophet in sight.  

Zedekiah sent his men to Jeremiah, to say,  
"Pray to Yahweh for us, guide our way."  

Jeremiah walked free, mingling with the crowd,  
Not yet in chains, still speaking aloud.  

Pharaoh’s troops advanced, breaking the siege wide,  
Chaldeans backed off, dispersed to the side.  

Then Yahweh spoke clear, through Jeremiah's voice,  
"Tell Judah's king, there's no other choice.  

Pharaoh's aid is fleeting; they'll retreat back in haste,  
Chaldeans will return, laying the city to waste.  

Don't fool yourselves, thinking they've gone,  
They'll rise again, and the city's forlorn.  

Even if wounded, their strength shall inspire,  
They'll come back and burn this city with fire."  

With Chaldeans gone, Jeremiah tried to leave,  
Heading to Benjamin, his portion to retrieve.  

At Benjamin's gate, a guard called him out,  
"You’re defecting," he claimed, with a shout.  

Jeremiah declared, "It's a lie, I'm true!"  
But they dragged him to the princes, who beat him black and blue.  

Locked up deep, in the scribe's prison kept,  
Jeremiah waited, while the city slept.  

Zedekiah called him, secret words exchanged,  
"What's Yahweh's word?" Jeremiah's voice ranged.  

"There is a word," he said, without a quiver,  
"You’ll be delivered to Babylon's river."  

Jeremiah then questioned, "Why am I here?  
What have I done to earn this fear?  

Your prophets lied, where are they now?  
They said Babylon would never come, with a bow.  

Please, my king, don't send me back there,  
To the scribe's house, where death's in the air."  

Zedekiah heard, and moved him with a nod,  
To the court of the guard, with daily bread from God.  

So Jeremiah stayed, held but fed,  
Awaiting the fate that lay ahead.
