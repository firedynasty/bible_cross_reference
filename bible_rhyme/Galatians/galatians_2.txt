Fourteen years passed, then back I went,  
To Jerusalem, with Barnabas sent.  

Titus came along, by revelation's lead,  
Shared the Good News, planting the seed.  

Spoke to the respected, in private tone,  
Feared my race, not well-known.  

Titus, a Greek, unpressured to change,  
False brothers sought to rearrange.  

Spies on our freedom in Christ they came,  
To subject us, to bind and tame.  

But we stood strong, not an inch we gave,  
Truth of the Good News we fought to save.  

Those considered great, no weight they bore,  
God's impartiality, at the core.  

They saw my mission, Peter's too,  
To Gentiles, uncircumcised, my view.  

Grace recognized, hands clasped tight,  
Go to Gentiles, with God's light.  

"Remember the poor," they asked, indeed,  
A passion I held, a righteous creed.  

Peter in Antioch, I faced him bold,  
His actions, they needed to be told.  

Ate with Gentiles, till James' crew came,  
Then he withdrew, avoiding blame.  

Jews followed, even Barnabas strayed,  
Hypocrisy thick, in their charade.  

Confronted Peter, in the circle's heat,  
"Why compel others, a Jew's life to meet?  

"We're Jews by nature, not sinners, yet see,  
Faith, not law, justifies, sets us free."  

Believe in Jesus, justified by faith's call,  
Not by the law, can't save us all.  

If rebuilding law proves me wrong,  
Through Christ, in God's life, I belong.  

Crucified with Him, I live no more,  
Christ in me, love like never before.  

By faith, I walk, in the Son who gave,  
Reject not grace, for law can't save.  

If law's our hope, and that's the scene,  
Then why did Christ die? What's it mean?
