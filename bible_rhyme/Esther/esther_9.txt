In the twelfth month's flow, Adar's thirteenth day,  
The king's decree was near, the stage set to slay.  

Enemies hoped to conquer, take control,  
But tables turned, Jews conquered, taking their toll.  

In cities they gathered, ready for the fight,  
No one could withstand them, their power, their might.  

Princes and governors stood by their side,  
For Mordecai's fame made all fear and abide.  

Mordecai grew mighty, his reputation soared,  
Throughout the provinces, his influence roared.  

Jews struck with swords, enemies laid low,  
Destruction reigned, their strong will did show.  

In Susa's citadel, five hundred met their end,  
Ten sons of Haman, they did apprehend.  

The king heard the news, five hundred were dead,  
"What's your request, Esther?" he graciously said.  

Esther spoke, "If the king pleases, one more day,  
Hang Haman's sons high, let justice have sway."  

The king agreed, the decree was proclaimed,  
On gallows hung, Haman's sons were framed.  

Jews in Susa gathered, another day they fought,  
Three hundred more slain, plunder they sought not.  

Seventy-five thousand in provinces wide,  
Fell to the Jews, the victors with pride.  

On the thirteenth day, they rested, found peace,  
Feasting and gladness, their joys did increase.  

Village Jews declared the fourteenth a feast,  
Sending gifts of food, their joy never ceased.  

Mordecai wrote, letters sent near and far,  
To keep the days Adar, a time of joy, a star.  

Days of gladness, sorrow turned to cheer,  
Feasting and gifts, every single year.  

Purim they named it, from the "Pur" that was cast,  
A time to remember those days that had passed.  

Letters sent out, Esther and Mordecai declare,  
Peace and truth, a legacy to share.  

The command confirmed, Purim etched in the book,  
A story of victory, the path they took.
