In the days of King Ahasuerus, from India to Ethiopia's might,  
He ruled over provinces, a hundred twenty-seven in sight.  

On his throne in Susa's grand palace, he sat with pride,  
In his third year, a grand feast he did provide.  

Princes and servants, all gathered for the show,  
The riches of his kingdom, he let them all know.  

For one hundred eighty days, the splendor was displayed,  
Then a seven-day feast for all in Susa was made.  

White and blue hangings, purple cords so fine,  
Golden vessels held the royal wine.  

In the garden's court, both great and small,  
Every man's pleasure, the king's decree for all.  

Queen Vashti, too, held a feast for her own,  
In the royal house, where her power was shown.  

On the seventh day, the king, merry with wine,  
Called for Vashti, his queen so fine.  

But Vashti refused the king's command,  
His anger burned, like fire on the sand.  

To the wise men, the king called for advice,  
"What shall we do? Vashti's disobedience isn't nice."  

Memucan spoke, "Not just you, my king, she wrongs,  
All the people, princes, where she belongs."  

"If word spreads, wives will defy,  
Contempt will rise like clouds in the sky."  

"Let a royal decree be written with haste,  
Vashti banished, replaced with no waste."  

"When proclaimed, wives will honor their men,  
From the greatest to the smallest, they'll know when."  

The king and princes agreed with the plan,  
Letters were sent, man's rule over his clan.  

So went the word, to each province and place,  
In every tongue, every people, no race.
