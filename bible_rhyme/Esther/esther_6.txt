The king couldn't sleep, tossing in his bed,  
Called for the chronicles to be read.  

Mordecai's deed was found in the scroll,  
Two eunuchs' plot, he had controlled.  

The king asked, "What honor has been shared?"  
"Nothing," said the servants, "nothing's been declared."  

"Who's in the court?" the king declared,  
Haman was there, his evil plan prepared.  

"Bring him in," the king did say,  
Haman entered, thinking he’d have his way.  

"What should be done for the man I favor?"  
Haman thought it was him, feeling like a savior.  

"Royal clothes, the king's own ride,  
Crown on the horse, let him stride."  

"Proclaim his honor through the city square,  
For the king’s delight, let all be aware."  

"Hurry," said the king, "do this for Mordecai,  
Leave nothing undone, don’t even try."  

Haman dressed Mordecai, led him through the streets,  
Humbled and mourning, with his pride in retreat.  

He went home, head covered, full of dread,  
Told his wife and friends everything he said.  

"If Mordecai’s Jewish, you can’t win this race,  
You'll surely fall, can't keep the pace."  

While they spoke, the king's men arrived,  
To take Haman to Esther's feast, he strived.
