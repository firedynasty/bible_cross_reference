On the third day, Esther dressed in royal wear,  
Stood in the court, bold, in the king's lair.  
The king on his throne, with eyes sharp and keen,  
Saw Esther and favored the queen.

He lifted his scepter, a golden rod,  
Esther approached, touched the scepter of God.  
“What do you wish, Queen Esther?” he said,  
“Half the kingdom's yours, just nod your head.”

Esther replied, “If it pleases the king,  
Join me and Haman at the banquet I bring.”  
“Bring Haman quickly,” the king gave the call,  
To Esther’s feast, they went, standing tall.

At the banquet of wine, the king was keen,  
“What do you desire, my gracious queen?”  
Esther answered, “If it pleases you still,  
Join me tomorrow, the feast I’ll fulfill.”

Haman left joyful, his heart a grand tune,  
Till Mordecai sat firm, a thorn in his bloom.  
Yet anger inside, Haman held it tight,  
Went home to boast of his glory and might.

Told his riches, his rise in king's place,  
And how Esther invited him alone to grace.  
“Still, it’s worthless,” he said with spite,  
“Mordecai sits there, in plain sight.”

His wife Zeresh and friends had a plan,  
“Build gallows tall, fifty cubits span.  
In the morning, speak to the king with pride,  
Hang Mordecai, then banquet with stride.”

This pleased Haman, so he made it be,  
A gallows stood tall, a grim decree.
