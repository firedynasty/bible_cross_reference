In the palace, King Ahasuerus made a decree,  
Gave Haman's house to Esther, set Mordecai free.  
The king took off his ring, once on Haman's hand,  
Passed it to Mordecai, who now took command.  

Esther fell at the king's feet, tears in her plea,  
"End Haman's evil plans, let my people be."  
The king held out his scepter, golden and bright,  
Esther rose to speak, standing tall in the light.  

"If it pleases you, king, and I find grace,  
Reverse Haman's letters, let my people embrace.  
How can I watch my kin face such doom?  
How endure their destruction, their impending gloom?"  

King Ahasuerus turned to Mordecai and the queen,  
"Haman's been hanged, I gave you his scene.  
Write now in my name, seal it with my ring,  
For no man can reverse the word of the king."  

Scribes were summoned in Sivan's month, day twenty-three,  
Mordecai's commands inked for all to see.  
From India to Ethiopia, provinces wide,  
Every tongue and tribe, the message couldn't hide.  

Letters sealed with the king's ring, swiftly sent,  
On royal horses, through each province they went.  
The Jews could gather, defend, and fight,  
Against any who'd attack, in Adar's twelfth night.  

The word spread fast, the decree was clear,  
Jews prepared for that day, without fear.  
Couriers pushed on by the king's command,  
In Susa, the news was grand.  

Mordecai in blue and white, crowned with gold,  
Entered the city, the story told.  
The Jews rejoiced, light and honor in their way,  
Feasting and gladness on this victorious day.  

In every town, where the king’s words rang,  
Jews celebrated, and their praises sang.  
Many joined them, feeling the fear,  
For the triumph of the Jews was crystal clear.
