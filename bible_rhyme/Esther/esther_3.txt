King Ahasuerus raised Haman high,  
Above the princes, reaching for the sky.  

Servants at the gate all bowed down,  
But Mordecai stood firm, didn’t frown.  

"Why disobey the king's command?" they said,  
Yet Mordecai stood, wouldn’t bow his head.  

Told Haman of the Jew who refused the call,  
Haman's anger rose, wouldn’t let it fall.  

Not just Mordecai, but all his kin,  
Haman plotted where the dark began.  

First month, Nisan, the lot was cast,  
Twelfth month, Adar, the day was fast.  

To the king, Haman spoke of a scattered race,  
"Different laws, they dishonor your face.  

If it pleases you, let them be erased,  
Silver will follow, your coffers embraced."  

The king gave his ring with mighty power,  
"The silver is yours, use it this hour."  

Scribes wrote all that Haman decreed,  
Letters sent out with a swift, urgent speed.  

Destroy young and old, every Jew in sight,  
On the twelfth month's thirteenth night.  

The letters spread, a decree so grim,  
The city of Susa felt its dim.  

The king and Haman sat to drink in glee,  
But Susa was confused, couldn’t see the spree.
