Do you know when mountain goats give birth, their silent call?  
Have you watched the doe when her fawns stand tall?  

Can you count the months they grow, fulfill their time?  
Do you see them bow down, young born in their prime?  

They rise up strong, in fields they roam,  
Out on their own, never coming back home.  

Who set the wild donkey free to roam the land?  
Who gave him the wilderness, the salt to stand?  

He hears not the city's chaotic cries,  
In mountain ranges, his pasture lies.  

Will the wild ox serve you, be your friend?  
Harness him to plow, will he work 'til the end?  

Trust him for his strength, will he bring home grain?  
Leave him your labor, will he sustain?  

The ostrich flaps her wings with pride,  
But leaves her eggs in the earth to hide.  

She forgets the foot that might crush them down,  
No fear in her heart while roaming around.  

God gave her no wisdom, no understanding in place,  
Yet she scorns the rider in an open race.  

Have you given the horse its mighty might?  
Clothed his neck with a mane, a quivering sight?  

He leaps like a locust, glory in his snort,  
Paws the valley, charging forth to court.  

Fearless he meets the armed, no sword can dismay,  
The quiver rattles, spears flash in the fray.  

Ground eaten with rage, trumpet calls out, "Aha!" he sings,  
Smells battle, captains' thunder, and the shout it brings.  

Is it your wisdom that makes the hawk soar high,  
Stretching wings south, painting the sky?  

Did you command the eagle to mount up and fly,  
Build his nest on the cliff, touching the sky?  

From the heights, he spies prey afar,  
His young taste blood where the fallen are.
