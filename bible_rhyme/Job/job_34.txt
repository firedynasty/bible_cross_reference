Elihu stepped up, he had something to say,  
"Listen up, wise men, don't walk away.  
The ear tests words like the tongue tests food,  
Let's choose what's right, let's find the good.  

Job claims he's right, says God's taken his side,  
But calls him a liar, like his wound won't hide.  
Who else drinks scorn like water, so bold,  
Walks with the wicked, his story told?  

He says, 'What's the profit in loving the Lord?'  
Listen, wise ones, hear my accord:  
God won't do wicked, no iniquity here,  
He pays back in full, no need for fear.  

God's not unjust, He won't twist the rules,  
Who put Him in charge? Who made Him the school's?  
If He kept His breath to Himself alone,  
We'd all turn to dust, back to the bone.  

If you're wise, pay heed, hear my call,  
Should a hater of justice govern us all?  
Who dares call a king 'Vile!' to his face,  
Or nobles 'Wicked!'—who would disgrace?  

God's no respecter of wealth or of fame,  
Rich or poor, He made them the same.  
In a moment, they fall, mighty and weak,  
At midnight, they're gone, no hand to seek.  

He watches all paths, no shadow's too deep,  
No hiding for those who sow what they reap.  
He breaks down the mighty, sets others in place,  
In night He destroys, leaves no trace.  

The cries of the poor reach His ear, loud and clear,  
When He gives peace, who condemns? Who'll steer?  
He's over nations and men, sovereign and true,  
To stop the godless from ruling the crew.  

Can anyone tell God, 'I'm guilty, but I'll learn'?  
"Teach me what I miss; no more will I spurn."  
Should the choice be yours? No, it's on you,  
Speak what you know, let truth shine through.  

The wise will say, 'Job's lost his way,  
No knowledge in his words today.'  
I wish Job's tested, see his end defined,  
He rebels with words, against God he's aligned."
