Job stood up with anguish, pain, and despair,  
Wishing his suffering could be weighed in the air.  

Heavy like sand, his words rushed out fast,  
God’s arrows hit deep, his spirit couldn’t last.  

"Does a donkey complain when the grass is lush?  
Or does an ox moan when his food's a plush?"  

Tasteless is life, like food with no salt,  
He’s tired of the blandness, his soul wants to halt.  

"Oh, let my desire be granted by God above,  
To crush me or release me from this love."  

In pain, he'd find solace, holding his ground,  
Not denying the Holy One's words that resound.  

Strength’s not endless like stones, nor flesh like bronze,  
All help’s gone, wisdom’s vanished, nothing on.  

To the faint, a friend's kindness is owed,  
Even if they've strayed from the Almighty’s road.  

Brothers like streams that dry and fade,  
Ice turns to water, in heat they evade.  

Caravans in search, left distressed in their call,  
Confident in nothing, terrified they fall.  

Did I beg for gifts or ask for your aid?  
Or seek liberation from the oppressor's blade?  

"Teach me now, I’ll hold my peace and stand,  
Show me my error, help me understand."  

Words of truth hold power, but yours miss the mark,  
You criticize words that fly in the dark.  

"Would you gamble on orphans, sell your friend away?  
Look at me truly, I’ve got nothing to betray."  

Let there be no injustice, return and see,  
Right is my cause, let truth set me free.  

Is my tongue unjust, or do I speak in vain?  
Can I not taste what’s mischievous and plain?
