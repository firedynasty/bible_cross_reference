Job spoke up, bold and strong, "Listen close, don't get me wrong.  
Let me speak, hold your jeer, once I'm done, mock all you hear.  

Why should I withhold complaint? Impatience? I won't paint it faint.  
Look at me now, amazed and hushed, my skin crawls, my spirit crushed.  

Why do the wicked grow so old, living strong, growing bold?  
Their kids are there right by their side, their offspring thrive, nowhere to hide.  

Their homes are safe, without a threat, God's rod is absent, no debt to be met.  
Their bulls are breeding, cows don’t slip, calves are born, no hardship.  

Children frolic, dance in glee, to tambourine and harp, they’re free.  
Prosperous days, sudden grave's call, they tell God, ‘Leave, we want no part at all.’  

"What’s the Almighty got for our gain? If we pray, is there profit to attain?  
Their hands hold not prosperity’s glow, the wicked's plan, far from me though.  

How often does the wicked’s light snuff out, when God’s wrath brings sorrows about?  
How often are they like chaff in the breeze, scattered away by storm with ease?  

You argue, ‘God saves his dues for their kin,’ let him show wrath while they live in sin.  
Let him see his own destruction, face Almighty’s fierce eruption.  

What’s it matter when he’s gone, when his months have all withdrawn?  
Can anyone teach God’s mind, when judgment on high, He’ll find?  

One dies full and at peace, milk flowing, bones at ease.  
Another passes in soul’s despair, never knowing good or care.  

Both lie in dust, side by side, worms cover them, nowhere to hide.  
I know your thoughts, your wrongful scheme, where’s the prince's house, his dream?  

Ask the travelers what they've learned, evil awaits, and wrath is earned.  
Who’ll confront him, hold him to task? Yet to the grave, he’ll be masked.  

Sweet earth clods cover his bed, men follow, but where are they led?  
So how can you comfort with falsehoods and lie? Your answers are vacant, I’ll tell you why."
