In the land of Uz, there lived a man named Job,  
Blameless and upright, dodging evil like a pro.  

He had seven sons and three daughters so fair,  
His wealth immense, beyond compare.  

Seven thousand sheep and camels by the thousands,  
Oxen, donkeys, his household was astoundin'.  

The greatest man in the East, no doubt,  
His family feasted, joy all about.  

After the feasts, Job would rise at dawn,  
Offerings made, for sins that might spawn.  

"Maybe my kids have cursed God in their heart,"  
So Job played his faithful, protective part.  

He did this continually, showing his belief,  
While in heaven's court, a brewing grief.  

God's sons gathered before the Lord,  
Satan slipped in, with his deceitful chord.  

"Where've you been?" God asked with a gaze,  
Satan replied, "Roaming earth's maze."  

"Seen my servant Job, upright and true?"  
God's words were firm, "In him, no evil brew."  

Satan challenged, "Job's piety isn't free,  
He's blessed on all sides, can't you see?  

But take it away, he'll curse you, no doubt,"  
God allowed the test, His faith devout.  

"All he has is yours to test,  
But spare his life, let him stand the rest."  

Job's day turned dark, disaster struck,  
Messengers ran, bringing tales of muck.  

"The Sabeans raided, took your oxen away,  
Servants fell by the sword that day."  

Another rushed in, "Fire from the sky,  
Sheep and servants, burned alive."  

Then another came, "Chaldeans swept like bands,  
Camels gone, servants slain by their hands."  

Finally, the worst news did unfold,  
A wind struck, left his kin cold.  

Yet in all this, Job did not falter,  
He tore his robe, shaved his head, fell to alter.  

"Naked I came, naked I'll return,  
God gives, God takes, His name I'll affirm."  

Job didn’t sin, he didn't accuse,  
Held firm to his faith, didn’t refuse.
