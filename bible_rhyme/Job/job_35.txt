Elihu spoke up, his voice clear and bright,  
"Do you claim your way's more right than God's might?  
Do you ask, 'What do I gain by this path I choose,  
More than if I had sinned and let myself lose?'  

I'll answer you, and all those at your side.  
Look up at the skies, see their vastness wide.  
If you've sinned, what effect do you have on Him?  
Your multiplied wrongs don't make His light dim.  

If you're righteous, what does God gain from you?  
What can you hand Him that He never knew?  
Your wickedness stings your fellow man, it's true,  
And your good deeds, they profit those like you.  

Oppressed folks cry out, 'neath the mighty's strong hand,  
They shout for help, but don't understand.  
No one asks, 'Where's God who gives night songs?  
Who teaches us more than beasts with their throngs?  

They cry out, but pride blocks their call,  
God won't heed empty cries, won't hear them at all.  
Much less when you claim you can't see His might,  
Your case is before Him; don't give up the fight.  

He's not moved by wrath, doesn't embrace pride,  
So Job talks empty, words with no guide."
