Jacob saw grain, knew where to go,  
"Why stand here, sons, all in a row?"  

He said, "I've heard of Egypt's grain,  
Go down, buy some, spare us pain."  

Ten brothers went, with Benjamin left,  
"Can't risk him harmed," said Jacob, bereft.  

In Canaan's famine, they went to buy,  
Joseph, governor, watched them nigh.  

They bowed low, faces to earth,  
Joseph knew them, but hid his mirth.  

"Where from?" he asked, harsh and keen,  
"From Canaan for food," their words between.  

Joseph knew, they knew him not,  
Dreams came back, a familiar plot.  

"You are spies," he accused with fire,  
"To see the land's weakness, your desire."  

"No, my lord, honest men in plight,  
Twelve brothers in Canaan, sons in sight."  

Joseph said, "You came to see the land,  
Prove you're true as I demand."  

He locked them up for three days,  
In their minds, guilt ablaze.  

On the third, Joseph spoke anew,  
"If you're honest, here's what to do:  

Leave one here, go, take the grain,  
Bring your youngest, prove you're plain."  

They felt guilt for Joseph's fate,  
Reuben said, "Too late, too late."  

Joseph turned, tears on his face,  
Bound Simeon in their place.  

Grain in sacks, money restored,  
They left Egypt, troubled, floored.  

Opened sacks, money saw,  
Fear struck, left in awe.  

Back to Jacob, tale to tell,  
Of Egypt's lord, and prison cell.  

He spoke rough, called them spies,  
"Bring Benjamin," his reprise.  

Sacks opened, money found,  
Fear, dread, all around.  

Jacob cried, "My sons are gone,  
Joseph, Simeon, Benjamin drawn."  

Reuben vowed, "Entrust him to me,  
I'll bring him back, wait and see."  

Jacob refused, his heart in woe,  
"To Sheol in sorrow, I can't let him go."
