Isaac was old, his eyes couldn't see,  
Called Esau, "My son?" "Here I am," said he.  

"I'm old now, don't know when I'll die,  
Grab your bow, hunt some venison, make it by and by."  

"Cook it up tasty, bring it to me,  
So my soul can bless you, let it be free."  

Rebekah was listening, she heard it all,  
Esau went hunting, obeying the call.  

Rebekah to Jacob whispers the plan,  
"Your dad's ready to bless Esau, understand?"  

"Get two goats, I'll cook them just right,  
Your father will bless you, in disguise you'll take flight."  

"But Esau's hairy, I'm smooth, what if he sees?"  
"Let the curse be on me, just do as I please."  

So Jacob fetched the goats, brought them back home,  
His mother prepared them, like Isaac's own.  

She dressed Jacob in Esau's best clothes,  
Put goat skins on his hands, on his neck they rose.  

He brought the dish to Isaac, said "I'm your son."  
Isaac asked, "Who?" "Esau," said the one.  

"How so quick?" Isaac questioned, surprised.  
"God gave me success," Jacob replied.  

"Come closer, let me feel your hand,"  
Isaac felt the hair, "Seems like Esau, grand."  

He didn't recognize, so the blessing came,  
Asked again, "Are you Esau?" Jacob claimed the name.  

Isaac ate the meal, enjoyed the wine,  
Blessed Jacob, "May nations serve, all align."  

"Fruits of the earth, skies' morning dew,  
Be master over kin, may curses fall on who curses you."  

Jacob just left as Esau walked in,  
With his own dish for the blessing to begin.  

"Who are you?" Isaac asked, his heart missed a beat,  
"I'm your firstborn, Esau," came the repeat.  

Isaac trembled, "Who got the blessing instead?  
I've blessed him already, your brother," he said.  

Esau cried out, "Father, bless me too!"  
"Your brother deceived me, the blessing's through."  

Esau lamented, "Twice he took my right,  
First my birthright, now my blessing, my plight."  

Isaac replied, "Made him your lord, can't change,  
I've given grain and wine, it's been arranged."  

Esau pleaded, "One blessing left for me?"  
Isaac spoke again, "Here's what yours will be."  

"Live by the sword, serve your brother,  
But when you break free, you won't be smothered."  

Esau burned with a grudge, plotted in mind,  
"After Dad's days, Jacob's fate I'll find."  

Rebekah warned Jacob, "Your life's in danger here,  
Flee to Uncle Laban, till Esau's wrath is clear."  

"Stay a while, let his anger subside,  
I'll call you back, when peace does abide."  

She spoke to Isaac, weary of her life,  
"If Jacob marries Heth's daughter, it'll bring me strife."
