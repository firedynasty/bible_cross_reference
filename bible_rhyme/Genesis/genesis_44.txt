Joseph said to his man, "Fill their sacks with grain,  
Put the silver cup in the youngest's bag, plain and simple, no refrain."

Morning light broke, they left with donkeys in tow,  
Joseph’s steward followed fast, the plan ready to go.

“Why repay good with ill?” the steward's words sprang,  
“You took my lord’s cup, from which his insights hang.”

Overtook them quick, spoke the words Joseph planned,  
The brothers protested, "Why blame us so grand?"

"We brought back the silver, found in each sack,  
Why would we steal? We’d never do that!"

"If the cup's found with one, let him face blame,  
He dies, we serve, we'll wear the shame."

The steward replied, "Let it be as you said,  
The one with the cup, a slave, others go ahead."

Sacks on the ground, they opened with dread,  
From oldest to youngest, the search methodically led.

The cup in Ben’s sack, despair in their eyes,  
They tore their clothes, to the city they rise.

They fell before Joseph, hearts weighed with sorrow,  
Joseph asked sharply, "What of this do you borrow?"

Judah spoke, "Truth's found us, we're all your slaves,  
God uncovered our sins, to Him we gave."

Joseph said, "No, only he holds the blame,  
The rest go in peace, no need for shame."

Judah stepped forth, voice steady and clear,  
“My lord, let me speak, give ear, please hear.

You asked of our father, of brothers and kin,  
We spoke of the boy, and our father’s love within.

You commanded, ‘Bring him, let my eyes hold your kin,’  
But our father feared harm like losing his twin.

We returned to our father, relayed your command,  
He wished for more food, we couldn't go without Ben's hand.

He said, ‘My wife bore two; one is torn, I know,’  
‘If this one’s harmed, to Sheol my sorrow would flow.’

Now, if I return and the boy’s not in sight,  
Our father will die, with grief he will fight.

I pledged my life for the boy's safe return,  
Let me take his place, my life I'll adjourn.

I can't face my father, if the boy's not with me,  
To see his despair, that sight I can’t see."
