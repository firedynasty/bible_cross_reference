God said to Abram, "Leave your land, your kin,  
Head to the place where new life will begin.  
I'll make you a great nation, your name shall rise,  
A blessing to many, beneath the skies."  

"Those who bless you, I'll bless them too,  
But curse those who treat you with a view askew.  
All earth's families through you will be blessed,"  
So Abram listened, and he did his best.  

At seventy-five, Abram hit the road,  
With Lot and Sarai, they shared the load.  
To Canaan they went with all their gear,  
Entering the land, they had no fear.  

Through Shechem's land, to Moreh's oak,  
Canaanites watched as Yahweh spoke:  
"I'll give this land to your seed," He said,  
An altar Abram built, where Yahweh led.  

He moved his tent east of Bethel's hill,  
Called on Yahweh’s name, obeyed His will.  
Southward he traveled, through the land's breadth,  
Till famine struck hard, with hunger and death.  

Down to Egypt, they journeyed afar,  
Famine was fierce, no food in the jar.  
Abram said to Sarai, "You're a beauty, no doubt,  
When Egyptians see you, they'll figure me out."  

"They'll kill me for you, so do this thing,  
Say you're my sister, and safety we'll bring."  
In Egypt's gates, Sarai's beauty shone,  
Praised by princes in Pharaoh's throne.  

Pharaoh took her, and Abram gained wealth,  
With flocks and servants, he thrived in health.  
But God struck Pharaoh's house with dread,  
For Sarai's sake, the plagues spread.  

Pharaoh called Abram, "What have you done?  
Why say 'sister,' hiding you're one?  
Take your wife back, now go your way,"  
Escorted by men, they left that day.
