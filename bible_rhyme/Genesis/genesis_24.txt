Abraham was old, blessed far and wide,  
Called his servant close, with a task to confide.  

"Put your hand under my thigh, make this vow,  
Don't seek a wife for Isaac from Canaanites now.  

Head to my homeland, where my kin reside,  
Find a wife for my son, make her his bride."  

The servant asked, "What if she won't come here?  
Should I take Isaac back?" Abraham said, "Hear:  

'The God of heaven, who brought me away,  
Will send His angel, guide your way.  

If she won't follow, you're freed from this vow,  
But don't take Isaac back, not then, not now.'"  

The servant swore, took camels, and set to roam,  
To Mesopotamia, Nahor's town, his home.  

By evening he stopped, where women drew water clear,  
Prayed for success, "Lord, let kindness appear.  

Let the woman who offers camels a drink be the one,  
For Isaac, my master's beloved son."  

Before he finished praying, Rebekah appeared,  
With her pitcher on her shoulder, she drew near.  

Beautiful and pure, she went to the spring,  
He ran to meet her, with hope to bring.  

"Please give me a little water," he asked with grace,  
She lowered her pitcher, a smile on her face.  

"Drink, my lord," she said with care,  
"I'll draw for your camels, make sure they're fair."  

He watched her in silence, till all was done,  
God had shown him, this was the one.  

He gave her gold, asked, "Whose daughter are you?"  
She replied, "Bethuel's, plenty room for you, too."  

The servant praised Yahweh, who guided his quest,  
To find a bride from Abraham's own nest.  

Rebekah's brother, Laban, saw the treasures and heard,  
Invited the servant, "Welcome, spoken word."  

Servant spoke of Abraham's wealth and God's plan,  
How he'd sworn to find Isaac's woman, not from Canaan.  

Rebekah's family agreed, "It's God's will, we see,  
Take her, let her be Isaac's, let it be."  

The servant bowed low, thanking the Lord,  
Gave gifts aplenty, his heart soared.  

Rebekah's kin blessed her, with wishes so grand,  
She left on camels, holding destiny's hand.  

Isaac was out in the field, deep in thought,  
Saw the camels, knew what they brought.  

Rebekah veiled herself, asked, "Who is this man?"  
"It's my master," the servant said, as planned.  

Isaac took Rebekah, into Sarah's tent,  
She became his wife, love's true testament.  

Isaac found comfort, with Rebekah there,  
A love story written, beyond compare.
