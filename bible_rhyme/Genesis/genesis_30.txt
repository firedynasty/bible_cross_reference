Rachel cried to Jacob, feeling cold and bare,  
"Give me children, or else I can't bear!"  

Jacob fired back, his patience worn thin,  
"Am I like God, who keeps life within?"  

Rachel had a plan, with a voice so sly,  
"Take Bilhah, my maid, let her give it a try."  

Jacob did as she said, Bilhah conceived and bore,  
Rachel named him Dan, her faith restored.  

Again Bilhah bore, another son to claim,  
Rachel said, "I've won," Naphtali was his name.  

Leah saw her time had ceased, and gave Zilpah to wed,  
Zilpah bore a son, and Leah laughed, "Gad, what a spread!"  

Once more Zilpah bore, and Asher's name did she declare,  
"Happy am I, they'll call me blessed everywhere."  

Reuben found some mandrakes ripe in the field,  
Rachel wanted them, her need revealed.  

Leah struck a deal, with a mandrake’s worth,  
"You can have him tonight," for Rachel's mirth.  

Leah bore Jacob yet another son so bright,  
"Issachar," she said, "God's given me my right."  

Then came Zebulun, Leah's sixth child,  
"Now my husband's heart is reconciled."  

And later, Dinah, a daughter gracing her line,  
Rachel prayed for her chance, her own son to shine.  

God remembered Rachel, her womb opened wide,  
She bore Joseph, joy she couldn't hide.  

Jacob said to Laban, "Let me take my leave,  
I’ve served you well, my own family I must retrieve."  

Laban wanted him to stay, for blessings he knew,  
"Just name your price, I'll make it come true."  

Jacob planned with wisdom, sheep and goats as his hire,  
"I'll take the speckled and spotted, that's my desire."  

Laban agreed, but slyly set a snare,  
Stripped the marked ones, leaving Jacob to fare.  

Three days apart, Jacob's plan took root,  
Rods peeled and placed, the strong ones bore fruit.  

He separated the flocks, the stronger became his,  
Feeble stayed Laban’s, Jacob rising in bliss.  

His wealth grew vast, flocks and servants galore,  
Camels and donkeys, a prosperous score.
