In the beginning, God sparked the earth and sky,  
Void and dark, the waters deep, Spirit hovered high.  

God spoke, "Let there be light," and light broke through,  
Saw it was good, split the night from day, pure and true.  

Named the light "day," darkness "night," thus began the sun's sway,  
Evening to morning, wrapped up the first day.  

"Let there be a vault in waters," God declared, so bright,  
Split the waters, high and low—He called the expanse "sky."  

Evening came and morning too, the second day did fly.  

To the waters God turned, "Gather up, land appear,"  
Called the land "earth," the seas "seas," everything clear.  

"Let the earth sprout green," He commanded with might,  
Herbs, seeds, fruit-bearing trees, all took flight.  

Earth obeyed, green spread wide, and God saw the sight,  
Evening to morning, completed the third day's light.  

"Let there be lights up high," God said with a plan,  
"Mark seasons, days, years, like only stars can."  

Two great lights, sun and moon, stars scattered wide,  
To shine on earth, rule day and night, side by side.  

Evening came, morning followed, fourth day in stride.  

Waters teemed with life, birds took to the sky,  
Great creatures and every winged thing, by His design did fly.  

"Be fruitful, multiply!" God blessed sea and air,  
Evening to morning, the fifth day’s fare.  

"Let earth bring forth creatures, each after their kind,"  
Livestock and creeping things, God had in mind.  

Then He said, "In our image, let’s create man,"  
"Rule fish, birds, beasts, and the land," was His plan.  

Male and female, He created, His blessing came next,  
"Be fruitful, reign over earth," His words direct.  

Every seed and fruit He offered for food,  
To every creature, His provision was good.  

God saw all He had made, and it was very good,  
Evening to morning, closed the sixth day’s brood.
