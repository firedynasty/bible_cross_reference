Jacob chilled in Canaan's land, his father's ground,  
Joseph, seventeen, with brothers, sheep around.  

He brought a tale back, didn't sound too bright,  
Israel loved him, his coat was a sight.  

Brothers saw that love, turned to hate,  
Couldn't talk nice, fueled by fate.  

Joseph had dreams, told them in pride,  
Sheaves bowed down, brothers couldn't abide.  

"Will you rule us?" they snapped with scorn,  
Hate grew stronger, more dreams were born.  

Sun, moon, stars bent down low,  
Father rebuked him, said, "I don't know."  

Brothers envied, but Dad kept his thought,  
Shechem held sheep, brothers were sought.  

Israel said, "Go find your kin,"  
Joseph said, "I'm in, let's begin."  

Wandered fields, a man gave word,  
"They're off to Dothan," directions heard.  

Brothers saw him, plotted his end,  
"Here comes the dreamer, let's pretend."  

Reuben stepped up, "Let's spare his life,  
Throw him in a pit, avoid the strife."  

Pulled his coat, tossed him in,  
Empty pit, no water within.  

They ate bread, saw a caravan near,  
Judah had a thought, "Let's sell, not smear."  

Sold to Ishmaelites, for silver they sought,  
Joseph to Egypt, a price he bought.  

Reuben returned to see no trace,  
Tore his clothes, distress on his face.  

Brothers dipped that coat in goat's red hue,  
Showed their father, "Is this Joseph's view?"  

Jacob wept, sackcloth adorned,  
Mourned his son, by grief, he was torn.  

Midianites sold him, Egypt was the game,  
To Potiphar's house, Joseph's new claim.
