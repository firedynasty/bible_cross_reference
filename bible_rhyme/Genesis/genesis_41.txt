Two years passed, Pharaoh had dreams so wild,  
By the river he stood, like a curious child.  

Seven fat cows rose from the river's embrace,  
Sleek and smooth, they grazed with grace.  

Then came seven thin, looking lean and mean,  
They devoured the fat in a shocking scene.  

Pharaoh awoke, but the dreams wouldn't cease,  
Seven full grains followed, then seven teased.  

Thin grains swallowed the full, the dreams did repeat,  
Pharaoh woke troubled, his spirit felt defeat.  

He called magicians, wise men to see,  
But none could unveil what his dreams might be.  

The cupbearer spoke, "I remember my past,  
In prison we dreamed, interpretations were cast."  

"There was a Hebrew, young and wise,  
He told us our dreams, like a true surprise."  

Pharaoh summoned Joseph, brought from the cell,  
Shaved and ready, he came to tell.  

"I've heard you can interpret dreams," Pharaoh said,  
Joseph replied, "God speaks, not me instead."  

Pharaoh shared his visions with fear in his eyes,  
Joseph listened closely, then replied wise.  

"Seven years of plenty the fat cows declare,  
Followed by famine—seven years of despair."  

"God’s shown you the future, Pharaoh, take heed,  
Find a wise man, one who'll take the lead."  

Pharaoh agreed, "Joseph, you're the man,  
In charge of Egypt, you'll guide the plan."  

He gave Joseph power, a ring, and a name,  
Zaphenath-Paneah, with honor and fame.  

In the years of plenty, Joseph stored the grain,  
So when famine struck, bread would remain.  

The earth cried famine, Joseph opened the store,  
Nations came knocking, seeking food more and more.  

The plan unfolded, Joseph's wisdom did shine,  
Through famine and feast, God’s plan was divine.
