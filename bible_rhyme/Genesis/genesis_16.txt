Sarai's heart was heavy, no child in sight,  
But Hagar was near, an Egyptian in her plight.  
Sarai spoke to Abram, "See, Yahweh's held me back,  
Go to my servant, maybe she'll pick up the slack."  

Abram nodded, listening to Sarai's plea,  
He took Hagar as his wife, that's how it'd be.  
She conceived a child, but trouble brewed,  
Hagar now looked at Sarai with a different mood.  

Sarai went to Abram, "This wrong's on you,  
I gave her to you, now look at what she's put me through.  
May Yahweh judge between you and me,  
For she's despised me, can't you see?"  

Abram shrugged, "She's yours to command,  
Do what feels right, it's in your hand."  
Sarai dealt harshly, Hagar ran away,  
To a wilderness fountain, where she'd stay.  

Yahweh's angel asked, "Where you from? Where you go?"  
"I'm fleeing my mistress," she let him know.  
"Return to her and submit," the angel said,  
"I'll multiply your offspring, they'll surely spread."  

"You're with child, a son you'll bear,  
Call him Ishmael, for Yahweh heard your despair.  
A wild donkey he'll be, against every hand,  
Living apart, in a solitary land."  

"You are a God who sees," Hagar proclaimed,  
By the well Beer Lahai Roi, it was named.  
Hagar bore a son, Ishmael his name,  
Abram was eighty-six when his boy came.
