He made the heavens and the earth, all complete,  
On the seventh day, God took a seat.  
He finished His work, sat back and rested,  
That seventh day, He blessed and invested.  

In history's tale, creation's debut,  
No plants or herbs, no rain but dew.  
No man to till, just mist on the ground,  
Then God shaped man from dust all around.  

He breathed life in, made man stand tall,  
A living soul, the first of them all.  
In Eden's garden, eastward He placed,  
The man He formed, with love embraced.  

Trees grew lush, a sight to behold,  
With fruit for food, and stories untold.  
A river flowed, to water the land,  
Split into four, by God's own hand.  

Pishon first, through Havilah it streams,  
Where gold and stones fulfill rich dreams.  
Gihon next, through Cush it winds,  
Hiddekel flows where Assyria finds.  

The fourth, the Euphrates, vast and wide,  
In Eden's garden, man did abide.  
"Eat freely," God said, "of every tree,  
But not that one, or die you'll see."  

God saw man alone, not good to be,  
"A helper I'll make, comparable to thee."  
From earth, beasts and birds came alive,  
To man they came, names to derive.  

Man named the creatures, one by one,  
But no partner found, none under the sun.  
A deep sleep fell, God took a rib,  
From that rib, a woman He did give.  

"Bone of my bones, flesh of my flesh,"  
Man saw woman, love did refresh.  
Man leaves his kin, to join his wife,  
Together as one, they start their life.  

Naked they stood, no shame to bear,  
Together in Eden, a perfect pair.
