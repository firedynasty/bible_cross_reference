Jacob heard the whispers, Laban's sons complain,  
"He's taken all our father's wealth, left us nothing to gain."  
He saw Laban's face, not friendly as before,  
Yahweh said, "Return home, I'll guide you and more."  

Called Rachel and Leah to the field, Jacob shared,  
"Your father's not the same, but God's been there, prepared.  
I've served with strength, though he deceived me ten times,  
But God didn't let him hurt me; justice in his rhymes."  

"If speckled, they're mine, if streaked, they bear the name,  
God took his livestock, handed me the acclaim.  
In a dream, I saw goats marked and leaped,  
The angel spoke, 'I'm the God you vowed to keep.  

Now rise, leave this land, return to your birth's plan.'"  
Rachel and Leah said, "We have no place in this man.  
What God gave us is ours, it's clear as you see,  
Now do what God told you, let it be."  

Jacob packed up, set his camels on the track,  
Took all his goods, not looking back.  
While Laban sheared sheep, Rachel took his teraphim sly,  
Jacob left without a word, under the night sky.  

On the third day, Laban learned Jacob fled,  
He gathered his kin, chased with anger and dread.  
In a dream, God warned Laban, "Speak no good or bad,"  
Laban caught up, frustrated and mad.  

"What have you done? Why sneak away, deceive?  
I would've sent you off with music, or so I believe.  
But God said beware, so here I stand,  
Yet, why take my gods from my hand?"  

Jacob replied, "I feared you'd strip me bare,  
But search my camp, find them if you dare."  
Laban searched tents, but Rachel was shrewd,  
She hid the teraphim, her father she eluded.  

"Forgive me, I can't rise, it's a woman's time,"  
Laban searched in vain, missed the hidden crime.  
Jacob's anger sparked, "What sin have I made?  
You've searched my things, found nothing to trade."  

"Twenty years I served, never a lamb I ate,  
Bore the loss myself, endured day and night without fate.  
Fourteen years for daughters, six years for flocks,  
God saw my toil, and struck the clocks."  

Laban said, "All you have is mine to see,  
But let's make a covenant, just you and me."  
Jacob raised a stone, set it as a sign,  
Laban and he agreed, "This stone marks the line."  

A witness heap, and Mizpah, a watchful decree,  
"Yahweh sees us both, here and when we're free."  
A warning not to harm, cross the line with ill,  
They swore by their fathers, sealing God's will.  

Jacob's sacrifice, a meal shared on the hill,  
Laban blessed his kin, then departed, the scene still.  
Early morning farewell, with kisses and grace,  
Laban departed, returned to his place.
