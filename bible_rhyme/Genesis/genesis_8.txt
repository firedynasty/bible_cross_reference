Noah and his crew stayed afloat, on the waves they did rely,  
God blew the wind, and watched as the flood began to die.  

Deep’s fountains stopped, sky’s windows closed, rain ceased to fall,  
Waters pulled back, slowly receding, answering the call.  

One fifty days went by, ship found its resting place,  
Seventh month, seventeenth day, on Ararat it embraced.  

Waters kept falling till the tenth month came around,  
Mountains' tops appeared, rising from the drowned.  

Forty days passed, Noah opened the window wide,  
Sent a raven flying, till water no longer cried.  

Next a dove he sent, searching for dry ground,  
Dove found none, back to Noah's hand she was bound.  

Seven days more, another dove was deployed,  
With evening came an olive leaf, Noah's hope was buoyed.  

Another week flew by, dove flew and did not return,  
Noah knew the earth was dry, life was ready to turn.  

Six hundred first year, first month, first day,  
Noah uncovered, saw the land where water once lay.  

By second month’s end, the earth was completely dry,  
God spoke to Noah, "Time to say goodbye.  

"Leave the ark, take your wife, sons, and their wives too,  
Bring out every creature, let them start anew.  

"Let them breed, let them spread, fill the earth wide,  
Be fruitful, multiply, let life not be denied."  

Noah and his family, animals in stride,  
Exited the ark, feeling the dry land abide.  

Noah built an altar, offerings to Yahweh burned,  
Pleasant aroma rose, God's heart then turned.  

He whispered, "No more will I curse the ground for man's sinful start,  
As long as the earth remains, seasons won't depart."  

Seedtime, harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter's sway,  
Day and night will not cease, they’ll continue their display.
