The king of Egypt's men, they fell from grace,  
Pharaoh got mad, sent them to a lowly place.  

In prison they went, a dark, stone-cold cell,  
With Joseph, captive, where they all would dwell.  

Joseph, the guard's trust, took care of them,  
They dreamt deep dreams, like jewels in a gem.  

Joseph asked, "Why the long face today?"  
"We've dreamed," they sighed, "no one shows us the way."  

Joseph nodded, "Dreams, they're God's domain,  
Tell me, I'll explain, no need to complain."  

The cup bearer spoke, "A vine I did see,  
Three branches blossomed, grapes came to be."  

"In Pharaoh's cup, the juice I did pour,  
Back to my job, like in the days before."  

Joseph replied, "In three days, you'll be free,  
Restored to serve, just you wait and see."  

"But remember me, when you're there in his hall,  
Tell Pharaoh of me, get me out of this wall."  

The baker then hoped, with a dream of his own,  
"Three baskets on my head, all bread, no stone."  

But Joseph's face turned grim, "In three days' time,  
Pharaoh will hang you, for your dark crime."  

The third day's feast, Pharaoh called his men,  
One found grace, the other met his end.  

The cup bearer was back, handing out the wine,  
But Joseph, forgotten, no one's sign.
