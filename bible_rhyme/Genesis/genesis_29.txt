Jacob traveled far, to the east he came,  
Saw a well and sheep, and learned their name.  

Big stone on the well, heavy to move,  
Flocks gathered round, waiting in a groove.  

"Where you from?" Jacob asked with care,  
"We're from Haran," they replied, standing there.  

"Know Laban?" Jacob asked, eager to meet,  
"We know him well," their answer complete.  

"Is it well?" Jacob asked with a smile,  
"Yes, Rachel comes," they said, "wait a while."  

Sun high in the sky, Jacob said,  
"Water the sheep, then onward be led."  

"We can’t," they replied, "till all gather round,  
Stone needs rolling, then water abounds."  

Rachel arrived with her father's sheep,  
Jacob saw her, his heart took a leap.  

He moved the stone, with strength and grace,  
Watered the flock, looked at Rachel's face.  

He kissed her, wept, and spoke with pride,  
"I'm kin to your father," he proudly cried.  

Rachel ran to tell her dad the news,  
Laban came running, no time to lose.  

Laban embraced him, said, "You're my own,"  
Jacob stayed, felt right at home.  

"You’ll work," Laban said, "but what's your fee?"  
"Seven years for Rachel, let that be."  

Laban agreed, "Better you than another,  
Stay with us, you’re like no other."  

Seven years flew by like a dream,  
Jacob's love for Rachel, a steady stream.  

"Give me my wife," Jacob said with a cheer,  
Laban prepared a feast, all were near.  

But in the night, Leah was there,  
Jacob awoke, caught unaware.  

"Why deceive me?" Jacob demanded,  
Laban's custom, not as he planned it.  

"One more week, then Rachel's yours too,  
Seven more years, you know what to do."  

Jacob agreed, worked seven more,  
Loved Rachel deeply, to his core.  

Leah bore sons, Reuben was first,  
"Yahweh saw my pain, quenched my thirst."  

Simeon came next, "Yahweh heard my cries,"  
Levi followed, hope in her eyes.  

Judah was born, "I'll praise Yahweh,"  
Then Leah's bearing ceased that day.
