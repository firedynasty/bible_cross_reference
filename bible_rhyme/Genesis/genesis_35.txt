"Jacob, arise, head to Bethel's land,  
Make an altar there, as I command."

To his household, Jacob did declare,  
"Rid yourselves of gods and clothes, make yourselves bare.  
We'll rise and head to Bethel's holy space,  
Where God answered me in my distressful chase."

They handed him their idols, without delay,  
Jacob hid them beneath an oak by Shechem's way.  
As they traveled on, fear struck the towns around,  
They left Jacob's sons alone, no enemies were found.

Jacob reached Luz, now Bethel in Canaan's land,  
With his people, he did stand.  
Built an altar there, called it El Beth El,  
For God had revealed Himself when Jacob fell.

Deborah, Rebekah’s nurse, met her fate,  
Buried below Bethel, by the oak's gate.  
God appeared to Jacob, once again,  
Blessed him with a name change, breaking the chain.

"No more Jacob, Israel's your name,  
A nation you'll become, kings will proclaim.  
The land I promised to Abraham and Isaac, it’s yours,  
To your offspring, this land endures."

God departed, left His mark in the place,  
Jacob set a stone, an altar of grace.  
Poured oil and drink, Bethel’s name he sealed,  
Then onward they traveled, destinies revealed.

Rachel labored hard on the road to Ephrath,  
A son was born, her life's final path.  
"Don't fear," said the midwife, "a son's here,  
Benoni she named him, Benjamin, his father held dear."

Rachel passed, her grave marked with stone,  
A pillar for her, her name set in bone.  
Israel moved beyond Eder’s tower,  
While Reuben's act brought a shadow to power.

Twelve sons of Jacob, a legacy so wide,  
Reuben, Simeon, Levi by Leah's side.  
Joseph and Benjamin, Rachel's pride,  
Bilhah's Dan and Naphtali, Zilpah’s Gad and Asher abide.

Jacob returned to Isaac, in Hebron near,  
Where Abraham and Isaac lived year by year.  
Isaac's life closed, one hundred eighty years done,  
Esau and Jacob buried him, two sons as one.
