Joseph cried, fell on his father's face,  
Kissed him tender, then took his place.  

He called the docs, "Embalm my dad,"  
Forty days they took, the process was sad.  

Seventy days Egyptians mourned strong,  
A nation in sorrow, the grieving long.  

When the mourning passed, Joseph made a plea,  
"If I've found favor, let Pharaoh hear me.  

My father's dying wish was clear as day,  
'Bury me in Canaan, don't delay.'"  

Pharaoh replied, "Go, honor your word,  
Bury him as he desired, let it be heard."  

Joseph gathered the elders, they joined the ride,  
A grand procession, Egypt's pride.  

Chariots and horsemen, a mighty show,  
To honor the man they had come to know.  

At Atad's floor, they mourned and wept,  
Seven days of lament, a promise kept.  

Canaanites watched, stunned by the scene,  
Named it Abel Mizraim, where grief had been.  

His sons did as he asked, took him away,  
To Canaan's cave, where he would lay.  

Joseph returned, his duty done,  
His father in peace, in Canaan's sun.  

Brothers feared Joseph's wrath might now rise,  
"Remember Dad's wish, his words were wise:  

'Forgive your brothers, their sins make right,'  
Joseph wept, he knew their plight.  

They fell before him, "Your servants we'll be,"  
Joseph said, "Fear not, God’s plan I see.  

You meant harm, but God turned it good,  
To save many lives, like He said He would."  

"Don’t fear, I'll care for you, understand,  
You and your children, by my hand."  

Joseph lived long, a hundred ten years,  
Saw generations, calmed his brothers' fears.  

"I'm dying, but God’ll visit you here,  
He’ll bring you out, His promise clear."  

He took an oath, "Carry my bones along,  
When God leads you to where you belong."  

Joseph passed, embalmed once more,  
In Egypt's land, his story to explore.
