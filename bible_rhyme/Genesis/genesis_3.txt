The serpent was sly, more subtle than the rest,  
He asked the woman, "God said you can't eat, is that the test?"  

The woman replied, "We can eat from the trees all around,  
But that one in the middle, touch it, and we're ground-bound."  

The serpent whispered, "You won’t really die,  
God knows if you eat, you'll see with His eye."  

She saw that the tree had a delightful taste,  
Took the fruit, ate it, not a moment to waste.  

Gave some to her man, and he ate too,  
Their eyes popped open, they knew what was true.  

They realized their nakedness, quick with a sew,  
Fig leaves fashioned, the first clothes they’d know.  

God's steps echoed in the cool evening air,  
They hid in the garden, trembling in despair.  

"Where are you?" called God with a voice deep and grand,  
"I heard you, got scared; I'm naked," said the man.  

"Who told you that truth you now bear?  
Did you eat from that tree over there?"  

The man pointed, "The woman, you gave her to me,  
She handed the fruit, and I ate from the tree."  

God asked the woman, "Why did you do this deed?"  
"The serpent deceived, it planted the seed."  

God turned to the snake, "For this trick you played,  
Cursed above all, on your belly you’ll stay.  

Between you and the woman, a battle will rage,  
Her offspring will crush you, head and heel engaged."  

To the woman He said, "In childbirth, much pain,  
Your desire for your man, in your heart he'll reign."  

To Adam He spoke of labor's tough call,  
"You listened to your wife, now the ground’s a thrall.  

Thorns and thistles, the land will churn,  
By sweat you’ll eat, till dust you return."  

The man named her Eve, life's mother, it's clear,  
God made them garments, to cover their fear.  

"Behold, man is like us, knowing both good and ill,  
Lest he eat from the tree of life, forever still."  

So out from Eden, God sent them away,  
To toil the ground, where man was of clay.  

Guarding the garden, cherubim stood,  
A flaming sword, turning every way it could.
