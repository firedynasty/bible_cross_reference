Joseph stepped to Pharaoh, said, "My family's near,  
From Canaan they’ve come, with flocks and gear,  

He picked five brothers, presented them with pride,  
Pharaoh asked, "What’s your trade?" as they stood side by side.  

"We’re shepherds," they said, "like fathers before,  
Famine’s hit hard, we can’t stay anymore.  

Let us settle in Goshen, if you please,  
The pastures there could set our minds at ease."  

Pharaoh turned to Joseph, "Your kin have arrived,  
Give them the best, let Goshen be their hive.  

And if they've got skill with cattle and grain,  
Let them handle my herds, on Egypt’s vast plain."  

Joseph brought Jacob, his father, face to face,  
With the Pharaoh, who asked, "How long’s been your race?"  

Jacob replied, "A hundred thirty years of few,  
Life’s been hard, not as long as my forefathers knew."  

Jacob blessed Pharaoh, then took his leave,  
Joseph settled his kin, gave them space to breathe.  

He gave them land in Rameses, like Pharaoh said,  
Ensured every mouth was fed, with daily bread.  

Famine gripped the land, Egypt and Canaan both,  
Joseph collected cash, as grain they did betroth.  

Money vanished, Egyptians cried out, "We're broke!  
Feed us, lest the famine leaves us to choke."  

Joseph said, "Bring your livestock here,  
I’ll trade you bread, keep away fear."  

They traded horses, flocks, donkeys, herds,  
Joseph fed them all, with grain and words.  

Another year passed, they faced him again,  
"Our money, our cattle, all spent, what remains?  

Buy us and our lands, let us be Pharaoh’s crew,  
Give us seed to sow, let life renew."  

Joseph bought the land, all over the Nile,  
Egypt became Pharaoh’s, mile by mile.  

Priests kept their ground, by Pharaoh's decree,  
Joseph gave people seed, made them sow freely.  

"At harvest, give Pharaoh a fifth of your yield,  
The rest is yours, for your homes and your field."  

Grateful, they cried, "You've saved our day!  
We’ll serve Pharaoh, come what may."  

Israel thrived in Goshen’s embrace,  
Jacob lived seventeen years in that place.  

When Jacob neared his final rest,  
He called for Joseph, made a solemn request.  

"Promise me, son, when I’m laid to sleep,  
Don’t bury me here, where Egypt’s secrets creep.  

Take me home, to our ancestors’ ground."  
Joseph swore, "I’ll do it," with a promise profound.  

Israel’s heart rested, his faith held tight,  
He bowed his head, faced the end with might.
