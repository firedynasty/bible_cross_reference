Joseph heard his father was sick; he went with his sons real quick.  
Manasseh and Ephraim by his side, to see Jacob before he died.  

Jacob sat up strong, knowing Joseph came along.  
He spoke of God Almighty's blessing, a promise never wrong.  

"God appeared to me, blessed my home,  
Said, 'I'll make you a peoples' dome.  

Your sons born in Egypt's land,  
Now they're mine, you understand.  

Ephraim and Manasseh, just like Reuben, Simeon,  
They'll be mine, my family extending on.  

Others born after them shall bear your name,  
United in inheritance, they'll claim the same.  

Remember Rachel, lost on the way,  
Buried near Bethlehem, she couldn't stay.'"  

Israel saw the sons and inquired, "Who are these?"  
Joseph replied, "They're mine, a blessing and a tease."  

"Bring them to me," Jacob said with a sigh,  
Though his eyes were dim, the blessing was nigh.  

"Didn't think I'd see your face, now God doubled His grace."  
Joseph bowed low, his gratitude to show.  

Ephraim to his right, Manasseh to the left,  
Jacob crossed his hands, a move quite deft.  

"God of my fathers, Abraham, Isaac,  
Bless these lads, their future iconic.  

Let my name and theirs entwine,  
Grow them into a people divine."  

Joseph saw his father's switch, his firstborn missed the hitch.  
"Not so, father," he tried to rearrange,  

But Jacob knew the plan wouldn't change.  
"I know, my son, I know,  
Manasseh is great, but Ephraim will grow.  

Greater he'll be, a multitude he'll sow."  
He blessed them, pronouncing their fame,  
Said, "Israel will bless with your name."  

"I'm dying," Jacob said, "but God is there,  
He'll take you back to your fathers' care.  

One portion more to you I give,  
Earned with my bow and where I live."
