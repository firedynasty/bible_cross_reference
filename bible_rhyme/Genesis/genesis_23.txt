Sarah lived long, a hundred twenty-seven years,  
In Hebron, she passed, bringing Abraham to tears.  

He rose up, spoke to the Heth crowd,  
"I’m a stranger here, but I ask proud.  

Give me a plot to bury my dead,  
A place for her, where she can be laid to bed."  

The children of Heth replied in grace,  
"A prince of God, you hold a special place.  

Choose any tomb, don't hesitate or dread,  
None will deny you, bury your dead."  

Abraham stood, bowed to the land's folk,  
He spoke again, respectful was his stroke.  

"Let Ephron know, I want his cave,  
At Machpelah's end, a price I’ll save."  

Ephron sat among them, he replied with ease,  
"My lord, the field and cave, take them, please.  

In front of all, this gift is shown,  
Bury your beloved, make it known."  

Abraham bowed, respectful in his plea,  
"Yet hear me out, let the price be."  

Ephron named it, four hundred silver shekels ring,  
"What's that between us? Let it bring—"  

Abraham weighed the silver right,  
The field and cave now his in sight.  

Before all Heth, the deal was made,  
The land was Abraham's, the price he paid.  

There in Machpelah, in Hebron's space,  
Abraham laid Sarah in her final resting place.  

The field and cave, deeded and done,  
A burial site secured, a legacy begun.
