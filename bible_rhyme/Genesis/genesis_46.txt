Israel packed up all he owned, headed to Beersheba's stone,  
Offered sacrifices to God, his father Isaac had known.  

In the night, God spoke clear in a vision so bright,  
"Jacob, Jacob!” He called, "Here I am," came the reply.  

"I am God, your father's God, don't be afraid,  
Go down to Egypt, that's where your nation'll be made.  

I'll go with you there, and I promise you'll rise,  
Joseph's hand will be there when you close your eyes."  

Jacob rose from Beersheba, ready to embark,  
His sons brought the wagons Pharaoh sent, to make their mark.  

With livestock and goods from Canaan's wide span,  
Jacob and his offspring journeyed to Egypt's land.  

Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah they did bring,  
Issachar, Zebulun, each with their kin under wing.  

Sons and daughters, offspring multiplied,  
Thirty-three souls from Leah's side.  

Gad and Asher, Zilpah bore to Jacob's name,  
Sixteen souls in total added to the fame.  

Joseph and Benjamin, Rachel's pride and joy,  
Manasseh and Ephraim, Egyptians by their ploy.  

Seventy souls in all, Jacob's family name,  
Heads to Egypt, in Goshen they would claim.  

Judah led ahead, showed the way to Goshen's gate,  
Joseph met his father, tears of joy, long-awaited fate.  

Israel said, "I can die now, I've seen your face alive,"  
Joseph promised Pharaoh he'd help his family survive.  

"Shepherds are we, keepers of flocks and herds,  
When Pharaoh asks, speak these words."  

"Egyptians shun shepherds, but Goshen's our place,  
There you'll dwell in peace, run your own race."
