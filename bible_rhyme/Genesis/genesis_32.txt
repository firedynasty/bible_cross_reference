Jacob on his journey, angels in his sight,  
"This is God's army," he knew it was right.  

To Esau, his brother, messengers he sent,  
To the land of Seir, his words were meant.  

"Tell Esau, your servant Jacob speaks,  
With Laban I've lived, so now I seek."  

"I've got cattle, donkeys, flocks in line,  
Please show me favor, let your face shine."  

Messengers returned, with news they bring,  
"Esau's coming with four hundred, like a king."  

Jacob felt fear, his heart it raced,  
Divided his people, each group in its place.  

"If Esau strikes one, the other will flee,  
A chance to escape, safety for me."  

He prayed, "God of my fathers, hear my plea,  
You promised good, now deliver me."  

From Esau's hand, protect me now,  
I fear his wrath, before him I bow.  

"You promised my offspring, like the sea's sand,  
Too many to count, by Your guiding hand."  

That night he stayed, gifts he prepared,  
Goats, rams, camels, all carefully shared.  

To his servants he said, "Go ahead in line,  
Tell Esau, Jacob's gift, a peace sign."  

He ordered them all, the same to declare,  
"Jacob comes behind, his gifts to share."  

The gift went ahead, while Jacob stayed,  
Night came, he crossed Jabbok, unafraid.  

Alone he wrestled, 'til dawn did break,  
The man touched his thigh, made him ache.  

"Let me go," the man said with the light,  
"Not unless you bless me," Jacob held tight.  

"What's your name?" the man did inquire,  
"Jacob," he answered, heart full of fire.  

"No more Jacob, Israel you'll be,  
You've wrestled with God, and found victory."  

"Tell me your name," Jacob asked,  
"Why ask?" the man said, in blessing he basked.  

Jacob named it Peniel, face to face he'd met,  
God preserved his life, he'd never forget.  

As the sun rose, he limped on his way,  
A reminder of the struggle, to this very day.  

The children of Israel keep the tale alive,  
The sinew of the hip, a legacy to survive.
