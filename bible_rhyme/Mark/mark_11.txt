Near Jerusalem, on Olives’ mount high,  
Two disciples sent, with a task to apply.  

"Go to the village, find a colt tied,  
No one has sat, bring him to my side."  

"If anyone questions, say, 'The Lord's call,  
He needs him now, He’ll return him and all.'"  

They found the donkey, tied at the door,  
Untied him swiftly, just like before.  

"What are you doing?" the onlookers cried,  
"The Lord needs him," the disciples replied.  

Garments on the donkey, Jesus took his seat,  
Crowds laid down branches, all down the street.  

They sang, "Hosanna! Blessed is the name,  
The kingdom of David is coming, proclaim!"  

He entered the temple, took in the scene,  
Evening fell, to Bethany with his team.  

Hungry next day, he sought a fig tree,  
But found only leaves, no fruit to see.  

"May no one eat from you," Jesus declared,  
The disciples listened, aware and prepared.  

In Jerusalem's temple, He stormed with might,  
Selling and buying, He turned tables in sight.  

"Isn't it written, house of prayer for all?  
But you've turned it into a den so small!"  

Priests and scribes feared, His words were a threat,  
Astonished crowds, they wouldn't forget.  

Morning came, the fig tree was dead,  
"Look, Rabbi, it’s withered," Peter said.  

"Have faith in God," Jesus began,  
"Say to this mountain, 'Move,' if you can."  

"Believe when you pray, and you will receive,  
Forgive others, and in grace you'll believe."  

Back in the temple, the leaders asked why,  
"By whose authority, do you defy?"  

"I'll ask one question, answer and see,  
By what authority, then answer me."  

"John's baptism, from heaven or men?"  
Caught in a bind, they reasoned again.  

"We don't know," they finally said,  
"Then I won't tell by whom I'm led."
