Pharisees and scribes from Jerusalem arrive,  
Saw the disciples eat with hands not washed, oh my, surprise!  

Traditions of elders, wash before they eat,  
Pharisees question, "Why don't your folks repeat?"  

Jesus replied, "Isaiah spoke of you,  
Words honor me, but your hearts ain't true.  

Worship in vain, teaching man-made rules,  
Holding to traditions, like ceremonial tools."  

"God's command you reject, for traditions you keep,  
Moses said honor parents, but you twist it deep.  

Saying 'It's Corban,' profit to God you dare,  
So parents get nothing, this ain't fair."  

To the crowd he called, "Listen real clear,  
What enters doesn't defile, it's what comes out, hear?"  

In private, disciples sought to know,  
"What defiles a man, please show?"  

"What enters the stomach, latrine it goes,  
The heart's where defilement grows.  

Evil thoughts, deceit, and pride,  
Come from within, can't be denied."  

Off to Tyre and Sidon, Jesus went on,  
A Greek woman begged, her daughter was drawn.  

"Children first," he said, "not dogs to feed,"  
She replied, "Even crumbs the dogs need."  

"For your words," said he, "the demon's out,  
Go back home, check it out."  

From there to Galilee, crossing Decapolis space,  
They brought a deaf man, speech impediment was his case.  

Took him aside, fingers in ears, spit and touch,  
"Be opened!" he sighed, and the man's speech was such.  

Commanded them, "Tell no one," but they all were hyped,  
"He makes the deaf hear, and the mute speak," they piped.
