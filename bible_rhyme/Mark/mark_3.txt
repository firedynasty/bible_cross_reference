In the synagogue, a man with a withered hand,  
They watched, wondering, “Will he heal on command?”  

Jesus asked the man, “Stand up, take a stand.”  
To the crowd he questioned, “Good or harm, what’s planned?”  

Their silence gripped him, hearts hard as stone,  
“Stretch out your hand,” he said, and the healing was shown.  

Pharisees plotted with Herodians to conspire,  
Jesus withdrew to the sea, escaping their ire.  

Crowds followed with hope, from near and far,  
He healed them all, his fame a blazing star.  

“Keep the boat nearby,” he told his crew,  
Pressed by the crowd, their numbers grew.  

Unclean spirits fell, cried, “Son of God!”  
He warned them, “Hush, don’t spread it broad.”  

Up the mountain, he called his chosen few,  
Twelve to preach, heal, cast demons too.  

Peter, James, and John—Sons of Thunder,  
Andrew and Philip, the twelve now numbered.  

Matthew, Thomas; James, Alphaeus’s son;  
Thaddaeus, Simon, and Judas, the one.  

Inside the house, they gathered tight,  
His friends thought, “He’s lost his sight.”  

Scribes from Jerusalem, spreading their lies,  
“He’s got Beelzebul,” they shouted their cries.  

Jesus replied with stories so wise,  
“A house divided surely dies.”  

“Bind the strong man, take his loot,  
But blaspheme the Spirit, and forgiveness is moot.”  

His family came, calling from outside,  
“Your mother and brothers,” the crowd replied.  

“Who are my family?” he asked, looking around,  
“Those who do God’s will are my kin, profound.”
