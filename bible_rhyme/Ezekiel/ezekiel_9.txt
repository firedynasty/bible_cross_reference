A voice shouted loud, echoing near,  
"Bring the city's leaders, armed, appear!"  

Six men came from the gate up north,  
Each with a weapon, ready to go forth.  

In their midst, a man in linen stood,  
With an inkhorn, in solemn mood.  

By the bronze altar, they took their stand,  
As God's glory moved across the land.  

He called to the man with the inkhorn near,  
Yahweh's voice was strong and clear:  

"Go through the city, mark each one,  
Who cries for the wrongs that have been done."  

To the others, the command was stark,  
"Follow him, strike, but spare the marked."  

"Old and young, woman and child,  
Let none escape, be fierce, be wild."  

"Start at my sanctuary, let justice unfold,"  
And so the old men met their fate, bold.  

"Defile the house, fill the courts with slain,  
Go out, let none remain."  

While they struck, I fell to the ground,  
Crying out as chaos abound.  

"Ah Lord Yahweh! Will all be lost?  
In your wrath, what will it cost?"  

"The land is full of blood and lies,"  
Came the reply from the skies.  

"My eye won't spare, pity won't be seen,  
Their own ways will come, they'll glean."  

The man in linen returned, his task complete,  
"I've done your bidding, made all meet."
