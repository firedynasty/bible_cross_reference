Yahweh spoke to me, said "Tell a riddle, son,  
A parable for Israel, let the story run.  
A great eagle soared, wings long and wide,  
Colorful feathers, to Lebanon it did glide.  

Took the cedar's top, the highest twig, you know,  
Carried it to merchants, where traffic flows.  
Planted in the city, in the soil of trade,  
A new tree grew where the eagle laid.  

Seed from the land, in fertile land it flowed,  
By waters placed, a willow it showed.  
Grew to be a vine, low and wide,  
Branches leaned back, roots by its side.  

Then came another eagle, wings spread wide,  
The vine reached for it, roots did hide.  
Planted in good soil, by waters, it aimed,  
Hoped to grow branches, fruit it claimed.  

Yahweh says, "Will it truly thrive?  
Won't he pull roots, make it wither, not survive?  
Strong arms and many can't make it grow,  
East wind hits, then its fate we know."  

Yahweh asked me, "Do you understand?  
Babylon's king took Jerusalem's land.  
Took the king, the princes, to his place,  
Made a covenant with them, to keep them in grace.  

Yet rebellion came, ambassadors sent,  
Horses sought, covenant was bent.  
Will he escape, this oath despised?  
In Babylon's heart, it's there he'll reside."  

Pharaoh's might can't save him from war,  
Forts will rise, cut off many more.  
Despised the oath, broke the deal,  
In my snare, judgment he'll feel.  

Fugitives fall, scattered by the wind,  
Yahweh's word is what I've pinned.  
"I'll take a cedar's top, plant it high,  
On Israel's peak, it will reach the sky.  

Branches will grow, fruit will bear,  
Birds find shade, in its care.  
High brought low, low exalted,  
Green dried up, dry tree vaulted.  

I, Yahweh, have spoken, see what's done,  
In the field, my will is the only one."
