In the eleventh year, third month's first light,  
Yahweh's word came clear, a vision so bright.  

"Son of man," to Pharaoh say,  
"Who's like you in your grand display?  

The Assyrian stood, a cedar tall,  
In Lebanon's forest, the king of all.  

Waters flowed, made it grow,  
Rivers surrounded, spreading below.  

Its height reached above the trees,  
Its branches long, swayed in the breeze.  

Birds nested high, beasts birthed low,  
Great nations thrived in its shadow's glow.  

Beautiful and grand, unmatched at root,  
In Eden's garden, none could dispute.  

I made it splendid, branches wide,  
Even Eden's trees envied with pride.  

But hear now what Yahweh has to say,  
Pride lifts you, now you must pay.  

Into the mighty's hand I'll deliver your fall,  
For wickedness, you'll lose it all.  

Strangers will cut, branches will break,  
Mountains and valleys, your shadow they'll forsake.  

Birds will nest where ruin stands,  
Beasts will roam across the lands.  

No tree by waters will rise so high,  
For all will fall, destined to die.  

Yahweh says, "On his descent to Sheol's gate,  
I caused the deep to mourn his fate.  

Lebanon weeps, trees faint away,  
The nations tremble on that day.  

To Sheol's depths, with sword's defeat,  
He joins the fallen in shadow's retreat.  

Who are you in Eden's story?  
Your fate is sealed with faded glory."  

"This is Pharaoh," Yahweh's decree,  
His multitude falls, no longer free.
