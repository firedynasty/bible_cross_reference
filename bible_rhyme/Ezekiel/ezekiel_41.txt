He brought me to the temple, its grandeur to explore,  
Measured the posts, six cubits each, on either door.  
Ten cubits wide the entrance, flanked by sides of five,  
Inside a space of forty long, and twenty wide it thrived.  

He moved inward, checking posts, each two cubits strong,  
Measured the entrance, six cubits, seven wide along.  
Twenty by twenty, holy place laid out with grace,  
"This is the most holy place," he spoke with solemn face.  

The house walls six cubits thick, rooms four cubits wide,  
Three stories high, thirty rooms, stacked side by side.  
Rooms expanded upward, house grew with grace,  
From lowest to the highest, middle was the place.  

Raised base wrapped the house, firm and profound,  
Foundations strong, six cubits, all around.  
Wall outside, five cubits thick, a mighty stance,  
Side rooms fitted snugly, giving structure a chance.  

Spaces between twenty cubits, house circled round,  
Doors faced open areas, north and south they found.  
Sixty cubit building, wall five cubits in might,  
Ninety cubits long, housed in the westward site.  

He measured the whole house, a hundred cubits strong,  
The place, the building, all its walls, a hundred long.  
Measured galleries and thresholds, windows closed and neat,  
Wood ceilings, ground to windows, where light and shadows meet.  

Cherubim and palm trees carved in every space,  
Cherub with two faces, palm trees interlace.  
Human faced a palm, lion faced another,  
Throughout the house, this design did cover.  

The temple's door posts squared and neat,  
Sanctuary's face mirrored temple's beat.  
Altar of wood, three cubits high,  
"This is the table before Yahweh," the sky.  

Two doors stood for temple and sanctuary both,  
Two leaves each they turned, like a solemn oath.  
Cherubim on doors, palm trees likewise,  
Thresholds of wood, beneath the skies.  

Closed windows, palms on each porch side,  
This arrangement of thresholds, in detail supplied.
