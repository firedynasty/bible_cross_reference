He brought me out to the court, north's the direction,  
Into the room, opposite the separation section.  

A hundred cubits long, north door wide as a stage,  
Fifty cubits was the width, like a book's open page.  

Twenty cubits from the inner court's center,  
Gallery on gallery, third floor I enter.  

A path ten cubits wide, led inward, space so tight,  
Doors faced the north, everything in sight.  

Upper rooms shorter, galleries took the space,  
No pillars like courts, upper rooms set back in the race.  

Walls outside by rooms, fifty cubits they spanned,  
Fifty cubits outer court, temple's hundred, grand.  

Entry from under, east side the groove,  
Through the wall's thickness, rooms they prove.  

Rooms to the north, rooms to the south, so profound,  
Exits and doors all matching around.  

"The north and south rooms, holy," he declared,  
Priests near Yahweh, offerings prepared.  

"Priests enter here, but out they won’t stride,  
Garments are holy, change them aside."  

Finished measuring, inner house now seen,  
Out to the east gate with a measured scheme.  

East side measured, five hundred reeds long,  
He wrapped the north side, the same song.  

South side, west side, all five hundred the same,  
Walls all around, holy and common, not one and the same.
