Yo, listen up, the word came down to me,  
"Son of man, lament Tyre by the sea.  

At the sea's gate, where you trade wide and far,  
You say, 'I'm perfect, beautiful by far.'  

Your borders deep in ocean's heart lie,  
Crafted by builders, reaching for the sky.  

Planks of cypress, masts of Lebanon's best,  
Oars from Bashan, ivory benches for rest.  

Sails of fine linen, banners high you fly,  
Blue and purple awnings catch the eye.  

Sidon and Arvad rowed with might,  
Your wise men steered you, pilots bright.  

Gebal's elders fixed your seams tight and clean,  
Ships gathered ’round, trading their scene.  

Persia, Lud, and Put armed your force,  
Helmets and shields showing beauty, of course.  

Arvad's troops upon your walls stood tall,  
Shields on towers, perfecting it all.  

Tarshish traded silver, iron in your throng,  
Javan, Tubal, Meshech brought bronze along.  

Togarmah's horses came with mules in a line,  
Dedan's islands brought ivory, ebony fine.  

Syria gave purple, emeralds in trade,  
Judah's wheat and balm, your wealth was made.  

Damascus brought you Helbon and wool,  
Vedan, Javan, yarns made your stores full.  

Dedan's cloths rode in splendor and grace,  
Kedar’s lambs and rams filled up your space.  

Sheba, Raamah offered spices and gold,  
Haran and Eden brought riches untold.  

Tarshish ships carried your goods vast and wide,  
You were glorious, ruling the tide.  

But great waters broke you, winds tore you apart,  
Riches, warriors sunk, deep in the heart.  

Pilots’ cries made the coastlands all shake,  
Sailors and mariners watched your fate break.  

On land they stood, weeping, dust on their head,  
Wailing for Tyre, like the silence of the dead.  

Your trades filled peoples, kings with your gold,  
But the seas broke you, the waters cold.  

Islands shook, kings trembled in fear,  
Merchants hissed, your end was near.  

You've become a terror, once mighty, now no more,  
Tyre, once great, washed up on the shore."
