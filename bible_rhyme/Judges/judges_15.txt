In the time of harvest, Samson came with a goat,  
To see his wife, but the father said, "No, you won't."  
"I thought you hated her, gave her to your friend,  
Her sister's more beautiful, take her instead, amend."

Samson replied, "This time I'm blameless when I fight,  
The Philistines will see my wrath, I'll set it right."  
He caught three hundred foxes, torches in between,  
Set them loose in fields, a fiery, furious scene.  

Grain and groves burned up in the blazing light,  
Philistines asked, "Who'd do such a spite?"  
"Samson, son-in-law of the Timnite," they said,  
For taking his wife, now rage and fire spread.  

Samson declared, "For this, revenge I'll take,  
Then I'll cease, but first, their lives I'll break."  
He struck them down, a great slaughter rose,  
Then he retreated to the Etam's rock to doze.  

Philistines encamped, in Judah spread wide,  
"Why come against us?" Judah's men cried.  
"We're here for Samson, to bind him tight,  
To repay him for setting our fields alight."

Three thousand of Judah went to Samson's abode,  
"Philistines rule us, why bring this load?"  
"As they've done to me, so I’ve done to them,"  
"Bind me, but harm me not, my own men."

"We'll bind you firm, deliver you," they swore,  
But not kill you, just settle the score.  
Bound in new ropes, up from the rock they led,  
To Lehi's land, where warriors tread.

Philistines shouted, ready for the fray,  
But God's Spirit came, tore the ropes away.  
With a donkey's jawbone, Samson laid waste,  
A thousand men fell, in a furious haste.  

"With a jawbone, heaps on heaps," Samson's boast,  
"With a donkey's jaw, I've struck down their host."  
He tossed the bone aside, named the place then,  
Ramath Lehi, where he slayed many men.  

Thirsty, he called, "Yahweh, will I now die,  
After this victory, in this land lie?"  
God split the ground, water flowed forth,  
Samson drank deep, his spirit renewed in worth.  

He judged Israel in Philistine days,  
Twenty years of justice, in mighty ways.
