Ephraim's crew gathered, marching with fire,  
"Jephthah, you left us out, now face our ire!"  

Jephthah replied, "We were in a bind,  
I called for help, but you weren't inclined.  

So I went alone, with no delay,  
Yahweh delivered them into my play.  

Now you come to fight me today?  
Why the beef, what’s this display?"  

Gilead and Ephraim clashed, fists in the air,  
Gilead struck hard, no mercy to spare.  

At the Jordan's ford, they held the line,  
"Are you Ephraim?" they’d ask every time.  

"Say 'Shibboleth,'" was the clever test,  
"Sibboleth," they'd stumble, wouldn’t pass the quest.  

Seizing those who failed, at the river they'd fall,  
Forty-two thousand, Ephraim's mighty would stall.  

Jephthah judged for six years, then he passed away,  
Buried in Gilead, his final stay.  

Ibzan from Bethlehem took the lead,  
With thirty sons, daughters—quite a seed.  

Seven years he judged, then he too was laid,  
In Bethlehem's earth, his dues were paid.  

Elon of Zebulun then took the throne,  
Ten years he judged, his wisdom well-known.  

Buried in Aijalon, in Zebulun’s land,  
His legacy carved by his quiet hand.  

Abdon followed, with sons on donkeys they’d ride,  
Seventy colts strong, with familial pride.  

Eight years he judged from Pirathon's core,  
In Ephraim's hills, he rests forevermore.
