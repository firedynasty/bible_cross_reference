Israel messed up, went the wrong way,  
With Ehud gone, they had to pay.  

Yahweh let Jabin, king of Canaan, reign,  
His captain Sisera caused much pain.  

Nine hundred chariots, iron and might,  
For twenty years, Israel lost the fight.  

Deborah the prophetess, wise and true,  
Judged Israel, knew what to do.  

Under her palm tree, she'd sit and decide,  
Israel came for justice, she was their guide.  

Called Barak from Kedesh, gave him the word,  
"Yahweh commands, lead the herd.  

To Mount Tabor with ten thousand men,  
From Naphtali and Zebulun, go and begin.  

I'll bring Sisera to the river's side,  
His chariots and army, where you can't hide."  

Barak replied, "If you come, I will go,  
If not, then I'll just say no."  

Deborah agreed, "I'll come along,  
But Sisera's defeat won't make you strong.  

Into a woman's hand, Yahweh will sell,"  
She rose with Barak, Kedesh they tell.  

Ten thousand men followed Barak's lead,  
Deborah joined them, planting the seed.  

Heber the Kenite's tent stood alone,  
News of Barak's march was quickly known.  

Sisera gathered chariots, iron fleet,  
To the river Kishon, they'd soon meet.  

Deborah declared, "This is the day,  
Yahweh's promise won't delay."  

Barak charged down with his men,  
Yahweh confused Sisera, again and again.  

Sisera fled, left his iron ride,  
Barak's sword defeated, none survived.  

Sisera ran to Jael's tent,  
A peaceful place, where he went.  

Jael met him, "Come in, don't fear,"  
Covered him up, kept him near.  

He asked for water, she offered milk,  
Covered him again, like soft silk.  

"Stand guard," he said, "if questions arise,  
Say no one is here, tell them lies."  

Jael took a peg and hammer in hand,  
While Sisera slept, she made her stand.  

Drove the peg through, Sisera fell,  
Barak arrived, she had news to tell.  

"See the man you chase, he's lying dead,"  
Tent peg in his temples, Sisera bled.  

God subdued Jabin that day,  
Israel pushed him further away.  

Stronger and stronger, they rose to fame,  
Till Jabin the king was just a name.
