In Mizpah, Israel made a vow, "No daughters to Benjamin, not now."  
In Bethel, voices raised, tears were shed, "God, why is one tribe's future dead?"  

They built an altar, offerings burned bright,  
Asked, "Who didn't come to Yahweh's sight?"  
Mizpah's oath was a heavy load,  
Those absent faced death's harsh code.  

Israel wept for Benjamin, their kin,  
"A tribe's cut off, how to begin?  
No daughters to give, what can we do?  
Find who missed the assembly view."  

Jabesh Gilead, not a soul came through,  
Counted heads, none in the crew.  
Valiant men, twelve thousand strong,  
"To Jabesh, right the wrong."  

"Strike with the sword," they did declare,  
Destruction left no one there.  
Four hundred maidens found alive,  
To camp Shiloh, they did arrive.  

Peace to Benjamin, at Rimmon's rock,  
Brought the women, yet still in shock.  
The tribe broken, a breach in the land,  
Elders questioned, made their stand.  

"An inheritance must stay, Benjamin's name,  
But our daughters can't join the claim."  
A plan was hatched, a feast at Shiloh,  
Daughters danced, and men lay low.  

"Watch the vineyards, catch your bride,  
When fathers protest, we'll provide.  
Say, 'Not taken by battle's might,  
Nor given, so you stay right.'"  

Benjamin moved, took the chance,  
Found wives among Shiloh's dance.  
Back to their land, cities rebuilt,  
Each tribe departed, paths were split.  

No king ruled in those days,  
Each did right in their own ways.
