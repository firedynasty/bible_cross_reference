Israel did wrong in Yahweh's sight,  
So Midian ruled them, power and might.  

Midian came strong, left Israel in dread,  
They hid in mountains, caves overhead.  

When Israel planted, Midianites came,  
Amalekites too, bringing Israel shame.  

All their crops were destroyed, land left bare,  
No sheep, ox, or donkey, just despair.  

Like locusts they came, too many to count,  
Camels and tents, a destructive mount.  

Israel cried out, in their lowly state,  
To Yahweh they called, for their dire fate.  

A prophet was sent, with words to relay,  
"Yahweh says, 'I brought you out that day,  

From Egypt’s land, from bondage and chains,  
Drove out your enemies, gave you these plains.  

I told you then, 'Fear not Amorite gods,  
But you didn't listen, against my odds.'"  

Under an oak in Ophrah, sat divine,  
Yahweh’s angel watched, Gideon, his time.  

Gideon, hiding, threshing wheat in fear,  
The angel appeared, his message clear:  

"Yahweh is with you, valiant man of might!"  
Gideon questioned, "Why this plight?  

Where are the wonders our fathers told?  
Now we’re cast off, stories grown cold."  

Yahweh spoke, "Go with your strength,  
Save Israel, it's time you go to great lengths."  

"But I'm the least, from a poor clan,"  
"Surely I'm with you," Yahweh's plan.  

Gideon asked, "Show me a sign,  
Prove it’s you, this task is mine."  

He brought a goat, cakes made of unleavened dough,  
Placed them on the rock, watched the miracle show.  

Angel stretched the staff, fire did ignite,  
Consumed the offerings, then left Gideon's sight.  

Gideon feared, said, "I've seen face to face!"  
Yahweh assured, "Peace, live by grace."  

Built an altar, "Yahweh is Peace," he named,  
In Ophrah, the legacy remained.  

At night he took his father's bull,  
Broke Baal’s altar, made his stand full.  

The Asherah cut down, altar built for Yahweh,  
Baal's altar in ruins, at break of day.  

The city men cried, "Who did this deed?"  
"Gideon," they heard, "He planted the seed."  

Joash stood firm, "Will you fight for Baal?  
If he's god, let him save his own tale."  

Named him Jerub-Baal, the challenge was set,  
All came together, not over yet.  

Midianites gathered, in Jezreel's vale,  
Gideon, with Yahweh’s Spirit, would prevail.  

He blew the trumpet, Abiezer came,  
Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun, all the same.  

Gideon prayed, "Prove your words, Lord,  
A fleece I'll lay, let dew accord."  

Dew on fleece, ground dry, proof in hand,  
Gideon knew Yahweh's command.  

One more test, God was asked,  
Fleece dry, dew vast.  

God answered, patience displayed,  
Gideon prepared, no longer afraid.
