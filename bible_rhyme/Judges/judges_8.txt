Ephraim's men questioned, "Why'd you leave us out the fight?  
When you battled Midian, you didn't call us, that ain't right."  
Gideon replied, "What have I done compared to your might?  
Your grapes' gleaning outshines my vineyard, that's my insight.  

God gave you the Midian princes, Oreb and Zeeb in your hand,  
What could I do with a victory so grand?"  
Their anger cooled down, his words put them at ease,  
Gideon moved on, but he was not at peace.  

He crossed the Jordan with three hundred men, tired but keen,  
"Give us bread," he asked Succoth, "for my men, who are lean.  
I'm chasing Zebah and Zalmunna, Midian's kings,"  
But Succoth questioned, "Where's your proof of such things?"  

Gideon warned, "When Yahweh hands them to me,  
I'll tear your flesh with thorns for your mockery."  
He went to Penuel, spoke the same lines,  
They answered with doubt, ignoring the signs.  

He promised, "I'll return in peace and break your tower down,"  
Zebah and Zalmunna camped with fifteen thousand, in Karkor town.  
Gideon struck their army, unprepared, unaware,  
Zebah and Zalmunna fled, but he caught them in his snare.  

Returning from battle, he found Succoth's men,  
"See the kings you mocked me for, remember them?"  
He taught them a lesson with thorns, much pain,  
At Penuel, he broke the tower, their resistance was in vain.  

To Zebah and Zalmunna, "Who were those you killed at Tabor?"  
"Men like you, royal in stature, nothing more."  
"They were my brothers," Gideon said with a tear,  
"If you'd spared their lives, you'd not face me here."  

He called Jether, "Rise, make them pay the cost,"  
But the boy hesitated, his courage almost lost.  
Zebah and Zalmunna taunted, "Man up, show your might,"  
Gideon rose, took their lives, in the fading light.  

Israel then asked Gideon to rule, "Be our king,  
You saved us from Midian, you did the right thing."  
Gideon declined, "Yahweh alone shall reign,  
But give me your earrings, from the battle's gain."  

They spread a garment, gold poured like rain,  
Gideon made an ephod, which became a chain.  
Israel worshipped it, a snare in disguise,  
Forty years of peace, then Gideon's demise.  

He had seventy sons, many wives to his name,  
His concubine bore Abimelech, adding to his fame.  
Gideon died, content, laid in his father's tomb,  
But Israel forgot, returning to idols too soon.  

They turned to Baals, forgetting Yahweh's grace,  
Ignoring Gideon's deeds, they abandoned their place.  
No kindness to his house, though he was their guide,  
Israel drifted away, with Yahweh set aside.
