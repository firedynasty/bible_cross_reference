In the days when Israel had no king to abide,  
A Levite took a concubine for his side.  
Out of Bethlehem Judah, she fled away,  
To her father's house, she went to stay.  

Four months passed, her husband came to plead,  
With donkeys and a servant, to meet her need.  
Her father welcomed him, with joy in his face,  
Three days they ate, drank, and took up space.  

On the fourth day, morning came, he sought to go,  
“Stay for bread,” the father said, “tarry slow.”  
They sat, they ate, the father urged with cheer,  
“Stay the night, make your heart merry here.”  

The man rose to leave, the dawn of day five,  
“Stay 'til evening,” the father did strive.  
But the Levite, his heart set to roam,  
Refused the stay, sought his way home.  

Near Jebus, as the sun began to set,  
The servant suggested they go in and rest.  
But the master pushed on, "Not here, not with these,  
To Gibeah we'll go, on the road with ease."

In Gibeah, they sat in the street,  
No one offered them a place to retreat.  
An old man from Ephraim, working late,  
Saw them there and joined their fate.  

“Where do you travel? From where do you hail?”  
“From Bethlehem Judah, on Yahweh's trail.  
No one has taken us in, yet we lack no need,  
Straw, fodder, bread and wine, all we feed.”  

The old man said, “Peace be upon your way,  
Come to my house, where you can stay.”  
They ate, drank, made merry through the night,  
When wicked men came, with evil in sight.  

“Bring out the man,” they wickedly shout,  
The master pleaded, “Don't carry this out!  
Take my daughter, his concubine instead,  
But spare this man, leave this dread.”  

They wouldn't listen, seized the woman by force,  
Abused her all night without remorse.  
At dawn she fell by the Levite's door,  
Her hands on the threshold, her spirit no more.  

“Get up,” he said, but her silence was grave,  
He placed her on the donkey, to home he gave.  
In his house, with a heart filled with grief,  
He cut her body, piece by piece.  

Sent twelve parts throughout the land,  
A message of horror, a call to stand.  
“All who saw it proclaimed with fear,  
No such deed had been done, or heard here.”
