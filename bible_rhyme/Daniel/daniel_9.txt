In Darius's first year, the Medes took the throne,  
Daniel read the books and knew the prophecy was shown.  
Seventy years for Jerusalem's desolation,  
So he turned to God with prayer and confession.  

He prayed, "Oh Lord, great and dreadful, true,  
You keep your promise with those who love you.  
We've sinned, turned our back, gone astray,  
Ignored your prophets, pushed your words away.  

To you, Lord, belongs righteousness, clear,  
But for us, confusion is what we fear.  
From Judah to Jerusalem, scattered wide,  
In lands where we dwell, because we defied.  

"Mercy and forgiveness belong to you,  
Though we rebelled, we see it through.  
We ignored your voice, your laws we broke,  
The curse of Moses upon us, like a yoke.  

Judgment came, your words confirmed,  
Jerusalem's plight, by evil wormed.  
Yet we didn’t seek your favor or truth,  
Our hearts stayed hardened, aloof.  

"Lord, you brought us from Egypt's might,  
Renown for your power, a glorious sight.  
But we've sinned, done wickedly, we plead,  
Turn your wrath from Jerusalem’s need.  

"God, hear the prayers of your servant now,  
Shine on your sanctuary, fulfill your vow.  
Turn your ear, see our desolation's call,  
Not for our sake, but for your mercy overall.  

"Lord, hear. Lord, forgive. Do not delay,  
For your city and people bear your name, we say.”  
While Daniel prayed, confessed, and sought,  
Gabriel swiftly came with wisdom he brought.  

"Daniel, beloved, I've come to give you sight,  
Seventy weeks are set, to make wrongs right.  
From command to rebuild, the Anointed will show,  
Through seven then sixty-two weeks you’ll know.  

Troubles will come, but it will stand strong,  
The Anointed cut off, yet not for long.  
The city and sanctuary will face destruction,  
War and desolation, a grim instruction.  

A firm covenant for one week will stand,  
Midway through, offerings will be banned.  
Abominations will spread, desolation will reign,  
Till the end, determined, wrath will gain.”
