Belshazzar threw a feast, big and grand,  
With a thousand lords, wine in hand.  
"Bring the vessels," he commanded with pride,  
From Jerusalem's temple, a holy ride.  

They drank and cheered to gods of gold,  
Silver, bronze, and wood, stories told.  
But then a hand appeared, writing on the wall,  
Near the lampstand, chilling them all.  

The king’s face changed, fear took the throne,  
His knees knocked, his courage had flown.  
"Bring the wise men!" he cried in fear,  
"Decipher this writing, make it clear.  

Rewards I promise, power and gold,  
If the mystery of these words is told."  
Yet no one could read, no one could say,  
The meaning hidden in the display.  

The queen advised, "Do not despair,  
There's one with wisdom beyond compare.  
Daniel, known for a spirit so bright,  
He'll solve the riddle, bring it to light."  

Daniel stood before the king, proud and wise,  
"No gifts I need, no worldly prize.  
I'll read the words, make known the decree,  
For it's God who holds your destiny.  

Remember your father, power he held,  
Nations trembled, in fear they dwelled.  
But pride took him down, humbled by God,  
Driven to live like a wild one, flawed.  

You knew all this, yet pride fills your heart,  
God's vessels defiled, you've played your part.  
Praised gods of stone and gold in vain,  
Ignoring the One who holds your reign."  

The writing read, "MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN,"  
Here's the meaning, plain and thin:  
"MENE, your kingdom's days are done,  
TEKEL, weighed and found wanting, none.  

PERES, divided, given away,  
Medes and Persians will rule the day."  
That night, the prophecy came to be,  
Belshazzar was slain, Darius had victory.  

The hand wrote truth, the king's end was near,  
Darius took the throne, the path was clear.  
A story told of pride and fall,  
Remember: God's voice speaks through all.
