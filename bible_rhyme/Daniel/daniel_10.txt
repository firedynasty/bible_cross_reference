In the third year of Cyrus, Persia's king,  
Daniel got a vision, a war it would bring.  

Three weeks of mourning, no bread, no wine,  
No anointing, just waiting on a sign.  

On the twenty-fourth day, near the river's side,  
A man in linen, pure gold on his thighs.  

Body like beryl, face bright like lightning's flash,  
Eyes like torches, arms and feet of bronze, a voice like a crash.  

Daniel alone saw; his men couldn't bear,  
They quaked with fear, ran to hide, unaware.  

Strength left Daniel, pale with no might,  
He heard the voice, then fell, face to the ground in the night.  

A hand touched him, lifted him from the floor,  
“Daniel, beloved, understand once more.  

Stand up, I've been sent to you this day.”  
Trembling, Daniel rose to hear what he'd say.  

“Fear not, Daniel, your words reached the throne,  
From day one you sought, I wasn't alone.  

The prince of Persia blocked me twenty-one days,  
Till Michael helped me, in heavenly ways.  

Now I'm here to tell what's yet to unfold,  
Visions of future days, as the stories were told.”  

Daniel mute, faced the ground deep in thought,  
A touch on his lips, strength again was sought.  

“My sorrows heavy, no strength in me,  
How can I talk, when I'm weak as can be?”  

Touched once more, he felt the power ignite,  
“Beloved, be strong, stand with your might.”  

“I’ll speak now, tell me what you must say,  
For your words have strengthened me today.”  

“Do you know why I'm here?” the man inquired,  
“I’ll return to fight, not yet retired.  

The prince of Greece will come, but hear this truth,  
Michael your prince stands with me, forsooth.”
