In the third year reign of Jehoiakim's call,  
Nebuchadnezzar came with a siege wall tall.  
God handed the king to Babylon's might,  
And sacred vessels vanished from sight.  

To Shinar's land, those treasures went,  
Stored in the house where false gods were sent.  
Ashpenaz was told to find,  
Israel's children, sharp of mind.  

Noble youth without defect,  
Smart and wise, the king's select.  
Taught the ways of Chaldean lore,  
To stand in the palace, a learning score.  

The king served them food and wine,  
For three years they'd grow, then shine.  
Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah came,  
New names they gained in Babylon's game.  

But Daniel stood firm, heart set apart,  
"No defilement for me," his strong start.  
God granted favor in the eunuch's sight,  
Compassion warmed, like morning light.  

The eunuch spoke, concerned and wary,  
"Your pale faces make me wary.  
My head's at risk if you don't thrive,"  
"The king's wrath won't let me survive."  

Daniel replied, "Please test us, I pray,  
Ten days on greens, water each day.  
Then judge our looks, our skin, our flesh,  
Versus those feasting on the king's best."  

The steward agreed, the trial began,  
Ten days passed, and health was the plan.  
Faces fairer, flesh robust,  
More radiant than the others' lust.  

The steward switched their diet so,  
Vegetables over dainties—their glow.  
God gifted wisdom and dreams to Daniel's mind,  
In visions deep, understanding he'd find.  

The time came to meet the king's gaze,  
Brought before Nebuchadnezzar's praise.  
No one like them, unmatched they stood,  
In wisdom's realm, tenfold good.  

From magicians to enchanters, they'd outshine,  
Daniel and friends, a celestial sign.  
Their journey continued, steadfast, just,  
Even when Cyrus took the throne's trust.
