In the third year of Belshazzar's reign,  
A vision to Daniel, clear and plain.  
In Susa's citadel, in my mind's eye,  
By the river Ulai, I watched the sky.  

A ram appeared with two horns high,  
One rose last, reaching toward the sky.  
West, north, south, it roamed with might,  
No beast could stand, no one could fight.  

Then from the west, a goat took flight,  
Never touching the ground in sight.  
A single horn between its eyes,  
Charged the ram with furious cries.  

The goat struck hard, broke the ram's horn,  
Left it powerless, defeated, torn.  
The goat exalted, strong and bold,  
But its great horn broke, as the story's told.  

Four horns rose where one had been,  
Toward the winds, a sight unseen.  
From one horn, a little one grew,  
To the south, east, land of the true.  

It reached the stars, brought some down,  
Trampled them beneath its crown.  
It challenged the Prince, took His space,  
Burnt offerings, truth erased.  

Heard a voice, a holy call,  
"How long will this vision fall?”  
“Two thousand, three hundred nights," he said,  
"Then the sanctuary shall be cleansed, not dead."  

I, Daniel, sought to understand,  
A figure like a man took a stand.  
A voice called, "Gabriel, make him see,  
The vision’s meaning, let it be."  

Gabriel approached, I hit the ground,  
"Understand, son of man, truth's profound.  
For the vision's set for the end of time,  
The ram and goat, their fates align."  

"The ram, Media and Persia's kings,  
The goat, Greece’s first king brings.  
Four kingdoms rise, but lack his strength,  
In the latter days, deceit's length."  

"A fierce king will rise, face severe,  
Destroy the strong, bring mighty fear.  
His power not his own, but great,  
Against the Prince, meets his fate."  

"The vision's true of days ahead,  
Seal it up, let it be said."  
Daniel fainted, weak and worn,  
Yet rose again, though visions torn.  

In the end, I wondered deep,  
The vision's secrets, yet to keep.  
The king's business resumed my days,  
Mysteries unknown, in their ways.
