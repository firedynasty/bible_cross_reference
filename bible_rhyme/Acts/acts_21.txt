Set sail from Cos, next stop was Rhodes,  
Then Patara called with her shiploads.  

To Phoenicia, we made our way,  
Cyprus on the left, didn't delay.  

Tyre's shores, where the cargo unloads,  
Met disciples, stayed on the roads.  

They warned Paul, "Don't go to Jerusalem,"  
Through the Spirit, the message was solemn.  

Days passed, we journeyed anew,  
Families joined, prayers on the beach, it's true.  

Boarded the ship, waves our last sight,  
They went home, as we took flight.  

Ptolemais greeted, one day we stayed,  
To Caesarea next, where we prayed.  

Philip's house, with daughters who prophesy,  
Agabus came with a prophetic tie.  

Bound himself with Paul's own belt,  
"Says the Spirit, Jerusalem will be felt."  

Begged him not to go, hearts in pain,  
Paul stood firm, ready for chain.  

He said, "Your tears, why break my heart?  
For Jesus, I'm ready to take any part."  

Couldn't change his mind, with a sigh,  
"The Lord's will be done," was our cry.  

Baggage packed, to Jerusalem we went,  
Mnason of Cyprus, with us he was sent.  

Brothers received us with joy and grace,  
Paul met James, elders in place.  

Gentile ministry, Paul reported it well,  
Glorified God, their praises did swell.  

But rumors about Paul had spread,  
Jews among Gentiles, misled.  

Assembly called, to clear the air,  
Four men with vows, Paul to share.  

Purify together, expenses paid,  
Show the truth, let rumors fade.  

Only Gentiles, rules to heed,  
From idols and immorality, take heed.  

Paul purified, temple he entered,  
Seven days until his offering rendered.  

Jews from Asia, stirred the crowd,  
"Against our law," they shouted loud.  

Dragged Paul out, the temple doors closed,  
Jerusalem's uproar was exposed.  

Commander came, soldiers in tow,  
The beating stopped, the crowd's anger slow.  

Bound with chains, seeking truth in the noise,  
Paul taken away, amidst the uproar's voice.  

Crowd's cries, "Away with him!" they shout,  
Soldiers held Paul, as they moved out.  

Paul spoke, "Can I address, a word to you?"  
The officer asked, "Do you speak Greek too?"  

Not an Egyptian, Paul made it clear,  
"I'm a Jew from Tarsus, hear me, dear."  

Permission granted, Paul stood tall,  
On the stairs with silence over all.  

In Hebrew he spoke, with hand held high,  
And the crowd listened, as Paul let his words fly.
