We're set to sail to Italy, Paul's in the mix,  
Julius the centurion, in charge of the fix.  

On a ship from Adramyttium, off we glide,  
With Aristarchus by Paul's side.  

We dock in Sidon, Julius gives Paul a break,  
To see his friends, for refreshment's sake.  

From there we move, 'neath Cyprus we cruise,  
The winds against us, we gotta choose.  

We hit Myra, switch ships, head to Italy's land,  
But slow winds hold us, and it's hard to stand.  

Paul warns, “This journey's bound for disaster,  
Cargo, ship, and lives may go faster.”  

The centurion listens to the ship's guide,  
Thinking Crete's Phoenix is a safer ride.  

South winds whisper, they think they're on track,  
But Euroclydon storms, wind at our back.  

We struggle and strain, the ship's tossed wild,  
With ropes, anchors, and fears compiled.  

Days without sun, stars, or hope in sight,  
Paul stands up, speaks of an angel's light.  

“Fear not, we'll lose this ship, not lives,” Paul said,  
“God’s got a plan, no need to dread.”  

Fourteen nights of rocking and rolling,  
Soundings say land, hope now controlling.  

Sailors plot an escape, but Paul declares,  
“Stay in the ship, God’s promise he shares.”  

They cut the ropes, trust is renewed,  
Paul urges, “Eat, keep up your mood.”  

Morning breaks, a bay comes in view,  
Anchors cut, sails raised, they pursue.  

The boat hits ground where seas collide,  
The stern breaks, but Paul’s hope abides.  

Soldiers plan to kill, but Julius saves,  
Paul’s life cherished, his faith engraves.  

Some swim, some float on planks and boards,  
All make it to land, safe with the Lord's accord.
