In Iconium they hit the scene, Jews and Greeks gathered, belief was keen.  
But some Jews stirred trouble, souls full of hate, tried to divide and desecrate.  

They stayed long, bold in the Lord's embrace, signs and wonders proved their case.  
City split, some with Jews, some with apostles aligned, tension mounting, hearts entwined.  

A plot to stone them brewed in the air, Gentiles and Jews, both prepared to snare.  
Aware of danger, they fled the scene, Lycaonia, Lystra, Derbe, preaching the dream.  

In Lystra a man crippled from birth sat, Paul saw his faith, knew where he was at.  
"Stand on your feet!" Paul commanded with might, he leaped and walked, a miraculous sight.  

Crowds cried out, "The gods are here!” Calling them Jupiter and Mercury, loud and clear.  
Priest brought offerings to make a grand show, but Paul and Barnabas said, "No, no, no!"  

"We're just men like you, with a message to share, turn from idols to the God who cares.  
He made the sky, earth, sea, all that you know, giving rain, filling hearts with gladness aglow."  

Even with words, they struggled to restrain, the crowd intent on their sacrifice campaign.  
But Jews from Antioch and Iconium came, stirred the crowd, stoned Paul, left him for shame.  

But disciples gathered, Paul rose from the ground, back to the city, his strength unbound.  
Next day with Barnabas to Derbe they went, preaching Good News, on their mission intent.  

Returned to Lystra, Iconium, Antioch's embrace, strengthening souls with words of grace.  
Through trials, they said, we enter God's land, appointed elders, prayed, and took a stand.  

Through Pisidia, Pamphylia, preaching done, Perga to Attalia, their journey won.  
To Antioch they sailed, mission complete, shared God's work, opened faith's door to the street.  

With disciples, they stayed, the story was told, of faith's unfolding, courageous and bold.
