Ananias and Sapphira, sold their land for some cash,  
Kept a part for themselves, thought they'd make a quick stash.  

Laid the rest at the apostles' feet, like it was all they got,  
But Peter saw through the game, called 'em out on the spot.  

"Ananias, why lie to the Spirit, to the Lord?  
The land was yours, no need for this fraud," he implored.  

Ananias heard the truth, fell down, breathed his last,  
Fear gripped the people, as they buried him fast.  

Three hours later, Sapphira came around,  
Unaware of her husband's fate, or the ground.  

Peter asked her, "Was this the price, tell me true?"  
"Yes," she replied, but the lie caught her too.  

"The feet of the men who buried your man stand at the door,"  
She dropped at Peter’s feet, breathed no more.  

Fear spread wide, as they laid her in the grave,  
The assembly learned, and their respect they gave.  

Many wonders were performed by the apostles’ hands,  
In Solomon's porch, they stood strong in faith bands.  

Believers grew in number, a mighty crowd,  
Peter's shadow healed the sick, drew 'em proud.  

But the high priest, filled with envy and spite,  
Seized the apostles, locked them away at night.  

An angel freed them, said, "Go and teach in the temple's hall,"  
At daybreak they preached, spreading the word to all.  

The council gathered, found the cell empty and bare,  
Confusion struck, as they wondered who dared.  

"Look!" someone cried, "they're teaching in the square,"  
The captain fetched them, careful not to stir the air.  

The high priest demanded, "Didn't we command you to cease?"  
But Peter stood firm, "We obey God, not you, in peace."  

"The God of our fathers raised Jesus, who you slew,  
Made Him a Savior, giving repentance anew."  

Their words cut deep, the council's anger burned bright,  
But Gamaliel stood, spoke wisdom in their sight.  

"Be wary," he warned, "leave these men be,  
If they're of God, you can't fight and won't see victory."  

The council agreed, beat them, and let them go,  
The apostles rejoiced, honored to bear such a blow.  

Daily in the temple, in homes, their voices rang,  
Preaching of Jesus, their Savior, as they sang.
