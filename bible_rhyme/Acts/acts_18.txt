Paul left Athens, arrived in Corinth's heat,  
Met Aquila and Priscilla, where trade and faith meet.  

Tent makers they were, so Paul joined in the work,  
In synagogues he preached, his words never shirked.  

Silas and Timothy arrived, and Paul's zeal ignited,  
To the Jews, "Jesus is Christ," his message recited.  

Opposition came, slander loud and fierce,  
Paul shook his clothes, "From you I now pierce."  

"I'll turn to Gentiles, your blood's not on me,"  
He left for Justus' house, next door, you see.  

Crispus believed, the synagogue chief,  
With all of his house, they turned to belief.  

In a vision God spoke, "Fear not, speak out,  
No harm will come; I've got folks about."  

For a year and a half, Paul taught, God’s word he spread,  
But trouble stirred when Gallio took the seat ahead.  

The Jews rose up, with Paul they took issue,  
But Gallio dismissed, "This I won't pursue."  

He drove them out, their cries left behind,  
Sosthenes beaten, Gallio paid no mind.  

Paul stayed longer, then set sail for Syria,  
With Priscilla and Aquila, from Corinth's area.  

At Cenchreae, a vow, shaved head he bore,  
To Ephesus he came, reasoned once more.  

Invited to stay, yet onward he pressed,  
"To Jerusalem I must go, if God wills, I'll rest."  

From Caesarea to Antioch, his journey was grand,  
Then through Galatia, Phrygia, strengthening the band.  

Apollos appeared, eloquent and bold,  
Knew only John’s baptism, yet his spirit cold.  

Priscilla and Aquila taught him anew,  
More accurately now, in Christ he grew.  

To Achaia he went, the brothers him sent,  
Refuted the Jews, with Scripture's consent.
