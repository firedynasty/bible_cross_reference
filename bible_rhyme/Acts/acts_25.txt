Festus rolled in, just three days new, from Caesarea to Jerusalem, he flew.  
Chief priests and top folks, with a plan so grim, begged Festus to summon Paul, aiming to dim him.  

"Keep Paul here," Festus said, "he's at Caesarea,  
If he's guilty, come with me, and we'll see ya."  

Ten days in the city, then off to the seat,  
Paul brought in, to face charges he couldn’t defeat.  

Jews circled round with tales full of strife,  
But lacked any proof, couldn't take Paul's life.  

Paul stood firm, saying, "I've broken no law,  
Not the temple, not Caesar, no sin to gnaw."  

Festus sought favor, asked Paul, "Will you go?  
To Jerusalem's judgment, is that a yes or a no?"  

Paul replied, "I'm here, right where I need,  
To Caesar's court, for Caesar's creed.  

If guilty, I'll face what's coming to me,  
But if not, then you can't make me flee.  

I appeal to Caesar!" the words echoed out,  
Festus conferred, had no doubt.  

"You've appealed to Caesar, to Caesar you'll ride,"  
Days later, Agrippa with Bernice by his side.  

Festus laid it out, "Prisoner left by Felix,  
Jews want him judged, but Rome has its tricks.  

Face-to-face, that's the Roman way,  
Accusers meet accused, no delay."  

Charges laid, but not as expected,  
Just talk of Jesus, dead but resurrected.  

Paul's appeal sent him to a higher throne,  
Festus needed clarity, not left alone.  

Agrippa wanted to hear the tale,  
"Tomorrow," Festus said, "with detail."  

Next day came, with pomp and flair,  
Paul brought in before all who cared.  

Festus spoke, "King Agrippa, and all,  
Here's the man, the Jews want to fall.  

But guilty of death, he's not, so I write,  
To Caesar he goes, for justice bright.  

Seems absurd to send him, without a clear vice,  
So listen and help, in this legal slice."
