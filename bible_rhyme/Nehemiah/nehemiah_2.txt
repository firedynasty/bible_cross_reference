In Nisan's month, in Artaxerxes’ reign,  
I served the wine, hiding my pain.  

The king looked up, "Why so down?  
You’re not sick, yet you wear a frown."  

Fear gripped my heart, but I spoke,  
"Long live the king!" My courage awoke.  

"Why should I smile when my city’s in flame,  
Where my fathers lie, it’s not the same."  

The king leaned in, "What do you seek?"  
So I prayed to God, my voice didn't squeak.  

"If it pleases you, let me go,  
To rebuild my city, restore its glow."  

The queen by his side, the king replied,  
“How long will you be on this ride?”  

I set the time, and the king agreed,  
His favor was with me, indeed.  

I asked for letters, to cross the streams,  
And timber for gates and temple beams.  

Granted, his hand was kind to me,  
I thanked God for this decree.  

To governors, I gave the letters in hand,  
With captains and horsemen, a strong band.  

Sanballat and Tobiah, they frowned in spite,  
But for Jerusalem, I rode through the night.  

Three days in Jerusalem, I kept it tight,  
With a few men, I moved in the night.  

Through gates and walls, broken and charred,  
I inspected the city, though the journey was hard.  

The rulers knew not where I went or why,  
I kept it secret, under the night sky.  

Then I spoke to them, laying the plan,  
"See the ruins, let's build, if we can."  

I shared God's hand and the king's decree,  
They said, "Let’s build, strong and free."  

Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem heard,  
They mocked us, with spiteful word.  

I stood firm, "God will aid our climb,  
You have no claim, not this time."
