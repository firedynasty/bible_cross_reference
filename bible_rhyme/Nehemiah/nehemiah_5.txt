The people cried out, voices loud in the night,  
"Too many mouths, not enough grain in sight."  

Fields and vineyards, mortgaged away,  
Houses for bread, in famine's sway.  

Borrowed money for the king's demand,  
Fields and vineyards slipped from our hand.  

Flesh of our flesh, brothers the same,  
Children in bondage—oh, what a shame!  

I burned with anger, heard their plea,  
Faced the nobles, confronted the greed.  

"You take from your kin, charging them debt,"  
Called a great meeting, their silence met.  

"The act is wrong, should you not fear?  
Nations observe, their reproach is near."  

"I lend them too, but without this weight,  
Let's end the usury, before it's too late."  

"Restore their lands, their vineyards, give back,  
The hundredth part, remove the lack."  

"We'll do as you say," they vowed and swore,  
I called the priests, they promised more.  

Shook out my lap, a warning made clear,  
"God shake you out if you don't adhere."  

"Amen," they cried, praised Yahweh's name,  
Kept their promise, upheld their claim.  

As governor in Judah, twelve years spent,  
No bread of the governor, our labor lent.  

Others took from the people's hand,  
But I feared God, worked the land.  

At my table, the rulers sat,  
One hundred fifty, no land we spat.  

Oxen, sheep, and wine were shared,  
For the people's burden, their welfare cared.  

"Remember me, God, for the good I've done,  
For helping these people, every last one."
