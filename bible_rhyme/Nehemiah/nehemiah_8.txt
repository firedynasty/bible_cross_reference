In front of the water gate, they gathered tight,  
Ezra with the law, ready to recite.  

Men, women, all who could understand,  
Listening closely, as he took the stand.  

From morning light till midday sun,  
Ezra read the law, and the crowd was one.  

On a wooden pulpit, he raised the book high,  
The people stood up, as if touching the sky.  

"Bless Yahweh, the great God," Ezra exclaimed,  
“Amen, Amen,” the people proclaimed.  

Heads bowed low, faces to the ground,  
In worship and awe, no sound but profound.  

Jeshua and the Levites made the meaning clear,  
Each word of the law, they helped the people hear.  

Nehemiah and Ezra, with the Levites beside,  
Told the people, “Don’t weep, don’t cry.”  

“This day is holy, let joy be your might,  
Eat, drink, and share, in Yahweh’s light.”  

The Levites calmed them, “Hold your peace,  
For this day’s sacred, let your worry cease.”  

So they feasted and shared, joy in their hand,  
Understanding the words, they now could stand.  

Next day, to Ezra, heads of families came,  
Studying the law, from which wisdom they’d claim.  

Found in the law, a command they’d missed,  
“Live in booths during the festival twist.”  

“Go gather branches, olive and palm,  
Build your shelters, stay calm.”  

The people went out, they gathered the wood,  
Made shelters everywhere, as they understood.  

From roofs to courts, the shelters stood tall,  
Since Jeshua’s days, they hadn’t done this at all.  

Great was the gladness, from the old to the youth,  
Seven days of feast, in the joy of the truth.  

Day by day, Ezra read from the law,  
On the eighth, they assembled, without a flaw.
