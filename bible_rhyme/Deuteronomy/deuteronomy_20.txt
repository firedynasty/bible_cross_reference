When you face your foes, their numbers and might,  
Don't fear their horses, chariots, or the fight.  
For Yahweh your God, who led you from Egypt's land,  
Is with you in battle, guiding your hand.  

The priest steps up, his voice loud and clear,  
"Hear, Israel, the battle is near.  
Let not your heart faint or be filled with dread,  
For Yahweh fights for you, ahead."  

Officers call out, checking who's ready to stand,  
"Built a new house yet to dedicate by hand?  
Go home now, lest in battle you fall,  
And another man claims it, having it all."  

"Planted a vineyard, but not sipped its wine?  
Go home, it's not yet your time.  
Pledged to marry, but not wed your love?  
Return, lest another wins the heart you're dreaming of."  

"Who fears the fight, whose heart is low?  
Go home now, lest others' spirits also slow."  
Once officers finish, leaders they appoint,  
Captains ready, put at the army's front joint.  

Approach a city, offer peace's chance,  
If they agree, let them enter servitude's dance.  
But if they refuse, and war's their call,  
Besiege the city, let Yahweh deliver it all.  

Strike the men, but spare the rest,  
Women, children, livestock—take the plunder, it's the test.  
For cities far, not in the promised space,  
Spare them no breath, erase each trace.  

Destroy the Hittite, Amorite, Canaanite too,  
Perizzite, Hivite, Jebusite, as commanded to you.  
Lest they lure you to sin with their ways,  
Yahweh's word, keep it, all your days.  

In siege, spare the trees, they're life to eat,  
Don't cut down those that offer food sweet.  
But those that don't, use them for war,  
Build your bulwarks, until the city's no more.
