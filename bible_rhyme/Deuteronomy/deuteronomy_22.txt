See your brother's ox stray, don't look away,  
Bring it back to him without delay.  

If he's far or you don't know his face,  
Take it to your house, give it space.  

Do the same for his donkey, his garment too,  
With anything lost, return what’s due.  

See his donkey fall, don’t act blind,  
Help him lift it, be kind.  

Don't wear clothes meant for another,  
God's word says "don't bother."  

Find a nest with eggs or young, don’t take the hen,  
Let her go, so good may come again.  

Build a house, make that rail strong,  
Prevent a fall, right a wrong.  

Don't mix seeds in your vineyard, heed the warning,  
Keep your fields pure, no defiling in the morning.  

Don't plow with ox and donkey combined,  
Keep clothes of wool and linen unaligned.  

Fringes on your cloak, four corners they adorn,  
Remember the commandments, the ones you've sworn.  

Man takes a wife, then spreads false shame,  
Parents present proof, clear her name.  

The elders hear, the man they chastise,  
Pay a fine, keep her, don't despise.  

But if his claim's true, and proof's not there,  
The city stones her, justice laid bare.  

Man lies with another's wife, both must die,  
Remove the evil, let justice fly.  

A pledged virgin in the city, her silence brings doom,  
Both are stoned, to cleanse the room.  

In the field, her cries unheard,  
Only the man punished, as per God's word.  

A virgin unpledged, caught in the act,  
He pays her father, marries her, that's the pact.  

No man shall take his father's wife,  
Respect the bond, honor life.
