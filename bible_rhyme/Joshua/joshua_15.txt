Judah's land stretches south to the edge of Edom,  
Past Zin's wilderness, at the south's utmost kingdom.  
From the Salt Sea's tip, the boundary sweeps,  
Climbing Akrabbim, across desert steeps.  

Through Zin, past Kadesh Barnea's southern line,  
Hezron and Addar, where borders intertwine.  
Azmon, to Egypt's brook, the border flows,  
Ending at the sea where the south wind blows.  

Eastward, the Salt Sea's endless reach,  
Toward Jordan's end, where the waters breach.  
North from the sea, at Jordan's last bay,  
Beth Hoglah and Beth Arabah mark the way.  

By Reuben's stone, the border ascends,  
Through Debir, Achor Valley, where paths bend.  
Northward past Gilgal, Adummim's rise,  
En Shemesh, En Rogel catch the skies.  

Jerusalem's face, the Jebusite's prize,  
Hinnom's valley, where the border lies.  
From mountain peak to Nephtoah's spring,  
Mount Ephron's cities, the border does cling.  

Baalah, Kiriath Jearim, the path turns west,  
Mount Seir and Chesalon, before it rests.  
Beth Shemesh and Timnah, the line does snake,  
To Ekron, Shikkeron, toward sea's wake.  

The children of Judah, their borders defined,  
Caleb's portion, where Hebron's heights bind.  
Caleb drove out Anak's three sons,  
Sheshai, Ahiman, Talmai, each one.  

Debir he took, Caleb's challenge was clear,  
"Take Kiriath Sepher, my daughter is near."  
Othniel rose, victory in sight,  
Achsah his prize, in Caleb's delight.  

She asked for a field, on donkey she came,  
"What do you want?" Caleb asked the dame.  
"Bless me with springs," Achsah replied,  
He gave both upper and lower, his pride.  

Judah's tribe settled, from south to shore,  
Cities and villages, a vast explore.  
From Kabzeel, Beersheba, Gaza's line,  
To Jerusalem where Jebusites still dine.
