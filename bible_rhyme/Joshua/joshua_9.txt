When the kings heard news, they gathered with spite,  
To fight Joshua, Israel, under one united might.  
But Gibeon's folk hatched a clever disguise,  
With old sacks and sandals, they aimed to surprise.  

Old wine skins, garments worn and beat,  
Moldy bread they brought as the trick's deceit.  
They reached Joshua in Gilgal, with a plea sincere,  
"We're from a distant land, make a covenant here."  

Israel’s men questioned, "Could you be near?"  
"Who are you? Where from? Make your story clear."  
"We're from far away, served by Yahweh's fame,  
He conquered Egypt, and kings in His name."  

"Our elders sent us with dry, moldy bread,  
Our journey was long, shoes and clothes now threadbare," they said.  
The men of Israel tasted but didn’t ask God’s voice,  
Joshua made peace, with the covenant their choice.  

Three days in, truth was found in disguise,  
Neighbors, living near, brought a new surprise.  
Israel reached Gibeon, their cities to see,  
But couldn’t strike, bound by oath and decree.  

The people murmured, but princes stood firm,  
"We swore by Yahweh, so here’s what we’ll affirm:  
Let them live, make them wood cutters and drawers,  
For Yahweh’s house, our covenant's powers."  

Joshua called them out, "Why deceive us with lies?  
You claimed distance, but lived under our skies.  
Now cursed, you’ll serve, cut wood, fetch water's flow,  
For my God’s house, this is the task you’ll know."  

They confessed, "We feared Yahweh’s mighty decree,  
He gave Moses the land, to destroy us utterly.  
In your hands now, do what seems right,  
We sought just to live, not bring on a fight."  

Joshua spared them from Israel’s wrath,  
Made them servants for Yahweh’s path.  
That day they became cutters and drawers of water,  
Bound by the covenant, a new role they sought after.
