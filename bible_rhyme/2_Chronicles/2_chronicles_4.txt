He built an altar, bronze, so grand,  
Twenty cubits each way it did stand.  

A molten sea, brim to brim, ten cubits wide,  
Round and high, thirty cubits to stride.  

Underneath were oxen, encircling in rows,  
Two tiers cast, as the sea arose.  

On twelve strong oxen, the sea took its place,  
Facing every direction, their hindquarters in space.  

A handbreadth thick, like a lily it spanned,  
Held three thousand baths, by His command.  

Ten basins he made, five left, five right,  
For washing offerings, pure in God's sight.  

Golden lampstands, ten in line,  
Set in the temple, with a holy sign.  

Ten tables, five left, five right, so bold,  
And a hundred basins, shimmering gold.  

Courts for the priests, with doors of bronze,  
Set the sea to the south, as the sun dawns.  

Huram made pots, shovels, and more,  
Finished his work for Solomon's store.  

Pillars, bowls, and capitals high,  
Networks adorned with pomegranates nigh.  

Bases and basins, as they were planned,  
One sea on oxen, in the Promised Land.  

Bright bronze vessels, cast with care,  
In the Jordan's clay, laid bare.  

Solomon's vessels in abundance, no weight known,  
For Yahweh's house, His glory shown.  

Golden altars, tables with show bread placed,  
Lampstands with lamps, pure gold they embraced.  

Flowers, lamps, and tongs, perfect gold displayed,  
Snuffers, basins, fire pans, in splendor arrayed.  

The holy inner doors, gold through and through,  
In God's house, His promise shone true.
