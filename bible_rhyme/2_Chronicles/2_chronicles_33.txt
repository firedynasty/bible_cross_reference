Manasseh was just a kid, twelve years old and green,  
When he took the throne, reigned like a king who'd never seen.  
Fifty-five long years in Jerusalem he stayed,  
But did evil in God's sight, in darkness he played.  

He rebuilt the high places, Hezekiah had torn down,  
Raised Baals, made Asheroth, served the skies without a frown.  
Altars in God's house stood, despite His word—  
"My name shall rest in Jerusalem," His voice was heard.  

But Manasseh turned from Yahweh’s eternal decree,  
Built altars for the skies, in God's courts they'd be.  
He even burned his children in Hinnom's fiery vale,  
Practiced dark arts and sorcery, like ships with evil sails.  

He set an idol in the house where God said clear,  
"In this place forever, my name draws near.  
I'll keep Israel steady if they heed my command,  
The laws of Moses, observe and understand."  

Judah was seduced, more wicked than before,  
More than nations Yahweh drove, they closed the righteous door.  
Yahweh spoke, but they turned a deaf ear,  
So Assyria's king came, bound Manasseh in fear.  

Chained, distressed, in Babylon he cried,  
Begged Yahweh, humbled, God heard his side.  
Brought back to his kingdom, Manasseh knew,  
That Yahweh was God, steadfast and true.  

He built a wall around David’s town's edge,  
Fortified Jerusalem, made a solemn pledge.  
Removed foreign gods, idols thrown away,  
Rebuilt Yahweh’s altar, peace offerings they'd lay.  

Judah was told, "Serve Yahweh, the God of our land,"  
But high places remained, despite the command.  
Manasseh’s story, his prayer and his fall,  
Written by the seers, a record of it all.  

He slept with his fathers, his reign put to rest,  
Amon, his son, took the throne, not blessed.  
Amon followed the path of idols and stone,  
Did not humble like Manasseh, his heart was his own.  

Servants conspired, took Amon's life away,  
But the people rose up, vengeance they did repay.  
Josiah, his son, was made king in his stead,  
A new chapter began, as the old was put to bed.
