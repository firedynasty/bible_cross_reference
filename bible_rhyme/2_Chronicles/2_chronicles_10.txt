In Shechem, Rehoboam aimed to take the throne,  
All Israel gathered, his reign to be shown.  
Jeroboam in Egypt heard the news loud,  
Returned from his refuge, joined the crowd.  

"Your father gave us burdens, made us sweat,  
Lighten it up, serve you, you bet."  
Rehoboam said, "Three days, then come back,  
I'll give you my answer, stay on track."  

He asked the old men, wise in their ways,  
"What should I say in these trying days?"  
They said, "Be kind, with good words please,  
And they'll serve you well, at ease."  

But he ditched their advice, turned to his crew,  
The young men he knew, seeking something new.  
"What should I say to lighten their load?"  
They replied with heat, in a harsher mode:  

"Tell them your small finger's thicker than his waist,  
More burdens will come, and whips be replaced.  
Whips for scorpions, yokes doubled, feel the sting,  
Let them know the weight your rule will bring."  

The third day came, the people arrived,  
Rehoboam's harsh words made his rule contrived.  
Ignored the elders' plea, followed the youth's advice,  
"My father whipped, but scorpions will suffice."  

The people saw he wouldn't listen, so they cried,  
"No part in David, to tents we’ll abide."  
Israel left, their tents was their only home,  
Rehoboam reigned Judah, all alone.  

Sent Hadoram, the labor's overseer,  
But stones rained down, his end was near.  
Rehoboam fled fast, back to his seat,  
Israel rebelled, David's house beat.  

From that day, Israel went their own way,  
In Judah, Rehoboam's rule did stay.
