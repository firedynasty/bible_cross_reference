Ahaz was young, just twenty, when he took the throne,  
Reigned sixteen years, but his heart was cold as stone.  

Didn't walk with Yahweh like his father, King David,  
Followed Israel's kings, with molten gods he played with.  

Incense burned in Hinnom's valley, children in the fire,  
Mimicking nations Yahweh did expire.  

Sacrificed on high places, hills, beneath green trees,  
Turned from Yahweh’s path, doing as he pleased.  

So Yahweh handed him to Syria’s king,  
Struck him hard, captives they'd bring.  

Pekah of Israel dealt a heavy blow,  
120,000 valiant men, all fell low.  

Zichri from Ephraim, a mighty foe,  
Killed the king’s son, the ruler, swift in woe.  

Two hundred thousand taken, women, sons, and daughters,  
Plunder to Samaria, chaos in Judah falters.  

Oded, a prophet, came to meet the host,  
“Yahweh’s anger on Judah, you've made a mighty boast.”  

“Do you plan to enslave your kin as slaves?  
Remember your sins, let Yahweh’s wrath be waived.”  

Ephraim's leaders stood, heads held high,  
“Don't bring captives here, or wrath will multiply.”  

They clothed the naked, gave them food and drink,  
Anointed, carried, to the city of palms, they'd sink.  

Ahaz cried to Assyria for aid,  
But Edomites and Philistines raided, unafraid.  

Cities of Judah fell to the foe,  
Ahaz's missteps made Judah's strength go.  

He stripped Yahweh's house, gave gifts to Assyria's king,  
But it brought no help, no strengthening.  

In distress, Ahaz strayed further still,  
Sacrificed to Damascus gods, hoping they'd fulfill.  

Ruined by false gods, Israel’s king,  
Ahaz closed Yahweh's temple, a dangerous thing.  

Altars in every corner, incense to idols burned,  
Provoked Yahweh’s anger, but lessons weren’t learned.  

All his acts, written down, from start to end,  
Ahaz slept with fathers, his reign at an end.  

Hezekiah his son took the throne's embrace,  
Judah’s hope renewed, in a different place.
