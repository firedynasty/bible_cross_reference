Rehoboam rose, his kingdom strong and wide,  
But turned from Yahweh's law, took Israel for a ride.  

In year five, Shishak from Egypt marched bold,  
With chariots and horsemen, numbers untold.  

Cities fortified fell to his might,  
Judah shook under that ominous night.  

Shemaiah spoke to Rehoboam and the crew,  
“Yahweh says, ‘You've left me, now Shishak's on you.’”  

Humbled, they cried, “Yahweh is just,”  
Bowed their heads low, laid down their trust.  

Yahweh observed, saw their hearts bend,  
Said to Shemaiah, “Destruction I’ll suspend.  

They'll taste some freedom, wrath not full blown,  
Still serve Shishak, lessons to be known.”  

Shishak took treasures from the Lord’s place,  
From the king’s house, left it a disgrace.  

Gold shields gone, Rehoboam made bronze,  
Gave them to the guards, for defense upon.  

Each time he walked through Yahweh’s door,  
The guards brought them out, then back once more.  

Humbled, Yahweh’s wrath turned away,  
Judah found good in that fateful day.  

Rehoboam reigned strong in Jerusalem's might,  
The city Yahweh chose, in his sight.  

He did evil, heart not set on the Lord,  
Writ in Shemaiah and Iddo’s accord.  

Wars with Jeroboam marked his days,  
Rehoboam’s end came, in David's tomb he lays.  

Abijah his son took up the crown,  
As Rehoboam’s tale was written down.
