I looked and saw oppression done under the sun,  
Tears of the oppressed—comfort there was none.  
The oppressors held power, their grip was tight,  
Yet no comforter for the ones in fright.  

I praised the dead, more peaceful in their rest,  
Than the living who still face life's cruel test.  
Better yet, is he who's not seen the day,  
Evil under the sun, kept far away.  

Labor and envy fill a man's plight,  
It's all vanity, chasing wind in flight.  
The fool folds hands, ruins himself with ease,  
Better quietness, than labor without peace.  

Vanity again, beneath the sun's glow,  
One stands alone with no son, no bro.  
"Why do I toil, soul left to deprive?"  
Such misery, no joy kept alive.  

Two together, stronger than one,  
Good reward for labor, when work is done.  
If one falls, the other lifts him high,  
Alone he falls, no help nigh.  

Two lying close, warmth they gain,  
One alone, cold's bitter reign.  
Together they stand, against one they fight,  
Threefold cord, strong and tight.  

Better a poor youth, wise in his way,  
Than a foolish king, stubborn in sway.  
Out of prison, came to reign,  
Born poor, yet broke the chain.  

I saw all living, under the sun's span,  
With the youth who led, the young man.  
No end to people, they followed in mass,  
But future forgot him—vanity's glass.
