The Preacher speaks, son of a king's decree,  
"Vanity of vanities, all's vanity," said he.  

What does man gain from working under the sun's heat?  
Generations come and go, but earth's still on its beat.  

The sun rises and sets, racing back to its start,  
The wind turns south, then north, a never-ending art.  

Rivers flow to the sea, yet the sea's never filled,  
Round they go, their courses, as the Maker willed.  

All things bring weariness, beyond what words can say,  
Eyes never satisfied, ears hear all day.  

What has been will be again; nothing new on this ground,  
"See, it's new!"—but it's just the same old sound.  

No memory of the past, nothing lasts in the mind,  
What comes won't be remembered by those who follow behind.  

I, the Preacher, once a king, sought wisdom's heavy toll,  
A burden God has given to weigh upon man's soul.  

I've seen all under the sun, it’s chasing after breeze,  
What's crooked can't be straightened, lacking can't appease.  

I said, "I've gained wisdom above all, above the rest,  
Wisdom and knowledge have filled my chest."  

Yet knowing madness and folly, I found it all wind's play,  
For with much wisdom comes grief, sorrow grows each day.
