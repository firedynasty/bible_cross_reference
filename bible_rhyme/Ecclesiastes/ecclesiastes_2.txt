I said to myself, "Come on, let's test with glee,  
Enjoying pleasure—but it's just vanity."  

Laughter’s just foolish, mirth has no gain,  
I searched for joy, for my heart to sustain.  

Wine to cheer, yet wisdom my guide,  
I sought folly's lure, to see what's inside.  

Built mighty works, houses tall and grand,  
Planted vineyards, works of my own hand.  

Gardens and parks, trees in lines,  
Pools of water, forests in design.  

Servants aplenty, born in my space,  
Herds and flocks, riches embraced.  

Silver and gold, treasures amassed,  
Singers and music, pleasures that last.  

I grew great, more than all before,  
In wisdom I stood, but wanted more.  

What my eyes desired, I didn’t refrain,  
Joy in labor, my heart's domain.  

But all my works, they were vanity’s thread,  
Chasing the wind, no profit ahead.  

I turned to wisdom, madness, and folly,  
What’s new for kings? It’s all melancholy.  

Wisdom's light beats folly's dark,  
Yet both meet the same end, life's stark.  

"As fools go, so will I," I said in my soul,  
Wisdom’s just vanity, a never-ending toll.  

The wise and the fool, both forgotten in time,  
Death takes all, erasing the climb.  

Life turned bitter, work under the sun,  
Vanity chased, nothing won.  

I hated my toil, left to another,  
Who knows if he'll be wise or blunder?  

It's all in vain, labor for naught,  
Despair in my heart, at what I've wrought.  

Wise labor leaves for fools to gain,  
It's vanity, a great evil's claim.  

What’s left for man, from toil's race?  
Sorrows by day, hearts at night misplaced.  

Best to eat, drink, enjoy what you do,  
This I saw, it's from God, true.  

Who can enjoy more than me?  
God gives wisdom, joy, generously.  

To the sinner, work with no end,  
Passing to those who God befriends.  

It's all vanity, a wind-chasing game,  
Yet from God's hand, the joys came.
