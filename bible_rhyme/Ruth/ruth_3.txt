Naomi said, "My daughter dear, let me find you rest,  
With Boaz, our kin, who truly is best.  
Tonight, he'll be winnowing barley grain,  
Wash, anoint, and dress—go seek what you gain.  

Wait till he's eaten, drunk, and then lay low,  
Once he's asleep, to his feet you'll go.  
Uncover his feet, and then lay down,  
He'll tell you what to do when he turns around."  

Ruth replied, "All you say, I will do."  
She went down, and followed Naomi's cue.  
Boaz ate, drank, and his heart was light,  
Lay by the grain heap, ready for the night.  

Ruth approached soft, uncovered his feet,  
Lay down, midnight came—Boaz stirred from sleep.  
"Who are you?" he asked, startled, to see,  
"I'm Ruth, your servant; spread your garment over me.  

You're a kinsman," she spoke, bold and true,  
Boaz replied, "Blessed be you!  
You've shown kindness, not seeking the young,  
Whether rich or poor, your worth is sung.  

Fear not, my daughter, I’ll do all you ask,  
The city knows your worth, a noble task.  
Yet there's one closer, a kinsman near,  
If he redeems, let him; if not, I'm here.  

Stay till the dawn, and rest your head,  
In the morning, see what lies ahead."  
At her feet, Ruth lay till morning light,  
Rose up quietly, before the first sight.  

Boaz said, "Keep this discreet and neat,  
Bring your mantle, hold it tight and neat."  
He filled it with six measures of barley, a load,  
She carried it back to Naomi’s abode.  

Naomi asked, "How did it go, my dear?"  
Ruth told all, with barley to share.  
"He said, 'Don't go empty—take this gift,  
Until it’s settled, in him I place my trust, swift.'"  

"Wait, my daughter, this man's resolute,  
He'll not rest till the matter's absolute."
