In the days of judges, famine hit the land,  
A man from Bethlehem, with a plan in hand.  
Elimelech, his wife Naomi, and two sons,  
Left for Moab, where their new life begun.  

In Moab, sadness came, Elimelech passed,  
Naomi was left, her sons the only cast.  
Mahlon and Chilion married women of Moab,  
Orpah and Ruth, the lives they now had.  

But tragedy struck, the sons died too,  
Naomi left alone, not knowing what to do.  
She heard that Yahweh gave bread to her land,  
So she rose up, with daughters-in-law by hand.  

Naomi said, “Turn back, find rest at home,  
May Yahweh bless you, wherever you may roam.”  
They wept and said, “No, we will stay with you,”  
But Naomi insisted, “This is what you must do.”  

“Go back, my daughters, I have no more sons,  
I’m too old now, my time has come.  
Why wait for what’s not to be?  
Yahweh’s hand has turned against me.”  

Orpah wept, kissed, and finally turned back,  
But Ruth clung tight, her resolve intact.  
Naomi urged, “Follow like your sister, go,”  
But Ruth replied, “I won’t leave, no, no.”  

“Where you go, I will go, where you stay, I stay,  
Your people, my people, come what may.  
Your God, my God, in life and in death,  
Not even death will part our breath.”  

Naomi saw Ruth’s firm resolve,  
Stopped urging, let the problem solve.  
Together to Bethlehem they traveled on,  
The city buzzed, “Is this Naomi, come and gone?”  

“Don’t call me Naomi, call me Mara now,  
For the Almighty’s dealt bitterly, somehow.  
Full I left, empty I return,  
Yahweh’s testified, in His ways I learn.”  

So Naomi returned, with Ruth by her side,  
Back to Bethlehem, where harvests abide.  
At the start of barley season, they arrived,  
Hope and new beginnings were revived.
