# Story Time — 3 John Chapter 1

---

### Setting the Scene

The apostle John sits at his writing desk, quill in hand, pondering the paper before him. The air is warm, filled with the scent of parchment and ink. John’s heart is full, overflowing with love for a dear friend miles away. This friend, Gaius, has become a beacon of faith and love, someone whose life reflects the truth of the Gospel. John begins to write, pouring out his encouragement and wisdom with the tenderness of a spiritual father.

### Walking Through the Chapter

**"To the beloved Gaius,"** John’s letter begins. There is warmth in these words, a familiarity that speaks of shared journeys in the faith. Gaius is not just a friend; he’s an ally in the work of the Gospel, someone whose **soul prospers**. John’s wish is simple yet profound—may Gaius’s body be as healthy as his spirit.

Gaius, we learn, is renowned for his hospitality. In a world where traveling preachers rely on the kindness of strangers, Gaius opens his doors—both to those he knows and to those he doesn’t. He doesn’t hesitate, doesn’t double-take. His faithfulness to the truth shines brightly, witnessed by brethren who return to John with stories of his generosity.

As the letter unfolds, John’s joy is palpable. “I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth,” he writes. Could there be a greater testament to one’s faith than having their deeds speak so loudly?

### Slowing Down

But let’s pause a moment on this: **Gaius’s soul prospers**. What an image. In a world obsessed with material wealth and health, John flips the script. Here, spiritual wellness is the currency of the Kingdom. Gaius’s charity, his truth-walking, and his open heart—these are the true measures of prosperity. How fitting, then, that his spiritual vibrancy is a blessing that John wishes to see mirrored in his physical health.

### Weaving in Key Insights

John warns Gaius of a contrasting figure—Diotrephes. Ambition and pride have clouded Diotrephes’s judgment. He has become consumed by a desire for preeminence, refusing to receive John’s messengers and, by extension, the truth they bear. **Pride turns him inward, where love and service would turn one outward**. It’s a cautionary tale, showing how unchecked ambition can lead astray even those in positions of authority.

### Closing Reflection

What is this chapter really about? **Truth and love**—inseparable twins in the life of a believer. Gaius exemplifies them through his hospitality and faithfulness, while Diotrephes illustrates the perils of straying from them. John’s message is clear: true faith must be lived out in love and service, and ambition must be tempered with humility. Let us be like Gaius, rich in the love of God, and steadfastly support the truth in all we do.
