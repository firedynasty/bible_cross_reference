## Chapter 40: Comfort My People—The Voice in the Wilderness

This section begins "the latter part of the prophecy," consisting of many blessings compared to earlier burdens. It extends from "The voice of one crying in the wilderness" to "The new heavens and the new earth," forming "a prophetic summary of the New Testament."

**The Command to Comfort**

"Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God. Speak ye comfortably to Jerusalem." The charge is doubled because "sometimes the souls of God's people refuse to be comforted, and their comforters must repeat things again and again."

Three comforts declared: (1) "Her warfare is accomplished"—the set time of servitude ended; (2) "Her iniquity is pardoned"—"Nothing can be spoken more comfortably than this: thy sins be forgiven thee"; (3) "She hath received of the LORD's hand double for all her sins"—either double correction for purging or the infinite value of Christ's satisfaction.

**The Voice in the Wilderness**

"The voice of him that crieth in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the LORD." Applied primarily to John the Baptist, "his business was to prepare the way of the Lord, to dispose men's minds for the reception and entertainment of the gospel of Christ."

The preparation: "Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill shall be made low." Henry explains: "Those hindered from comfort in Christ by dejections and despondencies are the valleys that must be exalted. Those hindered by a proud conceit of their own merit are the mountains and hills that must be made low."

The result: "The glory of the LORD shall be revealed, and all flesh shall see it together." This points to Christ's revelation after John Baptist has preached repentance.

The contrast: "All flesh is grass, and all the goodliness thereof is as the flower of the field...but the word of our God shall stand for ever." Peter applies this to "the word which by the gospel is preached unto us."

**Behold Your God**

"O Zion, that bringest good tidings, get thee up into the high mountain...say unto the cities of Judah, Behold your God!"

God comes with power: "The Lord GOD will come with strong hand, and his arm shall rule for him: behold, his reward is with him."

God comes with tenderness: "He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom, and shall gently lead those that are with young." Henry explains: "The same that rules with the strong hand of a prince leads and feeds with the kind hand of a shepherd."

**The Incomparable God**

"Who hath measured the waters in the hollow of his hand, and meted out heaven with the span?" His power is unlimited. "Who hath directed the Spirit of the LORD, or being his counsellor hath taught him?" His wisdom is unsearchable: "He needs not any counsellor to direct him in the government of the world."

"The nations are as a drop of a bucket...less than nothing, and vanity."

**The Folly of Idolatry**

"To whom then will ye liken God? or what likeness will ye compare unto him?" By contrast: "It is he that sitteth upon the circle of the earth...that bringeth the princes to nothing."

"Lift up your eyes on high, and behold who hath created these things...he calleth them all by names." Creation testifies to the Creator.

**Strength for the Weary**

"Why sayest thou, O Jacob...My way is hid from the LORD?" Henry reproves this despair: "It is worse to turn evil thoughts into evil words."

The remedy: "Hast thou not known?...the everlasting God...fainteth not, neither is weary."

The promise: "He giveth power to the faint...Even the youths shall faint and be weary...But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint."

Henry concludes: "Those who trust to their own sufficiency shall utterly fail. But those that wait on the Lord shall have grace sufficient for them."

## Chapter 41: God's Challenge to the Idols and Comfort to Israel

This chapter offers "conviction of idolaters" and "consolation of all God's faithful worshippers."

**The Challenge to Nations**

"Keep silence before me, O islands; and let the people renew their strength...let us come near together to judgment."

"Who raised up the righteous man from the east?" Henry sees dual application: Abraham and Cyrus. Abraham "believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness." God "gave nations before him, and made him rule over kings." Cyrus likewise: God "will raise him up in righteousness" as "a type of Christ, who is righteousness itself."

The foolish response of idolaters: "They helped every one his neighbour." Instead of abandoning idols when God demonstrated his power, "men's convictions often exasperate their corruptions."

**Comfort for Israel**

"But thou, Israel, art my servant, Jacob whom I have chosen, the seed of Abraham my friend." Henry notes: "For the father's sake the people of Israel were beloved."

The great assurance: "Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness." Henry applies: "Art thou weak? I will strengthen thee. Art thou destitute of friends? I will help thee."

The enemies destroyed: "All they that were incensed against thee shall be ashamed and confounded...they shall be as nothing...they shall perish."

The worm exalted: "Fear not, thou worm Jacob...I will make thee a new sharp threshing instrument having teeth: thou shalt thresh the mountains." Henry explains: "God's people are sometimes as worms in their humble thoughts of themselves—worms, but not vipers, as their enemies are."

Provision in the wilderness: "When the poor and needy seek water, and there is none...I the LORD will hear them...I will open rivers in high places...That they may see, and know, and consider...that the hand of the LORD hath done this."

**Challenge to Idols**

"Produce your cause, saith the LORD; bring forth your strong reasons." The test: "Let them show us what shall happen...Show the things that are to come hereafter, that we may know that ye are gods." They fail both tests.

The verdict: "Behold, ye are of nothing, and your work of nought." The final judgment: "Behold, they are all vanity; their works are nothing; their molten images are wind and confusion." Henry concludes: "Therefore, dearly beloved, flee from idolatry."

---

## Chapter 42: The Messiah's Mission—God's Servant Song

Matthew 12:17-21 explicitly applies these verses to Christ. This presents the first of Isaiah's "Servant Songs."

**The Servant Introduced**

"Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth; I have put my spirit upon him: he shall bring forth judgment to the Gentiles."

Henry identifies five titles of the Messiah: (1) "He is my servant"—he took upon him the form of a servant as a Mediator; (2) "He is my elect"; (3) "Whom I uphold"; (4) "In whom my soul delighteth"; (5) "I have put my Spirit upon him"—"not by measure, as on others...but without measure."

His gentle method: "He shall not cry, nor lift up, nor cause his voice to be heard in the street. A bruised reed shall he not break, and the smoking flax shall he not quench." Henry applies: "Jesus Christ is very tender toward those that have true grace, though they are but weak in it."

His perseverance: "He shall not fail nor be discouraged, till he have set judgment in the earth: and the isles shall wait for his law."

**The Messiah's Commission**

"I the LORD have called thee in righteousness, and will hold thine hand, and will keep thee, and give thee for a covenant of the people, for a light of the Gentiles; to open the blind eyes, to bring out the prisoners from the prison."

Henry explains the "covenant of the people": "A mediator, or guarantee, of the covenant of grace, which is all summed up in him." Two blessings Christ brings: "light and liberty."

The confirmation: "I am the LORD: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another...Behold, the former things are come to pass, and new things do I declare."

**The New Song of Praise**

"Sing unto the LORD a new song, and his praise from the end of the earth." Henry notes the "newness": "Whereas songs of the Lord were confined to the temple at Jerusalem...now the songs of holy joy and praise shall be sung all the world over."

**God's Victory Over Opposition**

"The LORD shall go forth as a mighty man...I have long time holden my peace...now will I cry like a travailing woman; I will destroy and devour at once."

The promise to seekers: "I will bring the blind by a way that they knew not...I will make darkness light before them, and crooked things straight. These things will I do unto them, and not forsake them."

**The Blindness of Israel**

"Hear, ye deaf; and look, ye blind, that ye may see. Who is blind, but my servant? or deaf, as my messenger that I sent?"

Henry laments: "Blindness and deafness in spiritual things are worse in those that profess themselves to be God's servants and messengers than in others."

Yet God is honored: "The Lord is well pleased for his righteousness' sake; he will magnify the law, and make it honourable." If men will not magnify it by obedience, "God will magnify it himself by punishing them for their disobedience."

The consequences: "This is a people robbed and spoiled; they are all of them snared in holes." Henry warns: "Those who are not humbled by less judgments must expect greater."

---

## Chapter 43: Fear Not—God's Redemption of Israel

Despite Israel's stubbornness in chapter 42, the next words declare: "But now, fear not, O Jacob!" This demonstrates that "God's goodness takes occasion from man's badness to appear so much the more illustrious."

**God's Precious People**

"Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine." Four grounds of assurance: (1) They are God's workmanship; (2) They are the people of his purchase; (3) They are called by name; (4) He is their God in covenant.

The great promise: "When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee: when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned." This was "fulfilled in the letter in the wonderful preservation of the three children."

God's love demonstrated: "Since thou wast precious in my sight, thou hast been honourable, and I have loved thee: therefore will I give men for thee, and people for thy life." Henry applies: "If this was reckoned so great a thing, to give Egypt for their ransom, what reason have we to admire God's love to us in giving his own Son!"

The gathering: "Fear not: for I am with thee: I will bring thy seed from the east, and gather thee from the west...bring my sons from far, and my daughters from the ends of the earth."

**God's Witnesses**

"Ye are my witnesses, saith the LORD, and my servant whom I have chosen: that ye may know and believe me, and understand that I am he: before me there was no God formed, neither shall there be after me. I, even I, am the LORD; and beside me there is no saviour."

Henry emphasizes: "See what it is that the great God glories in, not so much that he is the only ruler as that he is the only Saviour."

The declaration: "Before the day was I am he; and there is none that can deliver out of my hand: I will work, and who shall let it?"

**New Things Greater Than Old**

"Thus saith the LORD, which maketh a way in the sea...Remember ye not the former things, neither consider the things of old. Behold, I will do a new thing; now it shall spring forth."

Henry explains: "Though former mercies must not be forgotten, fresh mercies must in a special manner be improved." God promises: "I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert."

The purpose: "This people have I formed for myself; they shall show forth my praise." Henry applies: "The church is of God's forming...It is therefore our duty to show forth his praise."

**Israel's Sin and God's Grace**

"But thou hast not called upon me, O Jacob; but thou hast been weary of me, O Israel." Their sin: neglecting prayer and grudging worship costs.

The aggravations: "Thy first father hath sinned, and thy teachers have transgressed against me."

Yet the stunning gospel: "I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins." Henry marvels at this placement: "Now one would think it would follow: 'I, even I, am he that will destroy thee.' No, 'I, even I, am he that will forgive thee.' When God forgives he forgets."

---

## Chapter 44: The Promise of the Spirit and the Folly of Idols

**God's Blessings on His People**

"Yet now hear, O Jacob my servant; and Israel, whom I have chosen...Fear not, O Jacob, my servant; and thou, Jeshurun, whom I have chosen."

Israel is called "Jeshurun—the upright one." Despite their sins, "mercy steps in with a non-obstante—notwithstanding, to all these quarrels."

The great promise: "I will pour water upon him that is thirsty, and floods upon the dry ground: I will pour my spirit upon thy seed, and my blessing upon thine offspring." Henry explains: "The water God will pour out is his Spirit. This is the great New-Testament promise, that God, having sent his servant Christ, will send his Spirit to uphold us."

The result: "They shall spring up as among the grass, as willows by the water courses. One shall say, I am the LORD's; and another shall call himself by the name of Jacob."

God's unique claim: "I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God."

**The Absurdity of Idol-Making**

Henry exposes the foolishness in detail. The smith "works with the strength of his arms: yea, he is hungry, and his strength faileth." The carpenter "marketh it out with a line...and maketh it after the figure of a man."

The supreme irony: The same tree serves for fuel and for a god. "He burneth part thereof in the fire; with part thereof he eateth flesh...and the residue thereof he maketh a god, even his graven image: he falleth down unto it...and saith, Deliver me; for thou art my god."

The verdict: "None considereth in his heart...I have burned part of it in the fire...and shall I make the residue thereof an abomination?" Henry diagnoses: "A deceived heart hath turned him aside, that he cannot deliver his soul."

**The Call to Return and the Promise of Redemption**

"Remember these, O Jacob and Israel; for thou art my servant...Return unto me; for I have redeemed thee."

The pardon: "I have blotted out, as a thick cloud, thy transgressions, and, as a cloud, thy sins." Henry explains: "When God pardons sin he blots out this cloud...by the influence of the Sun of righteousness."

Universal praise: "Sing, O ye heavens; for the LORD hath done it: shout, ye lower parts of the earth...for the LORD hath redeemed Jacob."

God's power affirmed: "I am the LORD that maketh all things; that stretcheth forth the heavens alone; that spreadeth abroad the earth by myself."

The naming of Cyrus: "That saith of Cyrus, He is my shepherd, and shall perform all my pleasure: even saying to Jerusalem, Thou shalt be built; and to the temple, Thy foundation shall be laid." Henry marvels: "It was more the praise of Cyrus to be God's shepherd than to be emperor of Persia."

## Chapter 45: Cyrus God's Anointed—Salvation to All Nations

This chapter expands on Cyrus as "a type of the great Redeemer," foretold 210 years before it came to pass.

**God's Commission to Cyrus**

"Thus saith the LORD to his anointed, to Cyrus, whose right hand I have holden, to subdue nations before him...I will go before thee, and make the crooked places straight: I will break in pieces the gates of brass."

Henry explains: "Cyrus is here called God's anointed because he was designed and qualified for this great service by the counsel of God, and was to be herein a type of the Messiah."

The treasures: "I will give thee the treasures of darkness, and hidden riches of secret places, that thou mayest know that I, the LORD, which call thee by thy name, am the God of Israel."

The reason: "For Jacob my servant's sake, and Israel mine elect, I have even called thee by thy name...though thou hast not known me." Henry notes: "In all the revolutions of states and kingdoms...God is designing the good of his church."

**God Alone is Lord**

"I am the LORD, and there is none else...I form the light, and create darkness: I make peace, and create evil: I the LORD do all these things."

**The Potter and the Clay**

"Woe unto him that striveth with his Maker! Let the potsherd strive with the potsherds of the earth. Shall the clay say to him that fashioneth it, What makest thou?"

Henry applies: "The doctrine of God's sovereignty has enough in it to silence all our discontents and objections against the methods of his providence and grace."

**Cyrus's Mission Confirmed**

God invites Israel: "Ask me of things to come concerning my sons, and concerning the work of my hands command ye me." Praying rather than striving.

Cyrus's work: "I have raised him up in righteousness, and I will direct all his ways: he shall build my city, and he shall let go my captives, not for price nor reward."

The hidden God: "Verily thou art a God that hidest thyself, O God of Israel, the Saviour." Henry explains: "Though God hide himself, we are sure he is the God of Israel, the Saviour."

**The Universal Gospel Invitation**

"Look unto me, and be ye saved, all the ends of the earth: for I am God, and there is none else."

The oath: "I have sworn by myself, the word is gone out of my mouth in righteousness...That unto me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear." Henry notes this is "applied to the dominion of our Lord Jesus."

The confession: "Surely, shall one say, in the LORD have I righteousness and strength...In the LORD shall all the seed of Israel be justified, and shall glory."

Henry concludes: "In the Lord Jesus we have righteousness to recommend us to the good-will of God towards us, and strength to begin and carry on the good work of God in us."

# Chapters 46-53: The Incomparable God, Babylon's Fall, and the Suffering Servant

## Chapter 46: Bel Bows Down - The Incomparable God

This chapter contrasts Babylon's helpless idols with God who carries his people, designed to cure Israel of idolatry and give confidence in God.

**The Fall of Babylon's Gods (vv. 1-2)**: Bel and Nebo, Babylon's chief deities, must be carried. When Cyrus takes Babylon, the idols collapse. Far from delivering their worshippers, "they could not deliver the burden, but themselves are gone into captivity."

**The God Who Carries His People (vv. 3-4)**: "I am he; and even to your old age I am he; and even to hoar hairs will I carry you." The contrast is striking: idols must be carried; God carries his people. This promise spans all of life: "I have made, and I will bear; even I will carry, and will deliver you."

**The Absurdity of Making Gods (vv. 5-8)**: People lavish gold to hire a goldsmith to make gods, then carry them on their shoulders and set them in place where they cannot move. Yet "one shall cry unto him, yet can he not answer, nor save him out of his trouble." The appeal: "Remember this, and show yourselves men... O ye transgressors."

**God's Unique Sovereignty (vv. 9-11)**: "I am God, and there is none else... Declaring the end from the beginning... saying, My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure." Cyrus, identified by his eagle standard, "executeth my counsel from a far country." God assures: "I have spoken it, I will also bring it to pass."

**Salvation Near (vv. 12-13)**: "I bring near my righteousness... and my salvation shall not tarry: and I will place salvation in Zion for Israel my glory." The gospel takes rise in Zion; "thither the Redeemer comes."

---

## Chapter 47: The Fall of Babylon - Virgin Daughter in the Dust

This prophecy encouraged captive Israel and foreshadows the downfall of Babylon in Revelation, the great enemy of the New Testament church.

**Babylon Humiliated (vv. 1-5)**: "Come down, and sit in the dust, O virgin daughter of Babylon... for thou shalt no more be called tender and delicate. Take the millstones, and grind meal." The proud metropolis is reduced to slave labor. God declares: "I will take vengeance, and I will not meet thee as a man." Yet the redeemed respond: "As for our redeemer, the LORD of hosts is his name, the Holy One of Israel."

**Babylon's Sin: Cruelty to God's People (v. 6)**: "Thou didst show them no mercy; upon the ancient hast thou very heavily laid thy yoke." God used Babylon for correction, but "the Babylonians carried the matter too far," showing no mercy and specially abusing the elders.

**Babylon's Sin: Pride and Security (vv. 7-11)**: Three arrogant claims: (1) "I shall be a lady for ever"; (2) "I shall not sit as a widow, neither shall I know the loss of children"; (3) "No one sees me"—the delusion of secret sin. The cause: "Thou hast trusted in thy wickedness... Thy wisdom and thy knowledge, it hath perverted thee." The punishment comes suddenly: "These two things shall come to thee in a moment in one day, the loss of children, and widowhood."

**Babylon's Sin: Sorcery (vv. 12-15)**: "Stand now with thine enchantments, and with the multitude of thy sorceries." The challenge to astrologers: "Let now the astrologers, the stargazers, stand up, and save thee." The verdict: "They shall be as stubble; the fire shall burn them." This was literally fulfilled when "all his astrologers, soothsayers, and wise men, were quite nonplussed with the handwriting on the wall." The merchants abandon Babylon: "None shall save thee."

---

## Chapter 48: God's Rebuke and Gracious Deliverance

God shows He "hates sin wherever he finds it" in His own people, yet also extends mercy despite their sins.

**Israel's Hypocritical Profession (vv. 1-8)**: They are "called by the name of Israel... swear by the name of the LORD... but not in truth, nor in righteousness." Their credentials are impressive yet "their hearts were not true nor right in these professions." God prophesied "lest thou shouldest say, Mine idol hath done them." Their corruption runs deep: "Thou wast called a transgressor from the womb."

**For God's Name's Sake (vv. 9-11)**: "For my name's sake will I defer mine anger... for how should my name be polluted?" God refined them, though "not with silver" as thoroughly as a refiner works metal, "for they are all dross." Rather: "I have chosen thee in the furnace of affliction."

**God's Power to Deliver (vv. 12-15)**: "I am he; I am the first, I also am the last." Cyrus is commissioned: "The LORD hath loved him: he will do his pleasure on Babylon." God declares: "I have called him... he shall make his way prosperous."

**The Prophet's Commission (v. 16)**: "Now the Lord GOD, and his Spirit, hath sent me." The Spirit is spoken of as a person distinct from the Father and Son, with divine authority.

**God's Gracious Teaching (vv. 17-19)**: "I am the LORD thy God which teacheth thee to profit, which leadeth thee by the way that thou shouldest go." God laments: "O that thou hadst hearkened to my commandments! then had thy peace been as a river, and thy righteousness as the waves of the sea."

**The Call to Depart (vv. 20-21)**: "Go ye forth of Babylon, flee ye from the Chaldeans... The LORD hath redeemed his servant Jacob." The promise of provision: "They thirsted not when he led them through the deserts: he caused the waters to flow out of the rock"—applicable to treasures of grace in Jesus Christ, for "that rock is Christ."

**The Solemn Warning (v. 22)**: "There is no peace, saith the LORD, unto the wicked." Though God's thoughts concerning His people are thoughts of peace, those who are wicked "will have no peace." The quarrel sinners wage with God, if not ended by repentance, "will be an everlasting quarrel."

---

## Chapter 49: The Servant's Mission to the Gentiles

This chapter shows full accomplishment "in a redemption that should as far outdo these expressions... even the redemption of the world by Jesus Christ."

**The Servant's Commission (vv. 1-6)**: "Listen, O isles, unto me... The LORD hath called me from the womb." Christ's qualifications: "He hath made my mouth like a sharp sword"—the two-edged sword of God's word. He is "a polished shaft" in God's quiver, "hidden in the shadow of his hand" until the proper time. His office: "Thou art my servant, O Israel, in whom I will be glorified."

**The Servant's Lament and Comfort (vv. 4-5)**: "I have laboured in vain, I have spent my strength for nought." This points to the obstinacy of Jews who rejected Christ's preaching. Yet the comfort: "My judgment is with the LORD, and my work with my God... Though Israel be not gathered, yet shall I be glorious in the eyes of the LORD."

**The Light to the Gentiles (v. 6)**: "I will also give thee for a light to the Gentiles, that thou mayest be my salvation unto the end of the earth." Paul applies this: "Therefore, says he, we turn to the Gentiles." This is where the Redeemer was "truly glorious."

**The Despised Servant Exalted (vv. 7-12)**: "To him whom man despiseth, to him whom the nation abhorreth... Kings shall see and arise, princes also shall worship." Christ was "despised and rejected of men... crucified as a slave," yet "kings received his gospel, and submitted to his yoke." The acceptable time: "In an acceptable time have I heard thee... Behold, now is the day of salvation." The prisoners freed: "That thou mayest say to the prisoners, Go forth; to them that are in darkness, Show yourselves."

**Zion's Comfort (vv. 13-17)**: "Sing, O heavens... But Zion said, The LORD hath forsaken me." God's response: "Can a woman forget her sucking child... yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee. Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands."

**The Gentile Ingathering (vv. 18-23)**: "Lift up thine eyes round about, and behold: all these gather themselves together." The surprising growth: "Then shalt thou say in thine heart, Who hath begotten me these, seeing I have lost my children?" The church, after losing the Jews, "shall have abundance of children still." Royal protectors: "Kings shall be thy nursing fathers, and their queens thy nursing mothers."

**Deliverance from the Mighty (vv. 24-26)**: "Shall the prey be taken from the mighty, or the lawful captive delivered?" This objection is overruled: "Even the captives of the mighty shall be taken away... for I will contend with him that contendeth with thee."

---

## Chapter 50: The Obedient Servant - Facing Shame and Spitting

This chapter shows the Jews' ruin is self-inflicted, not due to God's inability or disobedience.

**Israel's Self-Inflicted Ruin (vv. 1-3)**: "Where is the bill of your mother's divorcement, whom I have put away?... Behold, for your iniquities have ye sold yourselves." God did not divorce arbitrarily. "You sold yourselves to work wickedness, and therefore God justly sold you into the hands of your enemies." Why no deliverance? "When I came, was there no man? when I called, was there none to answer?" God came through prophets, but "there was no man that had any regard to the warnings." The same happened with Christ. God's power is not diminished: "Is my hand shortened at all, that it cannot redeem?"

**The Servant's Equipment (vv. 4-5)**: "The Lord GOD hath given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary." The best learning of a minister is "to know how to comfort troubled consciences." Christ received "the tongue of the learned" and "the ear of the learned." "He wakeneth morning by morning... for continual fresh supplies of divine grace."

**The Servant's Suffering (vv. 5-6)**: "I was not rebellious, neither turned away back. I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not my face from shame and spitting." Christ submitted to scourging, buffeting, and spitting, "voluntarily, to convince us of his willingness to save us."

**The Servant's Confidence (vv. 7-9)**: "For the Lord GOD will help me; therefore shall I not be confounded: therefore have I set my face like a flint." Christ challenges all opposers: "Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect?" His enemies "shall wax old as a garment; the moth shall eat them up."

**Comfort and Warning (vv. 10-11)**: For despondent believers: "Let him trust in the name of the LORD, and stay upon his God." For presumptuous sinners: "Walk in the light of your fire... ye shall lie down in sorrow." A godly man's way may be melancholy, but his end is peace. A wicked man's way may be pleasant, but his end is utter darkness.

---

## Chapter 51: Awake, O Arm of the Lord - Comfort to Zion

This chapter comforts those who fear God and keep His commandments, "even when they walk in darkness and have no light."

**Look to Abraham (vv. 1-3)**: "Hearken to me, ye that follow after righteousness... look unto the rock whence ye are hewn... Look unto Abraham your father... for I called him alone, and blessed him, and increased him." Let this encourage you "to depend upon the promise of God even when a sentence of death seems to be upon all the means" to fulfill it. The promise: "The LORD shall comfort Zion... he will make her wilderness like Eden... joy and gladness shall be found therein."

**Everlasting Righteousness (vv. 4-8)**: "My righteousness is near; my salvation is gone forth... the heavens shall vanish away like smoke... but my salvation shall be for ever." Heaven and earth will pass away, "but the word of the Lord endures for ever." Therefore: "Fear ye not the reproach of men... the moth shall eat them up like a garment." If we have God's approbation, we may despise the censure of dying men.

**Awake, O Arm of the Lord (vv. 9-11)**: "Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the LORD; awake, as in the ancient days... Art thou not it which hath dried the sea... that hath made the depths of the sea a way for the ransomed to pass over?" The prayer: "Do for us now as thou didst for our fathers formerly." The promise: "The redeemed of the LORD shall return, and come with singing unto Zion; and everlasting joy shall be upon their head."

**God's Comfort to the Fearful (vv. 12-16)**: "I, even I, am he that comforteth you: who art thou, that thou shouldest be afraid of a man that shall die... And forgettest the LORD thy maker?" The absurdity: "Afraid of a man that shall die?" The impiety: "Our inordinate fear of man is a tacit forgetfulness of God." "Where is the fury of the oppressor? It is gone in an instant." God encourages: "I am the LORD thy God, that divided the sea... I have put my words in thy mouth, and I have covered thee in the shadow of mine hand."

**Jerusalem's Cup of Trembling (vv. 17-23)**: "Awake, awake, stand up, O Jerusalem, which hast drunk at the hand of the LORD the cup of his fury." Her distress: "Of all the sons whom she hath brought forth... there is none to guide her." The promise: "I have taken out of thine hand the cup of trembling... thou shalt no more drink it again: But I will put it into the hand of them that afflict thee." The oppressors' sin: "They have said to thy soul, Bow down, that we may go over."

## Chapter 52: Awake, O Zion - Beautiful Feet on the Mountains

This chapter speaks of deliverance from Babylon as a type of gospel redemption, with verses 13-15 introducing the Suffering Servant prophecy continued in chapter 53.

**Zion Called to Awake (vv. 1-6)**: "Awake, awake; put on thy strength, O Zion; put on thy beautiful garments, O Jerusalem." God calls: "Shake thyself from the dust; arise... loose thyself from the bands of thy neck." This applies to those "weary and heavily laden under the burden of sin" who find relief in Christ. The promise: "There shall no more come into thee the uncircumcised and the unclean." God explains: "Ye have sold yourselves for nought; and ye shall be redeemed without money." God's jealousy for His name: "My people is taken away for nought... and my name continually every day is blasphemed." Therefore: "My people shall know my name... that I am he that doth speak: behold, it is I."

**Beautiful Feet of Gospel Messengers (vv. 7-10)**: "How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace... that saith unto Zion, Thy God reigneth!" This applies to Christ and gospel ministers: "How beautiful are his feet! his feet that were nailed to the cross, how beautiful upon Mount Calvary!" The watchmen rejoice: "Thy watchmen shall lift up the voice; with the voice together shall they sing: for they shall see eye to eye." This was fulfilled "when the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us." Universal salvation: "The LORD hath made bare his holy arm in the eyes of all the nations; and all the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God."

**The Call to Depart (vv. 11-12)**: "Depart ye, depart ye, go ye out from thence, touch no unclean thing... be ye clean, that bear the vessels of the LORD." The manner: "Ye shall not go out with haste, nor go by flight: for the LORD will go before you; and the God of Israel will be your rereward."

**The Servant Introduced (vv. 13-15)**: "Behold, my servant shall deal prudently, he shall be exalted and extolled, and be very high." His humiliation: "His visage was so marred more than any man, and his form more than the sons of men." His face "was foul with weeping... he that really was fairer than the children of men had his face spoiled." His exaltation: "So shall he sprinkle many nations; the kings shall shut their mouths at him: for that which had not been told them shall they see." The gospel "brings to light things new and unheard of"—what "had long been kept secret is now revealed."

## Chapter 53: The Suffering Servant - Wounded for Our Transgressions

This chapter is "so replenished with the unsearchable riches of Christ that it may be called rather the gospel of the evangelist Isaiah than the prophecy of the prophet Isaiah," presenting "the sufferings of Christ and the glory that should follow."

**The Report Not Believed (vv. 1-3)**: "Who hath believed our report? and to whom is the arm of the LORD revealed?" Of many who hear the gospel, "few, very few, that believe it," because "the arm of the Lord is not revealed to them." Christ's humble appearance: "He shall grow up before him as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness," from "a mean and despicable family" when the royal house had "sunk." "He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief."

**He Bore Our Sins (vv. 4-6)**: "Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted. But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed." Our sins "were the thorns in his head, the nails in his hands and feet, the spear in his side." The universal confession: "All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."

**The Silent Lamb (vv. 7-9)**: "He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb." His innocence: "He had done no violence, neither was any deceit in his mouth." His grave: "He made his grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death"—crucified between thieves, yet buried in Joseph's tomb.

# Isaiah Chapters 54–61: Commentary by Matthew Henry

## Chapter 54: The Church's Future Glory

Following chapter 53, "The death of Christ is the life of the church," promising the church's flourishing as Christ's exaltation and reward for his humiliation.

**The Church's Expansion (vv. 1–5)**: "Sing, O barren, thou that didst not bear; for more are the children of the desolate than the children of the married wife." Paul applies this to the New Testament church. The Gentile church, long barren, shall exceed the Jewish church. "Enlarge the place of thy tent... for thou shalt break forth on the right hand and on the left." The gospel spread in all directions. The reproach of Christianity in its youth—that no rulers or princes embraced it—shall be forgotten. "Thy Maker is thine husband; the LORD of hosts is his name; and thy Redeemer the Holy One of Israel."

**God's Everlasting Kindness (vv. 6–10)**: "For a small moment have I forsaken thee; but with great mercies will I gather thee. In a little wrath I hid my face from thee for a moment; but with everlasting kindness will I have mercy on thee." The wrath is little and momentary; the kindness is great and everlasting. This "covenant of peace" is firmer than creation itself: "The mountains shall depart, and the hills be removed; but my kindness shall not depart from thee."

**The Church's Future Beauty (vv. 11–17)**: "O thou afflicted, tossed with tempest, and not comforted, behold, I will lay thy stones with fair colours, and lay thy foundations with sapphires... And all thy children shall be taught of the LORD; and great shall be the peace of thy children." Security is promised: "In righteousness shalt thou be established... No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper; and every tongue that shall rise against thee in judgment thou shalt condemn." "I have created the smith that bloweth the coals... and I have created the waster to destroy." Even enemies are under God's control. "This is the heritage of the servants of the LORD, and their righteousness is of me, saith the LORD."

## Chapter 55: The Gospel Invitation

This chapter presents "the covenant of grace made with us in Christ," with "the sure mercies of David" applied by Paul to "the benefits which flow to us from the resurrection of Christ."

**The Invitation (vv. 1–5)**: "Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price." Qualification requires thirst. Those satisfied with worldly enjoyments do not thirst. "Come to Christ; for he is the fountain opened; he is the rock smitten." We receive "wine and milk"—abundantly refreshing provision. "Buy without money... The gifts offered us are invaluable and such as no price can be set upon... The things offered are already bought and paid for. Christ purchased them at the full value... with his own blood." Warning: "Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread?" The things of this world "are not bread, not proper food for a soul." The promise: "Hear, and your soul shall live; and I will make an everlasting covenant with you, even the sure mercies of David." "Covenant-mercies are all his mercies; they are purchased by him; they are promised in him; they are treasured up in his hand."

**Seek the Lord (vv. 6–9)**: "Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near: Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon." Repentance requires not only a change of way but a change of mind. "For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, saith the LORD. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways." God meets returning sinners with pardoning mercy; he forgives freely.

**The Effectual Word (vv. 10–13)**: "So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please." The promises of God shall all have their full accomplishment in due time. The joyful return: "Ye shall go out with joy, and be led forth with peace: the mountains and the hills shall break forth before you into singing... Instead of the thorn shall come up the fir tree, and instead of the brier shall come up the myrtle tree." Gospel grace liberates those in bondage to sin and Satan. Those as thorns and briers, good only for fire, shall become graceful and useful as the fir tree and myrtle tree.

## Chapter 56: Strangers and Eunuchs Welcome—A House of Prayer

**The Call to Righteousness (vv. 1–2)**: "Keep ye judgment, and do justice: for my salvation is near to come." The fuller assurances God gives of performing his promises, the stronger obligations he lays for obedience. "Blessed is the man that doeth this... that keepeth the sabbath from polluting it." Sabbath-sanctification represents all duties of worship, as justice and judgment represent all duties to others.

**Comfort to Outcasts (vv. 3–8)**: "Neither let the son of the stranger, that hath joined himself to the LORD, speak, saying, The LORD hath utterly separated me from his people." To the eunuch who says "I am a dry tree," God promises: "Unto them will I give in mine house and within my walls a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters: I will give them an everlasting name, that shall not be cut off." Conditions: (1) "Keep my sabbaths"; (2) "Choose the things that please me"; (3) "Take hold of my covenant"—an entire and resolute consent. Blessings: (1) "I will bring them to my holy mountain"—assistance; (2) "Their burnt offerings and their sacrifices shall be accepted"—acceptance; (3) "I will make them joyful in my house of prayer"—comfort. "Mine house shall be called a house of prayer for all people." Jesus quoted this (Matthew 21:13). "The Lord GOD which gathereth the outcasts of Israel saith, Yet will I gather others to him." There shall be "one fold and one Shepherd."

**Indictment of Unfaithful Watchmen (vv. 9–12)**: "All ye beasts of the field, come to devour... His watchmen are blind: they are all ignorant, they are all dumb dogs, they cannot bark." Failures catalogued: (1) Wretchedly ignorant of their business; (2) Dumb dogs that would not warn or reprove; (3) Lazy, loving to slumber; (4) Greedy dogs never satisfied, each looking to his own way; (5) Epicures: "Come, I will fetch wine, and we will fill ourselves with strong drink"; (6) Secure: "To-morrow shall be as this day."

## Chapter 57: The Death of the Righteous and Divine Comfort

This chapter contrasts the peaceful death of the righteous, the wickedness of idolaters, and God's gracious condescension to the humble.

**The Righteous Taken Away (vv. 1–2)**: "The righteous perisheth, and no man layeth it to heart: and merciful men are taken away, none considering that the righteous is taken away from the evil to come." The death of good men should be considered more than common deaths, yet the wicked world takes no notice. Comfort: "He shall enter into peace: they shall rest in their beds, each one walking in his uprightness." The grave is a bed of rest to all the Lord's people; there they rest from all their labours.

**The Wicked Charged (vv. 3–12)**: "Draw near hither, ye sons of the sorceress... Against whom do ye sport yourselves?... are ye not children of transgression?" Sins exposed: (1) Scorning God's prophets; (2) Idolatry under every green tree; (3) Child sacrifice in valleys; (4) Worshipping stones. "Should I receive comfort in these?" "Thou art wearied in the greatness of thy way; yet saidst thou not, There is no hope." Prosperity in sin is a great bar to conversion.

**The High and Lofty One (vv. 13–16)**: "When thou criest, let thy companies deliver thee; but the wind shall carry them all away... but he that putteth his trust in me shall possess the land." "For thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones." He who dwells in the highest heavens dwells in the lowest hearts. "For I will not contend for ever, neither will I be always wroth: for the spirit should fail before me, and the souls which I have made."

**God's Healing of the Backslider (vv. 17–19)**: "For the iniquity of his covetousness was I wroth, and smote him: I hid me, and was wroth, and he went on frowardly in the way of his heart." One would expect destruction, but divine mercy follows: "I have seen his ways and will heal him." "I create the fruit of the lips; Peace, peace to him that is far off, and to him that is near, saith the LORD; and I will heal him." Paul applies this: "He came and preached peace to you who were afar off."

**No Peace for the Wicked (vv. 20–21)**: "But the wicked are like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt. There is no peace, saith my God, to the wicked." Two causes: (1) Unmortified corruptions making them restless; (2) Unpacified consciences under frightful apprehension of guilt and wrath.

## Chapter 58: True Fasting and Sabbath-Keeping

This chapter challenges religious hypocrisy and teaches the true nature of acceptable worship.

**The Prophet's Commission (vv. 1–2)**: "Cry aloud, spare not, lift up thy voice like a trumpet, and show my people their transgression." Though they "seek me daily, and delight to know my ways, as a nation that did righteousness," men may go far toward heaven yet come short; nay, may go to hell with a good reputation.

**False Fasting Exposed (vv. 3–5)**: "Wherefore have we fasted, say they, and thou seest not?" They went in the way of Cain, angry that offerings were not accepted. "In the day of your fast ye find pleasure, and exact all your labours... ye fast for strife and debate." External humiliation was worthless: "Is it such a fast that I have chosen? a day for a man to afflict his soul? is it to bow down his head as a bulrush, and to spread sackcloth and ashes under him?" The shows of religion will not be accepted without the substance.

**True Fasting Described (vv. 6–7)**: "Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke?" Acts of charity required: "Is it not to deal thy bread to the hungry, and that thou bring the poor that are cast out to thy house? when thou seest the naked, that thou cover him; and that thou hide not thyself from thine own flesh?"

**The Promises to True Worshippers (vv. 8–12)**: Seven blessings follow: (1) Light after affliction: "Then shall thy light break forth as the morning"; (2) Healing: "Thine health shall spring forth speedily"; (3) Protection: "Thy righteousness shall go before thee; the glory of the LORD shall be thy rereward"; (4) Answered prayer: "Then shalt thou call, and the LORD shall answer; thou shalt cry, and he shall say, Here I am"; (5) Divine guidance: "The LORD shall guide thee continually"; (6) Satisfaction: "And satisfy thy soul in drought, and make fat thy bones: and thou shalt be like a watered garden"; (7) Lasting influence: "Thou shalt be called, The repairer of the breach, The restorer of paths to dwell in."

**True Sabbath-Keeping (vv. 13–14)**: "If thou turn away thy foot from the sabbath, from doing thy pleasure on my holy day; and call the sabbath a delight, the holy of the LORD, honourable..." Avoid "doing thine own ways, nor finding thine own pleasure, nor speaking thine own words." Call it a delight, not a task and burden; we must be in our element when worshipping God. Even in Old Testament times the sabbath was called the Lord's day. Reward: "Then shalt thou delight thyself in the LORD; and I will cause thee to ride upon the high places of the earth, and feed thee with the heritage of Jacob thy father." The more pleasure we take in serving God, the more pleasure we shall find in it.

## Chapter 59: Sin Separates—The Redeemer Comes to Zion

This chapter shows "sin appearing exceedingly sinful, and grace appearing exceedingly gracious." Paul quotes verses 7–8 on universal corruption and verse 20 on Christ.

**Sin Separates from God (vv. 1–2)**: "Behold, the LORD's hand is not shortened, that it cannot save; neither his ear heavy, that it cannot hear: But your iniquities have separated between you and your God." If prayers are unanswered and salvation delayed, it is not because God is weary of hearing but because we are weary of praying.

**The Catalog of Sins (vv. 3–8)**: "Your hands are defiled with blood... your lips have spoken lies... They hatch cockatrice' eggs, and weave the spider's web... Their feet run to evil, and they make haste to shed innocent blood." Their webs shall not become garments; they cannot cover themselves with their works. Projects may hurt others but can never serve themselves. "The way of peace they know not; and there is no judgment in their goings."

**The Confession (vv. 9–15)**: "Therefore is judgment far from us... We grope for the wall like the blind... We roar all like bears, and mourn sore like doves." "Our transgressions are multiplied before thee, and our sins testify against us... In transgressing and lying against the LORD, and departing away from our God." "Truth is fallen in the street, and equity cannot enter... he that departeth from evil maketh himself a prey."

**God's Intervention (vv. 16–19)**: "He saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no intercessor: therefore his arm brought salvation unto him." God's armor: "He put on righteousness as a breastplate, and a helmet of salvation upon his head; and he put on the garments of vengeance for clothing." This allusion appears in Christian armour: "the breast-plate of righteousness." "When the enemy shall come in like a flood, the Spirit of the LORD shall lift up a standard against him." When God's people are weak and helpless, God gives a banner to those that fear him.

**The Redeemer and Covenant (vv. 20–21)**: "And the Redeemer shall come to Zion, and unto them that turn from transgression in Jacob." "My spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of thy seed... from henceforth and for ever." The Spirit and the word go together, keeping the church. On these foundations the church stands firmly and shall stand forever.

## Chapter 60: Arise, Shine—The Glory and Enlargement of the Church

This chapter describes God's covenant with his church, promising "the large extent of the church, even unto the utmost regions of the earth," looking beyond the Jews' return from captivity to "the kingdom of the Messiah."

**The Church Enlightened (vv. 1–2)**: "Arise, shine; for thy light is come." The call involves both receiving and reflecting light. Children of light ought to shine as lights in the world. While darkness covers the earth, the church, like Goshen, shall have light. The light is Christ himself: "When Christ arose as the sun of righteousness, and in him the day-spring from on high visited us, then the glory of the Lord was seen upon us."

**Gentiles Come to the Light (vv. 3–8)**: Fulfillment comes through "the bringing of the Gentiles into the gospel church." The purity and love of primitive Christians, their heavenly-mindedness, contempt of the world, and patient sufferings were the brightness drawing many. The beauty of holiness was the powerful attractive. Converts fly "as a cloud, and as the doves to their windows"—they fly to Christ, to the church, to word and ordinances, as doves by instinct to their own home.

**The Isles Wait for God (vv. 9–14)**: "The ships of Tarshish, transport-ships, shall lie ready to carry members from far distant regions to the church, or to carry the ministers of the church to remote parts." When we give ourselves to God, we must with ourselves give all we have to him. On open gates: "Christ is always ready to entertain those that come to him, is never out of the way, nor can they ever come unseasonably; the gate of mercy is always open, night and day." Nations not serving the church will perish—those who will not by faith submit to Jesus Christ the King of the church shall perish eternally.

**From Forsaken to Eternal Excellency (vv. 15–18)**: "Jerusalem had been forsaken and hated... But now it shall be made an eternal excellency, being reformed from idolatry and having recovered the tokens of God's favour." Improved conditions: "When we had baptism in the room of circumcision, the Lord's supper in the room of the passover, and a gospel ministry in the room of a Levitical priesthood, we had gold instead of brass." Officers shall be justices of peace, not instruments of trouble.

**Everlasting Light (vv. 19–22)**: Heavenly fulfillment: "In heaven there shall be no occasion for sun or moon, for it is the inheritance of the saints in light." Comforts and joys in heaven shall never know cessation or interruption when "the Lord shall himself be thy everlasting light." On all being righteous: There are no people on earth that are all righteous; mixtures exist in all earthly societies. But in heaven there are no mixtures. "I the Lord will hasten it in his time... though much time may pass before it is done, no time shall be lost."

## Chapter 61: The Anointing of the Messiah—Good Tidings to the Afflicted

Christ himself expounded these verses: "He that is the best expositor of scripture has no doubt given us the best exposition of these verses, even our Lord Jesus himself, who read this in the synagogue at Nazareth... and applied it entirely to himself, saying, This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears."

**The Spirit Upon Christ (vv. 1–3)**: The prophets had the Spirit moving them at times; Christ had the Spirit always resting on him without measure. He was anointed for fivefold work:

**1. Preacher**: "He must preach good tidings to the meek, to the penitent, and humble, and poor in spirit." The poor are commonly best disposed to receive the gospel.

**2. Healer**: "He was sent to bind up the broken-hearted, as pained limbs are rolled to give them ease." Those whose hearts are broken for sin, truly humbled under guilt and dread of wrath, are furnished in the gospel of Christ with that which will make them easy and silence their fears.

**3. Deliverer**: Two proclamations—peace to friends ("liberty to the captives") and war against enemies ("the day of vengeance"). Liberty: "By the guilt of sin, we are bound over to the justice of God... Christ lets us know that he has made satisfaction to divine justice."

**4. Comforter**: "He was sent to comfort all who mourn." There is enough in him to comfort all who mourn, whatever their sore or sorrow. He gives "beauty for ashes," "the oil of joy for mourning," and "the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness."

**5. Planter**: "The church is God's husbandry... that they may be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the Lord... and all this that he may be glorified."

**Promises to the Restored (vv. 4–9)**: First applied to returning Jews, then to the gospel church. "They shall build the old wastes." Christianity's establishment repaired the decays of natural religion. On being "priests of the LORD": "All believers are made to our God kings and priests... his service is perfect freedom, nay, it is the greatest honour." On double recompense: "As the miseries of their captivity were so great that in them they are said to have received double for all their sins, so the joys of their return shall be so great that in them they shall receive double for all their shame."

# Matthew Henry on Isaiah 61-66

## Chapter 61: Good Tidings to the Meek

The Spirit of God empowers the prophet to preach good tidings to the meek, comfort the broken-hearted, proclaim liberty to captives, and open the prison to those who are bound. This passage speaks of both temporal and spiritual deliverance.

God's hatred of "robbery for burnt offering" is explained: "Ritual services will never atone for the violation of moral precepts. Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, to do justly and love mercy better than thousands of rams."

**Song of Rejoicing**: "Those that rejoice in God have cause to rejoice greatly. Those, and those only, shall be clothed with the garments of salvation hereafter that are covered with the robe of righteousness now." Though the church may experience winter seasons when blessings seem to wither, "the root of them is fixed, a spring-time will come."

## Chapter 62: Zion's New Name—Hephzibah and Beulah

The prophet resolves to perform his office faithfully: "He will not hold his peace; he will not rest; he will mind his business, and herein he was a type of Christ, who was indefatigable in executing the office of a prophet."

**The Prophet's Resolution**: He will continue instant in preaching and prayer, never holding his peace at God's throne "till he has prevailed with God for the mercies promised." His motivation is for Zion's sake and the church's benefit, not personal interest.

**The Church Honored—Two Names Replace Reproach**:

1. **Hephzibah** ("My delight is in her"): "God by his grace has wrought that in his church which makes her his delight."

2. **Beulah** ("married"): "Thy land shall be married, that is, it shall become fruitful again, and be replenished." God's affection is described beautifully: "As the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall thy God rejoice over thee: he shall rest in his love to thee."

**Watchmen on the Walls**: "Ministers are watchmen on the church's walls. They must never hold their peace; they must take all opportunities to give warning to sinners." God encourages persistent prayer: "He suffers himself not only to be reasoned with, but to be wrestled with." The people are called "the Lord's remembrancers"—"they remember the Lord themselves and put one another in mind of him."

**God's Oath of Provision**: "The Lord has sworn by his right hand, and by the arm of his strength, since he can swear by no greater, he swears by himself." When enjoying God's bounty: "We must, when we have eaten and are full, bless the Lord. The greatest comfort that a good man has in his meat and drink is that it furnishes him with a meat-offering and a drink-offering for the Lord his God."

**Salvation Proclaimed**: "Christ is not only the Saviour, but the salvation itself; for the happiness of believers is not only from him, but in him." On new names for believers: "Christians shall be called saints, the holy people, for they are chosen and called to salvation through sanctification. None are to be called the redeemed of the Lord but those that are the holy people."

## Chapter 63: The Conqueror from Edom—God Remembered

This chapter presents both God's historical victory over Israel's enemies and His spiritual victory in Christ over our spiritual enemies.

**The Triumphant Conqueror**: "He is the Saviour. God was Israel's Saviour out of the hand of their oppressors; the Lord Jesus is ours; his name, Jesus, signifies a Saviour."

On Christ treading the winepress: "He trod the wine-press of his Father's wrath alone, and triumphed over principalities and powers in himself. Of the people there was none with him; for, when he entered the lists with the powers of darkness, all his disciples forsook him and fled."

Christ's redemptive joy: "With what pleasure he speaks of his people; they are his redeemed. Though their redemption is not yet wrought out, yet he calls them his redeemed, because it shall as surely be done as if it were done already."

**Acknowledging God's Goodness**: "I will mention the lovingkindnesses of the Lord; for, if we would count the fruits of his loving-kindness, they are more in number than the sand."

On divine compassion: "In all their affliction he was afflicted. He sympathizes with them, that he takes what injury is done to them as done to himself. Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?"

The "angel of his presence" is identified as "Jesus Christ, the eternal Word. He is the angel of the covenant, God's messenger to the world, the angel of God's face, for he is the express image of his person."

On Israel's rebellion: "They rebelled, and vexed his Holy Spirit. The ungrateful rebellions of God's children against him are a vexation to his Holy Spirit. He who had been so much their friend was turned to be their enemy and fought against them." This illustrates "the malignity and mischievousness of sin; it makes God an enemy even to those for whom he has done the part of a good friend."

**Prayer and Complaint**: The people plead:

- **God's compassion**: "Where is thy zeal and thy strength? We may ground good expectations of further mercy upon our experiences of former mercy."

- **God as Father**: "Doubtless thou art our Father. Abraham and Israel cannot help us; they have not the power that God has; they are dead long since, and are ignorant of us. It is God only that is the immortal Father."

- **God's ownership**: "We are thine, save us. Thy own, acknowledge us."

On hardened hearts, the people are not blaming God but lamenting: "They owned that they had erred from God's ways. They complain of this as their great misery and burden, that God had for their sins left them to this."

## Chapter 64: Oh That Thou Wouldest Rend the Heavens

This continues the church's prayer in times of distress, offering "direction to the church in other times of distress, what to ask of God and how to plead with him."

**Prayer for Divine Intervention**: "Oh that thou wouldest rend the heavens, that thou wouldest come down." This applies to Christ's second coming: "Come, Lord Jesus, come quickly." The purpose is "to make thy name known to thine adversaries, that the nations may tremble at thy presence."

**Pleading God's Past Works**: "Thou hast, therefore thou wilt, is good arguing at the throne of grace."

On God's invisible mercy: "Much of it was concealed in former ages, but in latter ages they were revealed by the gospel. Even when revealed, it cannot be fully comprehended by the human understanding; it is very great, and will far outdo the utmost of our expectations."

What God expects and provides: "We must work righteousness, we must rejoice and work righteousness, we must remember him in his ways. What we may expect from God—thou meetest him. This intimates the friendship, fellowship, and familiarity to which God admits his people; he will anticipate them with the blessings of his goodness."

Despite sin: "Though God has been angry with us for our sins, and justly, yet his anger has endured but for a moment; but in his favour is life, because in it is continuance."

**Humble Confession**: "We are all as an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags. The best of our performances are so. If we depend upon them as our righteousness and think to merit by them at God's hand, they are as filthy rags—rags that will not cover us—filthy rags that will but defile us."

On prayerlessness: "There is none that calleth upon thy name. Therefore people are so bad, because they do not pray. It bodes ill to a people when prayer is restrained among them."

**Claiming Covenant Relationship**: "But now, O Lord! thou art our Father. To thee therefore we return in our repentance, as the prodigal arose and came to his father." On being God's workmanship: "We are the clay and thou our potter. We will hope that thou who madest us wilt new-make us, new-form us, though we have unmade and deformed ourselves."

**Lamenting the Ruins**: The destroyed cities are "called holy cities, for the people were unto God a kingdom of priests." Their losses included "the holiness of it was in their eye the greatest beauty of it" and "the sabbaths and new moons, and all their religious feasts, their ministers and solemn assemblies."

Henry observes: "God's people reckon their sacred things their most delectable things; rob them of holy ordinances and the means of grace, and you lay waste all their pleasant things." Yet God has promised: "His people may depend upon it that their afflictions shall be neither to extremity nor to eternity, but light and for a moment."

## Chapter 65: New Heavens and a New Earth

Paul applied this chapter's teaching to "the calling in of the Gentiles and the rejection of the Jews, by the preaching of the gospel."

**The Gentiles Sought God**: "I was found of those that seek me not. Those who had long been without God in the world shall now be set a seeking him. Though in after-communion God is found of those that seek him, yet in the first conversion he is found of those that seek him not; for therefore we love him because he first loved us."

**Israel's Rejection**: "I have spread out my hands all the day unto a rebellious people. When Christ was crucified his hands were spread out and stretched forth, as if he were preparing to receive returning sinners into his bosom. He waited to be gracious."

Their character: "They walk after their own thoughts. If our guide be our own thoughts, our way is not likely to be good." Their provocations included idolatry, necromancy, and dietary violations.

On Pharisaic pride: "Stand by thyself, come not near to me; for I am holier than thou. Nothing in men is more odious and offensive to God than a proud conceit of themselves and contempt of others; for commonly those are most unholy of all that think themselves holier than any."

**The Remnant Preserved**: Using the grape cluster illustration: "When a vine is so blasted and withered, yet, if ever so little of the juice of the grape, fit to make new wine, be found, Destroy it not, for a blessing is in it. Good men are blessings to the places where they live."

**Contrast of Servants and Rebels**: "My servants shall eat, but ye shall be hungry; my servants shall rejoice, but ye shall be ashamed; my servants shall sing for joy of heart, but ye shall cry for sorrow of heart." On their doom: "When I called, ye did not answer. See the malignity of sin; it is evil in God's eyes, highly offensive to him, and yet it is committed before his eyes."

On the new name: "The children of the covenant shall no longer be called Jews, but Christians."

**New Heavens and New Earth**: "It is in the gospel that old things have passed away and all things have become new, and by it that those who are in Christ are new creatures."

New joys: "I will rejoice in Jerusalem's joy, and will joy in my people. What God rejoices in it becomes us to rejoice in."

New life: "If an infant end its days quickly, yet it shall not be reckoned to die untimely; for the shorter its life is the longer will its rest be. Conversely, the sinner, though he live to a hundred years old, shall be accursed; his long life is but a long reprieve."

New enjoyment: "They shall build houses and inhabit them. My elect shall long enjoy the work of their hands. As the days of a tree are the days of my people."

Answered prayer: "Even before they call, I will answer. These are high expressions of God's readiness to hear prayer."

New harmony: "The wolf and the lamb shall feed together." This applies to Jew-Gentile unity in Christ: "All hostilities ceased, and they fed together as one sheepfold under Christ the great Shepherd."

## Chapter 66: Heaven My Throne—The Final Vision

This final chapter looks forward to "the latter days, to the last day, to the days of eternity."

**Temple vs. Humble Heart**: "The heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool: where is the house that ye build unto me? He did not need it. Heaven is the throne of his glory and government. If God has so bright a throne, so large a footstool, where then is the house they can build unto God?"

Yet God "would not heed it as he would a humble, penitent, gracious heart. He overlooks them all, that he may look with favour to him that is poor in spirit, humble and serious, self-abasing and self-denying, whose heart is truly contrite for sin and who trembles at God's word. Such a heart is a living temple for God."

**Worthless Sacrifices**: "He that killeth an ox is as if he slew a man." Those who "continued to offer sacrifices, as if the law of Moses had been still in force" commit a grave sin: "He that now kills it, for God's altar, is as if he slew a man. It is as great an offence to God as murder itself; he that does it does in effect set aside Christ's sacrifice."

Their sin: "They had chosen their own ways, and their souls delighted in their abominations." God's response: "I also will choose their delusions. God will make their sin their punishment; they shall be beaten with their own rod and hurried into ruin by their own delusions."

**Comfort to Those Who Tremble**: "Hear the word of the Lord, ye that tremble at his word." Those persecuted believers are told: "Your brethren that hated you, that cast you out for my name's sake, said, Let the Lord be glorified: but he shall appear to your joy."

On Zion's sudden birth: "Before she travailed, she brought forth. A nation be born at once." Henry applies this to gospel success: "When the Spirit was poured out, and the gospel went forth from Zion, multitudes were converted in a little time and with little pains. The same day that the Spirit was poured out there were 3000 souls added to the church."

On God as mother-comforter: "As one whom his mother comforts, so will I comfort you; not only with the rational arguments which a prudent father uses, but with the tender affections and compassions of a loving mother."

**God's Coming Judgment**: "The Lord will come with fire, and with his chariots like a whirlwind. I know their works and their thoughts. God knows both what men do and from what principle and with what design they do it; and therefore is fit to judge the world."

**The Gathering of Nations**: "I will send those that escape of them unto the nations, and they shall declare my glory among the Gentiles. I will take of them for priests and for Levites"—meaning "pastors and teachers and deacons to serve tables."

**New Heavens, New Earth, New Worship**: "As the new heavens and the new earth shall remain before me, so shall your seed and your name remain. A change never to be changed, a new world that will be always new, and never wax old. The kingdom of Christ is a kingdom that cannot be moved."

On perpetual worship: "All flesh shall come and worship before me, every new moon and every sabbath. Where the Lord's day is weekly sanctified, and the Lord's supper monthly celebrated, and both are duly attended on, there this promise is fulfilled."

**The Final Vision of Judgment**: "They shall go forth, and look upon the carcases of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched." Christ applies this to "the everlasting misery and torment of impenitent sinners in the future state."

The purpose for the redeemed: "To affect their own hearts with the love of their Redeemer, when they see what misery they are redeemed from. To the honour of that free grace which thus distinguished them let the redeemed of the Lord with all humility, and not without a holy trembling, sing their triumphant songs."
