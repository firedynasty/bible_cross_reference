# Chapter 1: The Vision of Isaiah - God's Case Against His People

Isaiah's name means "salvation of the Lord," fitting for one who prophesies about Jesus the Saviour. He prophesied during the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah—spanning sixty to eighty years through both godly and wicked administrations.

**God's Charge of Ingratitude (vv. 2-4)**: Heaven and earth witness Israel's rebellion. God declares, "I have nourished and brought up children, and they have rebelled against me." Even the ox knows his owner and the ass his master's crib, "but Israel doth not know, my people doth not consider." This spiritual stupidity—knowing God's truth but failing to apply it—is rebellion's root. The nation is "laden with iniquity, a seed of evildoers, children that are corrupters" who have "forsaken the LORD."

**The Diseased Body Metaphor (vv. 5-9)**: Sin has made Israel like a body covered in "wounds, and bruises, and putrefying sores" from head to foot. Only because "the LORD of hosts had left unto us a very small remnant" were they not destroyed like Sodom.

**Rejection of Empty Religion (vv. 10-15)**: God addresses them as "rulers of Sodom" and "people of Gomorrah." Their "multitude of sacrifices" and religious observances are called "vain oblations" and "an abomination." Why? "Your hands are full of blood." Sacrifice without obedience is an affront to God.

**The Call to Repentance (vv. 16-20)**: "Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow." Sins deeply dyed in corruption can be thoroughly cleansed. The condition: "Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings... cease to do evil; learn to do well." The choice is stark: "If ye be willing and obedient, ye shall eat the good of the land: but if ye refuse and rebel, ye shall be devoured with the sword."

**Jerusalem's Degeneracy and Restoration (vv. 21-31)**: The "faithful city" has "become a harlot." Silver has become dross, wine mixed with water—justice corrupted, religion adulterated. Princes are "companions of thieves" who neglect the fatherless. Yet God promises restoration: "I will restore thy judges as at the first... afterward thou shalt be called, The city of righteousness."

# Chapter 2: The Mountain of the Lord's House and the Day of Judgment

This chapter contrasts the glory of the gospel church with the shame of unbelieving Israel.

**The Church Exalted in the Last Days (vv. 1-5)**: "The last days" refers to gospel times. "The mountain of the LORD's house shall be established in the top of the mountains... and all nations shall flow unto it." Christianity will be openly preached, firmly established, and exalted above all competing religions. The Gentiles will flow into the church "as streams of water," voluntarily and cheerfully. "Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord... he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths."

**Swords into Plowshares (v. 4)**: "They shall beat their swords into plowshares" represents the gospel's tendency to "dispose men to be peaceable" and reconcile Jew and Gentile into "one sheepfold under one shepherd."

**Israel's Doom and Reasons (vv. 6-9)**: God forsakes His people because they "replenished from the east"—adopting foreign customs instead of remaining distinct. They became "soothsayers like the Philistines," trusting divination over God's oracles. Their land was "full of silver and gold... full of horses... full of idols." Both rich and poor worship "what their own fingers have made." The verdict: "Therefore forgive them not."

**The Day of the Lord Against Pride (vv. 10-17)**: "The lofty looks of man shall be humbled... the LORD alone shall be exalted in that day." God's judgment comes upon "every one that is proud and lofty"—symbolized by cedars of Lebanon, oaks of Bashan, high mountains, towers, and fenced walls. Pride will have a fall; he who exalts himself shall be abased.

**Idols Abolished (vv. 18-21)**: "The idols he shall utterly abolish." In terror, men "shall go into the holes of the rocks, and into the caves of the earth, for fear of the LORD... when he ariseth to shake terribly the earth." They "shall cast their idols of silver, and his idols of gold... to the moles and to the bats."

**Cease from Man (v. 22)**: The chapter concludes with a timeless warning: "Cease ye from man, whose breath is in his nostrils: for wherein is he to be accounted of?" Man is a dying creature; our nostrils are like one standing at the door, ready to depart.

# Chapter 3: The Stay and Staff Removed - Judgment on Leaders and Pride

God threatens to remove every support from Jerusalem and Judah: "the stay and the staff, the whole stay of bread, and the whole stay of water" (v. 1). The Lord himself is the foundation; if that stay departs, all other stays break under us.

**The Supports Removed (vv. 1-8)**: God will take away every category of leader and resource: "the mighty man, and the man of war, the judge, and the prophet, and the prudent, and the ancient, the captain of fifty, and the honourable man, and the counsellor, and the cunning artificer, and the eloquent orator."

When capable leaders are removed, "children shall be their princes, and babes shall rule over them." The result: "the people shall be oppressed every one by another," and "the child shall behave himself proudly against the ancient, and the base against the honourable." It is an ill omen when the rising generation are generally untractable and ungovernable.

**Government Rejected (vv. 6-7)**: So desperate will conditions become that any man with decent clothing will be seized to rule. But even these will refuse: "I will not be a healer; for in my house is neither bread nor clothing." Rulers must be healers, uniting their subjects.

**The Root Cause (v. 8)**: "Jerusalem is ruined, and Judah is fallen: because their tongue and their doings are against the LORD, to provoke the eyes of his glory."

**The Righteous Distinguished (vv. 9-15)**: Their sin was shameless—"they declare their sin as Sodom, they hide it not." Those past shame are past grace and hope. Yet God distinguishes: "Say ye to the righteous, that it shall be well with him: for they shall eat the fruit of their doings. Woe unto the wicked! it shall be ill with him." The Judge of all the earth will not slay the righteous with the wicked.

God indicts the leaders: "Ye have eaten up the vineyard; the spoil of the poor is in your houses. What mean ye that ye beat my people to pieces, and grind the faces of the poor?" Nothing must more certainly be accounted for than the injuries and abuses done to God's people.

**The Daughters of Zion (vv. 16-26)**: God rebukes the proud women: "the daughters of Zion are haughty, and walk with stretched forth necks and wanton eyes, walking and mincing as they go, and making a tinkling with their feet." This contradicts the modesty and sobriety with which women ought to adorn themselves.

The punishment fits the crime: "Instead of sweet smell there shall be stink; and instead of a girdle a rent; and instead of well set hair baldness; and instead of a stomacher a girding of sackcloth; and burning instead of beauty." The final devastation: "Thy men shall fall by the sword, and thy mighty in the war. And her gates shall lament and mourn; and she being desolate shall sit upon the ground."

# Chapter 4: The Branch of the Lord and the Glory of Zion

This chapter moves from judgment's aftermath to Messianic promise. Gospel grace is a sovereign relief.

**Scarcity After Judgment (v. 1)**: "Seven women shall take hold of one man, saying, We will eat our own bread, and wear our own apparel: only let us be called by thy name, to take away our reproach." The slaughter of men in war creates desperate conditions where women abandon normal customs and pursue husbands to escape reproach.

**The Branch of the Lord (v. 2)**: "In that day shall the branch of the LORD be beautiful and glorious, and the fruit of the earth shall be excellent and comely for them that are escaped of Israel." This is Christ the Messiah—"the man the branch," "a rod out of the stem of Jesse." Christ is "beautiful and glorious" both in his exaltation with the Father and "in the esteem of all believers... the fairest of ten thousand." If Christ be precious to us, his gospel and its truths will be so.

**The Holy Remnant (v. 3)**: "He that is left in Zion, and he that remaineth in Jerusalem, shall be called holy, even every one that is written among the living in Jerusalem." This is "a remnant according to the election of grace"—those "written to life... designed and determined for it unalterably." All that were written among the living shall be found among the living, for Christ will lose none given to him. Those only that are holy shall be left; all chosen to salvation are chosen to sanctification.

**The Washing of Zion (v. 4)**: "When the Lord shall have washed away the filth of the daughters of Zion, and shall have purged the blood of Jerusalem... by the spirit of judgment, and by the spirit of burning." Reformation-work is God's work, done by the Spirit—enlightening the mind, convincing the conscience, and making men zealously affected in a good work.

**Divine Protection (vv. 5-6)**: "The LORD will create upon every dwelling place of mount Zion, and upon her assemblies, a cloud and smoke by day, and the shining of a flaming fire by night: for upon all the glory shall be a defence." This echoes Israel's wilderness experience. Though miracles have ceased, God is the same to the New Testament church. "There shall be a tabernacle for a shadow in the daytime from the heat, and for a place of refuge, and for a covert from storm and from rain." God himself becomes their hiding-place. In this world we must expect change of weather, but God is a refuge in all weathers.

# Chapter 5: The Song of the Vineyard and the Six Woes

This chapter presents a memorable parable followed by six devastating "woes" against specific sins.

**The Parable of the Vineyard (vv. 1-7)**: God planted Israel "in a very fruitful hill"—a land of plenty and privilege. "He fenced it" with special protection, "gathered out the stones" removing obstacles, "planted it with the choicest vine" of pure religion, "built a tower" (the temple), and "made a winepress" (the altar). "And he looked that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes."

God expects vineyard-fruit from those enjoying vineyard-privileges. Good purposes and beginnings are not enough; there must be fruit. Wild grapes represent either "the fruits of the corrupt nature" or "hypocritical performances in religion."

God appeals: "What could have been done more to my vineyard, that I have not done in it?" The verdict: "I will take away the hedge thereof... break down the wall... lay it waste... it shall not be pruned, nor digged; but there shall come up briers and thorns: I will also command the clouds that they rain no rain upon it."

The explanation: "The vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel... and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry."

**The Six Woes**:

**1. Woe to Covetousness (vv. 8-10)**: "Woe unto them that join house to house, that lay field to field, till there be no place." Those "inordinate in their desires to enrich themselves" never know when they have enough. As punishment, houses become "desolate, without inhabitant" and fields yield only a tenth of their seed.

**2. Woe to Drunkenness and Revelry (vv. 11-17)**: "Woe unto them that rise up early in the morning, that they may follow strong drink; that continue until night, till wine inflame them!" They have music at feasts "but they regard not the work of the LORD." Therefore "my people are gone into captivity, because they have no knowledge." Yet "the LORD of hosts shall be exalted in judgment, and God that is holy shall be sanctified in righteousness."

**3. Woe to Presumptuous Sinners (vv. 18-19)**: "Woe unto them that draw iniquity with cords of vanity, and sin as it were with a cart rope." Those who labor at sin mockingly say, "Let him make speed, and hasten his work, that we may see it!"

**4. Woe to Moral Confusion (v. 20)**: "Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!" Those who call drunkenness good fellowship, covetousness good husbandry, and seriousness ill-nature.

**5. Woe to Pride (v. 21)**: "Woe unto them that are wise in their own eyes, and prudent in their own sight!" Those who think they can outwit Infinite Wisdom.

**6. Woe to Corrupt Judges (vv. 22-23)**: "Woe unto them that are mighty to drink wine... which justify the wicked for reward, and take away the righteousness of the righteous from him!"

**The Judgment (vv. 24-30)**: "Their root shall be as rottenness, and their blossom shall go up as dust: because they have cast away the law of the LORD of hosts." God will "lift up an ensign to the nations from far, and will hiss unto them from the end of the earth: and, behold, they shall come with speed swiftly"—a foreign army unstoppable and terrifying. By keeping a good conscience, we may have light from above even when clouds and darkness surround us.

# Chapter 6: Isaiah's Vision - Holy, Holy, Holy and "Here Am I, Send Me"

This chapter records Isaiah's ordination to prophetic office through a magnificent vision, designed to confirm his faith and possess him with reverence of God.

**The Vision of God's Glory (vv. 1-4)**: "In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple." Israel's king dies, but Israel's God still lives. From the mortality of great men we should look up with faith to the King eternal, immortal. Isaiah saw Christ's glory (John 12:41)—an incontestable proof of Christ's divinity. The throne is "a throne of glory, before which we must worship,—a throne of government, under which we must be subject,—and a throne of grace, to which we may come boldly."

**The Seraphim (vv. 2-3)**: "Above it stood the seraphims: each one had six wings; with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet, and with twain he did fly." Seraphim means "burners"—they burn in love to God and zeal for his glory. Covering their faces shows great humility and reverence; even angels cannot bear the dazzling lustre of divine glory.

Their anthem: "Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory." This threefold holy expresses (1) the angels' zeal and fervency in praising God; (2) their particular pleasure in contemplating God's holiness; (3) the superlative excellency of God's holiness; (4) possibly the three persons of the Godhead. This holiness is the glory of all his other attributes; power without purity would be a terror.

**Isaiah's Conviction (v. 5)**: "Then said I, Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips: for mine eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts." Isaiah feels terror rather than blessing. We are undone without a Mediator between us and this holy God. The angels' purity exposes human sinfulness; the best men have reason to be ashamed when compared with holy angels. Those lowest in their own eyes are fittest for God's employment.

**The Cleansing Coal (vv. 6-7)**: A seraph "flew unto me, having a live coal in his hand, which he had taken with the tongs from off the altar: And he laid it upon my mouth, and said, Lo, this hath touched thy lips; and thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged." Nothing is powerful to cleanse and comfort the soul but what flows from Christ's satisfaction and his continual intercession. It must be a coal from his altar that gives us life and peace.

**The Commission (v. 8)**: "Also I heard the voice of the Lord, saying, Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Then said I, Here am I; send me." The plural "us" indicates the Trinity. Isaiah's response shows readiness (volunteering), resolution, and submission to God's will.

**Judicial Blindness (vv. 9-10)**: "Make the heart of this people fat, and make their ears heavy, and shut their eyes; lest they see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and convert, and be healed." These verses are quoted six times in the New Testament regarding Israel's rejection of the gospel. God sometimes, in righteous judgment, gives men up to blindness of mind and strong delusions because they would not receive truth in the love of it.

**How Long? (vv. 11-13)**: Isaiah asks, "Lord, how long?" The answer: "Until the cities be wasted without inhabitant... and the LORD have removed men far away." This found complete accomplishment in the final destruction by the Romans.

Yet mercy remains: "In it shall be a tenth, and it shall return... as a teil-tree, and as an oak, whose substance is in them, when they cast their leaves: so the holy seed shall be the substance thereof." The holy seed in the land keeps it from utter dissolution. Some identify this holy seed with Christ himself—the Jewish nation was saved from utter ruin because Christ was to come from it.

# Chapter 7: The Sign of Immanuel - A Virgin Shall Conceive

This chapter records the famous Immanuel prophecy during the Syro-Ephraimite crisis when Syria and Israel had formed an alliance against Judah.

**Ahaz's Fear (vv. 1-2)**: "His heart was moved, and the heart of his people, as the trees of the wood are moved with the wind." This fright came from guilt and weak faith. Those whose consciences are kept void of offence and whose hearts are fixed, trusting in God, need not fear evil tidings.

**The Prophet's Mission (vv. 3-9)**: God sends Isaiah with his son Shear-jashub ("A remnant shall return") to meet Ahaz. The message: "Take heed, and be quiet; fear not, neither be fainthearted for the two tails of these smoking firebrands." These powers are "but tails of smoking firebrands, in a manner burnt out already; their force is spent." The verdict: "Thus saith the Lord GOD, It shall not stand, neither shall it come to pass." Crucial exhortation: "If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established." The grace of faith is absolutely necessary to quiet the mind.

**The Offered Sign (vv. 10-12)**: God offers Ahaz: "Ask thee a sign of the LORD thy God; ask it either in the depth, or in the height above." But Ahaz refuses: "I will not ask, neither will I tempt the LORD." His true reason was dependence upon the Assyrians; having confidence in their forces and gods, he would not be beholden to Israel's God. A secret disaffection to God is often disguised with respect to him.

**The Immanuel Prophecy (vv. 13-14)**: Isaiah rebukes: "Is it a small thing for you to weary men, but will ye weary my God also?" Then: "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel."

This prophecy contains a double sign: First, "a sign in general of his good-will to Israel and to the house of David. You may conclude it that he has mercy in store for you... for of your nation, of your family, the Messiah is to be born." The virgin birth signifies "both the divine power and the divine purity with which he shall be brought into the world."

The name Immanuel means "God with us, God in our nature, God at peace with us, in covenant with us." If he had not been Immanuel—God with us, he could not have been Jesus—a Saviour. The strongest consolations in time of trouble are those borrowed from Christ.

**The Near Fulfillment (vv. 15-16)**: "Before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken of both her kings." Within a few years, both Pekah and Rezin would be dead. The immediate sign confirms the greater one.

**The Assyrian Threat (vv. 17-25)**: Despite deliverance from Syria and Israel, "The LORD shall bring upon thee... days that have not come, from the day that Ephraim departed from Judah; even the king of Assyria." Ironically, the power Ahaz trusted would become his scourge. The creature we make our hope commonly proves our hurt. God will bring foreign armies that swarm the land, leaving desolation. Many are beaten with the arm of flesh they trusted to rather than the arm of the Lord.

# Chapter 8: Maher-Shalal-Hash-Baz and "To the Law and to the Testimony"

This chapter continues the message of comfort and warning, introducing another prophetic child and the principle of Scripture as the test of truth.

**The Prophetic Sign (vv. 1-4)**: God commands Isaiah: "Take thee a great roll, and write in it with a man's pen concerning Maher-shalal-hash-baz." The name means "Make speed to the spoil, hasten to the prey"—predicting Assyria's swift conquest. Isaiah takes "faithful witnesses to record"—Uriah the priest and Zechariah. Isaiah's wife bears a son with this ominous name. The prophecy: "Before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother, the riches of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria shall be taken away before the king of Assyria." Within a year or two, both enemy capitals would fall.

# Chapter 8: The Waters of Shiloah Through Chapter 14: How Art Thou Fallen, O Lucifer

## Chapter 8 (continued)

**The Waters of Shiloah Refused (vv. 5-8)**: God explains judgment comes because the people "despise their own country and the government of it, and love to run it down, because it does not make so great a figure as some other kings and kingdoms do." They preferred impressive foreign powers to God's quiet providence.

Therefore: "The Lord bringeth up upon them the waters of the river, strong and many, even the king of Assyria." Yet God limits destruction: "the stretching out of his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, O Immanuel." Even though Immanuel's land may be deluged, it "shall not be destroyed."

**The Futility of Opposition (vv. 9-10)**: "Associate yourselves, O ye people, and ye shall be broken in pieces... speak the word, and it shall not stand: for God is with us."

**Sanctify the Lord (vv. 11-15)**: God instructs Isaiah: "Say ye not, A confederacy... neither fear ye their fear, nor be afraid. Sanctify the LORD of hosts himself; and let him be your fear, and let him be your dread. And he shall be for a sanctuary."

The believing fear of God is a special preservative against the disquieting fear of man. But for unbelievers, God becomes "a stone of stumbling and for a rock of offence... And many among them shall stumble, and fall, and be broken, and be snared, and be taken."

**Waiting on God (vv. 16-18)**: "Bind up the testimony, seal the law among my disciples." Despite God "hiding his face from the house of Jacob," Isaiah resolves: "I will wait upon the LORD... and I will look for him." He and his children are "for signs and for wonders in Israel"—gazed upon as peculiar people.

**To the Law and to the Testimony (vv. 19-22)**: When tempted to consult "wizards that peep, and that mutter," the answer is: "Should not a people seek unto their God?" The principle: "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them."

Those who refuse this counsel face despair: "They shall pass through it, hardly bestead and hungry... and shall curse their king and their God... and they shall look unto the earth; and behold trouble and darkness, dimness of anguish; and they shall be driven to darkness."

## Chapter 9: Unto Us a Child Is Born

This chapter contains one of Scripture's greatest Messianic prophecies, followed by warnings of judgment.

**A Great Light in Darkness (vv. 1-2)**: "Nevertheless the dimness shall not be such as was in her vexation." The famous prophecy: "The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light: they that dwell in the land of the shadow of death, upon them hath the light shined."

This was fulfilled when Jesus "began to appear as a prophet, and to preach the gospel in the land of Zebulun and Naphtali." Those that want the gospel walk in darkness; when the gospel comes to any place or soul, light comes—a great light.

**Joy Like Harvest (vv. 3-5)**: "Thou hast multiplied the nation, and not increased the joy: they joy before thee according to the joy in harvest." This is holy joy—"they rejoice in spirit before God."

The deliverance: "Thou hast broken the yoke of his burden... as in the day of Midian." The gospel brings liberty and deliverance to the captives. The design of the gospel and grace of Christ is to break the yoke of sin and Satan, and remove the burden of guilt and corruption.

**The Child Born (vv. 6-7)**: "For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace."

His names explained:
- **Wonderful, Counsellor**: He is both God and man, intimately acquainted with God's counsels from eternity.
- **The mighty God**: He possesses both wisdom and strength; no less power than that of the mighty God could accomplish redemption.
- **The everlasting Father**: The author of everlasting life and happiness.
- **The Prince of Peace**: As a King, he preserves peace, commands peace, and creates peace.

His government: "Of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end, upon the throne of David." The heart of God is much upon the advancement of Christ's kingdom among men.

**Judgment on Israel (vv. 8-21)**: The chapter shifts to warnings against the northern kingdom.

**Israel's Pride (vv. 9-10)**: "The bricks are fallen down, but we will build with hewn stones." Those whose hearts are unhumbled under humbling providences ripen apace for ruin.

**Unrepentance (v. 13)**: "The people turneth not unto him that smiteth them, neither do they seek the LORD of hosts." God designs judgment to turn sinners to himself; if lesser judgments fail, greater ones may be expected.

**Corrupt Leaders (vv. 14-16)**: "The leaders of this people cause them to err; and they that are led of them are destroyed." Both magistrates and false prophets shall be cut off.

**Universal Corruption (v. 17)**: "Every one is a hypocrite and an evil doer, and every mouth speaketh folly." Therefore even the fatherless and widows receive no mercy.

**Civil War (vv. 19-21)**: "Manasseh shall devour Ephraim; and Ephraim Manasseh: and they together shall be against Judah." Mutual enmity among the tribes of God's Israel ripens them for ruin.

The refrain: "For all this his anger is not turned away, but his hand is stretched out still." Judgments are prolonged because sinners are not brought to repentance.

## Chapter 10: Assyria the Rod of God's Anger

This chapter reveals God's sovereign use of wicked nations as instruments of judgment, while rebuking their pride.

**Woe to Unjust Rulers (vv. 1-4)**: "Woe unto them that decree unrighteous decrees, and that write grievousness which they have prescribed; to turn aside the needy from judgment, and to take away the right from the poor of my people." Both those who devise and those who record unjust decrees share the guilt.

The searching question: "What will ye do in the day of visitation, and in the desolation which shall come from far? to whom will ye flee for help?" It concerns all to consider seriously what they shall do in a day of affliction, death, and judgment.

**Assyria: God's Instrument (vv. 5-7)**: "O Assyrian, the rod of mine anger, and the staff in their hand is mine indignation." God sends Assyria "against a hypocritical nation"—even God's own people when hypocritical. Hypocritical nations are the people of God's wrath; dissimulation in religion is most offensive to God.

Yet the Assyrian king "meaneth not so, neither doth his heart think so; but it is in his heart to destroy and cut off nations not a few." God often makes even the sinful passions and projects of men subservient to his own great and holy purposes.

**The Assyrian's Pride (vv. 8-14)**: The king boasts: "Are not my princes altogether kings? By the strength of my hand I have done it, and by my wisdom; for I am prudent." He claims he will do to Jerusalem as he did to Samaria. His arrogance reaches: "My hand hath found as a nest the riches of the people... have I gathered all the earth."

**God's Rebuke (vv. 15-19)**: "Shall the axe boast itself against him that heweth therewith?" The tyrants of the world are but the tools of Providence.

God's judgment: "The Lord, the Lord of hosts, shall send among his fat ones leanness; and under his glory he shall kindle a burning like the burning of a fire." The same God who is a rejoicing light to those serving him faithfully will be a consuming fire to those who trifle with him.

**The Remnant (vv. 20-23)**: "The remnant of Israel... shall stay upon the LORD, the Holy One of Israel, in truth." By afflictions we may learn not to make creatures our confidence. The name "Shear-jashub" (a remnant shall return) is fulfilled: they shall return to God and to their duty.

Yet there is also "the consumption decreed"—though Israel be as the sand of the sea, only a remnant shall return. When we see how populous Israel is yet only a remnant shall be saved, we shall strive to enter in at the strait gate.

**Fear Not the Assyrian (vv. 24-27)**: "O my people that dwellest in Zion, be not afraid of the Assyrian: he shall smite thee with a rod... For yet a very little while, and the indignation shall cease."

Deliverance will come "according to the slaughter of Midian at the rock of Oreb." The yoke "shall be destroyed because of the anointing"—ultimately "for the sake of the Messiah, the Anointed of God... It is for his sake that the yoke is broken."

**The Assyrian March and Fall (vv. 28-34)**: The chapter describes Sennacherib's advance through towns toward Jerusalem: "He is come to Aiath, he is passed to Migron; at Michmash he hath laid up his carriages." At Nob "he shall shake his hand against the mount of the daughter of Zion."

But his advance ends: "The Lord, the LORD of hosts, shall lop the bough with terror... and the haughty shall be humbled." The Assyrian soldiers, like a forest of Lebanon, all became as dead corpses when the destroying angel struck.

## Chapter 11: The Branch from Jesse's Stump

This is one of Scripture's greatest Messianic prophecies, foretelling Christ's nature, government, and kingdom.

**The Rod from Jesse's Stem (v. 1)**: "There shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots." Jesse lived and died in meanness and obscurity; David was sometimes called "the son of Jesse" in reproach. When the royal house was cut down like a cedar, only the stump was left, yet it would sprout again. The house of David was reduced and brought very low at Christ's birth, witnessed in the obscurity and poverty of Joseph and Mary.

**The Sevenfold Spirit (vv. 2-3)**: "The Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD."

The Holy Spirit, in all his gifts and graces, shall rest and abide upon Christ; he shall have the Spirit not by measure, but without measure. Christ is the fountain and treasury of all grace to believers, that from his fulness they might all receive.

Those most truly intelligent are those intelligent in the fear of the Lord, in the business of religion, which is both the foundation and top-stone of wisdom.

**Righteous Judgment (vv. 3-5)**: "He shall not judge after the sight of his eyes, neither reprove after the hearing of his ears: but with righteousness shall he judge the poor."

Christ will judge by the hidden man of the heart and inward principles men are governed by; he is an infallible witness. He will judge the secrets of men. He will defend the poor and reprove with equity for the meek of the earth—the poor man's King.

Against the wicked: "He shall smite the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked." This applies to the destruction of the man of sin.

"Righteousness shall be the girdle of his loins, and faithfulness the girdle of his reins"—his constant companion and strength.

**The Peaceable Kingdom (vv. 6-9)**: "The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid... and a little child shall lead them."

Men of fierce and furious dispositions, who used to bite and devour all about them, shall have their temper so strangely altered by the gospel and grace of Christ that they shall live in love with the weakest. This describes unity between Jew and Gentile: when converted to the faith of Christ and united in one sheep-fold, the wolf and lamb dwell together.

Even "the asp and the cockatrice shall cease to be venomous"—a generation of vipers shall become a seed of saints.

**The Cause: Knowledge of God (v. 9)**: "They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain: for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea." The better acquainted we are with the God of love, the more shall we be changed into the same image. The knowledge shall be broad, extensive, deep, substantial, and long-lasting.

**The Ensign to the Nations (vv. 10-12)**: "In that day there shall be a root of Jesse, which shall stand for an ensign of the people; to it shall the Gentiles seek: and his rest shall be glorious."

When Christ was crucified, he was lifted up from the earth to draw the eyes and hearts of all men unto him. He is the ensign to which the children of God are gathered together and in him they meet as their center of unity.

"His rest shall be glorious"—whether his death, ascension, or the gospel church, that Mount Zion of which Christ has said, "This is my rest."

**The Gathering of Israel and Gentiles (vv. 11-16)**: "The Lord shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people... and shall assemble the outcasts of Israel, and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth."

Ephraim and Judah reconciled: "The envy also of Ephraim shall depart... and Judah shall not vex Ephraim." This typifies the uniting of Jews and Gentiles, made of twain one new man.

All obstacles removed: "The LORD shall utterly destroy the tongue of the Egyptian sea... there shall be a highway for the remnant." When God's time comes for bringing nations or particular persons home to himself, divine grace will be victorious over all opposition.

## Chapter 12: A Song of Salvation

This brief chapter is a hymn of praise completing the Messianic prophecies of chapters 9-11. As Israel sang after the Red Sea, so God's people "shall sing a song of praise" when "the root of Jesse shall stand for an ensign of the people."

**Personal Praise (vv. 1-3)**: "In that day thou shalt say, O LORD, I will praise thee: though thou wast angry with me, thine anger is turned away, and thou comfortedst me."

Even God's frowns must not put us out of tune for praising him; though he be angry with us, yet we must trust in him and give him thanks. Key truths:
- Though God may be angry with his people for a time, his anger shall at length be turned away; it endures but for a moment.
- By Jesus Christ, God's anger against mankind was turned away; for he is our peace.
- Those whom God is reconciled to he comforts.

**God My Salvation (v. 2)**: "Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust, and not be afraid: for the LORD JEHOVAH is my strength and my song; he also is become my salvation."

God is not only my Saviour but my salvation, in whom I am safe. Faith in God silences fears and supports hopes. "Jah" signifies God's eternity and unchangeableness. Many good Christians have God for their strength who have him not for their song; they walk in darkness, but light is sown for them.

**Wells of Salvation (v. 3)**: "Therefore with joy shall ye draw water out of the wells of salvation."

Out of the wells of salvation in God, the fountain of all good to his people, believers shall draw water with joy. Some translate this "wells of the Saviour"—in them the Saviour and salvation are made known and made over to us. It is our duty by faith to draw water out of these wells with pleasure and satisfaction.

**Corporate Praise (vv. 4-6)**: "In that day shall ye say, Praise the LORD, call upon his name, declare his doings among the people, make mention that his name is exalted. Sing unto the LORD; for he hath done excellent things."

Believers praise God three ways:
1. By prayer: "Call upon his name"—giving thanks for former mercy is a decent way of begging further mercy.
2. By proclamation: "Declare his doings among the people"—when the apostles preached the gospel to all nations, this scripture was fulfilled.
3. By jubilation: "Cry out and shout"—welcome the gospel to yourselves and publish it to others with loud acclamations.

**The Holy One in Zion (v. 6)**: "Cry out and shout, thou inhabitant of Zion: for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of thee."

Let the weaker sex be strong in the Lord, and out of their mouth praise shall be perfected. We praise God because his name is exalted, he has done excellent things, and he is and will be great among them. True goodness is true greatness. It is the honour and happiness of Israel that the God in covenant with them is infinitely great.

## Chapter 13: The Burden of Babylon

Here begin Isaiah's prophecies against the nations. He that is King of saints is also King of nations, and rules in the affairs of the children of men. These prophecies comforted God's people oppressed by powerful neighbors.

**The Burden Defined (v. 1)**: It is a burden, a lesson they were to learn but would be loath to learn, a load lying heavily upon them. Those that will not make the word of God their rest shall find it made a burden to them.

**God's Sanctified Army (vv. 2-5)**: "I have commanded my sanctified ones, I have also called my mighty ones for mine anger." The Medes and Persians are "sanctified ones"—designed for this service and set apart by God's purpose and providence. Cyrus was "God's anointed," a figure of him that was to come.

They are "mighty ones" because they had their might from God and used it for him. God's sanctified ones are his mighty ones. Those whom God calls he qualifies; and those whom he makes holy he makes strong in spirit.

The Lord musters his army: "The LORD of hosts mustereth the host of the battle." Great princes and armies are but tools in God's hand, weapons that he uses in doing his work.

**The Day of the Lord (vv. 6-13)**: "Howl ye; for the day of the LORD is at hand; it shall come as a destruction from the Almighty."

Men have their day now and think to carry the day; but God laughs at them, for his day is coming. The day comes "cruel both with wrath and fierce anger"—God will deal in severity with them for the severities they exercised upon God's people.

The terror: "All hands shall be faint, and every man's heart shall melt... pangs and sorrows shall take hold of them." Those that in peace were proud, haughty, and terrible shall, when trouble comes, be quite dispirited.

Cosmic signs: "The stars of heaven and the constellations thereof shall not give their light: the sun shall be darkened." It shall be as dreadful a time as it would be if all heavenly luminaries were turned into darkness.

**Pride Punished (v. 11)**: "I will punish the world for their evil... I will cause the arrogancy of the proud to cease, and will lay low the haughtiness of the terrible." A man's pride will bring him low.

**Scarcity of Men (v. 12)**: "I will make a man more precious than fine gold." You could not have a man to be employed in the affairs of state, enlisted in the army, or matched with a daughter. War depopulates.

**The Inexorable Medes (vv. 17-18)**: "I will stir up the Medes against them, which shall not regard silver... Their bows also shall dash the young men to pieces; and they shall have no pity on the fruit of the womb."

This reveals three things: (1) That men should be thus cruel and inhuman; (2) That the God of infinite mercy should suffer it, and make it execution of his justice; (3) That little infants, who have never been guilty of actual sin, should be thus abused, showing there is an original guilt.

**Babylon's Desolation (vv. 19-22)**: "Babylon, the glory of kingdoms, the beauty of the Chaldees' excellency, shall be as when God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah. It shall never be inhabited."

Babylon was "the head of gold... the lady of kingdoms... the praise of the whole earth." Yet it would become utterly desolate: "wild beasts of the desert shall lie there; and their houses shall be full of doleful creatures; and owls shall dwell there." Historical accounts describe Babylon as once thirty miles in breadth, now laid waste, with ruins of a palace of Nebuchadnezzar so dangerous with serpents and scorpions that people dare not enter.

Though about 200 years from Isaiah's prophecy, when Israel groaned under Babylonish tyranny, they could know that Babylon's day to fall was near. This connects to Revelation's account of New Testament Babylon's judgment: "In one hour has her judgment come."

## Chapter 14: How Art Thou Fallen, O Lucifer

This chapter continues Babylon's burden, featuring the famous taunt-song against the king of Babylon, then addresses Assyria and Philistia.

**Israel's Restoration (vv. 1-3)**: Babylon's fall means Israel's restoration. "The Lord will have mercy on Jacob, and will yet choose Israel, and set them in their own land." The same prediction is a promise to God's people and a threatening to their enemies.

Strangers shall join them: "Strangers shall be joined with them, and they shall cleave to the house of Jacob." Let not the church's children be shy of strangers, but receive those whom God receives. The former captives become the captors: "They shall rule over their oppressors"—applicable to the success of the gospel and believers' interest in Christ's victories over their spiritual enemies when "he led captivity captive."

**The Taunt-Song (vv. 4-20)**: "Thou shalt take up this proverb against the king of Babylon."

The king was "the oppressor... the golden city." Men get wealth by squeezing it out of others. He "smote the people in wrath with a continual stroke... ruled the nations in anger." He "made the world as a wilderness"—great princes usually glory in building cities, but he gloried in destroying them.

# Isaiah Chapters 15-20: Prophecies Against Foreign Nations

## Chapter 15: The Burden of Moab

**Purpose of Prophecies Against Foreign Nations**
Henry explains Isaiah's prophecy against foreign nations: (1) Though judgment begins at God's house, it does not end there; (2) To prove God's care and jealousy for His people; (3) To confirm the prophet's mission through swift fulfillment.

**Cities Destroyed Overnight (v. 1)**
"In the night Ar of Moab is laid waste, and brought to silence; because in the night Kir of Moab is laid waste." Henry observes: "Great changes and very dismal ones may be made in a very little time."

**Moab's Useless Idolatry (v. 2)**
Moab seeks help from false gods at high places. Henry contrasts: "Prayer to the true God is heart's ease, but prayers to false gods are not."

**Universal Grief (vv. 2-4)**
"Moab shall howl over Nebo, and over Medeba: on all their heads shall be baldness." Grief spreads everywhere—streets, rooftops, soldiers losing courage. Henry notes: "God can easily make weary of life those that are fondest of it."

**The Prophet's Compassion (v. 5)**
"My heart shall cry out for Moab." Though enemies, they are fellow-creatures. Henry commends this tender spirit: "It becomes God's ministers...to be like their master, who wept over Jerusalem even when he gave her up to ruin."

**Fugitives' Lament and Spreading Fear (v. 5)**
Fugitives cry out "as a heifer of three years old does when she goes lowing for her calf." Henry warns: "Grief is catching, so is fear, and justly, for trouble is spreading."

**Desolation and Famine (vv. 6-7)**
"The waters of Nimrim shall be desolate: for the hay is withered away, the grass faileth." War brings famine. Their wealth is carried away: "Those that are eager to get abundance of this world...little consider what may become of it."

**Waters of Blood (v. 9)**
"The waters of Dimon shall be full of blood...lions upon him that escapeth of Moab." Henry warns: "Those that continue impenitent in sin, when they are preserved from one judgment, are but reserved for another."

---

## Chapter 16: Moab's Pride and Ruin

**Counsel to Moab (vv. 1-5)**
"Send ye the lamb to the ruler of the land." This calls for submission to authority, with deeper application to Christ: "Send him the lamb, the best you have, yourselves a living sacrifice."

**Rejection's Consequence (v. 2)**
"As a wandering bird cast out of the nest, so the daughters of Moab shall be." Henry applies: "Those that will not submit to Christ...shall be as a bird that wanders from her nest...Those that will not yield to the fear of God shall be made to yield to the fear of every thing else."

**Shelter for God's Outcasts (vv. 3-4)**
"Hide the outcasts; bewray not him that wandereth." Henry draws applications:
- God owns His outcasts when men reject them
- God will find rest and shelter for His outcasts
- Those expecting favor when troubled must show favor to others in trouble

**The Messianic Promise (v. 5)**
"In mercy shall the throne be established...judging, and seeking judgment, and hasting righteousness." This points to Christ, the ultimate good magistrate.

**The Danger of Pride (v. 6)**
"We have heard of the pride of Moab; he is very proud." Henry warns: "Perhaps there are more precious souls ruined by pride than by any one lust whatsoever." Pride manifests in refusing counsel, rejecting godly examples, refusing submission to God, and refusing to help His people.

**The Prophet's Lament (vv. 7-11)**
Cities fall, vineyards are destroyed. The prophet weeps: "I will bewail with the weeping of Jazer the vine of Sibmah: I will water thee with my tears." Henry observes: "It well becomes God's prophets to acquaint themselves with grief...The afflictions of the world should be afflictions to us."

Henry contrasts worldly and spiritual joy: "Destroy the vines and the fig-trees, and you make all the mirth of a carnal heart to cease. But a gracious soul can rejoice in the Lord as the God of its salvation even when the fig-tree does not blossom." Therefore: "In God let us always rejoice with a holy triumph, and in other things let us always rejoice with a holy trembling."

**Useless Idolatry (v. 12)**
Moab comes to his sanctuary to pray but "shall not prevail." Henry notes: "Men are generally so stupid that they will not believe...the vanity of idols."

**The Three-Year Deadline (vv. 13-14)**
"Within three years, as the years of a hireling, and the glory of Moab shall be contemned." Henry explains: "A servant hired for a certain term keeps account to a day." Moab's glory—population and military strength—becomes contemptible. "Let not therefore the strong glory in their strength nor the many in their numbers."

---

## Chapter 17: Damascus and Syria's Doom

**Damascus Destroyed (vv. 1-3)**
"Damascus is taken away from being a city, and it shall be a ruinous heap." Cities become pastureland. Henry notes: "The Syrians were the ring-leaders in that confederacy against Judah, and therefore they are punished first and sorest." Principle: "Sinful confederacies will be no strength...when God's judgments come upon them."

**Israel's Decline (vv. 4-5)**
"The glory of Jacob shall be made thin, and the fatness of his flesh shall wax lean." Population is decimated like consumption. "It shall be as when the harvestman gathereth the corn." Henry comments: "The people had by their sins made themselves ripe for ruin."

**The Righteous Remnant (vv. 6-8)**
"Yet gleaning grapes shall be left in it...two or three berries in the top of the uppermost bough." Henry applies to the elect: "Such is the remnant according to the election of grace, very few in comparison."

This remnant looks to God: "At that day shall a man look to his Maker, and his eyes shall have respect to the Holy One of Israel." They abandon idols: "He shall not look to the altars, the work of his hands." Henry rejoices: "We have reason to account those happy afflictions which part between us and our sins."

**Forgetting God (vv. 9-11)**
"Because thou hast forgotten the God of thy salvation, and hast not been mindful of the rock of thy strength, therefore shalt thou plant pleasant plants." Root cause: "Our forgetfulness and unmindfulness of him are at the bottom of all sin."

Irony: they lavish care on imported luxuries, yet "the harvest shall be a heap in the day of grief." Henry warns: "This is a good reason why we should not lay up our treasure in those things which we may so quickly be despoiled of, but in that good part which shall never be taken away from us."

**The Assyrian's Swift Doom (vv. 12-14)**
"Woe to the multitude of many people, which make a noise like the noise of the seas...God shall rebuke them, and they shall flee far off." Sennacherib's armies "make a mighty noise to strike a terror" but God rebukes them with "an invisible hand."

Sudden judgment: "At evening tide trouble; and before the morning he is not." Henry explains: "It was in the night that the angel routed the Assyrian army. God can in a moment break the power of his church's enemies." This principle applies "for the encouragement of the people of God in all ages...when they find themselves an unequal match for their enemies."

---

## Chapter 18: God's Rest and Care for His People

This chapter addresses nations shadowing with wings—possibly Ethiopia, whose king Tirhakah attempted to help Judah against Assyria.

**Universal Attention Required (v. 3)**
"All ye inhabitants of the world...see ye, when he lifteth up an ensign on the mountains; and when he bloweth a trumpet, hear ye." God summons the world to notice: "All must observe the motions of the divine providence."

**God's Rest in Providence (v. 4)**
"For so the LORD said unto me, I will take my rest, and I will consider in my dwelling place." Henry explains: "It is not as if he were weary of governing the world...but the Eternal Mind is always easy." Though God may seem inactive, "he knows very well what men are doing and what he himself will do."

**God's Care for His People**
"He will regard his dwelling-place; his eye and his heart are...upon it for good continually." The imagery: "Like a clear heat after rain...like a dew and a cloud in the heat of harvest, which are very welcome."

Henry applies: "There is that in God which is a shelter and refreshment to his people in all weathers...Is the weather cool? There is that in his favour which will warm them. Is it hot? There is that in his favour which will cool them."

**The Pruning (vv. 5-6)**
"Afore the harvest...he shall both cut off the sprigs with pruning hooks, and take away and cut down the branches." When the enemy's plans ripen, God acts: "God shall destroy that army as easily as the husbandman cuts off the sprigs of the vine."

**The Present to the Lord (v. 7)**
"In that time shall the present be brought unto the LORD of hosts...to the place of the name of the LORD of hosts, the mount Zion." Henry sees: (1) conversion of Ethiopians in latter days; (2) spoil from Sennacherib's army brought to the Lord.

Principle: "The present that is brought to the Lord of hosts must be brought to the place of the name of the Lord of hosts; what is offered to God must be offered in the way that he has appointed."

---

## Chapter 19: The Burden of Egypt—Judgment and Conversion

Egypt had been Israel's house of bondage, yet Jews repeatedly trusted Egypt for help. Henry notes: "To prevent all this mischief, Egypt must be mortified."

**God's Direct Judgment (vv. 1-4)**
"The LORD rideth upon a swift cloud, and shall come into Egypt." Unlike other prophecies mentioning invaders, God Himself brings judgment. Idols—Isis, Osiris, Apis—"shall be moved at his presence."

**Civil War and Foolish Counsel (vv. 2-4, 11-15)**
"I will set the Egyptians against the Egyptians...city against city." God will "mingle a perverse spirit" among them. Famous counsellors "become brutish"; princes who boasted of wisdom and royal lineage are exposed as fools.

Henry observes: "A kingdom thus divided against itself would soon be brought to desolation."

**Cruel Tyranny (v. 4)**
"The Egyptians will I give over into the hand of a cruel lord." Henry sees poetic justice: "The barbarous usage which the Egyptian task masters gave to God's Israel long ago was remembered against them."

**The Nile Fails (vv. 5-10)**
Egypt depended on the Nile's flooding since "Egypt was never watered with the rain of heaven." When the river dries up, "every thing sown by the brooks will wither." Fishermen lament, workers are confounded. Henry notes self-infliction: "Kings and nobles...drain water from the main river to their own houses...preferring their private convenience before the public good."

**Terror and Weakness (vv. 16-17)**
"Egypt shall be like unto women...afraid and fear because of the shaking of the hand of the LORD." Even Judah becomes "a terror unto Egypt." Henry asks: "If judgment begin at the house of God, where will it end?"

**PROMISES TO EGYPT (vv. 18-25)**
Henry declares: "The sun of comfort here breaks forth...the sun of righteousness." Ultimate fulfillment comes "in the conversion of Egypt to the faith of Christ, by the preaching of Mark the Evangelist."

**Five Cities Speak Canaan's Language (v. 18)**
"Converting grace, by changing the heart, changes the language." Heliopolis, "the most infamous of all the cities of Egypt for idolatry," undergoes "a wonderful reformation."

**Altar and Pillar to the Lord (vv. 19-22)**
"There shall be an altar to the LORD in the midst of the land of Egypt"—representing Christ, "the great altar, who sanctifies every gift," with "gospel sacrifices of prayer and praise." A pillar appears: "an open profession" visible to strangers entering Egypt's borders.

God sends "a saviour, and a great one"—"doubtless Jesus Christ," who "delivered the Gentile nations from the service of dumb idols."

**Highway of Peace (vv. 23-25)**
Former enemies—Egypt and Assyria—"shall trade with one another, and every thing that passes between them shall be friendly." Henry urges: "Those who have communion with the same God...should put an end to all heats and animosities."

The climax: "In that day shall Israel be the third with Egypt and with Assyria, even a blessing in the midst of the land: Whom the LORD of hosts shall bless, saying, Blessed be Egypt my people, and Assyria the work of my hands, and Israel mine inheritance."

Though Egypt was a house of bondage and Assyria an unjust invader, "all is forgiven and forgotten." Henry concludes: "Those that are united in the love and blessing of God ought, for that reason, to be united to each other in charity."

---

## Chapter 20: Isaiah's Symbolic Nakedness—A Warning Against Trust in Allies

**Historical Context (v. 1)**
Dated to when the Assyrian general Tartan captured Ashdod, a Philistine stronghold.

**The Strange Command (v. 2)**
God commands Isaiah to remove his sackcloth and shoes, walking about in only undergarments. Henry acknowledges this was "a great hardship"—damaging his reputation, exposing him to "contempt and ridicule," endangering his health by risking "a cold, which might throw him into a fever."

Yet Isaiah obeyed: "When we are in the way of our duty we may trust God both with our credit and with our safety." The people needed visual instruction: "If the dress was scandalous, yet the design was glorious."

**The Meaning Explained (vv. 3-4)**
God calls Isaiah "my servant" precisely because of this costly obedience. The sign foretold that Assyrians would lead away "the Egyptians prisoners, and the Ethiopians captives, young and old, naked and barefoot."

Henry emphasizes pathos: "A very piteous sight...to see those who had gone all their days well dressed now stripped...to the shame of Egypt."

**Application (vv. 5-6)**
Those trusting Egypt and Ethiopia for protection "shall be ashamed of them." Judah learns: nations hoping Tirhakah or mighty Egypt would stop the Assyrians found instead that "by attempting to oppose the king of Assyria...did but expose themselves."

Henry's crucial application: "Those that make any creature their expectation and glory...will sooner or later be ashamed of it." But disappointment in creature confidences should "drive us to God; for, if we flee to him for help, our expectation shall not be frustrated."

# CHAPTERS 21-26: PROPHECIES OF JUDGMENT AND REDEMPTION

## CHAPTER 21: THREE BURDENS—BABYLON, DUMAH, AND ARABIA

This chapter contains three prophecies against nations Israel might trust instead of God. Foreign affairs must be observed to teach that creature confidences are temporary.

**I. THE BURDEN OF THE DESERT OF THE SEA (vv. 1-10)—BABYLON**

Babylon is called "desert of the sea" because it was flat and watered by the Euphrates. God repeatedly prophesies Babylon's fall so His people would not fear its rise nor despair of relief when captive there.

*The Medo-Persian Attack (vv. 1-2)*: Elam and Media invade "as whirlwinds from the south, so suddenly, so strongly." Treacherous Babylonian officers like Gadatas and Gobryas betray the king. Those who made prey of neighbors through fraud and violence meet their match—poetic justice.

*Contrasting Reactions (vv. 2-4)*: Captives rejoice at Babylon's siege—"all their sighing will cease." But the king grieves: "my loins filled with pain...the night of my pleasure hath he turned into fear." This was literally fulfilled in Belshazzar (Daniel 5:6) on the night of feasting. Such vain mirth must be checked; God brings all things into judgment.

*The Watchman's Report (vv. 6-9)*: God commands: "Set a watchman, let him declare what he seeth." The watchman reports seeing Persian and Median chariots. After standing faithful watch, he announces: "Babylon is fallen, is fallen; and all the graven images of her gods he hath broken unto the ground."

*God's Threshing Floor (v. 10)*: God calls His people "my threshing, and the corn of my floor," applying these lessons: (1) The church is God's floor; (2) True believers are corn, hypocrites are chaff; (3) God's people must expect to be threshed by afflictions; (4) This threshing is by His appointment and under His restraint.

**II. THE BURDEN OF DUMAH (vv. 11-12)—EDOM**

Someone cries out: "Watchman, what of the night?" Ministers are as watchmen who knock at every door. "What of the night?" means both "What time?" (Is it time to awake from sin?) and "What tidings?" (What dangers approach?).

The watchman's answer—"The morning cometh, and also the night"—reflects that in God's providence, morning and night succeed each other. God usually gives a morning of opportunity before sending a night of calamity. The exhortation follows: "If ye will enquire, enquire ye: return, come"—be inquisitive, penitent, willing, and obedient.

**III. THE BURDEN UPON ARABIA (vv. 13-17)**

The Dedanites (descended from Abraham through Keturah) and peoples of Tema and Kedar (descended from Ishmael) face invasion. Traveling companies of Dedanim, accustomed to the high roads, must lodge in forests as refugees.

Pastoral applications: (1) Look for distress ourselves—we know not what straits we may face. Those accustomed to hardship bear calamity better. (2) Look with compassion upon those in distress, not knowing how soon their case may be ours. The Temanites are praised for bringing water to the thirsty and bread to the fleeing.

Kedar's glory—famous archers and mighty men—"shall vanish away and fail." Within one year, judgment comes. Neither archer's skill nor warriors' courage can protect a people from God's judgments. The prophecy is "ratified by the truth of God": the Lord God of Israel has spoken, and His strength will not lie.

---

## CHAPTER 22: THE VALLEY OF VISION—JERUSALEM'S RESPONSE TO CRISIS

This chapter concerns Jerusalem itself—"the valley of vision"—where God was known and prophets received visions. A sobering principle: church privileges, if not improved, will not secure men from God's judgments. The higher people are lifted up in means and mercies, the heavier their doom if they abuse them.

**I. JERUSALEM'S PANIC (vv. 1-7)**

Sennacherib's siege creates chaos. The formerly "tumultuous city, a joyous city" is paralyzed—people crowd housetops to watch or hide. Let both businessmen and sportsmen "rejoice as though they rejoiced not."

Rulers "fled from far" to Jerusalem, abandoning their cities without resistance. Fear "bound them from the bow"—paralyzed so they could not fight. God can easily dispirit men; fear certainly will.

Isaiah weeps: "Look away from me; I will weep bitterly...because of the spoiling of the daughter of my people." Enemy forces—Elam with quivers, Kir uncovering shields—fill the choicest valleys with chariots.

**II. JUDAH'S TWO GREAT SINS**

*(1) Contempt of God's Goodness (vv. 8-11)*: Jerusalem made diligent preparations—inspecting armor, surveying wall breaches, securing water, numbering houses for contributions, building defensive ditches. We must do our best in times of danger, yet Jerusalem's sin was threefold: (1) They did not design God's glory in what they did; (2) They did not depend on Him for blessing; (3) They did not give thanks for natural advantages like the old pool.

*(2) Contempt of God's Wrath (vv. 12-14)*: God called to weeping, mourning, baldness, and sackcloth—repentance was His purpose. Instead: "Behold joy and gladness, slaying oxen, and killing sheep, eating flesh, and drinking wine: let us eat and drink; for to morrow we shall die." They mocked God, made jest of dying, and ridiculed the doctrine of a future state. If there were no afterlife, their logic might hold (1 Corinthians 15:32), "but if for all these things God shall bring us into judgment, it is at our peril."

The verdict: "Surely this iniquity shall not be purged from you till ye die." This is sin against the remedy, baffling the utmost means of conviction.

**III. SHEBNA DISPLACED (vv. 15-19, 25)**

Shebna held offices of "treasurer" and "over the house" from "boundless ambition and covetousness." Likely a foreigner, he possibly kept traitorous correspondence with Assyria's king. Even Hezekiah's reforms could not ensure good servants.

God reproves his pride: "What hast thou here? and whom hast thou here?"—a mere plebeian building an elaborate tomb. Those making stately monuments for their pride forget that however beautiful outwardly, within they are full of dead men's bones.

The judgment: "The LORD will carry thee away...will surely violently turn and toss thee like a ball into a large country." Those who toss others when in power will be justly turned and tossed themselves. Though Shebna thought himself "fastened like a nail," he would be "tossed like a ball; for here have we no continuing city."

**IV. ELIAKIM ELEVATED (vv. 20-24)**

God calls His "servant Eliakim," one who "has approved himself faithful in other employments." Those diligent in low spheres stand fairest for God's preferment.

He receives Shebna's "robe" and "girdle" and "the key of the house of David upon his shoulders": "He shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open." Henry immediately applies this to Christ: "Our Lord Jesus describes his own power as Mediator by an allusion to this" (Revelation 3:7). Christ's power in God's kingdom is absolute, irresistible, and uncontrollable.

Eliakim is "fastened as a nail in a sure place"—secure and permanent. He shall be "a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem," taking care of public interests with parental love. Rulers should be fathers to those under their government.

All his family's glory "hangs upon him." Henry sees this supremely fulfilled in Christ: "All the glory of his father's house hangs upon him, is derived from him, and depends upon him; even the meanest that belong to his church are welcome to him, and he is able to bear the stress of them all."

---

## CHAPTER 23: THE BURDEN OF TYRE—PRIDE BROUGHT LOW

Tyre was "one of the most celebrated cities for trade and merchandise" in the world—a wealthy seaport near Asher's territory. It was "their faithful ally" under David and Solomon, for "trading cities maintain their grandeur...by commerce with them," not conquest.

**I. TYRE FLOURISHING (vv. 1-8)**

Henry catalogs Tyre's greatness: (1) Zidon "replenished" Tyre—"the daughter had outgrown the mother"; (2) Egypt's trade enriched her—Nile's "harvest was her revenue"; (3) She became "the mart of the nations, the great emporium"; (4) She was "a joyous city, noted for mirth and jollity...balls, plays, operas"; (5) Her "antiquity was of ancient days," making her proud and secure; (6) She was "a crowning city"—"her merchants are princes...her traffickers are the honourable of the earth."

**II. TYRE FALLING (vv. 1-14)**

"Howl, ye ships of Tarshish; for it is laid waste." The prophecy fitly begins and ends with this cry, for Tyre's glory "depended upon its shipping." Desolation includes: the harbor ruined; inhabitants struck silent; neighbors ashamed and pained; merchants fleeing; those unable to escape "carried into captivity."

Henry asks poignantly: "Is this your joyous city, whose antiquity is of ancient days?" Those living in greatest pomp and splendor know not what hardships they may face before death.

*The Divine Purpose (vv. 8-11)*: "Who hath taken this counsel against Tyre, the crowning city?" Answer: "The LORD of hosts hath purposed it, to stain the pride of all glory, and to bring into contempt all the honourable of the earth."

Two lessons: (1) God convinced men "of the vanity and uncertainty of all earthly glory...a withering, fading, perishing thing even when it seems most substantial"; (2) He prevented "their being puffed up, and confident of the continuance of it." Tyre's ruins proclaim that "he who exalts himself shall be abased."

*The Instruments (v. 13)*: The Chaldeans destroy Tyre as Assyrians brought Babylon to ruin. "If we looked more upon the falling and withering of others, we should not be so confident...of the continuance of our own flourishing and standing."

**III. TYRE RESTORED (vv. 15-18)**

Tyre shall be "forgotten seventy years"—the same period as Jerusalem's captivity. Nebuchadnezzar's folly: making himself master of Tyre when all inhabitants were driven out. Cyrus likely released the Tyrians along with the Jews.

Tyre will "sing as a harlot"—aggressively recovering trade. "Take a harp, go about the city...that thou mayest be remembered." She "shall commit fornication with all the kingdoms of the world"—resuming commerce. "The love of worldly wealth is a spiritual whoredom."

*The Gospel Transformation (v. 18)*: "Her merchandise, and her hire, shall be holiness to the Lord." Her wealth "shall not be treasured nor laid up" for pride, "but it shall be laid out in acts of piety and charity" for priests and ministers—"to eat sufficiently, and for durable clothing."

Henry sees this fulfilled when Jews and Tyrians shared Babylonian captivity, leading some Tyrians to worship the true God. "Tyre and Sidon were better disposed to religion in Christ's time than the cities of Israel" (Matthew 11:21), and Acts records Christians at Tyre.

Application: "Both the merchandise (the employment we follow) and the hire (the gain of our employments) must be holiness to the Lord." Our wealth "need not be treasured and laid up on earth; for it is treasured and laid up in heaven, in bags that wax not old."

---

## CHAPTER 24: THE EARTH MADE EMPTY—UNIVERSAL JUDGMENT AND HOPE

Chapters 24-27 form an interwoven sermon of threatenings and promises. Henry suggests this represents "the calamitous state of mankind" in general and "the many miseries which human life is liable to." The purpose is to produce "a thorough conviction of the vanity of the world, and its insufficiency to make us happy," drawing us "towards another world."

**I. THE EARTH STRIPPED AND SHAKEN (vv. 1-12)**

"Behold, the LORD maketh the earth empty, and maketh it waste, and turneth it upside down." This is more than one nation's judgment: "Sin has turned the earth upside down; the earth has become quite a different thing to man from what it was when God made it to be his habitation." All earthly things "are empty of all solid comfort...a little thing makes them waste."

*Universal Scope (v. 2)*: Judgment falls on all ranks—"as with the people, so with the priest; as with the servant, so with his master; as with the buyer, so with the seller; as with the lender, so with the borrower." "Let not those that are advanced in the world set their inferiors at too great a distance, because they know not how soon they may be set upon a level with them."

*The Cause: Sin (v. 5)*: "The earth also is defiled under the inhabitants thereof; because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant." They violated: (1) "the laws of their creation"; (2) "the ordinance of revealed religion"; (3) "the everlasting covenant" with Noah and subsequent generations.

"Therefore hath the curse devoured the earth...the inhabitants of the earth are burned, and few men left." "The world we live in is a world of disappointment, a vale of tears, and a dying world."

*The End of Mirth (vv. 7-12)*: "All the merry-hearted do sigh. The mirth of tabrets ceaseth, the noise of them that rejoice endeth, the joy of the harp ceaseth." Wine and music—instruments of carnal joy—fail. "Such is the nature of carnal mirth, it is but as the crackling of thorns under a pot...Great laughters commonly end in a sigh."

"The city of confusion is broken down: every house is shut up." "How soon can God make a city of order a city of confusion, and then it will soon be a city of desolation!"

**II. THE REMNANT'S JOY (vv. 13-15)**

Amid universal ruin, "there shall be as the shaking of an olive tree, and as the gleaning grapes"—a small preserved remnant "who have laid up their treasure in heaven and not in things below, and therefore can keep up their comfort and joy in God even when the earth mourns."

"They shall lift up their voice, they shall sing for the majesty of the LORD." "When the mirth of carnal worldlings ceases the joy of the saints is as lively as ever...Those that rejoice in the Lord can rejoice in tribulation."

The exhortation: "Glorify ye the LORD in the fires"—even in affliction's furnace—"even the name of the LORD God of Israel in the isles of the sea." Henry recalls "the three children, and the martyrs that sang at the stake."

**III. CONTINUED TERROR FOR SINNERS (vv. 16-22)**

Even hearing the remnant's songs—"glory to the righteous"—the prophet laments: "My leanness, my leanness, woe unto me! the treacherous dealers have dealt treacherously." Sin pervades: "Men are false to one another...they are all false to their God."

"Fear, and the pit, and the snare, are upon thee, O inhabitant of the earth." Escaping one judgment brings another: "Those who are secure because they have escaped one judgment know not how soon another may arrest them...we are least safe when we are most secure."

Cosmic upheaval follows: "The earth is utterly broken down...is clean dissolved...is moved exceedingly...shall reel to and fro like a drunkard." Why? "The transgression thereof shall be heavy upon it." "Sin is a burden to the whole creation; it is a heavy burden, a burden under which it groans now and will sink at last."

*God's Judgment on Rulers (vv. 21-22)*: "The LORD shall punish the host of the high ones...and the kings of the earth upon the earth." "Hosts of princes are no more before God than hosts of common men." They shall be "gathered together as prisoners...and shut up in the prison, and after many days shall they be visited"—either for final punishment or (like Jehoiachin) granted relief.

**IV. GOD'S GLORIOUS REIGN (v. 23)**

"Then the moon shall be confounded, and the sun ashamed, when the LORD of hosts shall reign in mount Zion, and in Jerusalem, and before his ancients gloriously." God's glory outshines all creatures and earthly rulers: "The glory of the Creator infinitely outshines the glory of the brightest creatures. In the great day...the sun shall by his transcendent lustre be turned into darkness."

---

## CHAPTER 25: A SONG OF PRAISE—THE GOSPEL FEAST AND VICTORY

After chapter 24's dark prophecies, "this chapter looks as pleasantly upon the church as the former looked dreadfully upon the world." It points beyond immediate deliverances "to the grace of the gospel and the glory which is the crown and consummation of that grace."

**I. THE PROPHET'S PRAISE (vv. 1-5)**

"O LORD, thou art my God; I will exalt thee, I will praise thy name." When God punishes the kings of the earth, a poor prophet can say with humble boldness: "O Lord! thou art my God." "When we praise God we exalt him; not that we can make him higher than he is, but we must make him appear to ourselves and others to be higher than he does."

*Reasons for Praise*:

(1) "Thou hast done wonderful things; thy counsels of old are faithfulness and truth"—God's works are "wonderful proofs of his power...and wonderful proofs of his goodness beyond what such sinful creatures as we are could expect."

(2) "Thou hast made of a city a heap"—proud cities leveled. "How many of the cities of Israel have long since been heaps and ruins! God hereby teaches us that here we have no continuing city."

(3) "Thou hast been a strength to the poor...a refuge from the storm, a shadow from the heat." "God is a sufficient protection to his people in all weathers, hot and cold, wet and dry." When enemies rage "like a blast of wind, loud, and blustering, and furious...God will be such a shelter to his people that they shall be able to stand the shock."

**II. THE GOSPEL FEAST (vv. 6-8)**

This is "a prophecy of the salvation and the grace brought unto us by Jesus Christ." Gospel preparations are "often in the New Testament set forth by the similitude of a feast" (Matthew 22:1).

"In this mountain shall the LORD of hosts make unto all people a feast of fat things, a feast of wines on the lees, of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined."

Henry explains: (1) "God himself is the Master of the feast"; (2) "The guests invited are all people, Gentiles as well as Jews"; (3) "The place is Mount Zion"—the gospel church; (4) "The provision is very rich"—comforting like fine wine for those "of a heavy heart, being under convictions of sin and mourning for it, that they may drink and forget their misery...may be of good cheer, knowing that their sins are forgiven."

*The Veil Destroyed (v. 7)*: "He will destroy...the veil that is spread over all nations, for they all sit in darkness." "This veil the Lord will destroy, by the light of his gospel shining in the world, and the power of his Spirit opening men's eyes to receive it."

*Death Swallowed Up (v. 8)*: "He will swallow up death in victory." Paul quotes this in 1 Corinthians 15:54: "It is at our resurrection through Christ that the saying here written shall be brought to pass." Henry applies it fourfold: (1) Christ will triumph over death in His resurrection; (2) The happiness of the saints shall be out of the reach of death; (3) Believers may triumph over death: "O death! where is thy sting?"; (4) At the resurrection, death will be forever swallowed up of victory.

*Tears Wiped Away*: "The Lord GOD will wipe away tears from off all faces." This is partly fulfilled in gospel comfort for mourners over sin, but "in the joys of heaven, and nowhere short of them, will fully be brought to pass this saying."

*Reproach Removed*: "The rebuke of his people shall he take away." God's people suffer "calumnies and misrepresentations"—but "their righteousness shall be brought forth as the light."

**III. THE CHURCH'S TRIUMPH (vv. 9-12)**

"It shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the LORD; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation." With such joy will those entertain tidings of the Redeemer, and with such song will glorified saints enter the joy of their Lord.

Key lessons: (1) "God himself must have the glory of all: 'Lo, this is our God'"; (2) "The longer it has been expected the more welcome it is...it is not in vain to wait for him"; (3) "It is matter of joy unspeakable"; (4) "It is an encouragement to hope...as for God, our God, his work is perfect."

*God's Hand Rests on Zion (v. 10)*: "The church and people of God shall have continued proofs of God's presence with them." Meanwhile, "Moab shall be trodden down...as straw is trodden down for the dunghill"—representing all adversaries. God's active power is pictured as a swimmer: "he will be doing for them on all sides...he will easily and effectually put by the opposition."

"The destruction of Moab is typical of Christ's victory over death...his spoiling principalities and powers in his cross...his pulling down Satan's strong-holds by the preaching of his gospel."

---

## CHAPTER 26: A SONG OF TRUST—PERFECT PEACE AND RESURRECTION HOPE

This chapter continues the song begun in chapter 25, teaching God's people "to triumph in the safety and holy security" of divine protection, even in "the worst and darkest times." It is "written for the support and assistance of the faith and hope of God's people in all ages."

**I. THE STRONG CITY AND PERFECT PEACE (vv. 1-4)**

"We have a strong city; salvation will God appoint for walls and bulwarks." The church is "incorporated by the charter of the everlasting covenant...strong, for it has walls and bulwarks, and those of God's own appointing."

"Open ye the gates, that the righteous nation which keepeth the truth may enter in." The righteous "keep the truths of God, a firm belief of which will have a commanding influence upon the regularity of the whole conversation. Good principles fixed in the head will produce good resolutions in the heart and good practices in the life."

*The Famous Promise (v. 3)*: "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee." Henry expounds: "peace, peace"—"inward peace, outward peace, peace with God, peace of conscience, peace at all times, under all events." The key is having a mind "stayed upon God...firmly and faithfully adhering to him, with an entire satisfaction in him."

*The Command (v. 4)*: "Trust ye in the LORD for ever: for in the LORD JEHOVAH is everlasting strength." "Whatever we trust to the world for, it will be but for a moment...But what we trust in God for will last as long as we shall last. For in the Lord Jehovah...there is a rock of ages, a firm and lasting foundation."

**II. GOD'S WAYS WITH PROUD AND HUMBLE (vv. 5-11)**

"He bringeth down them that dwell on high; the lofty city, he layeth it low...The foot shall tread it down, even the feet of the poor." "He does not say, Great armies shall tread them down; but...even the feet of the poor shall do it."

"The way of the just is uprightness: thou, most upright, dost weigh the path of the just." God "considers it, and will give them grace sufficient for them."

*Waiting on God in Darkness (vv. 8-9)*: "In the way of thy judgments, O LORD, have we waited for thee; the desire of our soul is to thy name...With my soul have I desired thee in the night." Henry applies: (1) "Our great concern must be for God's name"; (2) "Our great comfort must be in the remembrance of that name"; (3) "Our desires towards God must be inward, fervent, and sincere"; (4) "Even in the darkest night of affliction our desires must be towards God"; (5) "Those that would seek God and find him must seek betimes."

*Judgments Designed to Teach (v. 9)*: "When thy judgments are in the earth, the inhabitants of the world will learn righteousness." "The intention of afflictions is to teach us righteousness."

*The Incorrigible Wicked (vv. 10-11)*: "Let favour be showed to the wicked, yet will he not learn righteousness." "They will not see; but they shall see, and be ashamed...the fire of thine enemies shall devour them."

**III. REFLECTIONS ON GOD'S DEALINGS (vv. 12-18)**

"Thou wilt ordain peace for us: for thou also hast wrought all our works in us." "Whatever good work is done by us, it is owing to a good work wrought by the grace of God in us."

"Other lords beside thee have had dominion over us"—foreign oppressors and idols. "The same may be our penitent reflection: Other lords, besides God, have had dominion over us; every lust has been our lord."

"In trouble have they visited thee, they poured out a prayer when thy chastening was upon them." Afflictions "bring us to God, quicken us to our duty...Afflictions bring us to secret prayer."

The church's failed efforts: "We have been with child, we have been in pain, we have as it were brought forth wind; we have not wrought any deliverance in the earth." "A righteous cause may be strenuously pleaded both by prayer and endeavour...and yet for a great while may be left under a cloud."

**IV. THE RESURRECTION PROMISE (v. 19)**

"Thy dead men shall live, together with my dead body shall they arise. Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust: for thy dew is as the dew of herbs, and the earth shall cast out the dead."

Henry applies this threefold: (1) To the church's restoration—"those who were thought to be dead...shall appear again in their former vigour"; (2) To "the spiritual resurrection of those that were dead in sin, by the power of Christ's gospel and grace"; (3) To "the last resurrection, when dead saints shall live, and rise together with Christ's dead body."

**V. HIDING IN THE CHAMBERS (vv. 20-21)**

"Come, my people, enter thou into thy chambers, and shut thy doors about thee: hide thyself as it were for a little moment, until the indignation be overpast."

# Chapters 27-32: Isaiah Commentary

## CHAPTER 27: LEVIATHAN SLAIN AND THE VINEYARD KEPT

This chapter concludes a four-chapter sermon (24-27) mixing "judgment and mercy"—applicable to the grace of the gospel and God's providences concerning the Christian church.

### I. LEVIATHAN DESTROYED

"In that day the LORD with his sore and great and strong sword shall punish leviathan the piercing serpent... and he shall slay the dragon that is in the sea."

The church has many enemies, but God addresses the most formidable ones—compared to leviathan for strength, dragons for rage and fury, and serpents for penetrating counsel. Great princes opposing God's people are in God's account as dragons and serpents.

Christ fulfills this: He disarmed and cast out the prince of this world, and with his strong sword—the virtue of his death and the preaching of his gospel—destroys the devil, that great leviathan, that old serpent.

### II. GOD'S VINEYARD

"In that day sing ye unto her, A vineyard of red wine. I the LORD do keep it; I will water it every moment: lest any hurt it, I will keep it night and day."

The church is a vineyard yielding the best grapes. God protects it night and day, and nourishes it: the dews of God's grace and blessing continually descend upon it.

**Making Peace with God**: "Fury is not in me"—yet if briers and thorns resist him, he will burn them. The alternative: "Let him take hold of my strength, that he may make peace with me; and he shall make peace with me."

This gospel summary presents: (1) a quarrel supposed between God and man; (2) a gracious invitation to reconcile—Christ crucified is the power of God; (3) arguments: time and space are given us; it is vain to contest with God; this is the only sure way to reconciliation.

**The Growing Church**: "He shall cause them that come of Jacob to take root: Israel shall blossom and bud, and fill the face of the world with fruit." The gospel church shall be more firmly fixed than the Jewish church and spread further. The preaching of the gospel brought forth fruit in all the world.

### III. JUDGMENT MIXED WITH MERCY

God's people may suffer, but "it shall not be as those are smitten and slain" who persecuted them. The seed of the woman has only his heel bruised, but the serpent has his head crushed.

**Measured Affliction**: "In measure, when it shooteth forth, thou wilt debate with it: he stayeth his rough wind in the day of the east wind." God deals out afflictions as a wise physician prescribes medicines, never suffering them to be tempted above what they are able.

**Affliction's Purpose**: "By this therefore shall the iniquity of Jacob be purged; and this is all the fruit to take away his sin." This is great kindness—the harm affliction does them is taking away their sin, especially destroying idolatry completely. Since the Babylonian captivity, the Jewish people have had a rooted aversion to idols.

**Warning to the Senseless**: Wicked people, however wise in other things, are of no understanding in their greatest concerns. "The defenced city shall be desolate... it is a people of no understanding."

### IV. THE GREAT TRUMPET

"In that day... ye shall be gathered one by one, O ye children of Israel... the great trumpet shall be blown, and they shall come which were ready to perish in the land of Assyria, and the outcasts in the land of Egypt, and shall worship the LORD in the holy mount at Jerusalem."

The scattered shall be gathered one by one—the remnant saved consists of few, and those saved with difficulty, scarcely saved.

The great trumpet shall be blown—fulfilled by Cyrus's proclamation like the jubilee trumpet. But this also refers to the preaching of the gospel by which sinners are gathered to grace, and the archangel's trumpet at the last day by which saints shall be gathered to glory.

Their purpose: To worship the Lord in the holy mount. Liberty to worship God is the most valuable and desirable liberty.

---

## CHAPTER 28: EPHRAIM'S DRUNKARDS, ZION'S CORNERSTONE

This chapter addresses both Ephraim (the ten northern tribes) and Judah, written for admonition and warning.

### I. WOE TO EPHRAIM'S DRUNKARDS

"Woe to the crown of pride, to the drunkards of Ephraim, whose glorious beauty is a fading flower."

Ephraim's fertile valleys became occasion for pride and drunkenness. Those rich in the world are apt to be high-minded. Drunkenness makes men yield to sin, bringing them into bondage and ruin—constitutions broken, health ruined.

God's judgment: The Lord has a mighty and strong one which as a tempest of hail shall cast them down. Their glorious beauty is but a fading flower—like hasty fruit before summer, quickly devoured.

**Promise to the Remnant**: "In that day shall the LORD of hosts be for a crown of glory, and for a diadem of beauty, unto the residue of his people." God becomes a spirit of judgment and strength. This refers to Christ—he is made of God to us wisdom, righteousness, and strength.

**Judah's Drunkenness Too**: Priest and prophet have erred through strong drink—they err in vision and stumble in judgment. All tables are full of vomit and filthiness. What an odious thing is drunkenness!

### II. THE UNTEACHABLE PEOPLE

"Whom shall he teach knowledge?... them that are weaned from the milk."

God taught them patiently: "Precept upon precept... line upon line; here a little, and there a little." Teachers should accommodate themselves to learners' capacity.

The prophets offered rest: "This is the rest wherewith ye may cause the weary to rest; and this is the refreshing: yet they would not hear." The service of God is the only true rest for those weary of serving sin.

But the people mocked, making a song of the prophet's words in a mocking rhyme. It is great impiety to make a jest of sacred things.

Therefore judgment: "With stammering lips and another tongue will he speak to this people"—foreign invaders whose language they won't understand. If the word doesn't convert it condemns, that they might go and fall backward, broken, snared, and taken.

### III. SCORNFUL RULERS AND ZION'S CORNERSTONE

The scornful men ruling Jerusalem boasted: "We have made a covenant with death... the overflowing scourge shall not come unto us: for we have made lies our refuge." They thought political cunning would save them.

**The True Foundation**: "Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make haste."

Jesus Christ is a foundation of God's laying—a tried stone, a trying stone, a precious stone, a cornerstone. He that believes these promises and rests upon them shall not make haste, no panic or shifting to and fro. He shall not be confounded; his expectations shall not be frustrated.

**False Refuges Swept Away**: "The hail shall sweep away the refuge of lies... your covenant with death shall be disannulled." When the scourge comes, "it shall be a vexation only to understand the report." "The bed is shorter than that a man can stretch himself on it: and the covering narrower than that he can wrap himself in it." Those not building on Christ as their foundation can never be easy, safe, nor warm.

**God's Strange Work**: "The LORD shall rise up as in mount Perazim... that he may do his work, his strange work." It is strange work if he turn to be their enemy, for he rather delights in showing mercy.

Warning: "Be ye not mockers, lest your bands be made strong." Mocking the messengers of the Lord was Jerusalem's measure-filling sin.

### IV. THE PARABLE OF THE HUSBANDMAN

God is like a wise farmer who knows when to plough and when to sow. "The fitches are not threshed with a threshing instrument... but the fitches are beaten out with a staff, and the cummin with a rod." Bread corn is bruised; he will not ever be threshing it.

God's church is his husbandry. If Christ is the true vine, his Father is the husbandman. The word is sown suitably; afflictions are threshing-instruments designed to loosen us from the world. God makes use of them as there is occasion—never heavier than needed. If the rod and staff will answer the end, he will not use his cart-wheel.

"This also cometh forth from the LORD of hosts, which is wonderful in counsel, and excellent in working." God's working is according to his will; he never acts against his own mind. He is excellent in working because he is wonderful in counsel.

---

## CHAPTER 29: WOE TO ARIEL - SIEGE AND DELIVERANCE OF JERUSALEM

"Ariel" is Jerusalem—the lion of God—the city where David dwelt. As the lion is king among beasts, so was Jerusalem among cities. The repeated "Woe to Ariel, to Ariel" echoes Christ's "O Jerusalem, Jerusalem."

### THE DISTRESS OF JERUSALEM

External religious observance will not exempt them from judgment: "Add year to year; let them kill sacrifices"—continue your annual feasts, but as long as lives are unreformed and hearts unhumbled, let them not think thus to pacify an offended God. Hypocrites may be found in constant devout exercises but can never please God.

God will distress Ariel with siege and war. When men fight against us, we must see God contending with us. Jerusalem shall be humbled: "Thou shalt be brought down, and shalt speak out of the ground... thy speech shall whisper out of the dust"—faint and feeble, fearful, tame, and obliged to submit.

### THE DESTRUCTION OF JERUSALEM'S ENEMIES

Yet enemies "shall be like small dust... as chaff that passeth away: yea, it shall be at an instant suddenly." The Assyrian army was destroyed by an angel in an instant. The invaders' hopes shall prove vain dreams—like a hungry man who dreams of eating but awaketh, his soul empty.

### SPIRITUAL STUPIDITY REBUKED

The prophet stands amazed at the people's stupidity: "They are drunken, but not with wine"—intoxicated with love of pleasures and prejudices against religion. God has poured out a spirit of deep sleep—a righteous judgment to punish them for loving darkness rather than light. This applies to unbelieving Jews who rejected the gospel of Christ.

Even prophets, rulers, and seers are blindfolded. Divine revelation becomes "as the words of a book that is sealed"—the unlearned say "I am not learned," the learned say "it is sealed." Both excuses are groundless; God's prophets have been no unfaithful debtors either to the wise or unwise.

### CONDEMNATION OF LIP-SERVICE RELIGION

"This people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men."

Many whose religion is lip-labour only worship not according to God's appointment but their own inventions. Christ applies this to the Pharisees: "In vain they do worship God."

The punishment: "The wisdom of their wise men shall perish." They played the hypocrite and put a cheat upon God, so they are left to themselves to play the fool. Judgments on the mind, though least noticed, are most to be wondered at.

### REBUKE OF SECRET COUNSELS

"Woe unto them that seek deep to hide their counsel from the LORD... and they say, Who seeth us?" This is practical disbelief of God's omniscience. God will overturn their schemes with ease and absolute power as the potter forms his clay. Those denying God sees them effectively deny "He made me not" or that he has understanding.

### PROMISES OF TRANSFORMATION

God will turn things upside down his way: "Lebanon shall be turned into a fruitful field"—fulfilled when a great harvest of souls was gathered to Christ from among the Gentiles.

The deaf shall hear, the blind shall see "out of obscurity"—the gospel opens eyes. "The meek also shall increase their joy in the LORD"—even the poor among men may rejoice in the Holy One of Israel. The terrible one is brought to nought, the scorners cut off—those who made a man an offender for a word and laid a snare for him that reproveth in the gate.

The persecutors' methods are exposed: they watch for iniquity, seize on any slip of the tongue, and turn aside the just for a thing of nought. This applies to those who persecuted Christ and his apostles with strained innuendoes.

### JACOB'S VINDICATION

"Thus the Lord saith who redeemed Abraham"—He who called Abraham from idolatry will redeem his seed. "Jacob shall not now be ashamed, neither shall his face now wax pale." He shall see his children—the spiritual seed of faithful Abraham—sanctifying God's name. "Those that erred in spirit shall come to understanding, and they that murmured shall learn doctrine."

---

## CHAPTER 30: WOE TO EGYPT'S ALLIES - TRUST IN GOD ALONE

This chapter addresses Judah's sinful reliance on Egypt against the Assyrian threat. Israel's pattern: against the Syrians they sought the Assyrians; against the Assyrians they seek the Egyptians.

### THE SIN OF TRUSTING EGYPT

"Woe to the rebellious children, saith the LORD, that take counsel, but not of me." They walk to go down into Egypt without asking at God's mouth. If we distrust God's providence, we withdraw ourselves from our allegiance. They add sin to sin—their trouble came from sin, and instead of repenting, they trespass yet more.

They spare no expense—loading beasts with riches as bribes—travelling through perilous terrain. God would have helped them gratis; if they will have help from the Egyptians, they must pay dearly.

The verdict: "The Egyptians shall help in vain, and to no purpose... Their strength is to sit still." If we sit still in distress, hoping and quietly waiting for the LORD's salvation, this will be the strength of our souls. Those trusting God are never made ashamed; those putting confidence in creatures will sooner or later find it a reproach. The Creator is a rock of ages; the creature a broken reed.

### THE WRITTEN RECORD OF REBELLION

God commands Isaiah to write it in a book for the time to come forever: "This is a rebellious people, lying children, children that will not hear the law of the LORD."

They command the prophets: "See not... Prophesy not unto us right things, speak unto us smooth things, prophesy deceits... cause the Holy One of Israel to cease from before us." Those that silence good ministers and discountenance good preaching are rebels against God. Those deserving to be deceived desire to be so.

The punishment: "This iniquity shall be to you as a breach ready to fall... whose breaking cometh suddenly at an instant." Like the breaking of a potter's vessel in pieces—so thoroughly that there shall not be found in its bursting a sherd to take fire from the hearth or take water.

### THE REJECTED PRESCRIPTION

God's gracious offer: "In returning and rest shall ye be saved; in quietness and in confidence shall be your strength."

We must retire into ourselves with holy quietness, suppressing turbulent passions, and rely upon God with holy confidence. "And ye would not." Instead: "We will flee upon horses... we will ride upon the swift." They think themselves wiser than God. The consequence: your sin shall be your punishment—you will flee, therefore you shall flee. They shall become as a beacon upon a mountain—isolated survivors warning others.

### PROMISES TO THOSE WHO WAIT

"Therefore will the LORD wait, that he may be gracious unto you... for the LORD is a God of judgment: blessed are all they that wait for him." He will wait till you return to him and do it in the best and fittest time.

"He will be very gracious unto thee at the voice of thy cry; when he shall hear it, he will answer thee." The people shall dwell in Zion and weep no more. Though God may give the bread of adversity and water of affliction—coarse fare—"yet shall not thy teachers be removed into corners any more." Brown bread and the gospel are good fare. A famine of bread is not so great a judgment as a famine of God's word.

**Guidance promised**: "Thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it, when ye turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left." There are right-hand and left-hand errors, extremes on each side virtue. It is happy if by particular counsels of a faithful minister, friend, conscience checks, and God's Spirit strivings, we be set right.

They shall reject idols with disgust: "Thou shalt cast them away as a menstruous cloth." To all true penitents sin has become odious; they loathe it and themselves because of it.

Then prosperity follows: "He shall give the rain of thy seed... bread of the increase of the earth." And extraordinary joy: "The light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold"—partially fulfilled in deliverance from Assyria, more fully in gospel light exceeding Old Testament light as the sun does the moon.

### JUDGMENT ON ASSYRIA

"The name of the LORD cometh from far, burning with his anger... his tongue as a devouring fire."

Five instruments of destruction: an overflowing stream reaching the midst of the neck; a sieve of vanity to shake the nations; a bridle causing them to err; a grounded staff passing every place; and Tophet—ordained of old for the king.

Tophet refers to hell: Our Saviour calls future misery of the damned Gehenna, in allusion to the valley of Hinnom. This is prepared of old for the devil and his angels, for the greatest of sinners, the proudest—even for kings it is prepared. It is deep and large, sufficient to receive the world of the ungodly.

The result: "Ye shall have a song, as in the night when a holy solemnity is kept." Our joy in the fall of the church's enemies must be a holy joy.

---

## CHAPTER 31: GOD'S PROTECTION OVER JERUSALEM

This brief chapter continues rebuke of trusting Egypt and promises divine deliverance. This is the last of four chapters beginning with woe—to drunkards of Ephraim, to Ariel, to rebellious children, and now to those going down to Egypt for help.

### THE SIN OF TRUSTING EGYPT

"Woe to them that go down to Egypt for help; and stay on horses, and trust in chariots, because they are many... but they look not unto the Holy One of Israel, neither seek the LORD!"

They were attracted to Egypt's military power. The fundamental error: "The Egyptians are men, and not God; and their horses flesh, and not spirit." Do not make gods of them by making them your hope and confidence. Do not forsake the rock of ages for broken reeds nor the fountain of living waters for broken cisterns.

The consequence: "When the LORD shall stretch out his hand, both he that helpeth shall fall, and he that is holpen shall fall down."

### GOD AS LION AND BIRD

**The Lion**: "Like as the lion and the young lion roaring on his prey, when a multitude of shepherds is called forth against him, he will not be afraid of their voice... so shall the LORD of hosts come down to fight for mount Zion." The shepherds make noise but dare not approach; the lion ignores them. Whoever appear against God are but like poor simple shepherds shouting at a lion, who scorns notice.

**The Bird**: "As birds flying, so will the LORD of hosts defend Jerusalem." God was ready to gather Jerusalem as a hen gathers her brood under her wings. Like birds hovering over young ones to protect them, the Lord of hosts shall defend Jerusalem.

The language echoes the Passover: "Passing over he will preserve it." The Assyrian army would be routed by a destroying angel passing over Jerusalem, drawing his sword only against the besiegers.

### THE CALL TO REPENTANCE

"Turn ye unto him from whom the children of Israel have deeply revolted." The rebellion was deep—the stain of their sins has gone deeply into their nature; they have sunk deep into misery.

The promise: "In that day every man shall cast away his idols of silver, and his idols of gold." This will be: (1) a general reformation—every man beginning with his own idols; (2) a thorough reformation—parting with beloved sin; (3) a reformation upon a right principle—casting away idols because they have been a sin. Sin is the work of our own hands, but in working it we have worked our own ruin.

### THE ASSYRIAN'S DOOM

"Then shall the Assyrian fall with the sword, not of a mighty man; and the sword, not of a mean man, shall devour him." Not forcibly by a mighty man nor surreptitiously by a mean man, but by an angel's sword. God needs not armies of men who has legions of angels at command.

Sennacherib shall pass over to his strong hold for fear—fleeing what he cannot fight. His princes shall be afraid of every ensign, trembling at every banner.

Who accomplishes this? "The LORD, whose fire is in Zion and his furnace in Jerusalem"—meaning: (1) whose residence is there, the city of the great King; (2) who is there a consuming fire to all his enemies; (3) who has his altar there, on which holy fire is continually kept burning. If we keep up the fire of holy love and devotion in our hearts and houses, we may depend upon God to be a protection to us and them.

---

## CHAPTER 32: THE RIGHTEOUS KING AND THE OUTPOURING OF THE SPIRIT

This chapter prophesies of Hezekiah's reign with an eye to the kingdom of the Messiah, whose government was typified by the thrones of the house of David.

### THE KING WHO REIGNS IN RIGHTEOUSNESS

"Behold, a king shall reign in righteousness, and princes shall rule in judgment."

Good government requires the king to have princes under him as officers by whom he may act. They shall use their power according to law—protecting the good and punishing the bad. Christ reigns by rule and in righteousness will judge the world.

Such rulers become blessings: "A man shall be as a hiding place from the wind, and a covert from the tempest; as rivers of water in a dry place, as the shadow of a great rock in a weary land." When the greatest evils befall us—not only wind but tempest—they drive us to Christ, and in him we are not only safe but satisfied. The rock receives the battering to save those sheltering in it; so Christ bore the storm himself to keep it off from us.

### THE TRANSFORMED PEOPLE

When reformation comes, "the eyes of those that see shall not be dim"—God will bless prophets with visions and readers with understanding. "The ears of those that hear shall hearken diligently." The hearing ear and seeing eyes the Lord has made, has new-made, even both of them.

# Isaiah Commentary: Chapters 33-38

## Chapter 33: Woe to the Spoiler - God's Protection of Zion

This chapter addresses Judah and Jerusalem's distress from Sennacherib's invasion and their deliverance. Henry notes the prophecy alternates between distress and deliverance.

**Woe to the Assyrian Spoiler (vv. 1-4)**: "Woe to thee that spoilest, and thou wast not spoiled; and dealest treacherously, and they dealt not treacherously with thee!" The Assyrian spoiled those who never injured him and dealt treacherously with the faithful. "The righteous God often pays sinners in their own coin."

**The Prayer of God's People (v. 2)**: "O LORD, be gracious unto us; we have waited for thee." They pray for leaders and salvation in trouble. In spiritual warfare, "our own hands are not sufficient for us... God must not only strengthen our arms, but be himself our arm."

**God Exalted Through Deliverance (vv. 3-6)**: "At the noise of the tumult the people fled; at the lifting up of thyself the nations were scattered." The Assyrian camp becomes spoil for Jerusalem. God receives glory: "The LORD is exalted; for he dwelleth on high: he hath filled Zion with judgment and righteousness."

"Wisdom and knowledge shall be the stability of thy times, and strength of salvation: the fear of the LORD is his treasure." True religion is the true treasure of any prince or people.

**The Distress Described (vv. 7-9)**: "Their valiant ones shall cry without: the ambassadors of peace shall weep bitterly." The enemy "hath broken the covenant, he hath despised the cities, he regardeth no man."

**God Arises (vv. 10-12)**: "Now will I rise, saith the LORD; now will I be exalted." When all helpers fail, God's time to help has come. The Assyrians' destruction: "Ye shall conceive chaff, ye shall bring forth stubble: your breath, as fire, shall devour you."

**Sinners in Zion Afraid (vv. 13-14)**: "Hear, ye that are far off, what I have done; and, ye that are near, acknowledge my might." The hypocrites are terrified: "Who among us shall dwell with the devouring fire? who among us shall dwell with everlasting burnings?" God's wrath will burn those everlastingly who made themselves fuel for it.

**The Righteous Man's Security (vv. 15-16)**: "He that walketh righteously, and speaketh uprightly; he that despiseth the gain of oppressions, that shaketh his hands from holding of bribes, that stoppeth his ears from hearing of blood, and shutteth his eyes from seeing evil"—this man escapes the fire.

His reward: "He shall dwell on high: his place of defence shall be the munitions of rocks: bread shall be given him; his waters shall be sure." He shall have communion with God who becomes to him a rejoicing light.

**Jerusalem's Glorious Future (vv. 17-24)**: "Thine eyes shall see the king in his beauty"—Hezekiah restored, and Christ the King of kings. "They shall behold the land that is very far off"—believers behold the heavenly Canaan.

The terror remembered becomes joy. The fierce invaders gone, "Thou shalt not see a fierce people, a people of a deeper speech."

"Look upon Zion, the city of our solemnities: thine eyes shall see Jerusalem a quiet habitation, a tabernacle that shall not be taken down." God's church on earth is a tabernacle that, though it may be relocated, shall not be taken down while the world stands.

**The Lord Our Protector (vv. 21-22)**: "The glorious LORD will be unto us a place of broad rivers and streams; wherein shall go no galley with oars." God's presence and power are sufficient to make up any deficiencies. The great confession: "For the LORD is our judge, the LORD is our lawgiver, the LORD is our king; he will save us."

**The Enemy's Ruin, God's People's Gain (vv. 23-24)**: The Assyrians, like a ship with loose tackling, are becalmed when victory seemed certain. "Then is the prey of a great spoil divided; the lame take the prey."

The final blessing: "The inhabitant shall not say, I am sick: the people that dwell therein shall be forgiven their iniquity." Sin is the sickness of the soul. When God pardons sin, he heals the disease.

---

## Chapter 34: The Day of the Lord's Vengeance

This chapter pronounces doom on all nations that are enemies to God's church, particularly Edom (Esau's descendants), due to ancient enmity with Jacob. Henry sees fulfillment by Nebuchadnezzar's conquests, wars of nations, and ultimately the final dissolution at the day of judgment.

**The Universal Summons (vv. 1-4)**: "Come near, ye nations, to hear; and hearken, ye people: let the earth hear, and all that is therein." All nations must hear, for what God does concerns them all.

"The indignation of the LORD is upon all nations, and his fury upon all their armies: he hath utterly destroyed them, he hath delivered them to the slaughter." Armies before God's fury are dry stubble before consuming fire.

The cosmic dissolution: "All the host of heaven shall be dissolved, and the heavens shall be rolled together as a scroll: and all their host shall fall down, as the leaf falleth off from the vine." These expressions will have literal fulfillment in the dissolution of all things at the end of time.

**The Sword of the Lord (vv. 5-8)**: "My sword shall be bathed in heaven: behold, it shall come down upon Idumea, and upon the people of my curse, to judgment." God's sword is bathed in his counsel, decree, justice, and power.

The slaughter described as sacrifice: "The LORD hath a sacrifice in Bozrah, and a great slaughter in the land of Idumea." Sacrifices honored God, demonstrating he hates sin and demands satisfaction. The whole earth would be soaked with sinners' blood if Jesus Christ had not shed his blood as the great propitiation. Those who reject him will themselves fall as victims to divine wrath.

The reason: "It is the day of the LORD's vengeance, and the year of recompences for the controversy of Zion." Zion has a just quarrel with her neighbors.

**Perpetual Desolation (vv. 9-17)**: "The streams thereof shall be turned into pitch, and the dust thereof into brimstone, and the land thereof shall become burning pitch." Like Sodom, the fire shall be universal: "It shall not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever."

Three applications: (1) Melancholy changes often made by divine Providence in countries and cities; (2) Destroying judgments that are effects of God's wrath; (3) Final desolation of this wicked world, reserved unto fire at the day of judgment.

The line of judgment: "He shall stretch out upon it the line of confusion, and the stones of emptiness"—the same words as Genesis 1:2. Sin turns paradise into chaos and sullies creation's beauty.

The wild creatures' possession: "The cormorant and the bittern shall possess it; the owl also and the raven shall dwell in it... It shall be a habitation of dragons, and a court for owls." What was a court for princes becomes a court for owls. Sin turns fruitful land into barrenness and a frequented city into wilderness.

**The Certainty of Fulfillment (vv. 16-17)**: "Seek ye out of the book of the LORD, and read: no one of these shall fail." We must not only read the book of prophets but search into it. God's word will accomplish all that his Spirit will effect, for no word of God shall fall to the ground.

The permanence of judgment: "They shall possess it for ever, from generation to generation shall they dwell therein." God's Jerusalem may be laid in ruins but shall be rebuilt and continue until it gives place to the heavenly Jerusalem. The enemies of the church shall be forever desolate, punished with everlasting destruction.

---

## Chapter 35: The Desert Blossoms - The Way of Holiness

After chapter 34's dark scene, this chapter presents a bright, pleasant one, looking beyond Hezekiah's prosperity to the kingdom of Christ and heaven. When the world lying in wickedness shall be laid in ruins, the gospel church shall be set up and made to flourish.

**The Desert Rejoicing (vv. 1-2)**: "The wilderness and the solitary place shall be glad for them; and the desert shall rejoice, and blossom as the rose." When Christ was preached in Samaria, great joy came to that city. Converting grace makes the soul that was a wilderness to rejoice with joy and singing.

"The glory of Lebanon shall be given unto it, the excellency of Carmel and Sharon, they shall see the glory of the Lord." Whatever was excellent in the Mosaic economy is translated into evangelical institutes. The more we see by faith of God's glory, the more joyful and fruitful we shall be.

**Encouragement to the Fearful (vv. 3-4)**: "Strengthen ye the weak hands, and confirm the feeble knees. Say to them that are of a fearful heart, Be strong, fear not." This is the gospel's design—to strengthen the weak and confirm them. Among true Christians are many weak babes in Christ, yet it is our duty to strengthen our brethren.

"Behold, your God will come with vengeance, even God with a recompence; he will come and save you." The Messiah will take vengeance on the powers of darkness and "will come again at the end of time, will come in flaming fire."

**Miraculous Healing (vv. 5-7)**: "Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped. Then shall the lame man leap as a hart, and the tongue of the dumb sing."

Dual fulfillment: physical miracles often done by Christ ("with a word's speaking") and spiritual wonders. By Christ's word and Spirit, the spiritually blind were enlightened and those deaf to God's calls were made to hear readily.

"In the wilderness shall waters break out, and streams in the desert... the parched ground shall become a pool." This was fulfilled when the Holy Ghost fell upon the Gentiles and habitations of dragons became fruitful fields.

**The Highway of Holiness (vv. 8-9)**: "A highway shall be there, and a way, and it shall be called The way of holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it."

Four characteristics:

(1) An appointed way—"the King's highway, the King of Kings' highway... the good old way."

(2) An appropriated way—reserved for God's chosen, "but the unclean shall not pass over it."

(3) A straight way—"The wayfaring men, though fools, shall not err therein." The way to heaven is plain and easy. God made foolish things wise to salvation.

(4) A safe way—"No lion shall be there, nor any ravenous beast." Those keeping to this way keep out of the reach of Satan the roaring lion; the wicked one touches them not.

**Everlasting Joy (v. 10)**: "And the ransomed of the LORD shall return, and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads: they shall obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away."

Two applications: (1) Coming "to serve and worship God in the church militant"—delivered from spiritual Babylon; (2) Coming "to see and enjoy God in the church triumphant"—entering heaven "with songs... singing a new song."

It shall be everlasting joy without mixture, interruption, or period. Our joyful hopes of eternal life should swallow up both all the sorrows and all the joys of this present time.

---

## Chapter 36: Sennacherib's Invasion and Rabshakeh's Blasphemy

Isaiah now becomes a historian. The story appears also in 2 Kings 18-19 and 2 Chronicles 32: "The key of prophecy is to be found in history."

**The Invasion (vv. 1-3)**: "In the fourteenth year of king Hezekiah... Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the defenced cities of Judah, and took them." The Assyrian general Rabshakeh came with a great army to Jerusalem, standing by the conduit of the upper pool—where Isaiah had met Ahaz years before.

Practical lessons: (1) A people may be doing their duty yet meet with trouble. Hezekiah was reforming, yet invaded. When doing well, God may send afflictions to quicken us to do better. (2) We must never assume peace will continue. Hezekiah had made peace with Assyria by a rich present, yet the perfidious prince suddenly invaded. (3) God sometimes permits the enemies of his people to prevail far against them.

**Rabshakeh's Taunts (vv. 4-10)**: The Assyrian mocks Hezekiah's confidence: "What confidence is this wherein thou trustest?" He dismisses Egypt as "the staff of this broken reed... whereon if a man lean, it will go into his hand, and pierce it." He blasphemously misrepresents Hezekiah's reforms and claims divine commission: "Am I now come up without the LORD against this land to destroy it?"

Proud men love to talk big, boasting of what they are, have, and have done, rendering themselves ridiculous and obnoxious to God's wrath.

**The Appeal to the People (vv. 11-20)**: Hezekiah's officials ask Rabshakeh to speak in Aramaic to prevent common people from hearing. But Rabshakeh intentionally speaks louder in Hebrew: "Hear ye the words of the great king, the king of Assyria."

His propaganda: "Let not Hezekiah deceive you... Neither let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD." He offers false promises of prosperity and blasphemes God by comparing him to defeated pagan gods: "Who are they among all the gods of these lands... that the LORD should deliver Jerusalem?"

Satan tempts men from trusting in God by suggesting they may better their condition by yielding to him, but no land compares to the holy land.

**The Response (vv. 21-22)**: "They held their peace, and answered him not a word: for the king's commandment was, saying, Answer him not." It is sometimes prudent not to answer a fool according to his folly, leaving it to God to stop his mouth.

Yet "they rent their clothes, in a holy zeal for the glory of God's name and a holy indignation at the contempt put upon it. They tore their garments when they heard blasphemy, taking no pleasure in their own ornaments when God's honour suffered."

---

## Chapter 37: Hezekiah's Prayer and God's Deliverance

This chapter records Sennacherib conquering and threatening in the previous chapter, now falling in answer to prayer and fulfillment of prophecies.

**Hezekiah's Response (vv. 1-7)**: "When king Hezekiah heard it, that he rent his clothes, and covered himself with sackcloth, and went into the house of the LORD." He sent to Isaiah for prayer: "This day is a day of trouble, and of rebuke, and of blasphemy: for the children are come to the birth, and there is not strength to bring forth."

The best way to baffle enemies' designs is to be driven to God and duty. Rabshakeh intended to frighten Hezekiah from the Lord but instead frightened him to the Lord. It well becomes great men to desire the prayers of good men and ministers.

Isaiah's response: "Be not afraid of the words that thou hast heard, wherewith the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed me. Behold, I will send a blast upon him... and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land." Sinners' fears are prefaces to their falls.

**Sennacherib's Blasphemous Letter (vv. 8-13)**: "Let not thy God, in whom thou trustest, deceive thee, saying, Jerusalem shall not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria." He boasts of destroying nations whose gods could not save them.

It is bad to speak proudly and profanely, but worse to write so, for this shows more deliberation and design, spreads further, lasts longer, and does more mischief.

**Hezekiah's Prayer (vv. 14-20)**: "Hezekiah received the letter... and spread it before the LORD." His prayer: "O LORD of hosts, God of Israel, that dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth: thou hast made heaven and earth... Now therefore, O LORD our God, save us from his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the LORD, even thou only."

Liberty of access to the throne of grace and liberty of speech there are unspeakable privileges of the Lord's people. The key is pleading God's glory: we must show how his glory is interested in our case.

**God's Answer Through Isaiah (vv. 21-35)**: "The virgin, the daughter of Zion, hath despised thee, and laughed thee to scorn; the daughter of Jerusalem hath shaken her head at thee. Whom hast thou reproached and blasphemed?... even against the Holy One of Israel."

God reminds Sennacherib who controls all: "Hast thou not heard long ago, how I have done it; and of ancient times, that I have formed it? now have I brought it to pass, that thou shouldest be to lay waste defenced cities." The most active men are no more than God makes them.

God's complete knowledge: "I know thy abode, and thy going out, and thy coming in, and thy rage against me." And control: "I will put my hook in thy nose, and my bridle in thy lips, and I will turn thee back by the way by which thou camest."

The promise to Judah: "The remnant that is escaped of the house of Judah shall again take root downward, and bear fruit upward." And to Jerusalem: "He shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there... For I will defend this city to save it for mine own sake, and for my servant David's sake."

**The Deliverance (vv. 36-38)**: "Then the angel of the LORD went forth, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians a hundred and fourscore and five thousand: and when they arose early in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses."

One angel laid a vast army dead in one night. Angels are employed, more than we are aware, as ministers of God's justice.

Sennacherib's end: "As he was worshipping in the house of Nisroch his god, that Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons smote him with the sword." God can quickly stop the breath of those who breathe out threatenings against his people. The Lord is known by the judgments he executes, known to be a God that resists the proud.

## Chapter 38: Hezekiah's Sickness, Prayer, and Thanksgiving

This chapter will entertain those confined by bodily distempers, directing their devotions and encouraging faith and hopes.

**The Death Sentence (vv. 1-8)**: "In those days was Hezekiah sick unto death. And Isaiah the prophet... said unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Set thine house in order: for thou shalt die, and not live."

Neither men's greatness nor goodness exempt them from sickness and death. This sickness came when Hezekiah triumphed over the Assyrian army, teaching us to always rejoice with trembling.

The command: "Set thy house in order, and thy heart especially; put both thy affections and thy affairs into the best posture thou canst." Being ready for death makes it come never sooner but much easier; those fit to die are most fit to live.

**Hezekiah's Prayer (vv. 2-3)**: "Then Hezekiah turned his face toward the wall, and prayed unto the LORD... Remember now, O LORD, I beseech thee, how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart... And Hezekiah wept sore."

He turned probably toward the temple, a type of Christ, to whom we must look by faith in every prayer. Though pleading his faithfulness, he does not demand a reward from God but modestly begs that God would remember how he had approved himself with a single eye and honest heart.

**God's Answer (vv. 4-8)**: "I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears: behold, I will add unto thy days fifteen years." A miraculous sign: "The sun returned ten degrees."

God has a gracious ear open to the prayers of afflicted people. When we pray in sickness, though God may not give such an answer as to Hezekiah, if by his Spirit he assures us our sins are forgiven and his grace shall be sufficient, we have no reason to say we pray in vain.

**Hezekiah's Thanksgiving Song (vv. 9-20)**: "The writing of Hezekiah king of Judah, when he had been sick, and was recovered of his sickness."

Writing down God's mercies is good: a memorial of affliction and the frame of our hearts under it, a memorial of mercies of a sick bed and release from it, that they may never be forgotten. Thanksgiving is good, but thanksliving is better.

His despair recalled: "I said in the cutting off of my days, I shall go to the gates of the grave: I am deprived of the residue of my years." Life compared to a weaver's work: "I have cut off like a weaver my life." When finished, the thread is cut off, and the piece taken out of the loom to be judged by our Master whether it be well woven.

## Chapter 38: Hezekiah's Deliverance

**Hezekiah's Cry and God's Response**

Hezekiah cried: "O LORD, I am oppressed; undertake for me." Henry applies this to Christ: "When we receive the sentence of death within ourselves, we are undone if the divine grace do not undertake for us to carry us through the valley of the shadow of death."

**Wonder at God's Goodness**

"What shall I say? he hath both spoken unto me, and himself hath done it." Henry: "What God has spoken he will himself do, for no word of his shall fall to the ground."

**Resolution and Thanksgiving**

Hezekiah resolved: "I will go softly all my years in the bitterness of my soul"—walking thoughtfully and gratefully, retaining the impression of trouble after it passes.

**True Comfort Found**

"Behold, for peace I had great bitterness: but thou hast in love to my soul delivered it from the pit of corruption: for thou hast cast all my sins behind thy back." Henry explains: "When God pardons sin he casts it behind his back, as not designing to look upon it with an eye of justice. When we set them before our face in true repentance, God casts them behind his back."

**Purpose of Life**

"The grave cannot praise thee, death cannot celebrate thee. The living, the living, he shall praise thee, as I do this day." Henry: "Hezekiah was glad to live that he might continue to praise God. The living must praise God; they live in vain if they do not."

**Final Lessons**

Isaiah prescribed "a lump of figs" for Hezekiah's boil, showing "God's promises are intended not to supersede, but to quicken and encourage, the use of means. We do not trust God, but tempt him, if, when we pray to him for help, we do not second our prayers with our endeavours."

Hezekiah asked: "What is the sign that I shall go up to the house of the LORD?" Henry concludes: "The exercises of religion are so much the business and delight of a good man that to be restrained from them is the greatest grievance of his afflictions, and to be restored to them is the greatest comfort of his deliverances."

## Chapter 39: Hezekiah's Pride and the Babylonian Embassy

This chapter concludes with a prophecy of captivity in Babylon, serving as a bridge to the later section's discussion of the Jews' captivity and deliverance.

**The Embassy**

"Merodach-baladan, the son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a present to Hezekiah: for he had heard that he had been sick, and was recovered." The Babylonians, who worshipped the sun, believed they should honor the king whose recovery the sun had miraculously assisted.

Hezekiah's response: "Hezekiah was glad of them, and showed them the house of his precious things, the silver, and the gold... there was nothing in his house, nor in all his dominion, that Hezekiah showed them not."

Henry warns: "It is a hard matter to keep the spirit low in the midst of great advancements. Hezekiah is an instance of it: he was a wise and good man, but, when one miracle after another was wrought in his favour, he found it hard to keep his heart from being lifted up." Even Paul "needed a thorn in the flesh, to keep him from being lifted up."

**Isaiah's Examination and the Judgment**

Isaiah asked: "What said these men? and from whence came they unto thee? What have they seen in thine house?" Hezekiah confessed: "All that is in mine house have they seen."

Henry applies: "We must expect to be called to an account for the workings of our pride. When we have had company that paid us respect, we ought to be jealous over ourselves lest our hearts have been lifted up."

God's judgment came: "Behold, the days come, that all that is in thine house...shall be carried to Babylon...And of thy sons that shall issue from thee...shall they take away; and they shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon."

Henry's lessons: "It is just with God to take that from us which we make the matter of our pride. Those that are fond of an acquaintance with irreligious men will first or last have enough of it. Babylon, who now courted Jerusalem, in process of time conquered her. Leagues with sinners will end thus."

**Hezekiah's Submission**

Hezekiah responded: "Good is the word of the LORD which thou hast spoken." Henry commends this: "Those that truly repent of their sins will take it well to be reproved for them. Hezekiah reckoned that word of the Lord good which discovered sin to him." True penitents accept correction, not only mitigation but "the sentence itself."
