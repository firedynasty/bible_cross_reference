I'll help summarize this text about King Solomon's decline from 1 Kings Chapter 11:

The passage describes Solomon's gradual fall from faithfulness to God, primarily due to his relationships with foreign wives. Here are the key points:

1. Solomon married many foreign women, including princesses from Moab, Ammon, Edom, Sidon, and the Hittites, in addition to Pharaoh's daughter. He had 700 wives and 300 concubines.

2. In his later years, these wives led Solomon to worship their gods, including:
- Ashtoreth (Sidonian goddess of fertility and war)
- Milcom/Molech (Ammonite god)
- Chemosh (Moabite god)

3. Solomon built high places (worship sites) for these foreign gods on the hills east of Jerusalem, directly contradicting God's commandments. This was particularly egregious as God had appeared to Solomon twice with warnings and promises.

4. As punishment, God raised up three adversaries against Solomon:
- Hadad the Edomite, who had fled to Egypt during David's reign
- Rezon of Damascus, who became king of Aram
- Jeroboam son of Nebat, Solomon's own servant

5. Through the prophet Ahijah, God revealed that the kingdom would be split after Solomon's death:
- Ten tribes would go to Jeroboam
- One tribe (Judah) would remain with Solomon's son, for David's sake and Jerusalem's sake

6. Solomon reigned for 40 years before his death, and his story is recorded in the books of Nathan the prophet, Ahijah the Shilonite, and other historical records.

This account illustrates how Solomon's marriages to foreign women led to religious compromise and ultimately the division of Israel's kingdom, despite his earlier wisdom and devotion to God.
