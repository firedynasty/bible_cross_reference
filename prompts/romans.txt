# Romans 1:1-17 & 1:18-32 & 2:1-16

## Romans 1:1-17

**Outline:**

I. Paul's base for authority (1-6)
II. Greeting (7)
III. Paul's concern for them (8-15)
   A. Manifested in Prayer (8-10)
   B. Manifested in His Desire to Visit Them (11-13)
   C. Because of his duty (14)
   D. To share the gospel (15)
IV. The gospel (16-17)
   A. Is the power of God (16)
   B. Reveals the Righteousness of God (17)

**Key Discussion Questions:**

What does it mean to be "set apart for the gospel"? What doctrines of Christ does Paul share in verses 2-4 and why? On what basis did Paul have authority? What can you tell about Rome at that time and what type of Christians lived there? How is Paul's love for the Roman believers shown? What were his reasons for wanting to visit Rome? What does it mean that Paul would impart a spiritual gift to them? What was Paul's obligation to share the gospel? Are we obligated to share the gospel today? If someone was ashamed of the gospel, what signs might there be? Why was Paul not ashamed? What does it mean "to the Jew first, and also to the Greek"?

**Commentary:**

Paul's conversion and apostolic authority establish his credibility. Rome, the world's largest empire's capital, had about one million people including many slaves, with both elite society and wretched slums. Christians there faced persecution—later Nero would torture and kill them. The church likely began with believers from Jerusalem's Pentecost and grew through Christian migrants.

Paul emphasizes the gospel throughout, focusing on Christ's incarnation, death, and resurrection. He highlights key doctrines: total depravity, God's judgment, sin, justification by faith alone, sanctification, and election.

The Roman believers had a shining testimony—their faith was proclaimed throughout the world. As the center of world commerce, their strong stand in darkness was magnified and heard everywhere.

Paul prayed for them though he hadn't visited. Later, imprisoned and taken to Rome, he ministered there. His desire to visit them shows humility—he would go not only to encourage but to be encouraged by them, receiving mutual blessing.

Paul was obligated to share the gospel with everyone, especially Gentiles. This duty came from God. He was faithful and never gave up. We are also obligated—it's a responsibility and privilege. We should approach it with Paul's eagerness.

Why might we feel ashamed of the gospel? We may not truly believe it, think it foolish to the educated, fear ridicule or persecution, or lack faith. Signs include fearing to identify as believers, apologizing for our faith, keeping it secret, or being reluctant to share. We should be confident because the gospel is God's power, not our ideas or opinions. We are merely messengers. An ambassador can be confident because his country's power backs him.

## Romans 1:18-32

**Outline:**

I. God's wrath against mankind's sin (18-32)
   A. God's Standard Made Clear to Men (18-20)
   B. Rejected By Men (21-32)
      1. Following man-made idols (21-25)
      2. Following perverse desires (26-27)
      3. List of their sins (28-31)
      4. Blatant and Enthusiastic (32)

**Key Discussion Questions:**

How could a loving God have wrath? Is His wrath like ours? What is the "truth" which wicked people suppress? How has God made His existence evident to all? Is there any legitimate excuse for not believing in God? What alternatives to God did people set up then instead of idols now? What phrase repeats three times (verses 24-28)? What does it mean God gave them over? Did He cause their sin? What natural function did women exchange? Why does Paul mention homosexuality specifically? What does verse 27 teach about reaping what you sow?

**Commentary:**

This passage shows the necessity of the gospel. If sin didn't make God angry, Jesus wouldn't need to die. God has holy anger—righteous wrath against ungodliness and sinfulness, not petty anger over personal slights but justifiable anger like toward child abuse or elderly exploitation.

God's general revelation makes basic truths evident to everyone. Creation clearly shows God's power and nature—the complexity, scale, order, variety, and perfection all point to the Creator. Every person is responsible to respond positively. Even those who haven't heard the gospel are responsible for what they know.

People know God's existence in their hearts but willfully reject it. God's wrath is stirred specifically because they reject known truth. There will be no excuse on judgment day. Creation ends the debate before it begins.

Humanity has chosen to reject God in favor of idolatry. During biblical times they substituted idols for God—worshiping animals, stone, wood, gold. People claim to be wise but are actually fools. Evolution is a modern parallel—people claim it's a brilliant discovery, but according to God they are fools. The wisdom of the world is foolishness to God.

God gave people over through judicial abandonment. The term "gave them over" is a judicial sentence—God removes barriers to sin and allows people to follow sin to its natural conclusion. Since human will was enslaved to sin at Adam's fall, this abandonment leads to progressively worse sins.

Twisted beliefs lead to twisted morals. What you believe shapes your worldview and character. When people turn to idolatry, other sins follow. Aaron's golden calf worship led to wild immorality. Israel's idol worship at Peor brought sexual immorality. Nations worshiping fertility goddesses practiced sexual immorality. Aztecs worshiping violent gods made human sacrifices. The same pattern holds with atheism, evolution, and agnosticism—without belief in a higher authority or judgment, people do as they please.

You reap what you sow. God established natural order where sins bring negative consequences. Disobeying parents generally leads to shorter life. The example in the passage: sexual immorality produces harmful effects like AIDS. Sin is destructive, leading to guilt-ridden, miserable life, then judgment.

The downward spiral continues with many sins. It worsens progressively. First, God gave them over to sexual immorality. Then to perverting God's natural order. Finally to a depraved mind, darkened almost like an animal following basic instincts. The final step isn't merely doing these sins privately but doing them publicly and enthusiastically, encouraging others. This describes many current sins including homosexuality, arrogance, gossip, and disobedience to parents.

# Romans 2:1-16 & 2:17-29 Study Notes

## Why Learn About God's Judgment?

Understanding God's judgment shows us we have no hope in ourselves—our only salvation is through God's grace. It reminds us that our actions matter and God is always watching. When sharing the gospel, we must clarify that people will face judgment and have no excuse.

## Key Teaching Points

### Romans 2:1-16

**We Are All Hypocrites**

Everyone violates the standards they set for others. You can see a person's true values by what they demand of others—if someone condemns lying in others but lies themselves, they violate their own standard. We uncover people's values by observing what they judge in others. Everyone violates not only God's moral law but also their own standards, leaving no one with an excuse.

**God's Judgment Is Certain and Inescapable**

There is no escaping God's judgment. Everything we do is observed by Him. We cannot hide from God any more than Jonah could flee His presence. We will face judgment (Hebrews 9:27).

**God Offers Kindness and Repentance**

Despite His judgment, God offers a way of escape through repentance. He is patient and longsuffering, wanting to give sinners opportunities to repent. However, most despise His kindness, testing His patience and refusing to turn to Him.

**Continued Sin Stores Up Wrath**

Like saving treasures in heaven, we make daily deposits to our spiritual accounts. We can either devote ourselves to God and deposit treasures, or reject Him and deposit wrath for ourselves.

**Judgment Is Based on Deeds**

God's judgment is perfectly fair. He records everything we have done, said, and thought. None of us will be proud of our records—all are wicked and condemning. Everyone must plead guilty. Thankfully, God erases the record through Christ's blood.

**Salvation Is Not Works-Based; Condemnation Is**

Verses 7-10 teach that those who do good works receive eternal life, but they can only do good works because they have already trusted in Christ. Good works are evidence of salvation, not its cause. Our works cannot save us, but they can condemn us. Everyone who does evil will face judgment and reap what they sow.

**Different Standards for Different Knowledge**

Those who received God's law will be judged more severely than those without it. God takes into account knowledge level—the Jews saw Jesus and His miracles personally, making their rejection even worse. Those without the law will still be judged for rejecting their own conscience and moral standards, but their punishment will be less severe than those who knew God's law.

**God Shows No Partiality**

God's judgment is perfectly fair and unaffected by wealth, color, age, race, position, or status. Unlike earthly judges, you cannot bribe God or receive a free pass for being rich or famous. The only way to get a "not guilty" verdict is through a relationship with the Judge's Son.

**Conscience Is God's Built-In Standard**

Everyone has a conscience that tells us right from wrong. God has placed His basic law principles in all hearts. Cultures worldwide share similar morals—governments forbid murder, lying, and stealing because people inherently know these are wrong. Parents and employers everywhere don't want others to lie to them. This conscience is a spiritual warning system; guilt to the spirit is like pain to the body. It warns us before sin and makes us guilty afterward, designed to teach us to avoid sin and repent.

### Romans 2:17-29

**Religious Rituals Cannot Save**

The Jews were extremely religious—they made sacrifices, performed cleansings, were circumcised, wore special clothes, attended meetings, went on yearly pilgrimages, memorized Scripture, debated doctrine, and made proselytes. Yet their religion could not save them. Trusting in religion or religious efforts cannot save anyone today either.

**Spiritual Pride Is Dangerous**

The Jews were proud of their religious heritage, knowledge of the law, and ability to teach others. They looked down on those less religious. Many "Christians" today are guilty of the same thing—we focus on correcting others while ignoring ourselves. We cannot be proud when we have received a gift; a gift is by nature undeserved.

**Knowledge Without Obedience Is Hypocrisy**

The Jews knew and taught the Law but didn't keep it. They told others not to steal and stole themselves. They told others not to commit adultery and did it themselves. They hated idols but robbed temples. This hypocrisy brought dishonor to God's name.

**Bad Testimony Causes Others to Blaspheme**

The Jews were chosen to be a light to nations, but instead their haughty and hypocritical behavior caused Gentiles to curse God rather than believe in Him. Their actions demonstrated that God's name was blasphemed because of them. Throughout history, those calling themselves Christians have sometimes had this same effect through pride, rudeness, and hypocrisy—which remains a top reason unbelievers avoid church.

**Circumcision Was Only a Symbol**

Circumcision separated the Jews from other nations and marked their covenant relationship with God. However, it became an empty religious ritual of pride. Most Jews trusted in the symbol itself to save them rather than what it represented. Circumcision cannot save anyone.

**Salvation Comes Only Through Faith and Obedience**

There are no magical rituals that save. You cannot earn salvation through obedience because you cannot keep every commandment perfectly. Just as circumcision couldn't save, neither does baptism, church attendance, fasting, prayer, Bible reading, or any other Christian act save someone today.

**God Looks at the Heart, Not Outward Appearance**

True circumcision is of the heart—a heart set apart and devoted to God. Similarly, real baptism is of the heart; our old self dies to sin and we live a transformed life. God looks at the inside, not the outside.

## Applications

**Evaluate Your Own Conduct**

Don't be hasty to tell others what they should do. Remove the forest in your own eye before the speck in your brother's eye. Pay more attention to your own actions than others'. Be merciful toward those who mistreat you since you've probably done the same.

**Take the Great Commission Seriously**

Understanding that everyone breaks their own standards should inspire us to share the gospel so everyone has opportunity to hear and repent.

**Avoid Spiritual Pride**

Never become prideful spiritually. Everything is a gift from God, and you did nothing to earn it. Don't look at others under a microscope while looking at yourself through a telescope.

**Be Authentic in Faith**

Don't rely on religion or religious acts for salvation. Real Christianity is dedication of the heart to Christ, allowing Him to change you from the inside out. It is faith in Christ's finished work on the cross that brings us into God's family and shapes us into His people.

# Romans 3:1-20 and 3:21-31 Study Guide

## Romans 3:1-20

### Paraphrase

Is there any benefit in being a Jew or being circumcised? Yes, many benefits. The first is that Jews were given the word of God.

What if some didn't have faith in God? Will their doubt affect God's faithfulness? Definitely not! God is truthful and every man a liar. It is written: "So that you may be proved right when you speak and prevail when you judge."

If our sin illustrates God's holiness more clearly, what should we say? Do we say God is unfair in bringing His wrath on us? Certainly not! If that argument is true, how could God judge the world?

Some people may argue, "If my lying illustrates God's truth better and thus gives Him glory, why am I still judged as a sinner?" Why not say, "Let us sin that good may result"? They deserve their condemnation.

So what is the conclusion? Are the Jews any better? No! We have already concluded that Jews and Gentiles are both under sin.

As it is written: "There is no one righteous, not even one; there is no one who understands, no one who seeks God. All have turned away and together become useless. No one does good, not even one." "Their throats reek of death; their tongues practice deceit." "Their lips are poisonous like viper's poison." "Their mouths pour forth cursing and bitterness." "They run quickly to kill; ruin and misery is their life, and they don't know the meaning of peace." "They have no fear of God before them."

Whatever the law says is for those who are under the law, so that every mouth will be silent and every person accountable to God. Not even one person will be righteous to God for keeping the law; but instead it will show us our sin.

### Verse-by-Verse Commentary

**Introduction:** Paul has established that all people everywhere—Jews and Greeks—are guilty before God and will face His judgment. Jewish readers naturally question whether God's choosing them was of any benefit. Paul anticipates and answers their objections.

**Verses 1-2: Benefits for the Jew.** While God's blessings don't guarantee salvation, they bring Jews closer to salvation. Because they received God's Word, His commands, promises, and prophecies, they had much more knowledge and opportunity to believe and repent.

**Verses 3-4: God's Faithfulness.** God is faithful to the Jews even when they are unfaithful. He preserves a remnant. However, many of God's promises come with conditions. Many Jews didn't receive some of God's promises because they broke the conditions. God is always true; every man is a liar.

**Verses 5-9: Man's Sinful Justification.** Man's sin highlights God's righteousness. Like a diamond shining against a black surface, God's holiness becomes visible through the contrast with human sin. When God judges sin, it demonstrates His holiness and His hatred of it. This in no way excuses our responsibility. We are still accountable for our actions.

**Verses 10-18: Total Depravity.** Neither Jews nor Gentiles are intrinsically better. All are under sin and totally depraved.

**Verses 19-20: The Law's Purpose.** The Law makes it so we have no excuse. The Law teaches us the knowledge of sin. Every person is accountable to God for his actions. No one can save himself. The law shows us our sin so we will be without excuse.

### Key Words

**Unrighteousness:** Describes human behavior throughout this passage. We consistently practice unrighteousness.

**Righteousness:** God is perfectly and completely righteous and holy in every way. He hates sin and pours out His wrath on it. His holiness provides the basis for His judgment.

**Truth:** God's truthful nature is contrasted with men's deceit and lies.

## Romans 3:21-31

### Paraphrase

Now the righteousness of God has been shown to us apart from the Law, but witnessed to by the Law and the Prophets. This righteousness from God comes through faith in Jesus Christ to everyone who believes. There is no discrimination.

All people have sinned and fallen short of God's glory. But we are justified freely because of His grace through the redemption we have in Jesus Christ. God presented Him as a sacrifice of atonement through faith in His blood. He did this to show His justice, because in His patience He had not yet punished the sins committed beforehand.

He did it to demonstrate His present justice, so as to be just and the one who justifies those who have faith in Jesus. So can we boast? There is none. Because of faith, not because of serving the law.

For we maintain that a man is justified by faith apart from keeping the law. Is God only God of the Jews? Or is He also God of the Gentiles? Yes, also of Gentiles, because there is only one God, who justifies the circumcised and uncircumcised based on the same faith.

So then, do we make the law void? Definitely not! Instead we establish the law.

### Verse-by-Verse Commentary

**Introduction:** Paul has established that all are sinners with no way to earn salvation through themselves. He now introduces hope: "But now." After describing judgment and failure, Paul contrasts this with good news. We cannot earn righteousness on our own, but we can receive it from God. One of Romans' main themes is justification by faith apart from works—foundational to salvation.

**Verse 21: Jesus Shows Righteousness.** Jesus shows us the righteousness of God in flesh. Whereas the Law provides principles and guidelines, Jesus demonstrated what God's righteousness looks like in action. He exemplified love, resistance to evil, forgiveness, and more. Jesus sympathizes with us because He faced all temptations we do, except without sin.

**Verses 22-24: Righteousness Through Faith.** We can have God's righteousness through faith. This process is called imputation—Christ's righteousness is transferred to us. There are two kinds of righteousness: positional (God sees Christ as our substitute; our slate is wiped clean) and practical (we are freed from slavery to sin and have power for victorious living). God is not partial regarding who He saves. Anyone can be saved regardless of their past. Money, position, influence, or education cannot buy salvation.

**Verse 23: The Standard.** All people have sinned and fall short of God's glory, His standard.

**Verse 24: Justification.** Being justified means we are declared righteous by the Judge—just as if we have never sinned. God puts our sins away as far as the East is from the West. Justification is a gift. The imputation puts righteousness on us; justification judicially removes the crime.

**Verses 25-26: The Sacrifice.** God presented Jesus as a sacrifice of atonement through faith in His blood to demonstrate His justice. He had patiently not yet punished previously committed sins. He now demonstrates His present justice, being both just and the justifier of those who have faith in Jesus.

**Verses 27-28: Faith Alone.** We cannot boast because justification comes by faith apart from keeping the law.

**Verses 29-30: Universal Availability.** God is not partial—He justifies both the circumcised and uncircumcised based on the same faith.

**Verse 31: Establishing the Law.** Faith establishes the Law rather than nullifying it.

# THEOLOGICAL FOUNDATIONS AND BIBLICAL TEACHINGS

## Core Doctrinal Points

**Jesus' Public Crucifixion**
Jesus was displayed publicly on the cross because He was the representative of humankind. His sacrifice and shame were for all to see—not hidden. God had nothing to hide and wanted it well documented, witnessed, and verified as a historical fact.

**Propitiation**
This is the aspect of satisfying God's holy wrath. Sin must be punished. A sacrifice or payment must be made. The only thing that could properly satisfy God's wrath is Jesus on the cross.

**God's Patience**
God could have immediately destroyed Adam and Eve and the entire human race when they sinned, but He didn't. He was patient, waiting until the right time for Jesus to come. Every sin is punished, but God didn't immediately deal with Old Testament sins or those who committed them.

**No Boasting**
We must not boast or consider ourselves better than others (Ephesians 2:8-9; 1 Corinthians 1:31; 2 Corinthians 12:9; Galatians 6:14). Any gift or ability we have comes through God's mercy.

**Universal Salvation**
God is over all the world—not only God of America, India, the Jews, or the Western world. He is the ultimate, mighty, powerful God over all the universe and every region. God is consistent: the way of salvation has always been and will always be faith.

**Faith Fulfills the Law**
Faith in Christ fulfills the Law in three ways:
1. It provides payment for the penalty of sin, which the law required
2. By fulfilling the law's original purpose—to show mankind's inability to obey God's righteous demands and drive people to Christ
3. By giving believers the capacity to obey it

## Key Definitions

**Righteousness**
This refers to the righteousness of God, which enabled Jesus to live a sinless life. Apart from Christ we act totally opposite. With Christ, He gives us the strength and ability to live in victory. Christ freely gives us His righteousness when we put our faith in Him.

**Faith**
Faith is the ONLY way we can have the righteousness of God. It is the gift of God, available to all people regardless of ethnicity, culture, or language.

**Law**
Keeping the law is contrasted with faith. Keeping the law is not enough to bring us God's righteousness. Only a relationship with Him established by faith will do this.

# ROMANS 4:1-12: ABRAHAM AS EXAMPLE OF JUSTIFICATION BY FAITH

## Core Teaching

Abraham was the Old Testament example of God's enduring plan of justification by faith. He first demonstrated his faith by his actions. God credited him with righteousness, and then he received the sign of circumcision.

**What Abraham Found About Justification**
If Abraham was justified by works, he had reason to boast—but Scripture says he believed God and received God's righteousness. When a person works, he receives wages as a right, not as a gift. But to the man who puts his confidence in God instead of works, this faith is credited to him as righteousness.

**David's Testimony**
David says the same thing when speaking of the blessedness of man, to whom God credits righteousness apart from works:
- Blessed are those whose sins are forgiven and covered
- Blessed is the man whose sin against the Lord will never be taken into account

These blessings are for both the circumcised and uncircumcised. Abraham's faith was credited to him as righteousness before he was circumcised, not after. Because of the righteousness he had by faith while still uncircumcised, he received the sign of circumcision.

**Abraham's Spiritual Fatherhood**
Abraham is the father of all who believe but haven't been circumcised so that righteousness may be credited to them. He is also the father of the circumcised who don't rely on circumcision but walk in the way of faith that Abraham walked before he was circumcised.

## Key Theological Principles

**Justification Defined**
Justification is a free gift of God and one hundred percent as a result of faith in God, not works. After we are truly justified, we will do works as a result of our changed heart.

**Credited**
God's account is unlimited. He gives us fully out of it (Ephesians 1:6-8).

**Blessedness**
We have great reason to be thankful for the blessings He gave to us. His love is unlimited and so are His blessings to us.

**Abraham's Significance**
Abraham was credited with righteousness BEFORE he received circumcision. This demonstrates that faith comes first, then works. Abraham first believed God and showed it by his willingness to obey. God then gave him the sign and he did the good deed of circumcision. The sequence is: God loves us, draws us, saves us, and finally changes our hearts to cause us to do good deeds. Every step is about God, not us.

## Discussion Questions

What does it mean to "believe"?

In what ways can we follow in the steps of faith that Abraham had?
Are you willing to make the same leaps of faith and entrust yourself to God?

## Abraham's Leaps of Faith

Abraham demonstrated faith through:
- Leaving his home, country, family, and possessions for an unknown land, unknown culture, and unknown future
- Allowing Lot to choose the best land, trusting that God would bless him
- Trusting in God's righteous character enough to bargain for Sodom
- Believing God's promise that he would have a son
- Willingness to sacrifice his son Isaac

## Verse-by-Verse Commentary

**Abraham's Place in History**
Abraham was the ancestor of all Jews and the original Patriarch. He was revered as THE example of righteous living.

**Justification Not by Works**
Abraham was not justified by works, and neither are we, so he could have nothing to boast about.

**True Belief**
Belief is not just a set of facts. It is entrusting yourself to someone or something. Abraham illustrated this continually by putting himself and his family's lives into God's hands and following where God sent him in obedience.

**God Justifies the Ungodly**
If there were any godly people, they wouldn't need to be justified. The first step of repentance is recognizing that we are ungodly.

**David's Example**
David, after his sin with Bathsheba, received God's forgiveness. This teaches us that if Abraham and David could not earn their forgiveness through works, we have hope. It also shows that forgiveness isn't the absence of consequences—David faced consequences for his sin.

**Spiritual vs. Physical Fatherhood**
True believers in God are more closely related to Abraham than those who claim his physical ancestry but embrace a works-based salvation approach.

# ROMANS 6:1-14: FREEDOM FROM SIN THROUGH CHRIST

## Core Teaching

After justification comes sanctification. This passage shows the most important negative thing a believer should NOT be doing and the most important positive thing a believer should be doing.

## The Central Question and Answer

**Should Grace Be Used as an Excuse to Sin?**
Absolutely not. How can we who are freed from sin still practice it? (Proverbs 26:11 illustrates this with a dog returning to its vomit.)

## Key Doctrine: United with Christ

**Baptism Into Christ**
Through baptism we are united with Christ in His death, burial, and resurrection. This is not referring to water baptism but to the spiritual uniting with Christ that takes place when we place our faith in Him. If we were in a wheelbarrow being pushed by someone across Niagara Falls, his death would be our death and his victory would be ours. Similarly, when we trust in Christ, we are united with Him in His death and resurrection. As He was transformed after death and resurrection, so shall we be.

**Our Old Self Crucified**
Our old self has been crucified with Christ. We are dead to sin and no longer slaves to it. We are freed from the power and consequences of sin. At the moment we repent and trust in Christ, we face a turning point—we no longer belong to Satan but to Christ.

**Victory Over Sin**
You don't have to sin. Sin is not necessary. It is beatable. We can be victorious. You don't have to give in to old bad habits. You cannot say, "I had no choice" or "I couldn't help it." We do have a choice and we can help it. Don't be satisfied with being an average Christian. Victory is ours through God's grace.

**New Life in Christ**
When Christ died, that wasn't the end. He was raised and had new life. The same is true for us. When we die to sin, we don't cease to exist—we have a new nature capable of righteousness. Walk in newness of life shows that our lives have been transformed (John 3 calls it being born again). We are an entirely new person, like a butterfly coming out of a cocoon.

## Practical Application

**Consider Yourself Dead to Sin**
Consider (unreserved confidence) that you are dead to sin but alive to God in Christ Jesus. This doesn't mean we trick ourselves into thinking we aren't sinners. It means that God has revealed the truth to us that believers can be victorious. Christ has already paid for our sin. We are to claim this truth as our own and live in light of it.

**Do Not Let Sin Reign**
Based on the doctrine that we are dead to sin, we are to make specific application in our own lives. Do not allow sin to reign in your mortal body or present the parts of your body as instruments of unrighteousness to sin. This implies we have a choice. God sanctifies us but doesn't free us from human responsibility. We still have to work at ensuring sin is not reigning in us. Be merciless towards sin—kill it before it can grow and spread.

**Present Yourself to God**
Present yourself to God as someone alive from the dead. We are to think how we can serve God. Our bodies are temples of the Holy Spirit and belong to Him. We are to present the very best we have to God each and every day. When a person almost dies but is saved, he feels life is a gift and treasures each minute. Others would say he is a new man. That is what others should say about us when they look at our lives.

## Key Words

**Baptism**
Baptism is a picture that we are dead to sin and have new life in Christ. Because of our sin we deserve to suffer the same fate Christ did, but He took that punishment for us. Baptism represents that we are accepting what He did for us and taking His victory over sin and death as our own.

**Death**
Sometimes referring to the physical death of Christ; other times to the death of our old self. It symbolizes that we are no longer under the power of sin and Satan. The price of sin has been fully paid for in Christ and we need not remember it anymore.

**Live**
Jesus' physical resurrection is symbolic of our new life in Him. Sin and death are gone. Christ imparts His righteousness to us and gives us the power to live a life of victory.

**Grace**
Grace should not be used as an excuse to sin. Grace is only bestowed on the truly repentant. Instead, God's great love and grace should spur us on to serve Him with our whole hearts. As we remember His loving sacrifice, we should be willing to thankfully give ourselves completely to His service.

## Key Verses Explained

**Verses 3-4**
These verses establish the comparison between our baptism and Christ's death, burial, and resurrection.

**Verses 5-7**
Our old body has been put to death. This doesn't mean it has no presence in our lives—we still face continual struggle as we fight against old desires. But it means the chains are broken. We are free from its power and free to do what is right. Christ gives us the power we need to have victory over sin.

**Verses 12-13**
Doctrinally it is clear we are no longer slaves of sin. But practically we still yield to it sometimes. We must make a firm decision that we will not give in to temptation. We cannot stand against it on our own strength, but we must take the strength God gives us and refuse to give in. We should flee from the place of temptation. Not only must we not do wrong things, we must make a whole-hearted effort to do right things. It takes a conscious decision and strong will to do what is good even when it is hard.

## Application

Think of one sin you really struggle with. Write out steps to deal with it and kill it in your life. Write out steps for how to serve God in this area of your life.

# ROMANS 6:15-23 & 7:1-12 & 7:13-25 INDUCTIVE BIBLE STUDY

## ROMANS 6:15-23

**Paraphrase**

Should we sin because we are under grace instead of the law? Of course not! If you give your life to someone as a slave, you are a slave to the one you obey. You can be a slave of sin, leading to death, or a slave of obedience, leading to righteousness.

We greatly thank God that even though you were previously slaves of sin, you now obey the teaching you have been given with a whole heart. You have been set free from the power of sin and become slaves for good deeds.

I am speaking in human terms because naturally you are weak. In the same way you used to give the parts of your body in slavery to wicked sins that continued to increase, so now give the parts of your body as slaves to righteousness that will lead to holiness.

When you were slaves to sin, righteousness had no meaning to you. What good did all of those wicked deeds, that you are now ashamed to do, do for you? The result is death!

But now you have been set free from sin and have become slaves to God. The great benefit is that this will lead to holiness and result in eternal life. For the fair result of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

**Outline**

- The Question Reiterated (15)
- There Is No Middle Ground (16)
- One-Hundred-Eighty Degree Turn (17-20)
- Contrasting Results of Each Lifestyle (21-23)

**Key Words**

**Slaves (to righteousness, to obedience, to sin):** This refers to what is our master. We can only serve one side—God or Satan. It is impossible to serve both or to please both. The two sides are exactly opposite. If we are slaves of sin, the desire of our heart is to do evil. We love the evil. We cannot rest or live without it. We are completely under its sway, completely under its power.

When we trust in Christ, He sets us free by His power. We don't set ourselves free. Our heart has a complete reversal of direction. No longer do we desire and covet sin. Instead we despise sin. If we make a mistake and do sin, we hate it, feel miserable, and desire to repent. Our heart wants to serve God in all things. He is our Lord, our master. We should submit our life to Him in all areas. Our life is not our own.

**Benefit:** This word refers to the results of the two paths. One side brings emptiness, despair, depression, loneliness, and death. The other side brings peace, love, joy, and contentment in Christ combined with eternal life.

**Discussion Questions**

- What is the main point of this passage?
- What does it mean to be a slave of sin? What about a slave of righteousness?
- Can there be a neutral position?
- What does it mean to present yourselves to "someone"? Who would an unbeliever present themselves to? How about a believer?
- Are we slaves of God? Isn't it bad to be a slave?
- What is the significance of the phrase "obedient from the heart"? Why would someone be obedient if it isn't from the heart?
- Explain the phrase "to that form of teaching to which you were committed." Are you committed to obeying from your heart biblical teaching?
- Can someone be saved if they aren't willing to make Christ the Lord and master of their lives?
- Does the slave pick and choose what he wants to obey?
- What are the results of these two ways of life?
- What benefit do we get from sin? What benefit do we get from righteousness?

**Verse by Verse Commentary**

We can be either slaves of sin or slaves of righteousness. We can have only one Master, either Christ or Satan/self. If we have Christ as our Master, we will do righteous deeds. If we have Satan/self as our master, our lives will be governed by sin.

Being a slave of God is good. Normally when we think of being a slave, we have a negative opinion. But what is our position like as slaves of God? First, He is a perfect Master. He cares for His slaves, loves them, and even treats them as children. He lavishes His slaves with every imaginable blessing and appoints them as heirs of His kingdom. While He lays down principles for us to follow, these are for our protection and good. By following them we will have more joy. He also gives us a certain amount of Christian freedom as long as we live within the principles He lays down. The Bible calls us friends and children of God, yet also calls us slaves, because the relationship we have with God is multi-faceted. Each description refers to a different aspect.

**Obedient from the heart:** God holds us to a higher standard than earthly masters. He looks at our heart, our motivations, our attitudes, not only our actions. God looks at the heart. Why is it important that we obey from our hearts? We are to worship God not only in truth, but also in Spirit. Doing the right things for the wrong reasons is also sin. Our hearts are deceitful and desperately wicked. While we might go to church or give money to God, we might do so to earn recognition or respect rather than because of a personal relationship with God. In this case, our actions are selfish, not pure and spiritual.

**Committed to that form of teaching:** This must refer to correct biblical instruction from God's Word. We aren't committed to a particular church, mega-preacher, or home-church leader. We are committed to God's Word and God's message wherever we can find it.

**The result of the two courses:** One brings lawlessness and then death. The other brings sanctification and eternal life. Verse 23 gains new insights in context of the entire passage. We have two courses of life to choose from, two possible masters. One results in death, the other in eternal life. This eternal life is not in a vacuum. Although it is a free gift, we still need to make Christ the Lord, to be slaves of righteousness, and to change the way we live.

## ROMANS 7:1-12

**Paraphrase**

Brothers, don't you know that the law has authority over someone only while he is living? A married woman is bound to her husband as long as he is alive. If her husband dies, she is free from the law of marriage. If she marries another man while her husband is still alive she is an adulteress, but if she marries another man after her husband dies, she is free from the law and is not an adulteress.

So brothers, you also died to the law through Christ, and you now belong to someone else, to Him who was raised from the dead. This happened so that you can bear fruit to God. Before we were controlled by the old sinful man and committed sins because we were aroused by the law. This was bearing fruit for death.

But now we are dead to the sin that bound us and have been set free from the law so that we serve in the way of the new way of the Spirit, instead of the old way according to the law. Is the law sin? Of course not! Without the law I would not have known sin. I only know what coveting is because the law told me, "Do not covet."

But sin took advantage of this opportunity that came because of the command and produced all kinds of coveting in me. For apart from the law, sin is dead. Once I was alive apart from the law, but when the command came, sin sprung forth and I died. I realized that the commandment intended to bring life really brought death instead. For sin jumped at the chance given it by the command, tricked me, and through the command put me to death.

Then we know that the law is holy and the command is holy, righteous, and good.

**Outline**

- The Law's Relation to the Believer (1-12)
  - Jurisdiction of the Law (1-6)
    - Ends at Death (1)
    - Example from Marriage (2-3)
    - Through Christ, the Believer is Dead to the Law (4-6)
  - Practical Effects of the Law (7-12)
    - Brings Knowledge of Sin (7)
    - Stirs up Evil Desires that Bring Death (8-11)
    - It is Holy (12)

**Key Words**

**Law:** Paul explains a basic principle: the law doesn't govern people's lives after they die. The Christian died to the law through Christ's death, who acted as a representative for us. No one can serve two masters. The law, or the covenant, in the Old Testament no longer has jurisdiction over believers. Instead of following the law out of fear of punishment, they serve Christ with their whole hearts out of love and thanks. This is a complete reversal of direction.

The law's purpose before we believe is to bring us knowledge of sin. This is the most important purpose of the law—it brings conviction of sin. This is the first step necessary before repentance. If someone repents, it will bring life. If they don't, an indirect result of the law will bring death. It will give them new ideas to sin. When someone's "freedom" is restricted, often the first response is to stretch the limits and break the boundaries. Tell someone not to do something and often they will do it. For the person that does not repent, the law has a bad side-effect of increasing their sin and also the punishment that would come because of that sin.

**Dead:** When the law comes, a person knows they are condemned. The wages of sin is death. The law also increases sin for the unrepentant person. Therefore they are relatively more dead and more sinful when the law comes than before they knew of it.

**Alive:** Without the law we are relatively alive because sin is relatively dormant and we don't have the conviction or guilt that comes as a result of the law. But in Christ we are completely alive, living a new life and serving God in a new way.

**Discussion Questions**

- What is the relationship of a Christian to the law?
- Are we dead to the law now? If so, how did we die to the law if we have not died yet?
- What does this passage teach us about marriage? In the world's eyes, how long is a marriage for?
- What does the Christian hope to accomplish in marriage?
- How are we made to "die to the law?" Who made us die to the law? Does that mean we don't have to obey God's principles? Then what does it mean?
- What is the purpose mentioned for why we were made to die to the law? (to bear fruit)
- Then what is the purpose for a believer?
- In verse 6, what is the "new way" that we serve God?
- How does the Law arouse sinful passions in us?
- What does it mean, apart from the Law, sin is dead?
- What can we see about sin from verses 8-11?

**Verse by Verse Commentary**

**Basic principle:** The law doesn't govern people's lives after they die. Those who have heard the commands of God's law are under it and can't escape it unless they die. Once Eve heard the command not to eat the fruit, she couldn't ever go back to that naive state of ignorance. While some earthly laws have a statute of limitation, God's laws never do.

**A married person is bound:** A married person is bound to his/her spouse as long as he/she is living. This illustrates the principle that one cannot escape the law except through death. At the same time we learn important principles about marriage. God's design of marriage is sacred, a picture of Christ and the church. Christians must not marry non-believers. The husband must love his wife as himself. The husband must provide for the needs of his wife and care for and protect her. The wife must submit to and respect her husband and follow his leadership. The two become one. Once they are joined together, only death should separate them. A married person is bound to their spouse. This bond only death can break, which is why most weddings have a "until death do us part" vow. It is meant for life, a lifelong commitment.

For single people, be careful. Don't make hasty decisions based on emotion or romantic feelings. Follow Biblical principles before you get married. Make sure the prospect is committed to the Lord fully. Understand the real person and not the facade. It is healthy to have just a little bit of fear at the prospect of marriage, enough to make you think long and hard about it before you commit, because once you do you can't back out.

For those who are married, invest in your marriage and keep making it stronger for the future. Weather any storms that you face and keep your vows to stick together for better or for worse.

**We were made to die to the Law:** We figuratively died with Christ. This is God's work in us—He made us die to the law and we were resurrected to new life. It is not something we could escape on our own. We died to the law, not because it was bad, but because there is something better we can be joined to—Christ.

**We are joined to Christ for bearing fruit:** You might ask why God made us die to the Law when He Himself made the Law. He made the Law to serve a purpose, but once that purpose is fulfilled He jettisons it. It is like a rocket using a large gas tank and then jettisoning it once used up. It is good for that moment of time, but to keep it longer would only be cumbersome and slow the rocket ship down.

The purpose of our justification, the purpose of our being made to die to the law, the purpose of our very life is to bear fruit to God. We are like a tree planted in God's orchard. He waters us. He fertilizes us. He gives us sunshine. All of these are for the purpose of making us fruitful. But if a fruit tree is diseased and infected and doesn't bear fruit, the farmer will lose patience with it, cut it down, and burn it up. A fruitless fruit tree is worthless and we are also worthless to God if we don't bear fruit. With the Law out of the way, we can focus on bearing fruit to God without worrying about trying to keep every list of right and wrong. We should be motivated to serve God out of love and gratitude for His grace.

**Serve in newness of the Spirit and not in oldness of the letter:** This doesn't mean we don't obey God's laws, but we are freed to think of how to please God by following His principles from our hearts rather than only following a list of routines. The Jews are a great example of obeying in oldness of the letter. Many obeyed the letter of the Law, but in their evil twisted the meanings. Examples: The man who asked who was his neighbor knew the letter of the law but defined neighbor in a way convenient to himself. A person obeying in spirit isn't trying to justify not showing love, but is instead thinking of how to love others in action. Those who called out the woman adulteress were following the letter of the law, but their motivation was to trap Jesus. They were punishing this woman out of hate for Jesus rather than zeal for following God's law. Following God in newness of life instead of in oldness of the letter, we will be as far from it as we can get rather than getting right to the line and saying "I didn't break it."

**The Law is good and holy:** Sin is deceitful and tricky and will seek every chance to catch us. The heart is deceitful and desperately wicked. In addition to learning of the goodness of the Law, this passage tells us about the wiles of sin.

**Reasons this passage is discussing Paul's current life (and by implication the struggle all believers face):**

1. He uses the personal pronoun throughout, "I," referring obviously to himself and not to a carnal or legalistic Christian.
2. He uses present tense verbs throughout, which would be very strange if he was referring to his past life.
3. He is describing a vicious struggle between doing right and wrong, a struggle that really only becomes a major struggle when someone turns to Christ.
4. He mentions many times his desire to do what is right (verses 15, 18, and 19). Before in Romans he said an unbeliever does not seek after God. This new desire of his mind to serve God implies a new nature.
5. In verse 18, when he says there is no good in him, he qualifies that by saying "in his flesh." This qualification is unnecessary if he was an unbeliever, since there is no good anywhere in him, not only in his flesh.

In Ephesians 4:22, Paul says to "lay aside the old self, which is being corrupted in accordance with the lusts of deceit," and in Colossians 3:9-10, "Do not lie to one another, since you laid aside the old self with its evil practices, and have put on the new self who is being renewed in knowledge according to the image of the One who created Him."

# Romans 7:13-25 and 8:1-39

## The Struggle with Sin (Romans 7:13-25)

Both Romans 6:6 and 7:6 talk about the old man, the old self, or the old nature. While on earth in this body, we still have this old nature with desires and temptations to do wrong. In Christ, we have victory by taking the escape offered in 1 Corinthians 10:17 and following Colossians 3:2-3: "Set your mind on the things above, not on the things that are on earth. For you have died and your life is hidden with Christ in God."

In Romans 7:15-23, Paul describes the struggle with the old nature that every believer faces. The opposite side is the "good" that Paul can do because Christ lives in him. The good and evil have a constant war. Satan "roams around like a prowling lion searching for someone to devour" (1 Peter 5:8).

**Verses 13-14:** The law is not evil; it shows us our sin. The law has a purpose but cannot save us because all people are slaves to the old sin nature before trusting in Christ.

**Verses 15-23:** Paul faces continuous battle to do right. The old nature is strong and tempts us to sin. Paul's struggle is universal: "I love God; I want to put Him first; I want to serve Him with my life; I know sin is wrong and I hate to do it. But why do I keep doing it?"

A true Christian will hate sin. If anyone doesn't feel guilty when they sin, they are not a true Christian. Psalm 97:10 says: "Hate evil, you who love the Lord." We cannot serve two masters—either we love God and hate evil, or we love evil and hate God. We must show this love by our actions, not only with words (James 1:22).

The purer and holier the heart, the more quickly it feels remaining sin. The believer sees the beauty of holiness and the excellence of God's law. His earnest desires to obey increase as he grows in grace. But though his will is bent toward good, he often does evil through remaining corruption against his fixed determination.

There are three stages a true believer takes: First, we are born as slaves of sin and enemies of God. Second, after trusting Christ, He forgives our sins, adopts us as sons or daughters, and makes us friends with eternal life. We are no longer slaves of sin and can have victory in Christ. However, the old sin nature remains until we die because of Adam's curse. When we trust Christ, this nature is defeated, meaning we are not slaves to it. We wish to do good and joyfully want to follow God's law, but evil remains.

The answer is Jesus Christ—He sets us free from the body of death and gives us victory over sin. In the future, He will bring us to the third stage: heaven. When we enter heaven, God will give us a new, perfect body without curse, sin, or temptation (Revelation 7:17). This happens when we die or Christ returns.

For now, we must grow closer to Christ daily through prayer and reading His word, use His strength and power to defeat sin, hate sin, and put on God's armor to protect ourselves (Ephesians 6).

## Free From Condemnation (Romans 8:1-17)

**Verses 1-4:** We have NO condemnation because we believe in Christ. The Old Testament law was insufficient—repeated sacrifices never took away sin, only covered it. Jesus Christ, the perfect Lamb of God, was the final sacrifice. Through His blood alone could sins be taken away and we be redeemed (Hebrews 9:22b).

**Verses 5-6:** These verses show completely different goals after believing in Christ. "Set your minds" shows a firm decision and complete focus. Before Christ, we are "set" on evil deeds of the flesh. After Christ, we live by the Spirit, "set" on following the Spirit of life and peace. We must choose one or the other—give our hearts completely to God or completely to the flesh.

"Set your mind" means deliberate meditation. If we allow our minds freedom to roam, they naturally turn to worries, worldly dreams, or temptations. Do you control your mind or does it control you? The solution is to take it to God in prayer and occupy your mind with something else (Proverbs 4:23, Psalm 26:2, Proverbs 18:15).

**Verses 7-8:** If we set our mind on serving ourselves, we make ourselves enemies of God. Only non-believers do this. Believers are regenerate, born again with radically shifted priorities (Titus 3:5). The unregenerate cannot please God. Though good works may seem virtuous, Jesus condemned the Pharisees for their motivations. God looks at hearts. In the flesh, we cannot please God because the flesh is inherently sinful. Only by walking in the Spirit can we please God.

**Verses 9-11:** The regenerate are different. All born-again believers are in the Spirit. Christ describes Himself as "life" and promises life to those who believe. Non-believers lack true life—they are just vapor that appears briefly and disappears (James 4:14), with no goal, purpose, or meaning. For us, life in Christ has goal, meaning, peace, hope, and light and love (John 6:35, 11:25, 14:6, 17:3, 20:31). The Holy Spirit lives in every believer—there is no question about this.

**Verses 12-17:** We must "put to death" the deeds of the flesh through a changed life. If someone lives just like unbelieving colleagues or family members, probably they don't believe either. We must be different (1 Peter 1:13-16).

We have a high position in Christ. We were enemies, now friends (reconciliation). We were strangers, now children (adoption). In verse 15, "Abba" means "daddy"—a very close and personal word showing intimate relationship with God. We are God's children with a great inheritance. One requirement exists: we must be willing to suffer for Christ, and He will glorify us (Ephesians 1:4-5).

We must put to death the deeds of the flesh and follow the fruits of the Spirit. The Holy Spirit is always with us and will help us. We should thank God and rejoice in the wonderful life we have, set our minds on spiritual goals, and thank God for the high position of adoption.

## Future Glory and God's Love (Romans 8:18-39)

**Verses 18-23:** Present sufferings are nothing compared with the coming glory. All difficulties are temporary. Even with problems, you will find more blessings than difficulties when you count your blessings.

The curse is still in the world during this second stage. Creation feels the weight of sin. Yet creation hopes for the third stage when we will be in heaven, free from the curse God placed after Adam sinned. We will see God's glory when sin is gone and be in glorified bodies able to see God's glory. This third stage cannot be compared with present trials and difficulties.

Even creation will be freed from its burden. When sickness, sin, and disease are lifted, creation will want to shout in joy as the stones did when Christ came (Luke 19:38-40). We have the "first fruits of the Spirit"—a guarantee of what is coming. We groan, waiting for redemption of our bodies. We have hope for the time in heaven with Christ when sin, death, sickness, and disease will be gone (Revelation 21:4). We must be eager for this day and persevere until it comes.

**Verses 26-30:** The Spirit prays for us through intercession. We don't always know how to pray as we should, but the Holy Spirit intercedes on our behalf. God works all things together for the good of those who love Him and are called according to His purpose. This doesn't mean nothing bad happens to believers, but that God can use all circumstances—others' sin against us, persecution, job loss, sickness, loss of loved ones—for our ultimate good. This teaches us God's sovereignty. Many biblical examples show seemingly bad things working out for good (Genesis 50:20). This knowledge should change our attitude and outlook daily.

God foreknew us before the foundation of the world, predestining us to be conformed to the image of Christ (verse 29). Christ is the firstborn among many brethren. From start to finish, the process is: God foreknew, predestined, called, justified, and glorified (verse 30). There is no room for anyone to slip through the cracks. We are currently being called and justified, moving toward glorification.

**Verses 31-39:** Paul asks what response we should have toward these truths. No one can successfully bring charges against God's elect. Satan cannot succeed. Besides the Spirit, Christ Himself intercedes for us (verse 34), showing His care. We achieve monumental victory: "in all these things we are more than conquerors through Him who loved us" (verse 37).

God's love in Greek is "agape"—unconditional and supreme. No matter what you do, God still loves you. This doesn't excuse sin, because fellowship with Christ suffers if we sin, but He never stops loving us. His love is greater than anything in creation. Our hope is sure because God's love never changes (verses 38-39).

# Romans 8:18-39 and Romans 9-10 Study Guide

## CREATION'S FUTURE AND OUR HOPE (Romans 8:18-39)

After the fall of man, sin entered the world and God cursed creation. It became subject to futility—no longer as fruitful, peaceful, or paradisiacal as before. Thorns, thistles, weeds, infertile soil, and violence followed. Creation groans under this curse, and as believers trapped in corrupted bodies, we long for freedom from it just as creation does.

The pains of childbirth represent short-term sufferings that fade when compared to future joy. Our present sufferings will similarly fade when compared to what God will give us in the future (Colossians 3:4). We are already adopted as God's children now, but we haven't yet seen Him face to face or received our full inheritance and new bodies. This is like a formally adopted child who hasn't yet met his adopted parents—legally adopted but not yet experiencing full family life.

Many slaves in bondage groaned but held hope for freedom, which kept them from despair and gave them energy to continue. Our hope is far greater. Do you hope eagerly for heaven? We cannot see it, but it is coming. Don't become discouraged with life's problems; remember your blessings and live eagerly awaiting that final stage.

This hope differs from worldly hope—it is certain, though not yet realized. Two words are crucial: First, **eager**—we should long for that day. If we don't, we love this world too much and should set our minds on heaven. Second, **perseverance**—we must persist in the tasks God has given us, being faithful. This hope energizes our service to God until He says, "Well done, good and faithful servant."

## GOD'S ACTIVE INVOLVEMENT WHILE WE WAIT

While we await Christ's return, the Trinity actively works on our behalf. God controls all circumstances (John 10:27-30). The Holy Spirit intercedes for us—stepping in to guide us toward God's will when we don't know what to pray. He lives in our hearts; if we're in God's will, He brings peace; if not, He shows us. His prayers focus on our deeper needs—wisdom, character growth, faith, hope, and victory over sin—rather than temporal concerns. Jesus also intercedes for us at God's right hand (Hebrews 34), and God receives those prayers.

## GOD'S SOVEREIGNTY AND GOOD PURPOSES (Romans 8:28)

God is sovereign and supervises everything that enters the lives of those who love Him and are called to His purpose. This applies to believers, not all people. God controls all things—nothing slips through cracks, and Satan never gets something past God without His notice.

**How God's sovereignty should affect our attitudes:**

1. Never complain about any situation; doing so is complaining about God.
2. Always be thankful in everything.
3. Don't worry about present or future; your life is in God's capable and loving hands.
4. Look to Christ in faith regardless of circumstances rather than assuming they're insurmountable.
5. Be patient and loving with those who mistreat you—they're tools in God's hand meant to teach you lessons.
6. Continuously evaluate circumstances to find the good and learn God's intended lessons.
7. Maintain a bright, optimistic, joyful outlook—this testifies to others.

**Biblical examples of apparent evil becoming good:**
- Lot's choice allowed Abraham to inherit the land God prepared for him.
- Joseph's slavery and imprisonment led to becoming second-in-command in Egypt.
- Satan's persecution of Job taught Job valuable lessons.
- Herod's threat to Jesus led His family to Egypt, fulfilling Messianic prophecy.

## PREDESTINATION AND ELECTION

God chose us before the world began (Ephesians 1:3-5). This doesn't mean God simply knew ahead of time what would happen; rather, He predetermined or decided to set His love on us. "Knew" in Scripture often points to relationship, not mere knowledge. God made the choice to choose us for His family.

The process continues: He works in us to change and grow us, makes us acceptable through justification, and will give us new bodies and exalt us. One hundred percent of those beginning this process finish it—God loses none. This teaches both eternal security (John 10:28-30; Philippians 1:6) and perseverance of the saints. True saints bear fruit and persevere. Jesus said He lost none of those God gave Him except Judas, who was never saved (John 17:12).

This doctrine is comforting—we needn't fear God changing His mind about our salvation. Yet it's also challenging because God's sheep follow Him and must obey faithfully and bear fruit in keeping with repentance (Matthew 3:8).

## GOD'S LOVE IN DETAIL (Romans 8:31-39)

God's love is greater than any enemies, government, exam, colleague, job, family, or problem. God gave us His only Son and will not withhold anything else we need. Jesus, standing at God's right hand in the position of honor, intercedes for us. We have such a loving, kind Friend—a King with unlimited resources poured out with unconditional love. This is why God sent His Son as a baby over 2,000 years ago. Because of this love, we celebrate Christmas.

# PAUL'S HEART FOR THE ISRAELITES (Romans 9:1-13)

Paul affirms three times that what he says is truly true: he has great sorrow and anguish for the Israelites, his kinsmen according to the flesh. He would be willing to be accursed and separated from Christ for eternity if it would save them. This is love greater than that described in John 15:13—he would sacrifice not just his life but his eternal soul.

Paul didn't merely grieve; he acted. His normal practice upon entering a city was to go to the synagogue first, witnessing to Jews countless times despite their enmity and hatred. He dedicated his entire life to sharing the gospel.

Verse 4 lists Israel's benefits from divine election: adoption, glory, covenants, the law, temple service, prophets, and promises. Adoption means God chose the entire nation to receive His calling and covenant, though not necessarily every individual.

## GOD'S SOVEREIGN PLAN (Romans 9:6-13)

God's plan didn't fail when Israel rejected Christ. His plan never included saving every Jew. Being Jewish wasn't sufficient; no good deed was enough. God's promise was given to Isaac, not Ishmael, and to Jacob, not Esau—before either had done anything good or bad. God is the one who calls; we don't call Him (John 15:16; Romans 3:10-12). History is His story.

A child of the promise is one chosen by God. Though Abraham had many descendants, only Isaac received the promise—the true child of God. Among many "good" people, only those specially chosen by God are His true children. God chose them based on His purpose and plan, which stands firm and cannot fail.

**Interpreting "God hated Esau" (verse 13, citing Malachi 1:2-3):**

1. God emotionally hated Esau and predestined him to judgment, as He predestined Isaac to blessings.
2. The meaning is comparative—as Jesus says believers must "hate" father and mother compared to love for Christ. God chose Isaac but "left Esau alone"; compared to His great love for Isaac, His actions toward Esau seemed like hatred, though He blessed Esau too. Esau lacked strong faith and wasn't chosen for God's covenant.
3. Malachi wrote 1,500 years after Jacob and Esau. The love/hate applies to their descendants' nations. God sovereignly elected Israel but didn't choose Esau's descendants, so their sins brought divine punishment. Love means "chose for intimate fellowship"; hate means "not choosing for intimate fellowship."

The focus isn't God condemning certain people to hell but that He chose Israel and conveyed blessings through no merit of their own. Scripture shows God gives opportunities to believe to all the lost, including those not elect. Repeated invitations throughout the prophets and New Testament call unbelievers to hear and repent. God's election doesn't negate genuine choice—people reject the gospel by their own accord anyway, making divine election for hell unnecessary.

# THE WRONG AND RIGHT PATHS TO RIGHTEOUSNESS (Romans 10)

The Israelites pursued righteousness the wrong way. They had zeal for God, yet "not in accordance with knowledge." They didn't subject themselves to God's righteousness but pursued their own righteousness through outward works and flesh—the wrong path.

Christ is the end of the law for righteousness (verse 4). Faith-based righteousness doesn't need to ascend to heaven to prove Christ is there or descend to the abyss to prove He died—these are already accomplished facts requiring only genuine faith from the heart.

**The process of salvation:**

Salvation requires two things: believe in your heart and confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord (verses 9-10). Confession validates genuine inner belief. "As Lord" means accepting His authority and rulership over your life. No one who believes in Him will be disappointed (verse 11)—this applies to all people regardless of background; the same Lord is Lord of all.

The gospel invitation extends to all who call upon the Lord (verse 13). Hearing the Word of Christ is essential for faith (verse 17)—not songs or lectures but the actual Word of God.

## ISRAEL'S REJECTION AND THE GENTILES' CALLING (Romans 10:14-21)

God intended to make Israel jealous through the Gentiles' response to the gospel (verse 19). This jealousy, paradoxically, is good—it motivates Israel to recognize what they've missed and reconsider the gospel. This will occur when the fullness of the Gentiles is brought in.

God extended His hands to Israel—a disobedient and contrary people (verse 21)—showing His patience and character. Israel's rejection of the gospel doesn't negate God's character or make the gospel invalid; rather, it opened salvation to the Gentiles. Gentiles might have wondered why Jews rejected Jesus and whether they should believe. By showing Israel rejected Christ, Paul clarifies that Christianity isn't merely a Judaism sect but a universal faith.

# Romans 10:1-21 & Romans 11

## Key Teachings on Israel's Rejection and God's Plan

**God's Sovereignty and Israel's Purpose**

The entire Old Testament documents God's work with the Israelites. When Israel rejected the Messiah, some wondered if God's plan had failed. However, this was part of God's sovereign plan, demonstrating His absolute authority over history.

God's blessing is not based on ethnicity but on faith. We learn from Israel's mistakes so we don't repeat them. God's grace extends to all people—Jews and Gentiles alike—who have faith in Him. This is an encouraging doctrine.

**Paul's Sincere Desire for Salvation**

Paul grieved deeply for the Jews, motivated by love rather than obligation. His heartfelt longing for their salvation was connected to prayer: if you care for someone, you pray for them. Paul prayed specifically for their salvation, addressing the most important issue in their lives. Do not be preoccupied with worldly requests.

**Zeal Without Knowledge**

The Jews demonstrated great zeal for God—tithing meticulously, keeping the Sabbath, traveling to Jerusalem for feasts, and making proselytes. Some did these things from genuine belief, like Paul before his conversion. However, their zeal lacked a solid foundation because it wasn't based on truth.

An analogy: A hiking guide confidently leads people on what he thinks is a shortcut up a mountain, moving fast and enthusiastically. Unknown to him, the shortcut leads the opposite direction. All his zealous effort is worse than useless because the entire route must be re-hiked. This is zeal without knowledge. It is not good to hold a wrong belief passionately, no matter how sincere. Worship must be in spirit and truth (John 4:24).

**Man's Righteousness vs. God's Righteousness**

The Jews' key problem was attempting to work their way to heaven instead of realizing that true righteousness comes only through Christ. This is the core of all false religions: the belief that we must do enough good things to satisfy God.

Man's righteousness may be incorrect because it's evaluated by human logic (e.g., white lies seem acceptable to humans but not to God). Human good deeds often stem from wrong motivations—not from the heart but as means to an end. God's righteousness, however, stems from a pure heart.

Christ is the end of the law—He fulfills it. The law foreshadows Christ's coming and shows we cannot solve the sin problem ourselves. Christ solved it for those who believe in Him. No one can completely fulfill the law in every detail, which is why we need Christ.

**Faith, Not Sight**

Faith believes without seeing. It is disrespectful to Christ's character to demand proof of His existence in heaven. We don't need to journey through the universe or descend into the abyss to verify Christ's death and resurrection. Faith trusts His Word as taught by Himself, the prophets, and the apostles.

**Elements of the Gospel Message**

1. **Believe in Jesus as Lord**: We must not merely accept facts about Christ but surrender our lives to Him. Demons acknowledged Jesus as God's Son but rebelled against Him. Lordship means willingness to submit our will to Christ's, though believers aren't suddenly perfect.

2. **Confess openly**: Public confession shows genuine and firm belief and identifies us with Christ before the world. Jesus warned He would be ashamed of those ashamed of Him. Some fear public confession due to persecution (like Nicodemus).

3. **Believe genuinely in your heart**: Some confess insincerely—to please parents, partners, or for church activities. True belief must be sincere. If belief is genuine, we will not be disappointed in Christ.

4. **Only Christ brings salvation**: Believing anything other than Christ raised from the dead is insufficient for salvation.

Disappointment in Christianity often stems from wrong expectations—thinking Christ guarantees health, wealth, and a problem-free life. When trials come, people blame God and become bitter, having believed in a false god of their own making. The true Living God never promised freedom from trials. We should have Job's attitude (Job 2:10-11).

**No Distinction in God's Kingdom**

Just as there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, there is none between any nationalities. All believers are members of God's kingdom regardless of origin. Serving God is equally difficult or rewarding everywhere.

**The Gospel Must Be Heard**

Whoever calls on the Lord's name is invited to salvation—the invitation is for anyone.

These well-known verses show the need for both hearing and believing. While we cannot force belief, we can ensure people hear—the necessary first step before believing. Many don't believe despite hearing, just as many didn't believe Isaiah, the great prophet. Belief isn't accomplished through miraculous presentations but normally through God's people sent to preach.

**Application**: Ask yourself who among your friends, family, coworkers, and classmates hasn't heard the gospel. Give them the opportunity. If you do, you will have beautiful feet (Romans 10:15).

Faith grows through knowledge of truth from studying God's Word. Faith in the gospel begins with hearing God's Word and continues to grow as we study it. Seeing God's fulfilled promises, care for His people, miracles, power, and answered prayers strengthens our faith when facing similar situations.

**Israel Heard and Rejected**

Paul anticipated the objection that Israel never truly heard or surely would have received it if they had. He refutes this by showing God's words went out to all the world, not just Israel.

Even in Old Testament times, God prophesied Israel's rejection, demonstrating His sovereignty—this didn't surprise Him. God planned to use the Gentiles as an example to encourage the Jews to repent and return to Him.

Jealousy itself isn't necessarily evil. God was jealous when Israel worshiped other gods. A husband's jealousy when seeing his wife with another man is natural and proper since they are one. Jealousy is evil only when misguided—when we have no right to someone's affections yet desire them. Through His great power, God will use Gentiles' blessings to show Israel what they're missing and encourage repentance. God's amazing patience and longsuffering contrast with the Jews' rebellion and hard hearts (Romans 10:21).

## Romans 11: God Has Not Rejected Israel

**Overview**

Perhaps Israelites reading Romans 9-10 wondered if they could be restored to God. Gentiles might think they were now superior to Jews. Both were wrong. God has not rejected His people, and restoration will come one day.

**I. God Preserved a Remnant (Romans 11:1-10)**

Paul uses the strongest possible language to confirm God has not rejected Israel. The word "rejection" means discard or thrust away—the difference between disciplining a son for wrongdoing and casting him out of the home entirely.

Though Israel rejected the gospel as a nation—a clear historical fact that continues today—God has not rejected His people. Paul offers three proofs:

1. **Paul himself**: An Israelite believer in Christ. While Israel as a nation was condemned, the door remained open for individuals. All disciples, the seventy, and 3,000 at Pentecost were Israelites who came to faith. Thousands of Israelites now believe in Christ and are part of the church.

2. **God's sovereign election**: God foreknew the nation of Israel from eternity. His many unconditional OT promises to the Jews wouldn't be broken. He sovereignly controls their rebellion and would not cast away His chosen people.

3. **A preserved remnant**: God always preserved faithful Jews. During Israel's darkest hour under Jezebel's Baal worship—when Elijah thought he alone remained faithful—God had preserved 7,000 faithful men. Even during early NT times, thousands remained. Today, though most Israelites reject Christ, many thousands believe.

**Lessons from God's Remnant**:
- No matter how dark things appear, God hasn't forgotten His people.
- This demonstrates God's grace, not human merit. It's God's choice to show kindness to those He selects.
- When you feel alone in faith, remember you likely aren't the only believer in your place or vocation. Elijah-like despair is especially common under persecution, but comfort exists in knowing other believers are having victory.

God judicially hardened some as part of His plan to bring salvation to the Gentiles through Jewish rejection. God is not hardening people truly seeking Him or blocking seekers from finding Him. All people are sinful and totally depraved—they would reject God anyway. The hardened ones receive what their own nature deserves.

**II. Israel's Stumbling Leads to Gentile Salvation and Jewish Restoration (Romans 11:11-16)**

"Stumble so as to fall"—stumbling refers to short-term rejection; falling refers to permanent rejection. Israel's rejection is neither final nor permanent.

Their transgression was crucifying Jesus and refusing to believe He was the Messiah despite His declarations, miracles, and fulfilled prophecies. God planned their rejection as a means to save the Gentiles, which would make the Jews jealous and result in their salvation. In a sense, Gentiles are saved through the Jews, and Jews are saved because of the Gentiles.

The world was blessed by Israel's rejection of the Messiah. Their very act of killing Him provided the way for our salvation. When Israel finally accepts Christ as King over nation and world, it will usher in the millennial kingdom—a time of peace, prosperity, and joy for the whole earth.

If the root (Israel) is holy, the branches will be as well. Eventually, the majority of Israel will accept Christ, causing Gentiles to become even more fervent in faith through the Israelites' example.

**III. Gentiles Grafted In—Humility Required (Romans 11:17-24)**

Paul explains the relationship between Israel and the Gentiles through grafting. This process fuses two kinds of plants together.

- **The cultivated olive tree**: Israel, cultivated by God for centuries.
- **The wild olive tree**: The Gentiles.

God broke off branches (most Israelites) due to unbelief and fused Gentiles to the plant. The resulting church historically emerged from Israel, was started by Israelites, has an Israelite (Jesus) as its leader, and uses scriptures written by Israelites, especially the Old Testament.

Paul admonishes Gentiles: instead of arrogance, be grateful. The root nourishing you was the original plant—the Jews. The adopted son has the same rights and privileges as the biological son but should remember his humble beginnings and the kindness of his adoptive parents.

**Two Practical Applications**:

1. **Be humble and grateful**: Your position in the church comes through God's kindness. Becoming arrogant toward a biological son who has wandered away wouldn't be acceptable.

2. **Persevere in faith and remain alert**: If God is just enough to punish the natural plant (Jews) who turn from Him, He is just enough to punish the wild plant (Gentiles) who turn from Him. Both are treated the same without favoritism. Both are forgiven if faithful; both are cast off if they rebel (see John 15:6). Neither side should consider itself superior.

**IV. Israel's Final Restoration (Romans 11:25-26)**

God will restore Israel fully after "the fullness of the Gentiles has come in." The church age—a time of Jewish rejection and vast Gentile salvation—is a "mystery" (a major doctrine hidden in the OT, revealed in the NT). Knowing this was part of God's eternal plan should prevent our pride. Our position results from God's grace, not our merit.

The hardening of Israel is partial—neither permanent nor complete. It doesn't apply to all Jews, as some are elect. This partial hardening lasts until God mostly finishes His work among the Gentiles.

God's work resembles a vast harvest, cultivating one major field at a time. In the OT, He worked primarily in Israel. In the NT and until now, He works primarily among Gentiles. When He finishes harvesting the Gentiles (and they see the blessings given to us), He will return to the Israelites. This will occur during the tribulation period, when He will save at least 144,000 Jews—possibly more if this number refers to Jewish evangelists called to reach their nation.

**V. God's Eternal Choice Stands (Romans 11:27-32)**

God's eternal choice of Israel as a nation will stand. He will show mercy to those who are disobedient. The mercy shown to Gentiles helps Jews receive mercy from God.

**VI. God's Ways Are Higher Than Ours (Romans 11:33-36)**

Paul concludes by explaining that God's ways—His choice of the Jews and His plans through history for Gentile salvation and eventual Jewish restoration—are higher than our ways. Our response should be humility and wonder at God's infinite wisdom and plan.

# Christian Perspectives and Romans 12-13

## On Jews and God's Plan

At the time Paul wrote, Christians and Jews were in conflict. Jews rejected Christ and persecuted Christians. However, we must not treat them as enemies. From God's perspective, they are beloved. He loves them and has a plan for them. Christians should be pro-Israel because God blesses those who bless Israel. This has shown itself true throughout history. Unfortunately, some professed Christians have been anti-Semitic, blaming Jews for their problems or for Christ's death.

Why did God plan salvation this way—choosing one nation first, then extending to Gentiles? We often don't know the "why" behind God's plans. Sometimes He reveals it; often He doesn't. We don't have to understand. As one priest said after twenty years of ministry: "First, there is a God. Second, I am not Him." God is God and we are not. If that is not enough for you, you are doubting God and setting yourself up as a judge over Him.

Verse 36 concludes the doctrinal section of Romans. The first 11 chapters deal mostly with doctrine; the last five are practical application. Doctrine by itself only builds knowledge, but knowledge puffs up while love edifies. These truths should change how we live.

## Romans 12: Living as a Sacrifice

**I. Present Yourselves as Living Sacrifices (Verses 1-2)**

Based on God's theological truths in the first chapters, Christians should dedicate their lives in service to the Lord. This is the wise response. Jesus said the foolish person who heard but didn't practice His words was like someone building a house on sand. We want to be like the wise builder who builds on rock.

Remember what God has done for us. Though we were totally depraved and destined for His wrath, He called and justified us because of His choice—His initiative, His grace, not our work.

God requires much more than small gestures like donating money, buying blankets, singing songs of praise, or wearing Christian jewelry. Neither does it necessarily require huge ministry accomplishments like becoming a missionary or pastoring thousands. God doesn't just want your money, possessions, or time. He wants your life. We are to be living sacrifices, giving Him all our energy, thoughts, and actions, all the time. We serve Him in a thousand small ways every day. We are to put His will above ours in every area.

Our lives are also to be holy. Old Testament sacrifices were unblemished and pure. God wants to use clean vessels, not dirty ones.

Second, we are not to be conformed to this world. The world system is controlled by Satan, God's arch-enemy. He uses education systems, governments, peer pressure, cultural influences, friends, family, and relatives to brainwash both unbelievers and believers. Many believers are told to stop pursuing God and start pursuing money, to stop waiting for a Christian spouse, or not to discipline their children. When you make life plans—choosing college, careers, locations, spouses—do you just follow the world's path without thinking twice, or do you seek if God has a better way?

But Paul gives us the perfect alternative: renew your minds. Charge your minds with energy from God's ultimate source, the Bible. Wash away the world's influences and study God's way. From the Fall, the world's systems are corrupted and cursed. We need to renew our minds to understand God's original purposes, will, and design.

**II. Use Your Spiritual Gifts (Verses 3-8)**

To some, spiritual gifts are a source of pride. But Paul shows these gifts are just that—gifts given by God's grace. We must receive and use them humbly. The church includes many people but is united, with each person serving one another. Each person has his own role.

We must use our gifts and not let them go to waste. We are to use our gifts fully in accordance with God's purposes.

The source of gifts is not people or demons, but the Holy Spirit, Jesus, and God the Father—the Trinity. If He is the source, they are also for Him and always conducted in accordance with His will. These gifts are supernaturally bestowed on believers.

Three general categories exist: verbal gifts (teaching, preaching, prophecy, knowledge, wisdom—for edifying the church), serving gifts (leadership, giving, mercy, faith, discernment), and sign gifts (miracles, healing, tongues, interpretation). There are all kinds of gifts and perhaps combinations, but the same God gave all of them. Therefore, there is no basis for pride that one gift is better than another. Every gift and every believer is vital and fills a distinct, important role.

Although there are many gifts, the purpose is the same: the common good. They build up the body so it can better glorify the Lord and reach the world for Him.

Key reminders:
- We need unity.
- We have the same goal and should work for it.
- You have a special gift from the Lord.
- You should use your gift by actively serving others.

Every believer has received God's grace in the form of spiritual gifts. Every believer has at least one gift, not just some. This is freely given and completely undeserved. The giver is Christ.

**The Reality of Church Service**

Unfortunately, in most churches around the world, 10% of people do 90% of the work. The congregation arrives on time or late and leaves right after. They may comment on the sermon or drop money in the offering plate, but they are largely uninvolved. Meanwhile, pastors and teachers labor all week putting together classes, studies, sermons, lessons, directing choirs, cleaning, preparing materials, visiting the sick, conducting weddings, dedicating babies, and conducting funerals. The balance is disproportionate.

Every believer must be involved in serving the Lord. Ask yourself: Are you? This Sunday, ask how you can help or volunteer for a specific ministry. Whatever you are given to do, do it with all your heart. Don't consider it beneath you. Eventually, as you serve, your special gifting will become apparent.

**III. Christian Qualities and Character (Verses 9-21)**

This passage covers many qualities Christians should have:

1. Love
2. Hate evil
3. Love what is right
4. Be unselfish
5. Be diligent
6. Be fervent in spirit
7. Serve the Lord
8. Rejoice in hope
9. Persevere through trials
10. Be devoted to prayer
11. Practice hospitality
12. Bless those who persecute you; do not return evil for evil
13. Be compassionate and concerned
14. Be humble
15. Be considerate
16. Be peaceful
17. Do not take revenge

Since this list includes many characteristics, you likely see some as your strengths. However, none of us can read through and conclude we are doing well in all areas. Look through the list and mark the three worst areas where you see the most need for improvement. This weekend, write down one or two specific things you can do to improve in those areas and apply them the following week.

How can we possibly have the power to practice all these qualities? (1) Have the grace of God. (2) Get strength from the Holy Spirit. (3) Discipline ourselves.

## Romans 13: Submission to Authorities and Love

**Paraphrase**

Every person should obey governing authorities. There is no authority that God didn't establish. If a person disobeys authority, he is disobeying authority that God established and will be judged.

Those who do right need not fear the government, but those who do wrong should. Do what is right and you will be commended. Government is God's tool to do good. But if you do wrong, be afraid, because authorities punish wrongdoing. It is God's agent of punishment.

Submit to government to avoid punishment and to maintain a clear conscience. This is why you pay taxes—authorities are God's agents whose job is governing.

Give people what you owe them. If you owe taxes, pay them. If revenue, respect, or honor are owed, give them. Do not remain in debt, except the debt to love one another, for loving others fulfills the law.

The commandments—"Do not commit adultery," "Do not murder," "Do not covet," and all others—are summed up by the rule: love your neighbor as yourself. Love does not hurt others. So love fulfills the law.

Love each other, understanding the present age. Wake up from your sleep; our salvation is nearer than we first thought. The night is almost over; the day is almost here. Put off all acts of darkness and put on the armor of light. Behave decently, not in carousing, drunkenness, sexual sins, arguing, or jealousy. Instead, clothe yourselves with the Lord Jesus Christ and do not think about fulfilling your sinful desires.

**Verse by Verse Commentary**

**Submission to Authorities (Verses 1-7)**

Primarily this concerns governing authorities, but also refers to any authorities God places in our lives. God established authority to keep sin in check. Most governments have generally good laws maintaining social order.

Every believer is to be in submission to authorities. There is no authority except from God. Whoever resists authority opposes God's ordinances. This applies to everyone, all governments—even dictatorships are established by God.

Even the worst governments act as a deterrent to sin. No country legalizes theft, bombing others' property, or murder (except to the unborn). Sometimes governments themselves commit these acts while prohibiting citizens from doing them. No country has a perfect government. Everywhere you go, people complain about theirs.

What would happen without government? Crimes would increase. The economy would crumble. Bartering would replace currency. Gangs, warlords, and mini-fiefdoms would rule. It would be person against person, family against family. Everyone is totally depraved. Without order and deterrent to sin, anarchy would rule. Look at Genesis 6 and Judges to see the world without established governments. Everyone does what is right in their own eyes. There is no standard of law to arbitrate who is actually right.

Three reasons are given for submitting to authorities:

First, it is established by God. If we disobey the government (except when it tells us to do something clearly wrong), it is the same as disobeying God. The government acts as His representative. God gave governments authority to establish laws. Resisting government is resisting against God.

Second, if you disobey, you will be punished. This is how negative deterrents work—like disciplining disobedient children. While not necessarily the purest motive, it is very effective. It should be enough to know God wants us to obey. But for self-centric people, Paul reminds them of the consequences. Verses 3-4 are general truths. Sometimes governments do punish those doing good, but these cases are rare. It is far more common to punish wrongdoing. If we do what is right, we live in peace, knowing we need not fear the government. Imagine the lives of criminals, always looking over their shoulder. Those who do right will sometimes be commended through medals, praise, or media highlights.

Third, we should obey governments to maintain a clean conscience. This motive is perhaps more pure than the previous one, speaking more to our mental well-being than physical well-being. Our mental well-being is even more important. If you feel guilty, it is hard to have a joyful life no matter how prosperous. But if you have a clean conscience, you can lead a joyful life even if poor.

**Secondary Issues**

Capital Punishment: This passage shows that governments have the right to bear a sword and punish citizens. We can also see from what God said to Noah that a government has this right.

Paying Taxes: The Bible is very clear that we are to pay taxes. Jesus said to give to God what is God's and to Caesar what is Caesar's. Government workers serve God by maintaining public order and deserve to be paid like church workers.

Exception to Obedience: Peter and John in Acts show an exception. If the government gives an order clearly contradictory to Scripture, we are to obey God first rather than man. If you were a believer in Germany in WWII, should you obey the government to hunt down and imprison/kill Jews? How about in America—should you have revolted against England?

Pray for Authorities: 1 Timothy 2:1-3 calls us to pray for those in authority. It is much easier to complain. But instead of complaining, get on your knees to pray for those in authority. From Daniel, we know governments are battlegrounds between spiritual forces.

Change Government Policies: This doesn't mean you can't work to change government policies (such as right-to-life prayer chains). It doesn't mean you must be passive and silent. Work within to change the law, not like abortion clinic bombers.

God's Establishment of Authorities: The fact that God establishes authorities doesn't mean He condones or approves of their sins. A perfect government would require wiping out sin immediately. Short of that, God uses sinful humans to restrain worse sin and turns their sin to accomplish His purposes.

We don't have to agree with policies, but we should obey the law and not make excuses. Being a good Christian includes being a good citizen and supporting the government where we can. In China, it is easy to resist the government since it is anti-Jesus, but still it was established by God. Obey the laws where you can do so with a good conscience.

**Debt and Love (Verses 8-10)**

This verse addresses not remaining in debt. Proverbs gives many reasons, but the main idea is love. It fulfills the law. We don't have to know every single rule and command. It is enough to act out of love for others and we will naturally keep the law.

**Living Expectantly (Verses 11-14)**

We are in the night now—Christ has not yet returned. He could return at any time. Therefore we need to be alert, awake, and ready. The negative aspect is putting away sinful behaviors. The positive aspect is putting on Christ's righteousness. It is important not to think about sinful desires or we will fall into temptation more easily.

### Outline
- Do not cause others to stumble (13)

### Key Discussion Questions

- Should Christians avoid foods based on inherent uncleanness? What does verse 14 actually teach about conscience?

- What is a conviction? How is it formed? Why is importance in having convictions "before God"?

### Teaching Point: Convictions

Convictions are standards rooted in God's Word, not changeable circumstances. They must be formed through personal study of Scripture, held with certainty, and motivated by faith to please God. A "convictionless" person will compromise when circumstances pressure them—they lack an anchor.

True convictions have three characteristics:
1. **Personal**: We must form them ourselves through Scripture study, not simply follow what others do.
2. **Before God**: We answer to God alone for our convictions. They are between us and God, not imposed on others.
3. **Of Faith**: Motivated by a desire to serve God, with confidence that He will sustain us when we uphold His standards.

God is pleased with believers who maintain Scriptural convictions despite opposition.

### Principles for Grey Areas

When developing convictions, these principles apply:

**Prerequisites:**
- Abide in Christ
- Be fully convinced of your beliefs
- Be guided by faith, not circumstance

**In Your Actions:**
- Never cause another to stumble (13)
- Love is the primary motivating factor (15)
- Consider your testimony before unbelievers (16)
- Edify and build up other believers (19)
- Put others' well-being above your own preferences (20-21)
- Maintain a clear conscience (22-23)

### Key Words
- **Stumbling block**: An obstacle or snare that causes one to fall
- **Edification**: Building up, not tearing down
- **Distressed**: To be sad or troubled
- **Conviction**: A firmly held belief based on Scripture, held before God in faith

## Romans 15:1-13

### Outline
I. Follow Christ Jesus in Unity (verses 1-13)
   A. Put others first (verses 1-2)
      - Christ's example (verse 3)
      - Scripture's instruction (verse 4)
      - Made possible through God (verse 5)
      - Purpose: to praise God (verse 6)
   B. Accept one another (verse 7)
      - Christ's example (verse 8)
      - Christ's purpose: salvation for all people (verses 9-12)
      - Result: hope, joy, and peace (verse 13)

### Teaching Point: Christian Unity and Service

This passage moves beyond merely refraining from judging and offending to *actively pursuing* the well-being of others.

**Bear with the Weak**
Practically, "bearing weaknesses" means lifting and carrying the weight together, like two travelers where one helps when the other tires. This includes:
- Praying for one another
- Sharing Scripture to address struggles
- Coming alongside and encouraging
- Listening to understand others' struggles
- Speaking grace and encouragement

**Please and Build Up Your Neighbor**
This requires intentional thought, careful consideration, and planned action. The immediate purpose is edifying the body of Christ; the ultimate purpose is God's glory.

**Christ's Example**
Of all people, Christ alone had the right to please Himself. Yet He gave that up, endured hate and scorn, and built us up through His sacrifice. He willingly imparts strength to us so we can follow in His footsteps.

**Scripture as Instruction**
The Old Testament provides both clear principles and examples of selflessness: Jonathan (who pleased David rather than himself), Joseph (who forgave and gently encouraged), and Abraham (who gave Lot choice of the land, then saved him and interceded for his city).

**Accept One Another**
We are to accept each other as Christ accepted us—to the same degree, with the same warmth. This begins with forgiveness and extends to embracing others with love. Like the father in the parable of the prodigal son, acceptance means running to embrace, not begrudging tolerance.

### Key Discussion Questions
- How can we bear others' weaknesses? What is the opposite of "please ourselves"?
- Did Christ have the right to please Himself? Did He? Do we have that right?
- What two things help guide us in pleasing and building up our neighbor? (Christ's example and Scripture)
- To what degree are we to accept one another? How did Christ accept us?
- What is the point of verses 9-12 regarding Jews and Gentiles?

### Teaching Point: Paul's Ministry and Authority

Paul reassures the Romans that his strict teaching was not an attack on their character. He knows they are mature and good-hearted. His teaching reminds them of what they already know so they won't forget or neglect important truths.

**Source of Authority**: Paul bases his authority on the "grace of God." He is God's chosen instrument to the Gentiles. Through three missionary journeys, he established churches and built up believers. None of this was for his own benefit—it was entirely to serve God and bring an offering of believing Gentiles to maturity.

Paul gave all glory to God for the fruit. He relied completely on God's strength. When we share God's Word, we must remind others that it is God's message we relay, not our own opinions.

**Principle of Ministry**: As an early evangelist, Paul spread the gospel where it hadn't been taught, to people who hadn't heard it. This principle remains a high calling, though not all ministers are called to the same work.

### Key Words
- **Minister**: One who serves God through public ministry
- **Priestly**: Every believer is a priest to God; our offering is our service
- **Struggle**: Public ministry is not easy; all believers should encourage those in ministry through prayer
- **Remind**: The Romans already knew much taught; reminders prevent forgetting and neglecting truth

### Application

- Examine whether you are obeying authorities in your life with a good attitude. If not, work on correction this week.
- Consider how you can actively bear others' weaknesses through prayer, Scripture sharing, encouragement, and gracious speech.
- Form your own convictions through personal study of Scripture, held firmly before God in faith.
- When sharing God's Word, remember you are relaying God's message, not your own ideas. Be bold and confident in what you know is true from Scripture.

# Romans 16 Study

## Content Overview

Paul discusses his future plans: going to Jerusalem, delivering an offering, then visiting Rome en route to Spain. He subjects these plans to God's will. Paul emphasizes that ministry is corporate—believers should support one another through prayer, financial help, hospitality, and encouragement. His desire to visit shows he cared deeply about personal relationships with those he ministered to.

## Greetings and Key Figures

Paul greets numerous individuals and groups, demonstrating his far-reaching ministry and deep personal relationships. These believers lived in societies that condemned and persecuted them, yet persevered in service to God.

**Phoebe** – A servant of the church in Cenchrea deserving hospitality and support. The lesson: all church members should help one another, and we should extend hospitality to traveling believers.

**Priscilla and Aquila** – Paul's fellow workers who risked their lives for him. They hosted a church in their home. Paul was not ministering alone; he had integral partners supporting him at every step. Even great missionaries need help from other believers.

## Final Instructions and Warnings

Paul warns the Romans to watch out for divisive people and false teachers who serve their own desires rather than God, using smooth talk and flattery to deceive. They should stay away from such people.

Paul also warns them to be wise about good things and innocent regarding evil. This separation from evil involves both physical action and mental discipline. We should know as much as possible about God and His Word, but desire to know as little as possible about evil's ways. Peer pressure is powerful—we become like those we associate with. It is easier to pull down than to pull up, so we should spend time productively away from bad influences.

## Benediction

Paul closes by praising God and giving Him all glory, a fitting conclusion to Romans.

## Overview of Romans

**Chapters 1-5:** Chapter 1 presents the gospel of Christ as the theme. Chapters 2-3 explain why it's necessary—all humans are unrighteous and condemned for rejecting God's law. Chapters 4-5 present true righteousness, which comes from Christ through faith, not works.

**Chapters 6-8:** These discuss how righteousness is lived out. Through Christ's power, we are sanctified and set free from sin, and nothing can separate us from His love.

**Chapters 9-11:** These address God's historical plan of salvation and how Jews and Gentiles fit within it. Salvation is completely God's choice and grace, not human goodness.

**Chapters 12-15:** These return to living out righteousness with instructions for behavior toward ourselves, the church, society, government, and other believers. Christ is our example.

**Chapter 16:** Greetings and closing remarks.
