# 2 Corinthians Teaching Notes

## I. Paul's Apostolic Authority
Paul establishes his God-given authority—not pridefully, but for others' well-being. Parents similarly remind children of authority established by God for their good.

## II. The God of All Comfort (3-7)

**Key Theme:** God comforts believers in affliction and suffering.

Paul experienced severe persecution—shipwrecks, beatings, whippings, stoning. In Asia, he was "utterly burdened beyond our strength," despaired of life, and "felt that we had received the sentence of death." When facing such moments, believers must turn to God and seek His comfort.

**Cross-References:**
- Psalm 23:4 – God's comfort in the valley of death
- Psalm 119:76, 50 – Steadfast love and promise bring comfort
- Matthew 5:4 – The mourning are blessed and comforted

**Application:** In times of affliction, pour out your heart to God and seek His comfort alone.

## III. Paul's Defense of His Ministry

**Motivation Matters:** Paul defended himself out of love for the Corinthians, not pride. False teachers had attacked his character and apostleship, threatening not just his reputation but the gospel's credibility. By proving himself credible, Paul protected the Corinthians from rejecting the gospel message.

Unlike Jesus, who did not defend Himself out of love for our salvation, Paul's defense served a different purpose: to preserve the gospel's integrity among the Corinthians.

**Application:** When you talk with others, does your focus consistently return to Jesus? This reveals what occupies your thoughts.

## IV. God Opens and Closes Doors

When Paul came to Troas to preach, "a door was opened for me in the Lord." God controls all factors affecting ministry—resources, lodging, local reception, government hostility, timing. He channels outcomes like streams of water (Proverbs 21:1).

**Application:** Do not force closed doors with a crowbar. Approach God with humility, petition Him to open doors you should enter, and trust Him to close doors He wants shut. If a door remains closed, rest peacefully knowing God's will is being done.

## V. Confidence in God, Not Self

**The Distinction:** Self-confidence is pride and misplaced (Philippians 3:1-4). The Bible reveals its flaws: we are sinners with deceitful hearts and limited abilities. Many self-confident people have failed (Proverbs 16:18).

**God's Confidence:** Paul and his team boldly proclaimed truth, stood against opposition, and preached with power. Their confidence came not from themselves but from God. "Such is the confidence that we have through Christ toward God. Not that we are sufficient in ourselves...but our sufficiency is from God" (3:4-5).

**Reflection:** Is confidence inherently good or bad? What is the difference between self-confidence and confidence in God?

## VI. The Ministry of Death—The Law's Purpose

The old covenant revealed God's standard and showed that no one could keep it. The law functions as a mirror emphasizing sin and confirming people are "hopelessly mired in sin which brings death."

**Its Functions:**
- Gives clear standards of right and wrong
- Shows no person can keep this standard
- Emphasizes sin
- Prepares people for a Savior by proving self-salvation is impossible

The bad news must come first so people recognize they need the good news. Someone must know they are sick before seeking the cure. Jesus then offers life and resurrection (John 11:25).

**Application:** When sharing the gospel, have nothing to fear. Jesus has won the victory. Proclaim truth boldly.

## VII. Jesus Is the Only Hope

Blind men came to Jesus for healing when no doctor could help. Jesus opened their eyes and declared Himself "the light of the world." Only Jesus can open spiritual eyes and remove Satan's deception.

When people resist the gospel, more of you is not the answer—more of Jesus is. Resistance should drive believers to their knees in prayer.

**Application:** Never share the gospel without prayer. Pray before, during, and after sharing.

## VIII. The Treasure and Our Bodies

Salvation is the greatest treasure. Ministers pass it on so others can have it.

**The Temporary Body:** Our bodies are like tents—temporary dwelling places that wear out in harsh conditions. Though Paul and his team faced persecution (beatings, whippings, imprisonment), and all people face aging, our outer selves waste away.

**Application:** Do not get attached to your physical body. It makes no sense to constantly upgrade a tent instead of investing in a permanent home. While physical health matters, soul health is far more important. Do not fear death; invest in God's kingdom and prepare for eternity.

As long as we are in this body, we are "away from the Lord." Knowing this, fear the Lord—understand God is Creator and Judge. This fear motivated Paul's evangelism and discipleship.

## IX. A New Perspective on People

**The Flesh vs. The Spirit:** People judge "according to the flesh"—making snap judgments based on wealth, job, status. Paul no longer does this. "The love of Christ controls us." Jesus "died for all, that those who live might no longer live for themselves."

Unbelievers are controlled by love for success; believers are to live for Christ. View people through a spiritual lens, God's standard, not worldly perspective.

## X. God's Work in Us

All is from God—He makes us sufficient. "All this is from God, who has reconciled us to himself through Christ" (5:18).

**Working Together:** We cannot bring salvation by our own efforts. No argument, no matter how skilled, changes hearts—that is God's work. Yet He expects us to work fully, sharing the gospel with effort. We are "working together with him"—cooperating with God's Spirit.

**Removing Obstacles:** Do not put obstacles in people's way through false teachings (works-based salvation, man-made traditions) or poor personal testimony. While Christ offends some, our attitudes should not. Maintain an upright testimony.

**Application:** Give glory to God for what He has done. Continue relying on His strength, not your own.

## XI. The Irony of Serving God

Serving God may mean poverty, obscurity, difficulty, and affliction. Yet God constantly "flips the script"—He sees and works on behalf of His servants.

## XII. Communication and Relationships

**Open Communication:** "We have spoken freely to you...our hearts are wide open" (6:11). Paul's simple, truthful communication prevented misunderstandings. "Through pride and presumption come nothing but strife" (Proverbs 13:10).

Many arguments stem from vague hints and double meanings: "I thought you meant..." "You didn't tell me..." Open, clear communication eliminates guessing games.

**Application:** Practice clear, truthful, loving communication. Say what you mean. Don't expect others to read your mind. Avoid presumptions.

## XIII. Do Not Be Unequally Yoked

A yoke binds two oxen together, forcing them to work as one team toward one goal. A believer should not bind himself to an unbeliever, losing freedom to follow God's will.

**Examples:**
- **Business:** An unbeliever partner may pressure unethical practices (bribery, lying, faulty products, tax evasion). The Christian faces conflict choosing between sin and righteousness.
- **Romance:** Dating and marriage with unbelievers create fundamental worldview conflicts.

**The Promise:** "I will be a father to you, and you shall be sons and daughters to me" (6:18). Those who separate from the world and live holy lives have God as their Father. God's grace is not a license to sin; a true believer says, "God has cleansed me. I should serve Him and strive to be holy like Him."

## XIV. Ministry Built on Love

"Make room in your hearts for us" (7:2). Paul did not use people for followers or view them as numbers. Each was important; he valued them and acted out of love.

**Key Lesson:** Without love, ministers burn out and view service as annoying rather than a blessing.

**Application:** God comforts the downcast (7:6). He is the God of all comfort—the only true, lasting source. Praise God naturally in conversation, reflecting His prominent place in your heart.

## XV. Generosity and Giving

**Not Compulsory:** "I say this not as a command" (8:8). Giving is voluntary. Like a parent who prefers a child share willingly rather than by force, God wants generous giving as a response to His love, not obligation.

**The Blessing:** Paul wanted Corinthians to experience the blessing of giving. Learning to give benefits them far more than the money itself.

**Fairness and Compassion:** Should a believer live luxuriously while ignoring fellow believers lacking necessities? No. "As a matter of fairness your abundance should supply their need" (8:14). Believers are stewards of God's resources for His kingdom.

Biblical principle: Landowners left unharvested fields for the poor to glean (Leviticus 19:9-10). Those with abundance shared with those in need.

**Application:** Look out for fellow believers. Recognize the "I made this and it is mine" mentality is wrong. Jesus, who was rich, became poor for us. How will you practically share with those in need?

## XVI. Sowing and Reaping

"Whoever sows sparingly will reap sparingly and whoever sows generously will reap generously" (9:6). A farmer who sows much gets a large crop; one who sows little gets little back.

**Clarification:** This does not mean giving money to God guarantees material wealth (prosperity gospel). Jesus, Paul, and disciples were not wealthy. Those "who desire to live a godly life, will be persecuted" (2 Timothy 3:12). Physical wealth on earth is never promised.

**Correct Understanding:** Sowing is investment in God's kingdom, not worldly business. When you sow a tomato seed, you reap tomatoes. Giving to God's kingdom builds His spiritual kingdom (John 18:36), not earthly wealth.

**Application:** Gratitude to God should constantly flow from believers. Acknowledge that good deeds originate in God's grace. This perspective brings joy, humility, and less complaint. Do not take personal credit; God started the good work in you.

---

**Final Theme:** "God is able to make all grace abound to you" (9:8). Believers rely on God's sufficiency, not their own strength, in all areas of faith and service.

# Clean and Condensed Text

## God's Material Provision (Grace)

God is able to provide for all your needs and your family's needs. Everything in the universe belongs to Him (Psalm 50:10-11). He spoke the universe into existence and knows all your needs.

Many self-reliant people attribute everything to their own hard work, forgetting that "every good gift is from above" (James 1:17). A person's skills, talents, opportunities, health, and mind all come from God. Without Him, we would not even be alive.

**Application:** God is able to make all grace abound to you. Our role is to believe this and place our full faith in Him. View yourself humbly, acknowledging that good things in your life result from His blessings.

## Thanksgiving and Gratitude

The ministry of giving produces "many thanksgivings to God" (verse 12). God's desired outcome is not just physical provision, but hearts full of thanks.

God delights when we thankfully receive His gifts and pass them on, just as parents are pleased when children receive gifts with gratitude.

## Satan's Deception

Satan is a master of deceit, whispering lies that spread like gangrene. God can open our eyes to the truth. Don't listen to false reports; ask God to reveal the truth.

False apostles are deceitful workmen. In some churches, people are far too tolerant of false teaching. Ezekiel 33:7 describes believers as watchmen who must warn others. Our eyes should be open and our minds discerning. If false teachers come, call them out.

**Application:** Knowing Satan is a master of deception, believers must guard themselves through discernment and close study of Scripture (1 John 4:1).

## Paul's Defense and Credibility

Paul's goal was the Corinthians' growth in the Lord. He spoke truthfully, without exaggeration as those seeking their own glory did. He stated in 2 Corinthians 5:20: "We implore you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God."

Paul established his apostolic credentials because his credibility directly affected the gospel message's credibility. All his words were spoken with God as witness, motivated by his love for and relationship with Christ.

Paul took money from other churches to support himself while preaching to the Corinthians so as not to burden them. Different believers are at different stages of spiritual maturity. Just as older siblings make sacrifices for younger ones, more mature churches helped the immature Corinthian church.

**Application:** Believers are at different stages in their walks with God. With more maturity comes more responsibility. Treat other believers understanding these differences.

## Self-Examination

The Corinthians had not been acting like believers should. Paul flipped the tables, encouraging them to examine their own salvation. He believed they were saved and hoped they would repent where they fell short.

The most vital question you can ask yourself is "Am I saved?" Test yourself with 1 John 3:9: "No one born of God practices sinning." Ask: Do I have a love for God? A lifestyle of obedience? A passion for the lost? Does hatred of sin and desire for holiness typify my life? Am I repentant when I sin?

**Application:** Every believer should periodically perform self-examination. Once saved, always saved (John 10:27-30), but many people think they are saved when they are not (Matthew 7:21). Jesus said "every good tree bears good fruit" (Matthew 7:17-18). If saved, there should be fruit in your life.

## Examining Others Fairly

When disciplining someone, follow biblical principles. The Old Testament principle of "two or three witnesses" (Deuteronomy 19:15) protects the innocent from slander and false testimony.

**Application:** Don't quickly believe rumors or bad reports against someone based on one witness. Get confirmation from others before believing a charge.

## Confidence in God

Christian workers and believers should place confidence in God, not themselves. We trust in education, skills, bank accounts, logic, and decision-making, but our confidence should come from God (Proverbs 3:5-6).

Self-confidence manifests in: lack of prayer, lack of preparation, lack of quality time with the Lord, assuming everything will go according to our plans, undue disappointment when failing, procrastination, and stress/anxiety.

John 15:5 states: "I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."

Every good thing—intelligence, strengths, logic, spiritual gifts—comes from God. You have never had one success apart from Christ's blessing. Even worldly successes depend on abilities God gave.

**Application:** Recognize that everything good comes from God. When self-confidence sneaks in (like forgetting to pray), quickly right course by returning to Jesus.

## God of All Comfort

God is the "God of all comfort." There is no real comfort apart from God. The Bible says every person is a sinner facing judgment. Without Christ, each person faces eternity in hell. Temporal comfort (a job loss, the flu passing) doesn't address real issues.

**He comforts us through:**
- Promising our pain and tears will be wiped away
- Promising forgiveness if we confess
- Promising to work everything together for good if we love Him
- Promising He will never leave or forsake us
- Promising to strengthen us to be overwhelming conquerors

Sometimes God helps us reach total dependency by allowing trials so difficult we realize our complete inadequacy and turn to Him in desperation.

## The Conscience

God gave us consciences as a built-in warning system alerting us to sin's dangers. Like the Ground Proximity Warning System on aircraft, consciences warn us to change direction. They are only as good as the input we receive, holding us to our highest perceived standard of right and wrong.

Consciences are not God's law or the final standard, but the way our souls reflect on themselves. They function like skylights—without their own light, they shine light from other sources onto our thoughts and actions.

**Application:** Guard your conscience by feeding it God's law found in Scripture.

## Giving and Kingdom Investment

"Whoever sows sparingly will reap sparingly." Giving to God is investing in building His kingdom through equipping saints, training disciples, sharing the gospel, and translating Scripture. Sowing seed in God's kingdom brings spiritual blessings to others, and you will reap the same type of blessing.

A person who invests little receives little. If you spend little time studying God's Word, you get little. If little on fellowship, you get little blessing. If little money on kingdom purposes, you miss greater blessings.

We cannot take money to heaven. The wisest investment is sending something ahead by building treasures in heaven through serving God on earth. One day all works will be tested by fire (1 Corinthians 3:13). Works done for God's kingdom remain; investments made for Him remain.

**Application:** Evaluate your giving. Why do you give? Do you have right motivation? Are you sowing abundant seed or just a little? Consider where you invest time, energy, and money. Our horizon is eternity. What do you need to do to invest in eternity?

# Clean and Condensed Text - Chunk 3/12

## Words and Promises

The words we speak are important. Jesus said to "let your yes be yes and your no be no." He taught a parable of two sons where one promised to work in the vineyard but didn't go—his words didn't match his deeds (Matthew 21:28-31).

James 4:13-15 teaches that we should say, "If it is the Lord's will, we will live and do this or that." Paul followed this principle, wanting to visit the Corinthians but accepting when God led otherwise. He didn't present this as a guarantee.

His opponents seized on this, accusing him of only pretending to care: "Paul doesn't really care about you. If he actually wanted to visit, he would be here. We are here. We care for you."

**Application:** Say what you mean and mean what you say. Don't make rash promises or agree to responsibilities to please others. Pray and seek God's will before giving your word. Your word should be your bond.

## Confronting Sin

Paul had caused the Corinthians "pain" by rebuking their sin. Though confronting sin is unpleasant and brings guilt and conviction, it's necessary because Paul loved them and wanted them to be faithful followers of Jesus.

He received no joy from rebuking them. Instead, he says: "Who is there to make me glad but the one whom I have pained?" Their repentance would bring him joy. It wasn't about being right or winning a debate—it was about restoring the sinner.

James 5:19-20 reminds us: "Whoever turns a sinner from the error of their way will save them from death and cover over a multitude of sins."

Like discipline, confronting sin "seems not joyful but sorrowful, but in the end it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness" (Hebrews 12:1).

## Church Discipline

The "punishment by the majority" referenced a person's sin being told to the whole church. Paul said it "is enough," indicating the person had repented. The goal of discipline is restoration, not revenge or prolonged punishment.

Like a hockey penalty box where a player serves a limited penalty and returns to the team, church discipline is loving, limited, and goal-oriented. Once repentance occurs, discipline ends and the person is restored.

**Application:** Parents should practice loving, limited, and goal-oriented discipline with their children.

## Letters of Recommendation

The early church used recommendation letters when believers moved to new towns. Church leaders would write letters introducing the person as a believer in good standing with upright character, protecting against troublemakers and false teachers.

Paul didn't need such introduction—they already knew him. This practice partially underlies modern church membership, with some churches still writing recommendation letters for members relocating.

## Ministers of the New Covenant

**Application:** If you are saved, you are a minister of the new covenant—not at some future time, but now. Every believer is called to be God's ambassador. Are you doing it?

## God's Mercy and Power

"Carved in letters on stone" refers to the law given to Moses on Mount Sinai (Exodus 31:18). No one is saved by his own merit, but by God's mercy.

Those serving by God's grace don't need to lose heart when facing rejection or failure. God is strong when we are weak. Philippians 1:6 assures us: "He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ."

During difficult seasons—like 2020's pandemic—we shouldn't lose heart. God continues building His church. He hasn't stopped working, and neither should we.

## Satan's Blindness vs. God's Light

Satan has blinded the minds of unbelievers, keeping them from seeing Jesus and accepting His truth. He pushes a distorted reality that many accept. This is a spiritual struggle.

Examples appear in worldly ideas that contradict Scripture. The lies of Satan are exposed when God grants us discernment to know the truth. God has shone light into our hearts, and as those who see the light, we have responsibility to guide those in darkness.

**Application:** How can you build your discernment? How can you testify of God's light to those around you?

## Treasure in Clay Jars

Paul says the gospel treasure is kept in "jars of clay"—weak, worthless, fragile vessels. A clay jar inadequately stores valuable jewels or gold.

The clay jar represents Paul's team—they were weak, beaten, whipped, often on the run or in prison, not the richest or brightest. Yet God chose to use them. Why? Because there is no person sufficient for the task of ministry (2 Corinthians 2:16).

## We Believe So We Speak

Like the psalmist, Paul's belief compelled him to speak with no ulterior motives. His ministry was a direct result of firm faith in Jesus. 1 Corinthians 9:16 captures this: "For if I preach the gospel, that gives me no ground for boasting. For necessity is laid upon me. Woe to me if I do not preach the gospel!"

Paul's faith was living—his belief changed his actions, speech, and everything about his life.

**Reflect:** How does your belief in Jesus affect your daily life?

**Application:** If we truly believe in Jesus, we must share the gospel. Every person will spend eternity in heaven or hell based on their decision to follow Jesus now. If we really believe this, woe to us if we don't speak. Many remain silent because they don't truly believe. Living faith compels us to share with family and friends.

## Renewal and Eternal Bodies

Our inner self is being renewed day by day.

**Reflect:** How can you be renewed? What practical steps can you take?

We have a building from God—an eternal body that will never wear out. Everything we see is declining under the second law of thermodynamics (entropy). But in eternity, God will suspend these laws and do something new and spectacular.

**Application:** Look forward with hope! Your new body will last forever.

**Reflect:** What aspect of your eternal body do you most look forward to?

## Faith, Not Sight

These promises are intangible—we cannot see or touch them. Faith requires trusting what we cannot see.

**Reflect:** Are there tangible ways to increase your faith?

## Viewing Christ Rightly

We once regarded Christ "according to the flesh"—by worldly standards. Jesus was poor, not part of the ruling elite. The mob turned against Him. He was mocked, beaten, whipped, and crucified—seemingly a failure.

By the world's standards, He didn't look like a Messiah. But according to God's perspective, He was perfectly successful. He fulfilled the law completely and accomplished the most important mission—reconciling man to God and providing forgiveness for our sins.

When we come to Christ, God replaces the lens over our eyes, giving us new perspective. We see Him as He truly is: the perfect Lamb of God, King of Kings, who lovingly gave Himself for us.

## Reconciliation and Imputation

God reconciled us to Himself through Christ.

**Application:** To be reconciled to others, you may need to take the first step.

Jesus knew no sin and could be the perfect sacrifice. On the cross, He substituted Himself for us, becoming as if He had done all our sins. God punished Jesus instead of us—this is imputation. Our sins were imputed to Him; His righteousness is imputed to us.

Jesus didn't actually become a sinner, but was treated as if He were.

**Application:** God offers the best trade imaginable. Exchange your sins and eternal punishment for Christ's righteousness. If you've made that trade, thank God for His great mercy. If not, what are you waiting for?

## Receiving Grace in Vain

Some can taste heavenly gifts, the goodness of God's word, and the Holy Spirit's power without coming to salvation. All these blessings are vain unless a person repents and embraces the gospel.

This is similar to Hebrews 6:4-6, which describes those enlightened but who fall away.

**Application:** Evaluate your spiritual condition. Are you just going to church as tradition? Have you truly repented of sins and placed trust in Jesus? Being "good," attending church, and reading the Bible don't save you.

## Ministering Despite Obstacles

Potential pitfalls for ministers include:
- **Honor/praise:** A leader receiving much honor may become prideful or seek honor and abuse position
- **Dishonor/slander:** Expected appreciation replaced by dishonor may discourage a servant into quitting

## Communication and Relationships

Paul asks believers to "widen your hearts to us also." Generally, communication is circular—how you communicate influences how others communicate with you.

**Application:** If dissatisfied with someone's communication, evaluate your own. Be the initiator of change. You will likely reap what you sow.

## Unequal Yoking with Unbelievers

Paul uses the yoke illustration to show fundamental incompatibility. Five contrasts show why believers shouldn't partner with unbelievers:

- **Righteousness vs. lawlessness:** Believers have Christ's righteousness; unbelievers are enslaved to sin. Their life directions are at odds.
- **Light vs. darkness:** Jesus has opened believers' eyes; unbelievers are blind to truth. Following an unbeliever's advice is listening to a blind guide.
- **Christ vs. Belial (Satan):** No one can serve two masters. Either we belong to Christ or Satan.
- **Believer vs. unbeliever:** Believers are new creations (2 Corinthians 5:17); unbelievers only have the sin nature. This creates fundamental incompatibility.

These contrasts apply especially to marriage but also to dating, business partnerships, and close friendships.

**Application:** Evaluate areas where you might be unequally yoked. Follow God's principles and experience the result—separation from worldly practices and full belonging to God.

## Sanctification Through God

Washing away filthiness comes through God's work. Romans 8:29-30 shows God's sovereignty: "For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son... and those whom he called he also justified, and those whom he justified he also glorified."

God took the initiative. He chose us before the foundation of the world (Ephesians 1:4)—not because we were good, but while we were sinners. Your salvation and sanctification result from His work, not your own.

Philippians 1:6 assures us: "He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ."

You cannot overcome sin by willpower alone. By your own strength, you cannot consistently choose righteousness.

## Paul's Defense

Paul defends himself: "We have wronged no one."

**Application:** When the gospel's credibility is at stake, defend yourself. The principle is "all things for the gospel." If silence furthers the gospel, remain silent. If speaking defends God's word, speak out.

## Titus Brings Good News

Titus returning brought news that comforted Paul—not Titus himself, but his report. The Corinthians had repented! Their changed attitude toward Paul demonstrated this:
- They longed to see him, wanting restoration
- They mourned over their sin
- They were zealous for Paul—concerned for his welfare through prayer, giving, and inquiry

When Paul heard this, he was overjoyed.

## Giving Motivated by Grace

The correct motivation for giving is response to God's grace—unmerited favor that can never be repaid. God's kindness should stir desire to serve Him with everything He's given us.

Incorrect motivations include:
- Meriting salvation (the worst case: selling letters of absolution)
- Being seen by people
- Prosperity gospel's "seed" mentality (giving to receive monetary blessing)

Since Jesus didn't spare His own life, we shouldn't spare anything for God's service.

**Application:** Evaluate your own motivation for giving.

## The Test of Love

Paul shared the Macedonians' generous example to inspire and expect similar response from the Corinthians. This response would prove their love was genuine—it was like a test.

Tests reveal a person's faith and commitment to God. Abraham's test revealed faith to himself, strengthening it and banishing doubts. God already knows the future but tests to reveal commitment to the individual tested.

This differs from temptation, which seeks to bring a person down and prove he's enslaved to sin. A test lifts a person up and strengthens him.

## Finishing What You Start

Paul reminded the Corinthians they started a year ago and should now finish it well. It's often easier to begin than to finish.

Many make New Year's resolutions but fail to carry through. Christians similarly begin projects passionately but don't finish—Bible reading plans, prayer schedules, evangelism programs, outreach projects.

**Reflect:** Share an example of something you started for God and didn't finish. Why? What can you learn?

Reasons for failure include not counting the cost, trials, temptations, and obstacles. God finishes what He starts in us; He expects His followers to do likewise.

**Application:** What practical ways can you become better at finishing what you start? How can you ensure you finish the Christian race?

## Mutual Supply

The principle is "you reap what you sow." If the Corinthians sowed generosity, one day they may reap it. They wouldn't want believers to harden hearts when they needed help, so neither should they now.

## Love Requires Sacrifice

Titus showed care by going on a long trip and giving his time for the Corinthians. It's easy to say "I care," much harder to sacrifice for others.

This heart for others ultimately comes from God. We should ask God to work in us and give us more love for others.

## From Readiness to Action

The Corinthians had made themselves ready, but being willing and actually doing are different. Many express willingness to help but need catalysts to action.

General church announcements for volunteers often fail. Approaching individuals for specific projects typically works better—people are willing but need a gentle nudge.

## God Sees the Heart

God looks at your heart regarding giving. He knows if you're giving for right reasons, if you're stingy, if you're making sacrifice, and the battles in your mind as you decide how much to give. He sees secret deeds and will reward (Matthew 6:4).

## Abounding in Good Works

Through generosity, believers abound in every good work.

# James 4:3 – God's Provision and Stewardship

You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, to spend it on your passions.

God doesn't always provide abundantly. He is a gift-giver, but when He knows a person will selfishly waste what they receive, He may refrain from giving. God seeks to see us "abound in every good work." The materials He gives us are to be used for building His kingdom. In the parable of the talents, the master expected his servants to use resources wisely and increase them. Similarly, God gives us grace so we can be wise stewards and turn these into increased blessings for His kingdom.

**Reflection:** List ways you can use money or physical resources to abound in every good work. What are various good works you can do with money besides giving to your local church?

# Glorifying God in Giving

When people give to get credit for themselves, they steal God's glory and commit a type of idolatry by putting man in God's place. We should give in such a way that the focus remains on God. Every gift is an opportunity to celebrate the grace we have received from the Lord. We should have no desire to manipulate situations for attention or credit. Instead, we should sincerely seek to focus our hearts on Christ's goodness and encourage others to appreciate the greatest giver of gifts.

# The Meekness and Gentleness of Christ

Matthew 11:29 – Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls.

Jesus described Himself as "gentle" and "humble in heart." He is not a strict or harsh taskmaster but kind and humble. Though He has all the power in the universe, He did not use it to free Himself when taunted on the cross, nor did He defend Himself.

Isaiah 53:7 – He was oppressed and afflicted, yet he did not open his mouth; he was led like a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before its shearers is silent, so he did not open his mouth.

Paul sought to emulate Jesus in his ministry, gently encouraging the Corinthians.

**Application:** Would your friends say you are gentle and meek, or are you harsh and quick-tempered? Pray that God will give you a humble heart like our Lord.

---

# Waging War According to the Spirit

Paul describes the Christian life as "war"—a serious matter with extremely high stakes. In war, one must be constantly alert for attacks: frontal assaults, sabotage, traps, and propaganda. A soldier must train and fight. There is no middle ground when entering into the Christian life. The enemy will not show mercy. The believer must not give ground or retreat.

**Reflection:** What does the word "war" imply about the Christian walk?

**Application:** You are in a battle. Are you trying to be a spectator or are you waging war? What is one specific thing you can do to be a better warrior?

# Divine Jealousy

Worldly jealousy is "bitter" and "selfish," often growing from sinful comparing. In contrast, there is "divine" or "righteous" jealousy. God is jealous when His people serve idols because He wants their devotion. This jealousy is justified—when people give Him devotion, it is best for them.

A righteous man would desire for his wife to be faithful because that is healthy and honors God. Similarly, Paul was jealous for the Corinthians, desiring a healthy relationship with God. He cared deeply when he saw them turning from the Lord. His jealousy was motivated by a heart of love.

**Application:** Care deeply for people. God wants us to have zeal to see His people following after Him. When you care for people this way, it will motivate you to pray for them, share with them, encourage them, and correct them when necessary.

---

# Satan's Cunning Deceptions

Satan is subtle, sneaky, cunning, and deceptive. He wants you to think he has your best interests at heart. His methods are well-refined over millennia. When attacking the church, one of his favorite methods is camouflage—his servants blend in, pretending to be servants of God. After gaining trust, they spread insidious teachings packaged with words like tolerance, unity, freedom, and happiness. Big promises are made, but he and his servants are peddling a lie.

At the first temptation, Satan convinced Eve that God was withholding something good. If she would only be bold enough to try it, she would find true contentment and become like God. It sounded nice. It was a lie. Satan came pretending to be light, and that is when his attacks are most effective.

**Reflection:** How can we make sure we see through this ruse and see Satan and his promises for what they are?

---

# Paul's Suffering for Christ

John 15:18-19 – If the world hates you, know that it has hated me before it hated you. If you were of the world, the world would love you as its own; but because you are not of the world, but I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you.

Jesus warned His disciples that they would face persecution. Paul faced far more than most—beaten, stoned, whipped, shipwrecked twelve times. Beyond direct persecution, he faced many dangers and inconveniences: treachery from within, sleepless nights, hunger, thirst, and constant anxiety for the churches. His was a life of constant work, toil, heavy responsibility, and frequent persecution.

**Application:** Do not compare your persecution to others. God does not call every believer to the same amount. Some face more, some less. God knows why. Do not feel lesser if you have seldom faced persecution.

---

# Paul's Vision and the Thorn in His Flesh

Paul refers to himself in the third person when describing a man caught up to the third heaven and hearing inexpressible things. This was Paul's own experience. In verse 7, he explicitly says the thorn in his flesh was sent to "me" because of the surpassing greatness of the revelations.

God allowed Satan to afflict Paul to accomplish a greater purpose. Satan desired to hinder Paul's ministry, discourage him, and torment him. God's motivation was clear: He wanted Paul to remain humble in the wake of the surpassing revelations he had witnessed.

---

# Paul's Defense and Superiority

When compared against God's standard, Paul fell woefully short. But when compared to these false apostles, Paul wasn't nothing. He and his ministry were superior in every way. Paul wasn't the one who sought this comparison—the Corinthians did. Paul was merely pointing out that if they wanted to compare, he would win hands down. His motivation was that the Corinthians would not be swayed by false teachers but would stand on the truth they had heard from him.

---

# Paul's Selfless Motivation

Paul declares: "I seek not what is yours, but you." Throughout church history, leaders have often failed to learn this lesson. They have exploited common people for personal gain. This problem still exists today. Some pastors of mega-churches live in luxury while claiming Christ's name.

**Application:** Be motivated by loving people. The desire to see their soul in heaven with eternal life should drive our ministry. Spend time in prayer asking God to give you supreme love for others. When we love others this way, we will be filled with compassion and see them as God's creation needing to be led to the Savior, not as stepping stones for personal gain.

---

# All for Your Upbuilding

Even after shameful treatment from the Corinthians, Paul still loved them and called them "beloved." Few would have blamed him for walking away. His conscience did not allow that. Everything he did and said was for them.

**Application:** Every parent, teacher, and minister should be able to truthfully say the same—that we are doing "all for your upbuilding." Evaluate your own relationships. Is your parenting all for the growth of your children? Is your ministry all for the growth of the flock?

---

# Dealing with Sin in the Church

Paul's lengthy letters make clear that sin in the church is a serious matter needing to be dealt with. Modern churches are often very tolerant toward sin. But sin is like a disease—when it gains a foothold, it spreads and infects everything it touches. Church leaders must take seriously their role to discipline sinning members. The goal is not punitive punishment but restoration.

**Application:** Parents must also be diligent to deal with sin in their children. David's soft attitude toward sin led to two rebellions from his sons. Dealing with sin is difficult, time-consuming, and uncomfortable, requiring you to get to the heart of the matter through digging and questioning.

---

# Paul's Desire for the Corinthians' Success

Though some considered themselves rivals with Paul, he wanted no part of rivalry or competition. His goal was not that they fail so he could say "I told you so." Their success was more important to him than being right or having the last laugh.

Philippians 2:3 – Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit.

**Application:** Care more about souls than about being right. In arguments with others, are you concerned with winning or with their spiritual condition? Would you not rather lose the argument and win the soul?

---

# Greetings Among Believers

"Greet one another with a holy kiss." The universal principle behind this instruction is to be warm and caring to other believers in how you greet them. In different cultures, the method varies—a strong handshake, a slap on the back, a hug, or a kiss. What matters is warm, genuine greeting.

---

# God of All Comfort

There is no real and lasting comfort apart from what God can give, and it is given only to those who experience salvation and have a relationship with Him.

Matthew 5:4 – Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted.

If a person mourns over sin, God provides comfort. He promises forgiveness of sins. There will be final comfort when every tear is washed away, every pain gone, every sadness turned to joy, and every sin confirmed to be paid for as we are made perfect forever in God's presence. But there is also comfort now for those who mourn for sin. God does not hold your sin against you. When you confess, He removes it as far as the East is from the West.

---

# The Testimony of Our Conscience

Believers should guard their consciences by feeding them God's law found in Scripture. Our consciences can reflect God's standards when we have the right input and are sensitive to how they lead us.

**Application:** First, feed, shape, and mold your conscience so it accurately reflects Scripture. Second, evaluate your thoughts and motivations. Third, be sensitive to your conscience. If you violate your conscience, it is sin.

---

# Keeping Your Word

Your word should be your bond. When ready to make a commitment, let it be known that according to God's will you will do this or that. Once you have made that commitment, be responsible and fulfill it.

---

# Believers and Unbelievers

Paul contrasts believers and unbelievers. Believers serve God, the highest being in the universe, the King of Kings. This belief should guide every aspect of your life. Unbelievers do not believe or accept this. The worldviews are completely incompatible.

Even our body as believers is a temple of the Holy Spirit. We are supposed to offer ourselves as a living sacrifice to God. Unbelievers are dedicated to fleshly desires: sex, money, security, big house, education, career, position, fame. These cannot mix.

Romans 12:2 tells us not to conform to the world or its ways. We have to be set apart and dedicated to God.

---

# Cleansing Ourselves

You cannot overcome sin through willpower alone. The Holy Spirit regenerates your heart. God is the Savior, justifier, and sanctifier. Our role is to cooperate with God as the clay cooperates with the potter. We submit our will to His, asking to be changed by Him.

Our role is not passive. We are to be active in turning to God and asking Him to change us. We are called to cooperate with God's sanctifying work by being zealous for Him and diligent to apply His word.

**Application:** What are you defiled with? What sin may you be entangled in? Take it to God now. Confess your sin. Ask Him to cleanse you and help you overcome it.

---

# Motives in Christian Ministry

What can we learn from Paul about conducting Christian ministry? How did Paul feel about the Corinthians? What filled Paul with comfort? Why is love so important in ministry?

Since Jesus did not spare His own life, surely we should not spare anything for God's service.

**Application:** Evaluate your motive for giving. Do you give because you feel pressured? Do you give because others are around or because you want to be seen? Ask God to give you the heart toward giving. When you struggle with generosity, remember what He has done for you.

---

# False Teachers and Deception

Satan keeps working to harden people's hearts. One way he keeps people from accepting Jesus is to radicalize them so that any mention of traditional God-given Biblical values is so offensive that they are unwilling to consider any God who espouses such ideas.

False "super-apostles" attempted to deceive the Corinthians through subtle methods. What concern did Paul have for them? What methods do enemies of Christ use to mislead His people? What do we learn from Paul's warnings about what is really important to look for in a Bible teacher or pastor?

**Reflection:** If we know the spiritual struggle behind souls, how will that change our approach to sharing the gospel? Since people are blind to the truth, what recourse do we have? How can we help the light pierce into the depth of their souls?

---

# The Light of Jesus

God has shone in our hearts. How did God shine the light into your hearts? Share your testimony of when you first saw the light. How has your perspective changed after seeing the light?

John 1:5 – The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.

# 2 Corinthians Study - Chunk 5

## Paul's Suffering for Christ - Persecution

Some face more persecution than others. God knows why we don't, so we should not feel less if we have seldom faced persecution. Rather, we should be thankful. While not a competition, Paul's life serves as a positive example—he persevered without complaining. We too should not allow pressure from those around us to derail us from whole-heartedly serving the Lord.

When you face persecution, you must choose: please God or please man. If you please man and deny Jesus, He will deny you. You might avoid trouble momentarily, but will face far greater trouble at judgment. If you choose to please God, you may suffer—even torture or death. But if you keep the faith, He will welcome and reward you.

**Revelation 2:11** – "Be faithful unto death, and I will give you the crown of life. The one who conquers will not be hurt by the second death."

Jesus promises the faithful will not be touched by hell. He does not always deliver us from momentary tribulations, but He delivers us from hell by paying the price in our place and gives the overcomer the crown of life.

## God of All Comfort

God does not hold your sin against you. When you confess, He removes it as far as the East is from the West. There is comfort in the promise of forgiveness.

If a loved one who is saved passes away, we don't grieve as those who have no hope. Knowing that person is in heaven with Christ is a comfort (1 Thessalonians 4:13).

God gives real comfort to those who repent. Though they may face temporary afflictions, these pale in comparison to eternal blessings in store for them. If you are facing trials today and are in God's kingdom, be comforted by this verse and use it to comfort others.

## Do Not Judge Others' Motivations

It is very difficult to know others' hearts. We can largely know our own heart and motivations, but we often cannot accurately judge the secret thoughts and intentions of others.

**Application A:** Do not be quick to judge others' motivations. Man sees the outside, but God sees the heart (1 Samuel 16:7). When we assign wrong motivations to people, it can be very hurtful. David's brother Eliab accused David of wicked intentions, saying "I know how conceited you are." Eliab did not know this—David came because his father told him to take food to his brothers.

**Application B:** Do not let false accusations cause you guilt. When accused, it is wise to listen and evaluate your own heart. Search carefully for sin, pray, and ask the Holy Spirit to enlighten you.

## Written vs. Face-to-Face Communication

Sometimes written communication can be better than spoken. Advantages of writing letters include:
- Time to think carefully about what needs to be said and how to word it
- Time for both sides to step back and cool off before responding
- Helps remove emotion from the discussion
- You can revise letters, but cannot take spoken words back
- Allows you to make your complete case without interruption
- Shows you are serious and "mean business" since it is more formal

When one party does not listen, interrupts, and argues to the point where conversation is impossible, writing a letter can be most useful.

## Forgive and Comfort the Repentant

Real forgiveness goes beyond ignoring sin, remaining silent, or not taking revenge. Real forgiveness is expressed in an outpouring of love for the sinner. They were not only to forgive but also to comfort.

If people do not forgive, the offender may become overwhelmed by excessive sorrow. True repentance should lead to joy, but it is hard to have joy if people around you are still blaming or shunning you.

If you keep bringing up a person's past sins, they may become discouraged and depressed, concluding there is no forgiveness and no joy in repentance. The application is simple: when someone repents, forgive. Don't hold it over them. Don't keep bringing it up. Restore the person into fellowship and comfort them, letting them know the full abundance of God's grace and mercy. We are called to forgive big sins because that is what Christ has forgiven us.

## God Always Leads Us in Triumphal Procession

Paul compares this procession to evangelists taking the gospel into new places. Evangelists are celebrating Jesus' victory on the cross—His triumph over sin, death, the grave, and Satan. Believers spread the fragrance of knowledge of Jesus, like incense accompanying Roman processions.

Believers should not be discouraged by afflictions or setbacks in sharing the gospel. A Roman general returning from victory need not be discouraged by minor troubles. He won already. The result is fixed. In like manner, the believer should not give up but remember Jesus has already won the victory.

**Application:** If you are on Jesus' side, you are on the winning team. Sharing the gospel is difficult. Perhaps you have been rejected. Take heart. God is leading you in a triumphal procession! Sharing the gospel is a statement of God's victory. One day all will see it when Jesus leads His church in the final triumphal procession and takes the throne in Jerusalem.

## You Yourselves Are Our Letter of Introduction

The Corinthians did not need to look elsewhere for evidence of Paul's credibility. All they had to do was look at themselves and their own changed lives.

## The Ministry of Reconciliation

God gave people the ministry of reconciliation, not another method such as angels. Our ministry is reconciliation—introducing people to Jesus so they can have personal relationship with God. It is not a ministry of persuasion (convincing of intellectual facts) or judgment. We care for the heart.

**Application:** When you share, remember why you are sharing. It is to bring people into a relationship with God. Apart from that personal relationship, there is no reconciliation.

## Now Is the Day of Salvation

Satan's most effective tactic is delay. He tempts people with: you have plenty of time; enjoy yourself first and repent later; it's a tough decision so wait for a sign; you're not good enough yet; do it tomorrow.

God's message is clear: now is the day of salvation! Tomorrow is not guaranteed. You do not know you will have a chance to turn to God in the future. At any moment, death could come.

The longer a person delays, the harder their heart becomes and the more difficult it is to turn to God. Bad habits become more ingrained and pride grows. Even if you could guarantee you would be willing to repent ten years from now, waiting ten years is a waste of your life!

## The Difficulties of Gospel Work

Being a minister of the gospel is no easy task. It is hard work with many difficulties. Most of these difficulties could be avoided by not engaging in the work of God.

If you are looking for a life of ease, look elsewhere. If you are looking for affirmation and appreciation of man, look elsewhere. If you are looking for pleasure and worldly security, look elsewhere. But if you are looking for an abundant and meaningful life which will impact others for eternity, there is no more abundant life than one lived in complete dedication to God. There is no more meaningful life than one lived in reconciling others to Him.

Are you willing to pay the cost? And if so, when will you start?

# God's Perspective and Kingdom Blessings

God's perspective is the true one. If you serve Him, He is on your side. You may be poor in this world, but you are rich in God's kingdom, a recipient of His abundant blessings (Ephesians 1). You may be persecuted, but your life is in God's hands—it will not end one second before He allows it. You may be insignificant in the sight of neighbors or family, but you are well-known to God. He knows your name (Isaiah 43:1). He knows the number of hairs on your head (Luke 12:7). You may face afflictions, but in Him you can still rejoice.

God is good. He has a good plan for you. If you dedicate yourself to serving Him, He will give you a full and meaningful life and make your ministry for Him fruitful.

---

# The Dangers of Unequal Yoking

Amos 3:3 asks: "Can two walk together, except they be agreed?" Your closest companion should be agreed with you on the most important thing in life. If two are not agreed on fundamental matters, there will be many problems later on, most likely resolved by the believer compromising.

Marriage is the most intimate and important relationship you can have. Whom you marry will dramatically affect your life. Your spouse influences you in big and small ways every day. If you date or marry an unbeliever, there will be fundamental disagreements in almost every arena:

**How to spend money:** Believers should practice giving offerings to God and use resources for His glory and kingdom. Unbelievers want to use money for satisfying their desires or getting more.

**How to raise children:** Believers want to prepare their children to face God by teaching them the Bible and Christian worldview. Unbelievers do not agree with this approach, and their entire approach to education differs.

---

# Rebuke and Repentance

Though Paul's letter caused temporary grief, it was necessary because the Corinthians' spiritual growth, healthy church, and eternal destiny were at stake. Such rebuke brings temporary discomfort to produce repentance.

Hebrews 12:11 states: "For the moment all discipline seems painful rather than pleasant, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it."

**Application:** According to 2 Timothy 3:16, God's word has four functions: teaching, reproof, correction, and training in righteousness. Reproof is the least pleasant but necessary. Many churches fail to take this role seriously, tolerating sin and valuing comfort over holiness.

Church leaders must rebuke sin seriously. However, every believer has responsibility to encourage those around them toward holy living. According to Matthew 18, when you see a person in sin, approach them directly. Member-to-member care is the first line of defense against sin. Do not remain silent expecting church leaders to do all the work. Humbly pray and follow the Spirit's leading to use Scripture to encourage repentance.

---

# Giving in Severe Affliction and Poverty

The Macedonians gave sacrificially out of "extreme poverty," showing that giving is not just for the wealthy. This reminds us of the widow in Luke 21:1-4, whom Jesus praised because she gave all she had. Though her offering was small, it was the most because it really cost her. She sacrificed more than the rich people who gave one thousand times more.

**Application:** From the Macedonians' example, we learn that giving is not dependent on how much you have. God calls every person to give generously. If you have delayed giving until a more convenient time, make the leap of faith and begin honoring God with what you have today. He who is faithful in a little thing is faithful also in much. Satan tells us to wait until a more convenient time to serve God. If you listen to him today, you will likely do so again in the future.

---

# The Example of Jesus

2 Corinthians 8:9 states: "For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you by his poverty might become rich."

The ultimate motivation for giving is not external pressure or command, but a response to the grace of Jesus. When feeling stingy and reluctant to give, look at what Jesus gave up for you. He had it all—no one was higher, no one had more. Yet no one has become lower. He gave up all His divine rights and riches (Philippians 2). He became sin so you might become righteous (2 Corinthians 5:21).

He gave up heaven so you could go there. He gave up His relationship with the Father (on the cross) so you might have one. He is perfectly holy yet became sin so you could become holy. He took your punishment so you wouldn't have to.

**Application:** When you feel stingy, look at the cross. When you want to hold back something for yourself, look at the cross. Jesus gave it all so we could have eternity with Him. How can we still hold back? His grace should motivate us to generosity. Spend time now in prayer. Ask God to forgive you if you have shown a stingy spirit. Thank Jesus for the cross. Ask Him to fill you with a heart of generosity.

---

# Proportionate Giving

Proportionate giving is "acceptable." God does not ask for what you don't have. He does ask for you to share what you do have. The same is true for sharing the gospel. You can't share what you don't know. You don't need to answer every difficult question, but you can share what you know. Hold out everything you have to God with open hands, no matter how little.

---

# Giving Is Sharing

The discussion questions include: Why does Paul call it "fairness" for those more well off to give to those with less? What would you say to someone who believes "I have earned what I have and it's fair for me to keep it?" How does reciprocating play a role in the church family?

Cross-Reference (Acts 4:32-35): "All the believers were one in heart and mind. No one claimed that any of their possessions was their own, but they shared everything they had. With great power the apostles continued to testify to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. And God's grace was so powerfully at work in them all that there were no needy persons among them."

Perhaps some elderly or weak people were not able to gather as easily, so others pitched in and helped. That is the heart of sharing Paul encourages. God's provision for His people was enough, but it sometimes went through intermediaries. Perhaps you too can be the conduit for God's blessing to others. As members of the body of Christ, take care of one another. Through that heart of love, the light of Jesus will shine and attract more people to Himself.

---

# Freewill Service

Ministry cannot be forced. It often comes with high cost—sacrifice of time, comfort, convenience, security, even oneself. The choice to do this must come from within. A person pressured into ministry or doing it for wrong reasons is likely to burn out.

**Application:** Every believer is responsible to God for their decisions. You should not do things because people are watching and expect you to live a certain way. Rather, strive to please God in all you do. In the Old Testament, sin offerings were mandatory, but peace offerings were freewill sacrifices where a person brought a gift to God of his own accord. When we offer our life as a freewill offering to the Lord, the sacrifice is most meaningful (Romans 12:1).

---

# The Power of Personal Example

Passion and modeling are important Biblical concepts. Jesus served as a model for His disciples. Paul said, "Follow my example, as I follow the example of Christ" (1 Corinthians 11:1).

The Bible often describes people as sheep, and one key characteristic is that they are good at following. Positive examples are extremely helpful for people who do not yet have experience applying God's Word to their lives. A young couple may look to a mature Christian couple for guidance on marriage. Young parents can gain wisdom from observing a godly family with older children. Setting an example of generosity can both inspire others and show them how to be wise stewards of entrusted resources.

---

# Willing and Joyful Giving

You have seen people who give reluctantly—perhaps inviting you to eat but worrying about how much you'll spend. You have also seen people who truly enjoy sharing what God has given them. They don't think about the cost but instead think about blessing others.

**Application:** Evaluate yourself in the area of giving. Confess your shortcomings to God. Ask Him to transform your heart, give you opportunities to give, and fill you with desire and willingness to participate freely and joyfully when these opportunities come.

---

# God Supplies the Seed

God supplies the seed to the sower. There is no such thing as a "self-made man." Every resource is from God.

In Genesis, Adam was caretaker of the Garden of Eden, but God created it and placed it in Adam's care. Adam's job was to work and cultivate it. He was the wealthiest person in the world, yet everything belonged to God. Adam was a steward with everything by grace as a gift. As steward, he was to watch over things in obedience to God and for His glory.

The same is true today. Whatever we have is given by God. He is the supplier. That fact demands humility and teaches us to be dependent on Him.

We are called "the sower," showing clearly what we are to do with the seed. God places resources in our hands to be used. He gives us seed that He expects to be sowed.

---

# Gentle But Bold Leadership

Paul says he is "humble when face to face" with the Corinthians but bold when absent. The goal was that they would heed his rebukes and repent. If they did, his face-to-face visit would be much more uplifting.

Gentleness and humility should not be confused with tolerance for sin. Though Jesus described Himself as "gentle" and "humble in heart," He reacted strongly toward sinners. He angrily cleansed the temple and courageously pointed out the hypocrisy of the Pharisees. His humility did not demand silence in the face of sin.

There is a time to be gentle and a time to be bold. The two are not mutually exclusive.

When a person recognizes their sin and readily turns from it, be gentle. Do not snuff out a smoldering wick. Matthew 12:20 states: "A bruised reed he will not break, and a smoldering wick he will not snuff out, till he has brought justice through to victory."

When people willfully pursue sin, confront them boldly.

---

# Spiritual Warfare

The believer does not wage war in the flesh. By our own strength, we cannot attain victory. Satan and his demons are supernatural. Their influence in this world is too strong for us to overcome. Our own flesh is too weak.

Satan's goal is your mind and soul. He wants you to sin and fall away from the Lord (1 Peter 5:8): "Be sober-minded; be watchful. Your adversary the devil prowls around like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour."

To fight successfully against a superior adversary, we need help. God gives that help. He is by our side, supporting and sustaining us. The first step in successful battle is coming to God and saying, "I need help. I can't win by myself. I am weak and I need you."

We don't trust in ourselves. Instead, we come to the only one who can give us victory. We come to Him day after day, returning to the cross asking for His deliverance. We must live in complete dependence upon Him.

---

# Defending Against False Accusations

Paul must defend himself against false accusations so the Corinthians would not reject him and his message, the gospel of Christ. While Scripture warns against boasting in arrogance, Paul's motives were pure—he had a clear conscience.

Acts 24:16 states: "So I always take pains to have a clear conscience toward both God and man."

**Application:** When people accuse you, it is wise to listen and evaluate your own heart. Search carefully for any sin. Pray. Ask the Holy Spirit to enlighten you. If after doing these things you have a clear conscience that you acted in obedience to God's Word and from right motivation, do not worry about accusations. Do not feel guilty just because others view you wrongly. Do not live for pleasing others or receiving affirmation. God is the one on the throne who will judge, and you will answer to Him alone.

---

# Authority Used for Building Up

Many people use their authority selfishly. Some leaders live in luxury on donations from the elderly, generating excitement through false promises. These things destroy the very people they are supposed to serve.

Jesus made clear that the correct model of leadership is serving: "The greatest among you shall be your servant" (Matthew 23:11).

Being a leader requires self-sacrifice. The ultimate goal is to build up God's people.

**Application:** Observe your Christian leaders. Do they use their authority for building you up, or do they use people? If your leaders are self-serving, it may be time to find a new church.

---

# Recognizing Deception

Paul was dismayed that the Corinthians might believe in "another Jesus, a different gospel." There is only one Jesus, one gospel, one truth. There are countless lies. Real currency is uniform, but there are vast numbers of different counterfeits. The closer you become acquainted with the truth, the easier it will be to see through deceptions.

**Application:** Focus on studying the truth in God's Word rather than learning about every cult. Learning the Bible requires much time and commitment—it is a long-term process. Keep reading and studying. Over time, your defense system will grow stronger and stronger.

False teachers disguise themselves as servants of righteousness (2 Peter 2:10-22). Such people will be judged. It is our duty to expose and oppose them.

---

# Forgiveness and Comfort

God has no limit on forgiving sin because of what Christ has forgiven us. To be a Christian means to forgive the inexcusable because God has forgiven the inexcusable in you. (C.S. Lewis: "Everyone says forgiveness is a lovely idea, until they have something to forgive.")

---

# The New Covenant Ministry

The old covenant is called the "ministry of death," but the new covenant is more glorious. Moses came down from the mountain with his face shining, reflecting God's glory to the people. Moses was God's mediator but could not bring God's full glory, nor could the people bear to see it. Through Moses they got some sense of the terrifying and awesome God.

Paul's point is simple: the old covenant was glorious—all powerful, infinite, holy God spoke to man, revealing Himself and His will. It was an integral part of God's plan revealing important aspects of His nature.

**Application:** Although not the main point, we too can reflect God's glory to people around us. We are to shine His light and attract people to God. The more time we spend with God, the more our lives and countenance change. We become more and more like Him and the light reflects stronger and stronger. The key is to spend more time with Him.

When people praise you, give God the glory—not with a cliché but with your entire mindset that you are a humble servant drawing attention to Jesus. Minister in such a way as not to get recognition for yourself.

---

# New Creation in Christ

If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation (2 Corinthians 5:16-21). Coming to Christ does not end the struggle; it starts it. You don't enter this struggle alone. He gives you all the resources needed to be an overwhelming conqueror (Romans 8:37).

Our bodies are not destroyed and made anew. After salvation, we have the same flesh, born of sin. Prior to Christ, there is nothing we can do to please Him—all good deeds are filthy rags (Isaiah 64:6) because done with wrong motivations. When we come to Christ, He gives us spiritual life. For the first time we are able to please God and love God. Our spirit is no longer dead and separated from Him.

---

# Ambassadors of Christ and Reconciliation

We are ambassadors of Christ. All reconciliation is from God. The ministry of reconciliation is given to us. Romans 5:10 states: "For if while we were enemies we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, now that we are reconciled, shall we be saved by his life."

Through Christ, we become the righteousness of God (2 Corinthians 5:21).

---

# The Urgency of Salvation

"Now is the day of salvation" (2 Corinthians 6:2). It is important to know that now is the day of salvation. We work together with Christ in the ministry of reconciliation. How do we work together with Him? Someone receiving grace of God in vain does not truly accept it.

If people knew there wasn't another chance in the future, they might act differently. This urgency should affect us as we are responsible for sharing the gospel.

Isaiah 49:8 states: "In the time of my favor I will answer you, and in the day of salvation I will help you."

# Clean and Condensed Text

## Now is the Day of Salvation

Now is the favorable time. Even if you could guarantee that you would be alive and willing to turn to God ten years from now, waiting ten years is a waste of your life.

Consider a candle that has been mostly burned down with only a stub remaining, compared to a fresh new candle with many hours of flame ahead. You would choose the new candle because it could burn longer. Similarly, it is better to come to God earlier. The earlier we come to God, the more flame we have left to burn for Him. Living life without God is lost, wasted, filled with guilt and shame.

Don't wait to trust in Jesus. Don't wait to serve Him.

**Application:** Perhaps you are called to serve God in a specific mission or ministry. Maybe you have been planning to start a Bible study, share the gospel with a friend, or write a Christian book. Why are you waiting? Now is a favorable time! Do it while you still have the chance. (John 9:4) What is one thing that perhaps you have been delaying which you should do for God?

---

## Servants of God Need Great Endurance

Servants of God must endure many obstacles and difficulties in ministry. Some obstacles are intentionally placed in someone's way to prevent them from receiving the gospel. Others are unintentional. Paul commends himself by listing difficulties to teach what doing the ministry of reconciliation requires. To face these difficulties, servants need great endurance and specific qualities: purity, knowledge, patience, kindness, genuine love, truthful speech, and God's power.

---

## Do Not Be Unequally Yoked Together with Unbelievers

Paul desires open communication with the Corinthians. Open communication is important in building relationships. When there is a problem, it is better to address it head-on rather than let time pass.

**The Dangers of Unequal Yoking**

One's worldview affects everything about one's life—every decision that one makes. Consider these potential areas of disagreement:

**Children and Education:** From a young age, believers are taught to know and follow God. Unbelievers do not agree with these things. Their approach to education differs fundamentally—preparing children to be successful in this world rather than following God. Will you give your kids a Christian education or a secular one? Will you send your kids to church or soccer league? Will you spend time having devotions with your kids or have them tutored to raise their grades? Will you discipline your kids or pamper them?

**Free Time:** Study the Bible, go to prayer meeting, or go see a movie? Spend Sundays worshiping God or shopping? There is a fundamental disagreement on what is worth doing.

**Careers:** One's worldview affects his choice of career. Some careers may be unethical. Others may require doing something unethical to advance. Career and family or God may be at odds. How will you solve these conflicts with your spouse if your priorities are so different?

**Three Possible Situations**

When a believer marries an unbeliever, three situations are possible:

A. There are few disagreements about worldview. The believer might say, "We don't really disagree. Our relationship is so good!" This means the believer is not really understanding and following God's Word in daily life. Her belief is intellectual only.

B. There are frequent disagreements. The believer wants one course of action and the unbeliever wants another. The believer gives in. This is harmful for the believer's faith and the whole family.

C. There are frequent disagreements. The believer sticks to his guns and refuses to give in. This happens again and again on many different issues. The marriage will be slowly torn apart and become unhealthy and tiresome. It is also "not fair" to the unbeliever to never get his way. The marriage may drift apart and break up over time. At minimum, the believer cannot enjoy marriage as God designed.

If you marry an unbeliever, you are putting yourself into a dangerous situation that will either stunt your spiritual growth or lead you away from the Lord completely.

## Accountability and Team in Collections

**Commendation of the Team**

The team handling the collection serves an important role. Accountability is essential in Christian work. Having multiple people involved provides safety and prevents blame. Paul asks the Corinthians to welcome these brothers and recognize their labor for Christ.

**Principles for Christian Workers**

Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil. For we are God's fellow workers. From the whole body, joined and held together by every joint with which it is equipped, when each part is working properly, the body grows and builds itself up in love.

---

## Generosity and Giving

**God Will Enrich You**

You will be enriched in every way to be generous in all situations. This is true in every way, not just in finances. When you learn from God's Word, you should pass that knowledge on to others. When you have a testimony of God's goodness, you should share it. If you have a home, you should use it hospitably. There are many areas in which we can be generous.

**Generosity Produces Thanksgiving**

Why does God enrich His people? To give you the ability to be generous. What outcome does generosity produce? Thanksgiving to God. Since a key goal of giving is generating more thanksgiving to God, this should affect your own attitude and motivation toward giving. When does giving glorify God? When it comes from the heart and produces gratitude to Him.

God supplies seed to the sower. God places resources in our hands to be used! He gives us seed that He expects to be sowed. When the worker finishes planting all the seeds in his bag, the farmer will give him another. God will always provide enough seeds for the industrious worker. He will "increase the harvest of your righteousness." Those seeds will grow and result in a crop. Your good deeds done for the Lord will not be in vain. (Galatians 6:9: And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up.)

**Application:** What is your perspective toward money? Money is given by God. It is to be used for God. Is there any area where your perspective toward money needs to change to align with what we have learned? Does your giving plan need to be adjusted?

## Paul's Sufferings for Christ

Paul compares himself with false apostles who boast about themselves. Paul finds the need to boast foolishly in order to be heard, though he says this not as the Lord would but as a fool. Other Scriptures teach that boasting should be avoided (James 4:16: As it is, you boast in your arrogance. All such boasting is evil; Proverbs 27:2: Let another praise you, and not your own mouth).

False teachers treated the Corinthians poorly, taking advantage of them and exalting themselves. When Paul says "we were too weak for that," he means they did not exploit the Corinthians as the false apostles did. Paul uses sarcasm throughout this passage, saying the Corinthians were so "wise" to tolerate the false apostles, and that the apostles must not be "fools" like Paul and his companions.

**Paul's List of Sufferings**

Paul lists extensive sufferings: beatings, imprisonments, riots, hard labor, sleepless nights, hunger, cold, and exposure. He endured dangers from rivers, bandits, countrymen, and Gentiles. He faced dangers in the city, desert, sea, and from false brothers. Through weariness, pain, tears, and often without food, Paul persevered in his ministry.

---

## Facing Persecution

Even when we face difficulties and trials or spiritual attacks, we can be sure that God has a divine purpose in allowing it. God will not allow one hair of our heads to be harmed apart from His purpose. So we should be confident to stand up to any spiritual attack knowing that God is with us and will use opposition for building us and His kingdom.

Serious persecutions may become a reality in our lives or our children's lives. Now is the time to prepare. Now is the time to be faithful. If you are swayed by hostile remarks now, how can you hope to stand when persecution increases?

We would not be able to stand by ourselves. The only hope is through Christ. We need to draw close to Christ now, depend on Him day by day, and be faithful in small things. We need to come to Him when we face small temptations so that when they grow more serious, we already have this lifestyle.

Where you face major persecution or just pressure from family or hostility, you can overcome it through Christ. He conquered it and will help you conquer it as well. The key is that you must depend on Him. Come to Him now. Ask Him for strength to be faithful. If you are faithful in the midst of persecution, He will give you the crown of life.

---

## When Love Hurts

**Love Sometimes Hurts**

Paul had written to the Corinthians and felt pain because he cared about them deeply. Love hurts when we see those we love living in sin. It hurts when we confront sin. But it is necessary. When you care about someone, you will want to pray for that person and use Scripture to guide them back to the right path. Just make sure that when you confront sin, you are doing it out of love! Pray and ask God to reveal your own motivations to you.

**Forgive the Sinner**

After someone has been punished by the church for their actions, it is time to forgive them. Forgiveness and comfort are related. True forgiveness looks like affirming your love and support for the person who has repented. Those who demonstrate excessive sorrow and genuine repentance should be forgiven and comforted.

People find it difficult to forgive those who have hurt them. Yet believers must forgive. The result of unforgiveness is that Satan can exploit divisions in the church. When Paul says "what I have forgiven… has been for your sake in the presence of Christ," he means forgiveness is granted in recognition of Christ's authority and for the sake of maintaining unity in the body.

---

## Compassion in Ministry

Compassion may not naturally be your strong suit. If that is true, God wants you to grow in that area. Brothers and sisters are struggling. Do not let them face it alone. Reach out. Show your care. Make a friend. Listen. Pray. Practice simple acts of love and kindness. Use the truth of God's Word to comfort believers with the promises of God and remind them of the ultimate comfort He gives to His children.

# 2 Corinthians Study Guide - Chunk 9

## Glory in Reconciliation vs. Condemnation

Paul compares the old and new covenants. The old covenant is like a doctor diagnosing terminal cancer—vital but painful. The new covenant is the cure. Our reconciliation to God through Jesus' sacrifice is the climax of world history. Everything before points forward to it; everything after looks back on it.

The gospel progression: God created the world. People sinned. Sin has consequences including death, judgment, and separation from God. No person can solve sin alone. But God loves you and made a way for reconciliation through Jesus, His Son, who died to take away sin and rose to prove He could. We have hope because the God of the universe became human, died, defeated death, and rose again.

If there is glory in condemnation, there is far more glory in reconciliation. The old covenant was temporary; the new covenant through grace and faith is permanent and final. Salvation, forgiveness, and God's adoption of believers are permanent. The church is permanent in God's kingdom.

## When One Turns to the Lord, the Veil Is Removed

Jesus opens spiritual eyes. This applies to everyone: those blinded by legalism, false religions, atheism, or cultural Christianity focused on rules rather than Jesus. Whatever blinds you, Jesus can remove and make you see clearly. Through Him you can see past lies, deception, and moral relativity to know the truth. The truth sets you free.

Scripture: Psalm 119:18; Ephesians 1:18

## By the Open Statement of Truth

**Our responsibility as ministers of the new covenant:** Clearly share the truth in simple terms without watering it down for acceptance or updating it for modern culture. Faithfully and accurately divide God's Word. We answer to God, not the world.

If people reject the message and call you narrow-minded, that is not failure. Jesus Himself is a rock of offense. Truth often offends people. However, your attitude and style should not offend. Avoid pride or contempt toward others. Share Scripture with love, praying God will touch hearts.

## Satan Actively Tries to Blind People to the Gospel

Satan uses various methods to blind people to truth. Knowing this, we must be vigilant and watchful (1 Peter 5:8). We should respond with love, giving blessings instead of returning insults, and overcoming evil with good (John 15:18-20).

## An Eternal Perspective Prevents Losing Heart

**Key principle:** Look not to things seen but to things unseen. Things seen are transient; things unseen are eternal. Your house, possessions, body, and all visible things are temporary and will be destroyed (2 Peter 3:10). Most people spend their lives pursuing these temporary things.

In contrast, important things cannot be seen or touched: God (spirit, invisible), our souls (spirit, invisible), and loved ones' souls. Set your spiritual eyes on the spiritual by meditating on God, His attributes, your relationship with Him, and eternity.

Our mortal life is temporary, but its end begins eternal life in heaven—more intense, vibrant, abundant, and superior in every way. Because we will face God one day, our current life gains meaning and purpose. This knowledge changes everything and should motivate us to aim to please Him.

Scripture: Colossians 3:2; Matthew 24:38-39; Romans 8:18

## Outward Appearance vs. the Heart

False teachers and some churches focus on outward things: membership numbers, offerings received, beautiful facilities, respectability, or social media likes. But God sees the heart (1 Samuel 16:7). Church leaders should focus on the hearts of their people, not external metrics.

## Repentance Bears Fruit

Genuine repentance proves itself through sincere response. The Corinthians showed righteous indignation toward sin, rooted it out, and sought holiness in fear of God. They developed zeal for righteousness and hatred of sin.

Fake repentance is words without action—saying "I'm sorry" then repeating the sin. Real repentance begins with changed attitude toward sin, recognizing its seriousness, followed by changed behavior.

**Application:** If you truly repent, there will be fruit of a changed life. Ask yourself: Am I sorry because I'm caught or because I've grieved God?

## Complete Confidence in Others

Paul exemplified how Christian workers should think of those they serve. He believed the best about them and expected positive response (1 Corinthians 13:7). He avoided cynicism despite continued sin among those he ministered to.

**Application:** Don't become cynical toward those you minister to. When tempted to complain or view them as an annoyance, repent and ask God to fill your heart with love. Jesus loved them enough to give His life. Attitudes toward people significantly influence their lives.

## Collecting Funds with Transparency

Money is frequently discussed in Scripture because greed, theft, bribery, and corruption relate to it. The love of money is the root of all kinds of evil (1 Timothy 6:10).

Paul was careful to collect funds blamelessly, avoiding the pitfall of entrusting a large sum to one person. Proper accountability protects the gift from misuse, the giver from wasting money, and those collecting from false accusations. Paul emphasized:
- For the glory of the Lord
- So no one should blame us
- We aim at what is honorable
- In the Lord's sight and in the sight of man

## Freewill Offerings, Not Coerced

Freewill offerings are completely voluntary—gifts given from the heart in response to God's love. These are most precious. Coerced gifts lack meaning and may remind the recipient of the giver's stinginess.

God gave His Son voluntarily and not by requirement. Jesus gave His life of His own accord (John 10:11). The Christian life is a relationship, not mere religion or rules. God gave us will and choice to respond to His love voluntarily, as He did from the beginning in the Garden of Eden.

**Application:** Voluntary, heartfelt giving motivated by love for God is what glorifies Him.

## Destroying Strongholds

Strongholds are areas the enemy believes he controls: addictions (pornography, drugs, alcohol), lost souls firmly in Satan's kingdom, wrong thinking that spreads through cultures or families, or nations enslaved to sin and darkness. But they can be broken by God's power.

Our weapons in this battle are the gospel, prayer, faith, salvation, God's Word, righteous living, and truth. These set us and others free from bondage.

**Application:** Identify one stronghold in your life or others'. How will you use God's weapons to destroy it?

## Consistency Between Word and Life

Paul responded to criticism about his weak bodily presence by affirming: "What we say by letter when absent, we do when present." Their lives and message were consistent, not hypocritical. They preached one thing and lived another—gaining credibility and respect.

Paul intentionally used letters for rebuke, allowing calm reflection and repentance, so in-person visits could be joyful fellowship rather than confrontation.

## Boasting in the Lord

True boasting magnifies the Lord and draws attention to Him, not oneself (Jeremiah 9:24). Instead of "I am great," say "God is great." Whatever Paul said positively about himself aimed to glorify God.

**Application:** Our mouths should be filled with God's praises. Our goal should be drawing others to Him.

## Final Reminders

Paul's life—including escaping Damascus in a basket lowered from a wall—was not heroic by worldly standards but was reality. God used this "weak" person to help build His church's foundation, receiving the glory.

**Application:** No matter what difficulties we face serving God, deflect glory back to Him. What is one way you can depend on God when facing affliction?

# 2 Corinthians Study Notes (Condensed)

## Key Teachings on Satan's Deception & Spiritual Vigilance

**Knowing that Satan actively tries to blind people, what should we do?**

Satan disguises himself as an angel of light and works to deceive believers. Be sober-minded and watchful, knowing your adversary prowls like a roaring lion (1 Peter 5:8). Guard your thoughts from being led astray from sincere devotion to Christ (2 Corinthians 11:3). Counter deception by standing firm in God's truth and putting on the whole armor of God (Ephesians 6:11).

**Cross-References:**
- 1 Peter 5:8, 2 Corinthians 11:14, 2 Corinthians 11:3, Matthew 13:19, Ephesians 6:11

---

## Eternal Perspective

Set your spiritual eyes on eternal realities by meditating on God, His attributes, and your relationship with Him. Riches, pleasure, position, and worldly recognition are temporary. God alone is eternal—He exists from eternity past to eternity future and is more important than anything else. Because God has made our souls eternal, their value is priceless. Invest in spiritual well-being rather than earthly possessions.

**Application:** How will having an eternal perspective change how you live now? Reflect on decisions made with short-term versus eternal perspective.

---

## Pleasing God & Living with Purpose

Because you will face God in judgment, this knowledge gives your current life full meaning and purpose. Make it your aim to please Him in all things—not just in great spiritual endeavors, but in simple daily interactions and how you communicate with family.

**Application:** How can you please God in the simple things of life?

---

## Salvation Without Regret

True repentance produces fruit of a changed life. Examine yourself: Are you sorry because you're caught, or sorry because you've grieved God? Evaluate whether you bear the fruit of repentance or merely speak it in words.

---

## Paul's Confidence in the Corinthians

**Believing the best in others:** Attitudes toward people significantly influence their lives. Jesus called Simon "Peter" (meaning "rock") before Peter became what that name described. Jesus saw what Peter could become and helped him get there. Believe the best about others and help them grow in Christ to become all God intends.

---

## Giving with Integrity

**Transparency in financial matters:** Ensure money handling (offerings, tithes, donations) is done transparently with clear processes and guidelines. Multiple people should always be present when counting money. Transparency is not about distrust—it minimizes temptation and protects those handling collections from false accusations.

Paul took precautions in collecting funds:
- Collection taken before his arrival so he wouldn't handle money himself
- Corinthians appointed their own representative to accompany the collection to Jerusalem
- Trusted colleague (Titus) finished the collection effort with accountability partners

**Application:** Use your influence to ensure money is handled transparently in your church or organization. There is safety in numbers.

---

## Willing Giving, Not Compulsion

God gave us will and choice. Willing, sacrificial giving in response to God's grace is true worship (Romans 12:1). If giving feels like an exaction or duty done reluctantly, return to the cross. Remember what Christ has done for you. As you meditate on that grace, your heart should warm and you'll experience renewal from the inside out, resulting in genuine desire for God's things—not as ritual, but from love.

**Application:** Do you feel church, Bible reading, prayer, or giving as an exaction? Come back to the cross and meditate on Christ's sacrifice. Let that transform your heart's motivation.

---

## Giving from the Heart

Each one must give as decided in their heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:6-7). Whatever you sow, you will reap—sow sparingly, reap sparingly; sow bountifully, reap bountifully. This applies to spiritual growth and God's provision, though not as a prosperity guarantee.

---

## Paul's Vision & the Thorn in the Flesh

**Visions and heavenly experiences:** Test all claims of visions or heavenly experiences against Scripture. Not every spirit claiming God's authority speaks for Him. Many false prophets have arisen. If any message contradicts the gospel, reject it (Galatians 1:8).

---

## Awaiting a New Body (Eternal Hope)

This earthly body is temporary—a "tent." God has prepared an eternal house in heaven for us. The Spirit dwelling in us guarantees what is to come. We should groan under the weight of earthly suffering, knowing better is ahead.

**Cross-References:** John 14:2-3, Romans 14:8, Ecclesiastes 3:11, 1 John 3:2, Philippians 3:21

---

## The Importance of Team

Although Paul was an apostle, he worked through partnerships. He did not operate alone or lord authority over others. He considered Titus his partner.

**Benefits of team:**
- Accomplishes more than one person
- Helps members who fall
- Keeps each other accountable
- Exercises positive peer pressure
- Acts as safeguard against temptation
- Deflects accusations
- Combines collective wisdom
- Covers weaknesses and strengthens gifts
- Keeps members humble
- Shares the load

**Application:** Are you on a healthy team? What can you do this week to be a better team member?

---

## Mental Warfare & Guarding Your Mind

Nations understand the power of propaganda to control minds. Satan operates similarly, trying to control people through false narratives. He begins with questions like, "Did God really say?" and "you will not surely die."

**Reflect:** What methods does the enemy use to influence minds? Examples of mainstream arguments against God include: evolution, hedonism, universalism, "all religions are equally valid," God is harsh, look out for yourself, you're self-made, traditional values are outdated, people are inherently good.

As God's warriors, we must fight these ideas while praying for people's souls to be freed from their grip.

---

## Comparing Ourselves with Others

Those who compare themselves with others are unwise. The root is self-commendation—people compare to appear better. Problems with comparing:
- Other people are not your standard; God is
- Always find someone worse to feel better about yourself
- Comparison breeds jealousy, pride, boasting, selfishness, empty conceit, and greed

Fix your eyes on Jesus (Hebrews 12:2). Strive to please Him—His is the only opinion that truly matters.

**Application:** Do you compare yourself with others? What's the root reason? If comparing is a habit, confess it to the Lord and any person you've harmed with rivalry or jealousy.

---

## Commendation from God

It is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends (2 Corinthians 10:18). Let another praise you, not your own mouth (Proverbs 27:2). God gives the final verdict on each person, so His opinion alone matters. Strive to please Him in all you do.

# 2 Corinthians Bible Study Guide – Selected Passages

## 2 Corinthians 11:1-15: Beware Of the "Super-Apostles"

**Outline**
I. Paul's jealousy for the Corinthians (1-2)
II. False "super-apostles" attempted to deceive the Corinthians (3-6)
III. Paul's sacrifices for the Corinthians (7-12)
IV. Satan and his servants are masters in deceit (13-15)

**I. Paul's jealousy for the Corinthians (1-2)**

Discussion Questions
- What "foolishness" did Paul find it necessary to engage in?
- Why did he feel forced to keep establishing his credentials with the Corinthians?
- When is jealousy the right behavior?
- Why did Paul feel jealous toward the Corinthians?

God's jealousy is mentioned in Scripture: "For you shall worship no other god, for the Lord, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God" (Exodus 34:14).

**III. Satan and his servants are masters in deceit (13-15)**

Satan's weapon of unforgiveness keeps division in the church and family, preventing effectiveness for Christ. Satan feeds excuses for not forgiving: that the person doesn't deserve it, that the hurt is too great, that the person keeps sinning again, or that forgiveness doesn't require restoration. But if God forgave you, you must forgive others. Forgiveness includes restoration.

True gospel preachers are not peddlers. Peddlers use:
- Outright lying
- Grandiose claims
- Deceptive advertising
- Fake reviews or plants in the crowd
- Flashy shows
- Pressure or intimidation
- False promises

True preachers commissioned by God speak in Christ with truth—the "power of God" (Romans 1:16). Jesus performed miracles without embellishment. Simon the sorcerer in Acts 8 was amazed by real power and wanted to buy the gifts of the Holy Spirit, but Peter rebuked him. Some such peddlers had infiltrated the Corinthian church.

**Application:** What deceptive argument or lofty opinion have you encountered recently? How will you counter it with truth?

---

## 2 Corinthians 2:12-17: The Aroma of Christ

**Discussion Questions**
- Where is Troas and why did Paul go there?
- What does it mean that God "always leads us in triumphal procession"?
- How do believers spread the "fragrance of the knowledge of him everywhere"?
- How can believers be "the aroma of Christ"?
- What does it mean to some this is a fragrance from "death to death" or "life to life"?
- What is a peddler of God's Word and what is their motivation?

**We Are Not Peddlers of God's Word**

A peddler uses deceptive practices because the product is poor quality. Peddlers employ lying, grandiose claims, deceptive advertising, fake reviews, flashy shows, pressure, and false promises. But the gospel is the real thing—the power of God. Therefore, there is no need for deceit or exaggeration.

Jesus performed miracles without show or embellishment. They were real and needed no enhancement. When Simon the sorcerer saw the real power of God (which he had been faking), he wanted to purchase the gifts of the Holy Spirit with money. Peter rebuked him for this.

**Application:** Don't try to make the gospel easier or more palatable for the current generation. Share it faithfully and accurately. Preach the gospel boldly, truthfully, and accurately—with sin, repentance, and Jesus front and center. Rely on the Holy Spirit to convince listeners of the truth.

# Giving to God and Responding to Visions

## Giving to God

We give to God by giving to people. Matthew 25:40 states: "The King will reply, 'Truly I tell you, whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me.'"

We love God by loving our brothers and neighbors. We serve God by serving His people. We give to God by supporting His workers and His church.

## How Should We Respond to Claims of Visions Today?

We should treat such claims with healthy skepticism. 1 John 4:1 commands us to "test the spirits": "Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, for many false prophets have gone out into the world."

In Bible times, God often communicated through dreams and visions. In the end times, He promises to do so again (Joel 2:28). However, we should not blindly accept accounts, as this assuredly brings in false teaching from people with selfish motives.

Test such claims against these standards:
- Does it agree with God's word (Acts 17:11)?
- Is it a different gospel (Galatians 1:8)?
- Is the person credible?
- Has this person made other statements or prophecies that have been disproven (Numbers 23:19)?
- Is God receiving the glory or man (Isaiah 42:8)?
- Is there practical benefit to the claim? Many grandiose stories sound exciting but don't contribute to believers living holy lives or seeking after God.

Application: Be discerning. Test the spirits. Do not blindly accept every claim of visions and special revelations. Compare them with God's Word.

## Living for Christ

Remember what God has done for us. Though we were totally depraved, without hope, and destined for His wrath, He called and justified us because of His choice. It was His initiative, not ours; His grace, not our work. Jesus gave His life for us.

God does not just want your money, possessions, or time. He wants your life. We are to be living sacrifices (Romans 12:1-2). This means giving Him all our energy, thoughts, and actions all the time. We are to serve Him in a thousand small ways every day.

We are to put His will above ours in every area. We live for Him and for His pleasure, not our own.

Application: How can you live for God in the coming week?

## Radical Generosity

Jesus' example shows radical generosity. Believers are called to excel at generosity as part of responding to Christ's sacrifice. This is not merely about donating to relief efforts or wearing Christian jewelry, but a complete commitment to serving God through generous giving.

Application: How can you excel at giving in the coming week? Based on this lesson, what adjustment should you make in your giving?

## Being a New Creation in Christ

Those in Christ are new creatures. 2 Corinthians 5:16-17 explains that we no longer regard anyone from a worldly point of view. How we regarded Christ before salvation differs from how we should regard Him after becoming Christians.

Being "in Christ" means transformation—from the old self to a new creation. This transformation involves putting off the old self and putting on the new self created after the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness (Ephesians 4:22-24).

Cross-references show this reality: There is no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1). Those in Christ are crucified with Christ—no longer living for themselves, but Christ living in them (Galatians 2:20).
