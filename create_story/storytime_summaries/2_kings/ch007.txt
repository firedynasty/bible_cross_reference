# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Kings Chapter 7 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes God's sovereignty in desperate circumstances, declaring that **"Man's extremity is God's opportunity of magnifying his own power."** The commentary centers on divine deliverance when human hope is exhausted, illustrating that God's timing often comes precisely when people stop expecting help. Henry connects this to Christ's words: "When the son of man comes shall he find faith on the earth?" (Luke 18:8).

The theme of faith versus unbelief runs throughout, with Henry noting that **"where sin abounded grace doth much more abound"**—God promises deliverance despite the king's threats against Elisha. The supernatural terror God places on the Syrian army demonstrates divine power working without human intervention.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons: First, unbelief deprives people of God's intended blessings, as seen in the doubting lord who sees but cannot partake of the abundance. **"Unbelief is a sin by which men greatly dishonour and displease God, and deprive themselves of the favours he designed for them."**

The lepers' reasoning provides wisdom for desperate situations—they chose action that might improve their condition rather than remaining in certain death. Their eventual decision to share good news teaches against selfishness: **"The most comfortable advantage is that which our brethren share with us in."**

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Elisha's prophecy of abundance comes when hope seems impossible. The doubting lord's demand for divine "windows in heaven" ironically echoes Moses's miracle, though Elijah's multiplication miracles should have sufficed.

**Verses 3-5**: The four lepers (possibly including Gehazi) reason that **"if they kill us, we shall but die"**—Henry notes this applies spiritually to those facing spiritual death versus eternal judgment.

**Verses 6-7**: God imposed false hearing on the Syrians just as He had imposed false sight on others previously. Henry observes that **"notices from the invisible world are either very comfortable or very dreadful, according as men are at peace with God or at war with him."**

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this narrative to Deuteronomy 32:36 regarding God appearing when human strength fails. He references Judges 5:11 about people returning to the gates in peace. The murmuring Israelites who saw but couldn't enter Canaan parallels the unbelieving lord's fate.

The prodigal son's return illustrates the lepers' reasoning about risking mercy rather than certain death. Henry also connects the Syrian panic to how **"the wicked flee when none pursues,"** demonstrating God's ability to **"make the stoutest heart to tremble"** without visible cause.

**Final Fulfillment**: The prophecy's exact fulfillment, including the lord's death by trampling, confirms that **"no word of God shall fall to the ground,"** emphasizing divine faithfulness despite human doubt.
