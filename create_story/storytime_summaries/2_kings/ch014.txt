## Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Kings Chapter 14: Summary

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **incomplete righteousness** as a central theme. While Amaziah "did that which was right in the sight of the LORD," Henry notes this was "yet not like David." He stresses that "it is not enough to do that which our pious predecessors did, merely to keep up the usage, but we must do it as they did it, from the same principle of faith and devotion." The persistence of high places illustrates how "corruptions which, by long usage, have gained both prescription and a favourable opinion" are difficult to eliminate.

**Divine justice and the danger of pride** form another key theme. Henry observes that God "resists the proud," explaining why the seemingly righteous Amaziah was defeated by the wicked Joash of Israel. The commentary reveals how "the root of all sin lies in the heart" and warns against pride that follows success.

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**Prudent Justice**: Amaziah's delayed execution of his father's murderers demonstrates that "Justice strikes surely by striking slowly, and is often executed most prudently when it is not executed presently." Henry advocates for wisdom over haste in administering justice.

**Contentment vs. Ambition**: Amaziah's challenge to Israel exemplifies dangerous overreach. Henry warns that "many would have had wealth and honour enough if they had but known when they had enough" and counsels against meddling "to thy hurt."

**Adhering to God's Law**: Amaziah's refusal to execute the murderers' children, following Deuteronomy 24:16, shows proper submission to divine law over human counsel for revenge or political expediency.

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On **verses 3-4**: Henry notes the danger of partial obedience, emphasizing that maintaining religious forms without David's heart represents insufficient devotion.

On **verse 6**: Henry provides detailed explanation of why God can visit fathers' sins upon children (since all owe God their lives) while earthly rulers cannot, as children "before them, are innocent."

On **verses 9-10**: Henry analyzes Joash's parable of the thistle and cedar as both prophetic warning and prideful boasting, noting how "two proud and scornful men set their wits on work to vilify and undervalue one another."

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to several biblical texts:

- **Deuteronomy 24:16** regarding not punishing children for fathers' sins
- **2 Chronicles 25:5** for expanded account of the Edomite campaign  
- **2 Chronicles 25:13, 16** explaining Amaziah's motivations and his mistreatment of God's prophet
- **Proverbs 20:3** on avoiding unnecessary meddling
- References to earlier Kings passages showing historical patterns

Henry concludes by noting the irony that Amaziah, though more righteous than Joash, suffered defeat because "God will honour those who honour him in his prophets, but those who despise them...shall be lightly esteemed." This emphasizes how specific acts of faithfulness or rebellion toward God's messengers can override general moral character in determining divine favor.
