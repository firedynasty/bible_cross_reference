# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Kings Chapter 4: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes that **God's power magnifies His goodness**, noting how "Elisha's miracles were for use, not for show" - demonstrating practical divine care rather than mere spectacle. A central theme is God's faithfulness to the righteous poor, as seen in the widow's situation where "the generation of the upright may expect help from God's providence." 

Henry also highlights the principle that **divine blessing works through human effort**: "It is in connexion with our own careful and diligent endeavours that we may expect the blessing of God to enrich us." The oil multiplied through the widow's own pouring, teaching that "it is not hoarding the talents, but trading with them, that doubles them."

## Key Practical Applications

**For Charity and Social Justice**: Henry advocates practical over superficial help, noting Elisha "did not say, Be warmed, be filled, but gave her real help" by establishing the widow in sustainable business rather than temporary relief. This represents "the best method of charity" - enabling people to improve through "their own industry and ingenuity."

**For Debt and Financial Responsibility**: The commentary stresses that the widow's first duty was paying debts before personal provision, emphasizing that "we render to all their due, pay every just debt...not only for wrath, to avoid being sued, but also for conscience' sake."

**For Faith and Providence**: Henry encourages trusting God's provision while acting responsibly, noting "we are never straitened in God...all our straitness is in ourselves. It is our faith that fails, not his promise."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry observes how ministers should "make themselves familiar with those that are every way their inferiors" and notes that even godly people may face insolvency through "afflictive providences" rather than personal irresponsibility.

**Verses 3-6**: The instruction to "borrow not a few" vessels demonstrates that our limitations constrain God's blessings - "when every vessel was full the oil stayed...We are never straitened in God...It is our faith that fails, not his promise."

**Verses 8-17**: Henry notes the Shunammite woman's discernment in recognizing Elisha as "a holy man of God" and emphasizes how hospitality to God's servants brings unexpected rewards.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects the oil miracle to **Job 29:6** ("The rock poured me out rivers of oil"), **Zechariah 4:12** regarding the golden oil from the olive tree, and **John 2:10** comparing the miraculous oil's quality to Christ's wine at Cana. He also references **Ecclesiastes 9:15** about the wise man who "delivered the army, but no man remembered," and draws parallels between the Shunammite's promised son and similar divine promises to Abraham, Isaac, and other biblical figures.

The commentary consistently emphasizes that while "we cannot now expect miracles, yet we may expect mercies, if we wait on God and seek to him," encouraging both practical wisdom and faithful dependence on divine providence.
