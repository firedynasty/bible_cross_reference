# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Kings Chapter 22: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as showcasing **divine grace triumphant over human corruption**. He emphasizes "the sovereignty of divine grace" in how Josiah became godly despite his wicked father Manasseh, noting that "Nothing is too hard for that grace to do." The commentary highlights God's providential preservation of Scripture, declaring that "If the holy scriptures had not been of God, they would not have been in being at this day; God's care of the Bible is a plain indication of his interest in it."

Henry interprets the discovery of the Law as a **token of God's mercy**, suggesting that "It is a sign that God has mercy in store for a people when he magnifies his law among them and makes that honourable."

## Key Practical Applications

The commentary draws several practical lessons:

**Scripture Study**: Henry emphasizes that "No summaries, extracts, or collections, out of the Bible (though they may have their use) can be effectual to convey and preserve the knowledge of God and his will like the Bible itself." He advocates for direct engagement with Scripture rather than substitutes.

**Faithful Stewardship**: Regarding the temple workers' honesty, Henry notes their good reputation but questions whether accountability was wise: "a man should count money (we say) after his own father."

**Divine Reward for Obedience**: Henry teaches that "Those that do their duty according to their knowledge shall have their knowledge increased. To him that hath shall be given."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry contrasts Solomon's warning about child kings with Josiah as an exception, comparing him to England's Edward VI. He marvels that despite "no good education nor good example," divine grace made Josiah "an eminent saint."

**Verses 8-10**: Henry suggests the found book was likely Moses' original manuscript from the Most Holy Place. He theorizes it was either "carelessly mislaid," "maliciously concealed," or "carefully laid up by some of its friends."

**Verses 11-13**: Henry interprets Josiah's clothes-rending as signifying "the rending of his heart for the dishonour done to God." He suggests the king's shock came from discovering the full promises and threatenings previously unknown to him.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to multiple scriptures: **Ezekiel 18:14** (children not following fathers' sins), **Romans 11:24** (grafting metaphor for grace), **Deuteronomy 17:18 and 31:10-11** (king's duty to write and read Law), **Isaiah 65:1** (finding what wasn't sought), and **Leviticus 26 and Deuteronomy 28** (the promises and threatenings that particularly affected Josiah).

The commentary presents this chapter as demonstrating how God preserves His Word and uses it to guide reformation, while showing His sovereign grace in raising up godly leaders despite corrupt circumstances.
