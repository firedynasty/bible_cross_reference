# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Kings Chapter 6: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes God's sovereign power over nature and human affairs throughout this chapter. The miracle of making iron swim demonstrates that "the God of nature is not tied up to its laws," revealing divine authority over natural order. A central theme is God's omniscience—"Nothing done, said, thought, by any person, in any place, at any time, is out of the reach of God's cognizance." Henry also highlights God's protective care for His servants, showing how "they that be with us are more than they that be with them" through spiritual realities invisible to natural sight.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**Contentment and Industry**: The sons of prophets exemplify proper attitudes—they were "content with mean things in this" world while "professing to look for great things in the other world." They demonstrated that honest labor is no "burden or disparagement."

**Integrity with Borrowed Goods**: The prophet's distress over the lost axe teaches that "we ought to be as careful of that which is borrowed as of that which is our own," reflecting the principle to "love our neighbour as ourselves."

**Trust in Divine Protection**: Elisha's calm response—"Fear not"—provides a model for facing overwhelming circumstances through faith in God's invisible armies.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that the expanding prophetic school indicated spiritual growth: "a good hearing, for it is a sign many are added to them." He notes the irony of Syria sending massive forces against one prophet: "Did he believe that Elisha...would not discover the designs laid against himself?" The generous treatment of captured enemies demonstrates that mercy can be more effective than violence—"the bands of Syria came no more into the land of Israel."

## Cross-References to Scripture

Henry connects the floating axe to spiritual truths: "God's grace can thus raise the stony iron heart which has sunk into the mud of this world." He references 2 Thessalonians 3:8,10 regarding honest labor and Nehemiah 4:11 about enemy tactics. The chapter illustrates broader biblical themes of God's protection of His servants, similar to other prophetic narratives, while emphasizing that spiritual realities transcend physical appearances—a truth central to biblical faith.
