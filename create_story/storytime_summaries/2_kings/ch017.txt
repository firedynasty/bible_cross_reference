# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Kings 17: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry's commentary on this pivotal chapter emphasizes **divine sovereignty in judgment** and the **consequences of covenant unfaithfulness**. He establishes that while the Assyrians were the instruments of Israel's destruction, "it was the Lord that removed Israel out of his sight" and "the Assyrian was but the rod of his anger." This destruction fulfilled prophetic warnings: "Rather shall heaven and earth pass than one tittle of God's word fall to the ground."

Henry highlights the **gradual nature of divine judgment**, noting that Israel "were for some time made tributaries before they were made captives." This demonstrates God's patience and provides opportunity for repentance before final judgment.

A key theological insight concerns the **doctrine of accumulated sin**. Though King Hoshea was relatively better than his predecessors, the judgment came because God intended "to punish not only the sins of that generation, but of the foregoing ages, and to reckon for the iniquities of their fathers, who had been long in filing the measure and treasuring up wrath."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several pointed lessons for readers. He emphasizes that **political alliances cannot substitute for divine favor**: "Had the king and people of Israel applied to God, made their peace with him and their prayers to him, they might have recovered their liberty, ease, and honour; but they withheld their tribute, and trusted to the king of Egypt."

The commentary warns against **gradual spiritual compromise**. Israel's sins progressed from following "the statutes of the heathen" to secret wickedness, then to open idolatry. Henry notes they "did secretly those things that were not right" before their public apostasy.

He emphasizes **personal responsibility despite poor leadership**: even when given freedom to worship properly under Hoshea, "the people were as bad as those that went before them," demonstrating that external permission cannot change internal rebellion.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On **verses 1-6** (Hoshea's reign and Israel's fall), Henry observes the ironic providence that "the destruction of the kingdom of Israel should come in the reign of one of the best of its kings." He notes how "men get nothing by struggling with the net, but entangle themselves the more" when describing Israel's failed Egyptian alliance.

Regarding **verses 7-23** (causes of judgment), Henry provides a detailed catalog of Israel's sins, emphasizing their ingratitude: they sinned "against the LORD their God, which had brought them up out of the land of Egypt." He stresses how "those that forgot God were themselves forgotten; those that studied to be like the nations were buried among them."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous scriptural connections throughout his commentary. He references **Isaiah 10:5** regarding Assyria as God's rod of anger, and **Isaiah 43:24** asking "Who gave Jacob for a spoil?" He connects to **Hosea's prophecies** about Israel saying "We have no king" and cites **Revelation 7** and **James 1:1** to show that remnants of all tribes survived despite the captivity.

The commentary also references **Galatians 6:16** regarding the "spiritual Israel" of the gospel church, demonstrating how the name of Israel finds ultimate fulfillment in the New Testament church rather than being permanently lost with the northern kingdom's destruction.
