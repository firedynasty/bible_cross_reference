# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Kings 23: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry's commentary on 2 Kings 23 centers on the paradox of faithful reform amid inevitable judgment. Despite God's pronouncement that Jerusalem's ruin was certain, Josiah exemplifies the principle that "he would do his duty, and then leave the event to God." Henry marvels at two striking realities: the shocking extent of idolatry that had infiltrated even God's temple, and how Jerusalem's destruction proceeded despite this thorough reformation, because "the generality of the people, after all, hated to be reformed."

The commentary emphasizes covenant renewal as central to reformation, noting that "the book of the law was the book of the covenant" - God's promise that "if they would be to God a people, he would be to them a God."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for leadership and spiritual reform:

**Leadership by Example**: Josiah "thinks it not below him to be a reader, any more than Solomon did to be a preacher." Henry advocates that "all people are concerned to know the scripture, and all in authority to spread the knowledge of it."

**Public Accountability**: He emphasizes the value of "fast bind, fast find" - making solemn commitments to obedience, noting that "it is of good use to oblige ourselves to our duty with all possible solemnity, and this is especially seasonable after notorious backslidings."

**Collaborative Governance**: Henry observes that "Parliaments are no diminution at all to the honour and power of good princes, but a great support to them."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides detailed analysis of the reform process, expressing amazement at the depth of corruption found even within the temple itself - "vessels...for the worship of Baal," "houses of the Sodomites," and even "horses...given to the sun" at the temple entrance. He notes the irony that "those did worst of all that made it...a brothel, in an impudent defiance of the holiness of God."

Regarding the Passover celebration (verses 21-23), Henry emphasizes its unprecedented nature as a high point of covenant faithfulness.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to several biblical themes: Romans 1:24 regarding how spiritual corruption leads to moral degradation; Jeremiah 6:29-30 about the futility of reform when hearts remain unchanged ("reprobate silver shall men call them"); and Psalm 19:5 in critiquing the absurdity of sun worship.

The commentary demonstrates how thorough reformation requires both systemic change and genuine heart transformation - a lesson as relevant for modern spiritual renewal as it was for ancient Judah.
