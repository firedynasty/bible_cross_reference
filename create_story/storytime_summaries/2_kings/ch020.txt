# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Kings 20: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a study in divine sovereignty over life and death, contrasting "blaspheming Sennacherib destroyed in the midst of the prospects of life" with "praying Hezekiah delivered in the midst of the prospects of death." The central theme is God's responsiveness to faithful prayer and His covenant faithfulness to David's lineage.

Henry emphasizes the uncertainty of earthly comforts, noting that "Hezekiah, in the midst of his triumphs in the favour of God, and over the forces of his enemies, is seized with sickness." This reinforces that "neither greatness nor goodness can exempt us from sickness" and that "we must therefore always rejoice with trembling."

The commentary highlights God's covenant loyalty, as He calls Himself "the God of David" and extends mercy "out of a regard to the covenant made with David and the promise that he would always ordain a lamp for him."

## Key Practical Applications

**On Death Preparation**: When facing mortality, Henry urges believers to "set the heart in order by renewed acts of repentance, and faith, and resignation to God, with cheerful farewells to this world and welcomes to another."

**On Prayer in Sickness**: "Is any sick? Let him be prayed for, let him be prayed with, and let him pray." Henry emphasizes that prayer serves dual purposes - either securing healing or preparing the soul for death.

**On Medical Means**: Henry advocates using ordinary remedies, stating "It is our duty, when we are sick, to make use of such means as are proper to help nature, else we do not trust God, but tempt him."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 3 (Hezekiah's Prayer)**: Henry interprets Hezekiah's appeal to his integrity not as earning salvation through works, but as pleading the covenant conditions God has wrought within him: "our own sincerity may be pleaded as the condition of the covenant which God has wrought in us."

**Verses 4-5 (God's Response)**: Henry marvels that "the prophet had got but to the middle court when he was sent back," emphasizing God's immediate response to heartfelt prayer. He notes God "honours his prayers" and "exceeds his prayers."

**Verse 7 (The Fig Remedy)**: Henry sees divine wisdom in using simple means, noting "Plain and ordinary medicines must not be despised" and "What God appoints he will bless and make effectual."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage extensively with other biblical texts. He references **Ezekiel 36:37** on God's purposes being fulfilled through prayer, **2 Corinthians 1:12** regarding conscience's testimony in facing death, and **Hebrews 5:7** comparing Hezekiah's tears to Christ's emotional prayers.

He draws parallels between Hezekiah's temple focus and **John 5:14**, where the healed man was found in the temple, emphasizing that recovery should lead to worship.

Henry's memorable phrase captures the theological tension: "Happy experiences of the prevalency of prayer are engagements and encouragements to continue instant in prayer." The commentary presents Hezekiah as a model of faithful leadership whose personal devotion and public responsibility intersect in his earnest prayer life.
