# Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Kings 21

## Main Theological Themes

Henry presents this chapter as a tragic reversal of the godly reign of Hezekiah, emphasizing the devastating consequences of apostasy. The central theological theme is divine judgment as the inevitable result of covenant unfaithfulness. He notes how "the holy seed degenerate" and become "commonly worse than the worst of the profane," highlighting the particular responsibility of God's people. The commentary emphasizes God's patience and warning through prophets before executing judgment, demonstrating His justice and mercy even in wrath.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several pointed applications for leaders and individuals. He warns against the dangers of coming to power too young, noting that Manasseh was "puffed up with his honour" and "easily wrought upon by seducers." He emphasizes the corrupting influence of ungodly leadership, observing that "one way or other, the holy city became a harlot, and Manasseh made her so." Henry particularly condemns those who not only sin themselves but "help to make others so," warning that "those will have a great deal to answer for" who lead others astray.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides vivid imagery throughout his exposition. He describes Jerusalem's transformation from "a quiet habitation" to complete moral collapse with the memorable phrase: "How has the gold become dim and the most fine gold changed!" Regarding Manasseh's temple desecration (verses 4-7), Henry notes he "defied God to his face" and "did what he could to alter the property, and to make the name of the God of Israel to be no more in remembrance."

On the prophetic judgment (verses 12-13), Henry explains the striking metaphor: "I will wipe Jerusalem as a man wipeth a dish, wiping it, and turning it upside down," emphasizing the thoroughness of coming judgment.

## Cross-References to Scripture

Henry weaves numerous scriptural connections throughout his commentary. He references Isaiah 33:17-20 to contrast Hezekiah's righteous reign with Manasseh's apostasy. He connects to Isaiah 28:17 regarding God's "line and plummet" of judgment. The commentary links Manasseh's reign to 1 Timothy 3:6 about novices being "lifted up with pride" and falling "into the condemnation of the devil." Henry also references Hebrews 11:37, suggesting Manasseh's persecution may have included sawing Isaiah asunder, and connects the chapter's themes to Matthew 23:32 regarding filling up "the measure of Jerusalem's blood-guiltiness."

Henry concludes by noting that while this chapter records only judgment, Chronicles reveals Manasseh's later repentance, offering hope even in the darkest apostasy.
