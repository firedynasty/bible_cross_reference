# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Kings 18: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes the sovereignty of **God's grace** in transforming hearts contrary to nature. Despite Hezekiah's wicked father Ahaz providing "a bad education as well as a bad example," Hezekiah became "eminently good," demonstrating that "when God's grace will work what can hinder it?" This illustrates that virtue isn't declining inevitably - "after many bad kings, God raised up one that was like David himself."

The commentary highlights **divine judgment and mercy** operating simultaneously. While Israel faces destruction for covenant-breaking, God preserves a faithful remnant in Judah through Hezekiah, showing "God has not quite cast off the seed of Abraham" (Romans 11:1).

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**Regarding Reformation**: Hezekiah's immediate action upon taking the throne teaches that "he began betimes to be so, fell to work as soon as ever he came to the crown, and lost no time." Good works should be pursued with urgency.

**On Idolatry**: Even divinely-instituted objects become dangerous when idolized. Hezekiah's destruction of the bronze serpent demonstrates that "good things, when idolized, are better parted with than kept." Henry notes Hezekiah called it "Nehushtan" - "nothing else but a piece of brass."

**On Prosperity and Trials**: Success in godliness doesn't guarantee freedom from trials. Though Hezekiah "prospered whithersoever he went," he still faced Sennacherib's invasion, showing that even reformers face opposition.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 3-4**: Hezekiah was "a genuine son of David" who did right "according to all that David his father did," unlike previous kings who did right "but not like David." His comprehensive reforms included destroying images, groves, high places, and even the bronze serpent.

**Verse 5**: Henry emphasizes Hezekiah's uniqueness in two areas: "courage and confidence in God" in facing political risks, and "constancy and perseverance" - "he clave to the Lord with a fixed resolution and never departed from following him."

**Verses 13-16**: Henry notes the tension between Hezekiah's earlier rebellion against Assyria (verse 7) and his later submission, showing how even good kings face complex political pressures.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this chapter to broader biblical themes through multiple references: Hosea 11:12 (Judah's faithfulness contrasted with Israel), Romans 11:1 (God's preservation of His people), Romans 11:24 (grace grafting wild branches), John 3:14 (bronze serpent as type of Christ), and Isaiah 14:28 (prophecy of victory over Philistines).

The commentary demonstrates how Old Testament narratives illuminate New Testament truths about grace, judgment, and God's faithfulness to His covenant people.
