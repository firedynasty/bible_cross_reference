# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Samuel 3 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents the prolonged civil war between David and Saul's house as divinely orchestrated. Though "David's house had right on its side, and therefore God on its side," God "for wise and holy ends prolonged the conflict." The gradual strengthening of David's position while Saul's house "waxed weaker and weaker" serves as an allegory for spiritual sanctification: "The contest between grace and corruption in the hearts of believers...may fitly be compared to this." Like David's house growing stronger, "grace...grows stronger and stronger, till it come to a perfect man."

Henry emphasizes God's sovereignty in using human failings to accomplish divine purposes. Abner's character flaws—ambition followed by revenge—ironically serve God's plan: "God by him serves his own purposes, makes even his wrath and revenge to praise him." This demonstrates how "God can find out ways to make those serviceable to the kingdom of Christ who yet have no sincere affection for it."

## Key Practical Applications

**On Marriage and Family:** Henry criticizes David's polygamy as contrary to Deuteronomy 17:17 and "a bad example to his successors." He notes that despite multiple wives, David had "not above one son by each" and warns that "we have reason to rejoice with trembling in the building up of our families," since three sons became infamous.

**On Leadership and Pride:** Through Abner's reaction to Ish-bosheth's accusation, Henry teaches that "Proud men will not bear to be reproved, especially by those whom they think they have obliged." He shows how inconsistent motivations lead to self-contradiction—Abner's ambition first supported Saul's house, then revenge made him support David.

**On Reconciliation:** David's insistence on reclaiming Michal demonstrates proper marital commitment: "If any disagreement has separated husband and wife...let them be reconciled, and come together again."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1:** The "long war" tested David's "faith and patience" while making "his establishment at last the more welcome."

**Verses 2-5:** Henry provides detailed commentary on David's sons, noting Chileab/Daniel's name change reflected his resemblance to David, countering accusations of illegitimacy.

**Verses 7-11:** Ish-bosheth's accusation against Abner shows poor judgment—"it would have been Ish-bosheth's prudence to be silent, considering how much it was his interest not to disoblige Abner."

**Verse 13:** David's demand for Michal's return showed "sincerity of his conjugal affection" and "respect to the house of Saul," demonstrating grace toward his former enemy's family.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to several biblical texts:
- **Psalm 127:5** regarding children as "arrows" strengthening one's position
- **Psalm 62:3** describing enemies as "a bowing wall and a tottering fence"
- **Deuteronomy 17:17** prohibiting kings from multiplying wives
- **1 Chronicles 3:1** for the alternate name of David's son

The commentary emphasizes how even human conflicts serve divine purposes, illustrating God's sovereignty in advancing His kingdom through imperfect human agents.
