# Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Samuel Chapter 1

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **divine providence** as the central theme, noting how "the secret springs of revolutions are unaccountable, and must be resolved into that Providence which turns all hearts as the rivers of water." God's sovereignty orchestrates David's rise to power through unexpected means.

The commentary highlights **God's justice** in Saul's death by an Amalekite, observing that "Saul, who spared the Amalekites in contempt of the divine command, received his death's wound from an Amalekite." This demonstrates divine retribution for disobedience.

Henry also emphasizes **respect for God's anointed**, showing David's reverence even for his enemy. The execution of the Amalekite messenger demonstrates that killing "the LORD's anointed" is inexcusable, regardless of circumstances.

## Key Practical Applications

**Genuine vs. selfish grief**: David's authentic mourning contrasts sharply with the messenger's calculated opportunism. Henry notes David was "kindly affected even to those that hated him," modeling forgiveness over revenge.

**Patient waiting on God's timing**: Rather than eagerly seeking news of Saul's death, David exemplifies that "he that believes does not make haste, takes good news when it comes and is not uneasy while it is in the coming."

**Proper response to enemies' downfall**: David's mourning illustrates that "if we rejoice when our enemy falls the Lord sees it, and it displeases him" (referencing Proverbs 24:17-18).

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry questions whether the Amalekite's story is true, suggesting he may have fabricated his role in Saul's death to curry favor with David. The messenger's presentation of Saul's crown and bracelet ironically fulfills God's purposes through "ill-designing men."

On Saul's final moments, Henry observes the spiritual dimension: "If his conscience now brought to mind the javelin he had cast at David...no marvel that anguish came upon him." He notes how "sense of unpardoned guilt will make death indeed the king of terrors."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects David's conduct to Proverbs 24:17-18 and 17:5 regarding rejoicing over enemies' falls. He references 1 Chronicles 12:1,8,20,22 describing the quality of men joining David's cause. The death account is compared to Peter's narrative of Judas's death (Acts 1:18, Matthew 27:5) as potentially complementary rather than contradictory accounts.

**Conclusion**: Henry presents David as demonstrating Christ-like character—patient, forgiving, and reverent toward God's authority, even when personally wronged. This chapter establishes David's moral fitness for kingship through his response to adversity and his enemy's downfall.
