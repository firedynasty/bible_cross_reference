# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Samuel 9: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a powerful illustration of covenant faithfulness and divine kindness. He emphasizes that David's actions toward Mephibosheth mirror God's grace toward undeserving sinners. The commentary highlights David's deliberate seeking of opportunities to show kindness, which Henry calls "the kindness of God" - not merely human compassion, but "kindness after God's example" and "kindness done after a godly sort, with an eye to God."

A central theological principle emerges: true faith actively seeks opportunities for mercy rather than merely responding when asked. Henry notes that David "sought an opportunity to do good" and emphasizes that "good men should seek opportunities of doing good."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**Covenant Faithfulness**: "We must be mindful of our covenant. The kindness we have promised we must conscientiously perform, though it should not be claimed." Christians should honor commitments even when not legally bound.

**Friendship Obligations**: "To be mindful of our friendships, our old friendships" - especially when Providence has elevated us while friends have fallen low. "Then our friends have most need of us and we are in the best capacity to help them."

**Forgiveness in Action**: David showed genuine forgiveness by actively blessing Saul's house despite past injuries. Henry emphasizes: "We must evince the sincerity of our forgiving those that have been any way unjust or injurious to us by being ready, as we have opportunity, to show kindness both to them and theirs."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1**: Henry notes this inquiry came years later, showing "David had too long forgotten his obligations to Jonathan, but now, at length, they are brought to his mind. It is good sometimes to bethink ourselves whether there be any promises or engagements that we have neglected to make good; better do it late than never."

**Verse 8**: Mephibosheth's response ("What is thy servant, that thou shouldest look upon such a dead dog as I am?") exemplifies proper humility. Henry observes: "It is good to have the heart humble under humbling providences" and "those who thus humble themselves shall be exalted."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout:
- Acts 13:36 - David "served his generation according to the will of God"
- Isaiah 32:8 - "The liberal deviseth liberal things"
- Matthew 5:44 - loving and doing good to enemies
- 1 Peter 3:9 - blessing rather than retaliating
- Proverbs 18:24 - "He that has friends must show himself friendly"
- Ecclesiastes 11:2 and Proverbs 11:25 - principles of generosity and reciprocity

The commentary particularly emphasizes how David's actions fulfill the covenant established in 1 Samuel 20:14-15, where Jonathan requested "the kindness of the Lord" for himself and his descendants.
