# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Samuel Chapter 2 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Guidance and Patience**: Henry emphasizes David's submission to God's timing despite his divine anointing. David "proceeded leisurely; for he that believeth doth not make haste, but waits God's time for the accomplishment of God's promises." This reflects the principle that God's people must seek divine direction even when the path seems clear.

**Typology of Christ's Kingdom**: Henry sees David's gradual rise as prefiguring Christ's kingdom: "David rose gradually; he was first anointed king in reversion, then in possession of one tribe only, and at last of all the tribes. Thus the kingdom of the Messiah, the Son of David, is set up by degrees."

**Opposition to God's Will**: The commentary notes how human ambition opposes divine purposes, as seen in Abner's support of Ishbosheth despite God's clear choice of David.

## Key Practical Applications

**Seeking God's Direction**: Henry stresses the importance of consulting God in all decisions, even when circumstances seem favorable. David's inquiry about where to go demonstrates that "we must apply to God not only when we are in distress, but even when the world smiles upon us."

**Honoring Opponents**: David's gracious message to Jabesh-Gilead for burying Saul exemplifies how believers should show respect even to former enemies. Henry notes this proved David sought the crown "not from any principle of ambition or enmity to Saul, but purely because he was called of God to it."

**Loyalty and Reward**: David's care for his followers teaches that "if we suffer with Christ, we shall reign with him" (2 Tim. 2:12), and that faithful service deserves recognition and reward.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-4**: Henry highlights David's methodical approach—first asking if he should move, then where specifically. God's direction to Hebron (a priest's city and city of refuge) symbolically represents God as David's sanctuary.

**Verses 5-7**: David's diplomatic outreach to Jabesh-Gilead demonstrates both gratitude and political wisdom, showing "good wishes are good things...but they are too cheap to be rested in where there is an ability to do more."

**Verses 12-17**: The tragic combat between the young men illustrates the futility of civil war and human ambition opposing God's will.

## Cross-References

Henry connects this passage to several scriptures: Micah 5:2 (regarding Bethlehem), 2 Timothy 2:12 (suffering and reigning with Christ), Luke 22:29-30 (Christ appointing a kingdom), Hebrews 2:8 (all things not yet under Christ), and Psalm 2:1-2 (opposition to God's anointed).

The commentary presents David as both a legitimate king patiently waiting for full recognition and a type of Christ, whose kingdom also faces opposition but will ultimately prevail through divine purpose rather than human force.
