## Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Samuel 7

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry frames this chapter around the **Davidic Covenant** with clear **Messianic implications**. He emphasizes that "in both these, there is an eye to the Messiah and his kingdom," interpreting God's promise to establish David's throne "forever" as pointing to Christ's eternal reign.

The commentary highlights God's **sovereignty in worship**, noting that "only worship which is acceptable is that which is instituted." God's rejection of David's temple plans demonstrates that good intentions must align with divine timing and purpose. Henry explains that David was "a man of war" whose role differed from Solomon's peaceful reign better suited for temple building.

A key theme is **divine grace preceding human effort**. God reminds David of past mercies—taking him "from the sheepcote" and giving him victory—showing that "God was beforehand with him" rather than indebted to David's good intentions.

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws powerful applications about **grateful stewardship**: "When God, in his providence, has remarkably done much for us, it should put us upon contriving what we may do for him and his glory. What shall I render unto the Lord?"

He emphasizes using seasons of rest purposefully, contrasting David's humble thoughts with Nebuchadnezzar's pride: "That proud man thought of nothing but the might of his own power...this humble soul is full of contrivance how to glorify God."

Henry notes that "gracious grateful souls never think they can do enough for God" and "cannot enjoy their own accommodations while they see the church of God in distress."

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On verse 2, Henry observes David's discomfort that he dwelt "in a house of cedar" while "the ark dwells within curtains," showing a heart grieved by the church's humble state.

Regarding God's response in verses 5-7, Henry notes God "regards not outward pomp in his service; his presence was as surely with his people when the ark was in a tent as when it was in a temple."

On the covenant promises (verses 12-16), Henry sees both immediate fulfillment in Solomon and ultimate fulfillment in Christ's eternal kingdom.

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to **Psalm 132:4-5** (David's unrest until finding a place for the ark), **1 Kings 8:18** (God's approval of David's heart intention), and **1 Chronicles 17** (parallel account).

He references **Daniel 4:29-30** to contrast David's humility with Nebuchadnezzar's pride, and **Amos 6:4,6** regarding those who enjoyed luxury while neglecting God's house.

The commentary frequently connects to Messianic psalms and emphasizes how this covenant forms the foundation for Christ's eternal kingship, making it both historically significant and prophetically crucial.
