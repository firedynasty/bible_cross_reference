# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Samuel 22: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry identifies this psalm as both personal thanksgiving and public worship, noting it later appears in the Psalter (Psalm 18). The central theological theme is **God's faithfulness to His covenant people** through deliverance. Henry emphasizes that "it has often been the lot of God's people to have many enemies," yet "those that trust God in the way of duty shall find him a present help to them in their greatest dangers."

The commentary highlights **God's sovereignty and incomparability**: "Who is God, save the Lord? All others that are adored as deities are counterfeits and pretenders." Henry presents God as the ultimate reality - "They are dead, but the Lord liveth" - contrasting the living God with false deities that "disappoint their worshippers when they most need them."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers:

**Immediate Gratitude**: "We ought to be speedy in our thankful returns to God: In the day that God delivered him he sang this song. While the mercy is fresh, and our devout affections are most excited by it, let the thank-offering be brought."

**Comprehensive Remembrance**: "We should in our praises, look as far back as we can, and not suffer time to wear out the sense of God's favours." Henry advocates for regular reflection on God's past mercies.

**Prayer in Distress**: David's example teaches that "when we are in distress, to cry unto God with importunity, as children in a fright cry to their parents."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich metaphorical interpretations of David's descriptions of God. On verses 2-3, he explains the "rock" imagery: God serves as both shelter ("the shadow of a great rock in a weary land") and foundation ("the rock on which I build my hope"). 

Regarding the dramatic theophany in verses 8-16, Henry notes God's "singular and extraordinary manner" of deliverance, interpreting the cosmic imagery as divine intervention.

Henry emphasizes verse 29's "Thou art my lamp, O LORD" as guidance "to show me my way, and thou wilt dispel my darkness."

## Cross-References to Scripture

Henry connects this psalm to broader biblical themes, referencing:
- **Psalm 34:19**: "though many are the troubles of the righteous, yet the Lord delivers them out of them all"
- **2 Timothy 4:18**: future deliverance in "that heavenly kingdom God will preserve all that are his"
- **Luke 1:69**: Christ as "the horn of salvation in the house of David"
- **1 Samuel 24:2**: David's literal hiding in rocks, contrasted with God as his ultimate refuge

Henry presents this psalm as both historical thanksgiving and prophetic worship, demonstrating how personal experience of God's faithfulness should lead to public proclamation of His glory.
