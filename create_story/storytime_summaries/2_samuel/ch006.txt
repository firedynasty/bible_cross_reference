# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Samuel Chapter 6

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents the ark as both "the token of God's presence and a type of Christ," making this narrative deeply instructive for Christian believers. He emphasizes God's jealousy for sacred things and the principle that **"a good intention will not justify a bad action."** The commentary stresses divine sovereignty, noting that God "can and will secure his ark, and needs not any man's sin to help him to do it."

The prolonged obscurity of the ark during Saul's reign parallels the church's wilderness experience, demonstrating that **"Perpetual visibility is no mark of the true church."** Henry argues that God remains graciously present with his people even when external tokens are absent.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons: First, believers should **"think and speak highly of God"** and **"honourably of holy ordinances."** He warns that familiarity with sacred things breeds contempt, as seen in Uzzah's presumption. 

The commentary emphasizes that **"public joy must always be as before the Lord, with an eye to him and terminating in him."** Henry advocates for public worship being "better the more public it is" while secret worship should be "better the more secret it is."

He applies Uzzah's judgment to contemporary believers, asking: **"If it was so great a crime for one to lay hold on the ark...what is it for those to lay claim to the privileges of the covenant that come not up to the terms of it?"**

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On verse 2, Henry provides extensive analysis of the ark's titles, emphasizing God as "Lord of hosts" who commands all creatures yet graciously dwells "between the cherubim." 

Regarding verses 3-5, he criticizes the cart method as improper since **"the Kohathites...had no wagons assigned them, because their service was to bear it upon their shoulders."** He notes that while Philistines could cart the ark safely, "if Israelites do so, they do it at their peril."

For Uzzah's death (verses 6-7), Henry explains this wasn't merely about touching the ark but represented cumulative errors and divine instruction to **"rejoice with trembling."**

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage extensively to other biblical texts. He references 1 Samuel 7:1-2 and 14:18 regarding the ark's history, and Revelation 12:14 concerning the church in wilderness. He suggests David composed Psalm 68 for this occasion, noting its opening words echo Moses' prayer at the ark's removal.

The commentary links to Leviticus 24:11,16, Numbers 4:6,15, and 7:9 regarding proper ark handling. Henry connects Uzzah's judgment to broader covenant principles, citing Psalm 50:16 and Hebrews 10:29 about covenant privileges and the blood of Christ.

Henry concludes by noting Obed-edom's blessing demonstrates that **"the word and ordinances of God are things that enrich those families that entertain them."**
