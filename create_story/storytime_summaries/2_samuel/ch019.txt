# Matthew Henry Commentary Summary: 2 Samuel Chapter 19

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a study in **providential restoration and wise governance**. The central theological theme is how God works through human relationships and political processes to restore rightful authority. Henry emphasizes that **David's return represents a model of princely wisdom** - choosing to "go back as a prince, with the consent and unanimous approbation of the people, and not as a conqueror forcing his way." This reflects divine kingship principles where true authority comes through the people's hearts rather than force.

The commentary also explores **divine justice tempered with mercy** through David's pardoning of Shimei and gracious treatment of others, demonstrating how godly leaders should balance justice with compassion.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several pointed applications for leadership and personal conduct:

**For Leaders:** He warns that "the more eyes we have upon us, and the greater our influence is, the more need we have to speak and act wisely and to govern our passions strictly." David's excessive mourning for Absalom becomes a cautionary tale about how leaders' emotional displays affect their followers.

**For Receiving Correction:** Henry emphasizes that "when we are convinced of a fault, we must amend, though we are told of it by our inferiors, and indecently, or in heat and passion." David's humble acceptance of Joab's harsh rebuke exemplifies proper response to correction.

**For Gratitude:** He stresses the importance of acknowledging good service: "Even those that may be commanded yet expect to be thanked when they do well, and ought to be."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On **David's excessive grief** (v. 4), Henry notes how David "proclaimed his passion, of which he ought to have been ashamed" and criticizes the prolonged mourning "for so bad a son as Absalom, like Jacob for so good a son as Joseph."

Regarding **Joab's rebuke** (v. 5-7), Henry observes that while Joab "speaks a great deal of reason," he did so "not with the respect and deference which he owed to his prince," noting that "a plain case may be fairly pleaded with those that are above us...but it must not be done with rudeness and insolence."

On **David's restoration strategy**, Henry praises David's wisdom in waiting for popular invitation rather than forcing his return, showing that "the prince that has wisdom and goodness enough to make himself his people's darling...looks greater" than one who rules by fear.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects David's position as rightful king to **Psalm 60:7** ("Judah was his lawgiver"), emphasizing Judah's special role in David's restoration. He also references **2 Samuel 3:36** regarding how "whatever [David] did used to please them," showing the people's historical trust in David's judgment.

The commentary implicitly connects David's merciful restoration to principles of divine kingship, though Henry focuses more on practical governance lessons than extensive scriptural cross-referencing in this particular chapter.

Henry concludes that this chapter demonstrates how "good services done to the public, though they may be forgotten for a while, yet will be remembered again when men come to their right minds," showing God's faithfulness in ultimately vindicating righteous leadership.
