# Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Samuel Chapter 15

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry presents Absalom's rebellion as divine judgment fulfilling God's word that He would "raise up evil against him out of his own house" (2 Sam. 12:11). The commentary emphasizes the irony that **"Absalom's name signifies 'the peace of his father,' yet he proves his greatest trouble"** - illustrating how earthly expectations often disappoint. Henry views this as consequence of David's failure to execute justice on Amnon, noting that **"had he done justice upon the murderer, he would have prevented the traitor."**

The text warns against unequal yoking, suggesting David's marriage to a heathen king's daughter contributed to Absalom's lack of concern for Israel's peace. Pride emerges as a central theme, with Henry observing: **"I have seen more young people ruined by pride than by any one lust whatsoever."**

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several pointed applications for parents and leaders:

**On Parental Indulgence:** Parents who "indulge a proud humour in their children" enable destructive patterns. David's tolerance of Absalom's ostentatious display demonstrates dangerous permissiveness.

**On Political Ambition:** Henry critiques those "commonly most ambitious of preferment that are least fit for it," while qualified individuals remain modest. True preparation involves humble service in subordinate roles, following the principle that "those that know how to obey well know how to rule."

**On Religious Pretense:** The commentary warns against using religion as a "stalking-horse" for selfish ambitions, noting how easily well-meaning people can be manipulated by false religious displays.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-6 (Absalom's Strategy):** Henry identifies Absalom's dual approach using "greatness and goodness" to win popular esteem. The chariots and fifty men represented forbidden royal magnificence, while his early rising and courteous behavior masked prideful ambition as **"abominable hypocrisy."**

**Verses 7-12 (The Hebron Plot):** Henry suspects Absalom's vow was fabricated, showing how easily parents believe the best about their children. The choice of Hebron was strategic, being where David began his reign and Absalom was born.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this narrative to broader biblical themes:
- **1 Samuel 8:11** - Samuel's warning about royal manner
- **Exodus 18:21** - Proper qualifications for judges  
- **2 Peter 2:10** - Warning against speaking evil of authorities
- **Colossians 2:23** - False humility in ambitious projects
- **Psalm 10:9-10** - Description of those who crouch to catch victims

The commentary demonstrates how Absalom's rebellion exemplifies timeless patterns of political manipulation, parental blindness, and the corruption of religious forms for worldly gain.
