# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Samuel 16 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a study in contrasts between worldly deception and divine providence. The central theological theme is God's sovereignty working through human wickedness for His purposes. He notes that "God makes use of bad men for good purposes to his people, and sends them meat by ravens." 

The commentary emphasizes divine chastisement and submission to God's will. Henry sees David's patient endurance of Shimei's curses as exemplifying proper response to divine discipline, arguing that afflictions often reveal true character - "The world's smiles are more dangerous than its frowns."

## Key Practical Applications

**On Discernment and Flattery**: Henry warns against the danger of flatterers like Ziba, advising that "Great men ought always to be jealous of flatterers, and remember that nature has given them two ears, that they may hear both sides." David's rash judgment regarding Mephibosheth serves as a cautionary tale about hasty decisions.

**On Patience Under Persecution**: David's response to Shimei models Christian forbearance. Rather than seeking revenge, believers should recognize God's hand in their trials: "Let him alone, and let him curse; for the LORD hath bidden him."

**On Self-Examination**: Henry draws a powerful contrast: "David bore Shimei's curses much better than he had borne Ziba's flatteries. By the latter he was brought to pass a wrong judgment on another, by the former to pass a right judgment on himself."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-4 (Ziba's Deception)**: Henry highlights how Ziba's strategic timing and generous gifts were calculated manipulation. The wine provision shows temperance - "for those that were faint, not for the king's own drinking" - contrasting proper leadership with Absalom's excess.

**Verses 5-8 (Shimei's Curses)**: Henry notes the irony that Shimei "kept at such a distance that the stones he threw could not reach David," showing "while his malice made him odious, the impotency of it made him ridiculous."

**Verses 10-12 (David's Response)**: The king's restraint demonstrates spiritual maturity: "It may be that the LORD will look on mine affliction, and that the LORD will requite me good for his cursing this day."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout:
- **Proverbs 18:16** on gifts making room for the giver (regarding Ziba's present)
- **Proverbs 31:6** on wine for the faint (David's proper use vs. Absalom's abuse)
- **Luke 9:55** comparing David's restraint to Christ rebuking disciples who sought vengeance
- **Psalm 69:26** on those who "talk to the grief of those whom God has wounded"
- **Exodus 22:28** on not cursing rulers (violated by Shimei)

The commentary consistently points to Christ as the ultimate example of patient suffering and proper response to persecution, making David's conduct a foreshadowing of Christian virtue under trial.
