# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Samuel 17: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **divine providence** as the central theme, noting that "the LORD had appointed to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel" (v. 14). He contrasts David's patient reliance on God's promises with Absalom's violent impatience, observing that David "waited patiently" because "his government was built upon a divine promise," while Absalom's "government is founded in iniquity, and therefore feels itself tottering."

Henry draws a striking **typological parallel** between Ahithophel's plot and Caiaphas's scheme against Christ: "Compare with this the plot of Caiaphas (that second Ahithophel) against the Son of David... 'Kill the heir, and the inheritance shall be ours.'" Both counsels were "turned into foolishness" by God's sovereignty.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry derives several practical lessons. From Ahithophel's urgency, he teaches spiritual diligence: "What our hand finds to do let us do quickly, and with all our might... particularly in our spiritual warfare. If Satan flee from us, let us follow our blow."

He warns against the corruption of **pride and ambition**, showing how "easy it is to betray proud men, by applauding them, and feeding their pride." Hushai succeeded by "humouring his revenge, as well as his pride."

Henry also notes the danger of despising enemies, referencing Israel's defeat at Ai as a cautionary example.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On **Ahithophel's counsel** (vv. 1-4), Henry observes the strategic wisdom of "Smite the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered," but notes Absalom's moral blindness in readily accepting "I will smite the king only"—showing he had lost all "sense of humor and virtue."

Regarding **Hushai's counter-strategy** (vv. 7-13), Henry highlights how Hushai masterfully appealed to Absalom's vanity by suggesting he personally lead the battle, while buying David precious time through delay.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to several biblical parallels:
- **Joshua 7:4** - Israel's defeat at Ai, warning against underestimating enemies
- **John 11:50** - Caiaphas's counsel about Jesus
- **Matthew 21:38** - The wicked tenants saying "Kill the heir"

He also references the historical parallel of David's earlier patience in Hebron versus Absalom's hasty violence.

The commentary concludes with hope, noting that while "everything has looked black upon poor David," now "the day of his deliverance begins to dawn," demonstrating God's faithfulness to His anointed despite apparent circumstances.
