## Matthew Henry Commentary on Judges Chapter 8: Summary

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry identifies several key theological themes in Judges 8. Central is the importance of humility and divine dependence in leadership. Gideon's peaceful resolution with the Ephraimites demonstrates that "humility of deportment is the best way to remove envy" and that true leaders credit God rather than themselves. Henry emphasizes that Gideon's subsequent failures—particularly the golden ephod that became "a great snare"—illustrate how even godly leaders can fall into spiritual compromise. The chapter also reveals the tragic cycle of Israel's forgetfulness: despite forty years of peace under Gideon, "both he and his God were soon forgotten by ungrateful Israel."

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living. He highlights the principle of spiritual perseverance, noting how Gideon's men were "faint, yet pursuing"—a model for believers who must continue "spiritual warfare...with what strength we have, though we have but little." He emphasizes that "a soft answer turned away their wrath," demonstrating conflict resolution through humility rather than pride. Henry also warns against spiritual compromise, showing how Gideon's later creation of an ephod illustrates how good leaders can make decisions that harm those they're meant to serve.

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich commentary on Gideon's diplomatic response to Ephraim's complaint (verses 2-3), calling it exemplary humility: "Is not the gleaning of the grapes of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abiezer?" He notes this demonstrates how "improving a victory is often more honourable...than the winning of it." Regarding the pursuit of the Midianite kings, Henry emphasizes Gideon's determination despite being "faint, yet pursuing," seeing this as a model of faithful perseverance under difficult circumstances.

### Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry connects this chapter to broader scriptural themes, referencing Proverbs 15:1 regarding soft answers, Isaiah 9:21 about tribal conflicts, and Ecclesiastes 4:4 concerning envy of righteous works. He links the "vintage" imagery to Revelation 14:18's description of divine judgment. The commentary also draws parallels between Gideon's humble leadership and broader biblical principles about pride, contention, and the importance of crediting God rather than human achievement in victory and success.
