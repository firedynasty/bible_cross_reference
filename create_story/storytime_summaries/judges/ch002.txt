# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Judges Chapter 2: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry identifies **covenant faithfulness versus apostasy** as the central theme. The chapter reveals God's unchanging covenant loyalty contrasted with Israel's repeated unfaithfulness. He emphasizes that "the covenant he entered into with them should never break, unless it broke on their side." 

The **cyclical nature of sin and redemption** dominates Henry's interpretation. He sees this chapter as outlining "not only the contents of this chapter, but of the whole book" - a pattern of adherence to God, revolt to idolatry, divine judgment, mercy through deliverers, and relapse into sin.

Henry also stresses the **progressive nature of sin's consequences**, noting how "God often makes men's sin their punishment" and that Israel "corrupted themselves more than their fathers."

## Key Practical Applications

**The danger of compromising with ungodly influences** receives strong emphasis. Henry warns that "those deceive themselves who expect advantage by friendship with those that are enemies to God." The Canaanites becoming "thorns in your sides" serves as a warning against tolerating sin.

**The importance of spiritual leadership and generational faithfulness** appears crucial. Henry notes how Israel served God "all the days of Joshua and the elders," but the next generation "knew not the LORD." This demonstrates how spiritual decline often occurs between generations.

**The necessity of genuine repentance beyond mere emotion** is highlighted. Though the people "wept" at Bochim, Henry observes: "Many are melted under the word that harden again before they are cast into a new mould... they wept, but we do not find that they reformed."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3**: Henry identifies the angel as likely "the Angel of the covenant... Christ himself," noting that only God could say "I made you to go up out of Egypt." The angel's coming from Gilgal reminds Israel of past covenant renewals.

**Verse 3**: Henry explains how tolerating Canaanites would "put a period to their victories," involve them in "continual troubles," and expose them to "constant temptation." This demonstrates how "their sin was made their punishment."

**Verses 11-13**: The phrase "served Baalim" represents complete apostasy - they "forsook the LORD God of their fathers" for "the gods of the people that were round about them."

**Verse 18**: God's compassion appears in how "it repented the LORD because of their groanings." Henry emphasizes divine mercy despite human unfaithfulness.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects the angel's message to **Isaiah 30:21** regarding hearing "a word behind them, saying, This is the way." He references **Micah 6:5** concerning Gilgal's covenant significance and **Revelation 3:2-3** about remembering what we have "received and heard."

The pattern of spiritual decline links to broader biblical warnings about compromise with ungodly influences, while God's raising up of judges demonstrates the consistent biblical theme of divine mercy providing deliverance despite human failure.
