# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Judges 6: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Judges 6 as a classic cycle of Israel's spiritual rebellion and God's redemptive response. The central theme is divine sovereignty in using even "despicable enemies" to discipline His people. He emphasizes that "the burnt child dreads the fire; yet this perverse unthinking people...upon a little respite of God's judgments return to it again." 

Henry highlights God's method of preparing His people for deliverance—sending a prophet before a deliverer to bring conviction of sin. He notes that God "sent a prophet to reprove them for sin, and to bring them to repentance" before raising up Gideon, demonstrating that spiritual preparation precedes physical deliverance.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several pointed applications about sin's consequences and God's mercy:

**On Israel's fearfulness:** "Sin dispirits men, and makes them sneak into dens and caves." He connects their cowardice to guilty consciences, showing how departure from God robs people of courage.

**On God's justice in their suffering:** Their neglect of proper worship led to economic devastation—"They had neglected to honour God with their substance in tithes and offerings...and now God justly sends an enemy to take it away."

**On divine patience:** Despite Israel's repeated apostasy, when they finally "cried unto the Lord," God responded with mercy, showing "how ready he is to forgive, how swift he is to show mercy."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich commentary on Gideon's calling, particularly noting the irony that Gideon was "threshing wheat by the winepress, to hide it from the Midianites" when called "thou mighty man of valour." He observes how God sees potential where humans see weakness.

The prophet's message (verses 8-10) receives detailed treatment, with Henry emphasizing how it reminded Israel of God's past faithfulness to highlight their present ingratitude. The charge "But ye have not obeyed my voice" is called "short, but very comprehensive."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout: Psalm 18:26 on God's response to the froward; Leviticus 26:21,24 on walking contrary to God; Deuteronomy 32:21 about provoking to jealousy with "those who were not a people"; and Hosea 2:8-9 regarding God's withdrawal of provision from unfaithful people.

He also references Job 33:23-24 about God sending messengers before granting recovery, and Psalm 80:19 about God causing His face to shine when people turn to Him.

The commentary masterfully demonstrates how this historical narrative illuminates timeless patterns of divine discipline, human weakness, and God's redemptive persistence in preparing unlikely instruments for His purposes.
