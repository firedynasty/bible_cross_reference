# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Judges 15: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Samson as a divinely appointed public figure whose extraordinary actions were "by the Spirit of the Lord coming upon him." He emphasizes that **"with God nothing is impossible"** when considering Samson's supernatural exploits. The commentary frames Samson not as seeking personal revenge, but as one who "looks upon himself as a public person" avenging "not his own, but Israel's quarrels." 

Henry sees divine justice operating through human conflicts, noting that **"the Lord is known by these judgments which he executes"** when enemies turn against each other. The principle that **"vengeance belongs"** to God is demonstrated through the Philistines' own brutal treatment of Samson's treacherous wife.

## Key Practical Applications

**On reconciliation:** Henry commends Samson's generous attempt to reconcile with his wife, teaching that **"when differences happen between near relations, let those be ever reckoned the wisest and the best that are most forward to forgive and forget injuries."**

**On consequences of treachery:** Samson's wife serves as a warning that **"the mischief we seek to escape by any unlawful practices we often pull upon our own heads."** Her betrayal to save herself ultimately resulted in the very destruction she feared.

**On doing one's duty:** Before taking action, we should fulfill our obligations toward peace, for **"when differences arise we ought to do our duty in order to the ending of them, and then, whatever the ill consequences may be, we shall be blameless."**

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3:** Henry notes Samson's noble gesture in bringing a reconciliation gift, despite being "the party offended and the superior relation."

**Verses 4-5:** The fox and firebrand strategy demonstrates how **"the church's adversaries, that are of different interests... have often united in a fire-brand"** to waste God's people.

**Verse 8:** The phrase "hip and thigh" suggests Samson **"trod them in his anger, and trampled them in his fury,"** indicating complete dominance.

**Verses 11-13:** Henry harshly criticizes the men of Judah as **"degenerate branches of that valiant tribe!"** for betraying their deliverer out of tribal jealousy.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects Samson's betrayal to **Christ as "that blessed Samson"** when "a whole band of men was sent to seize our Lord Jesus." He references **Leviticus 18:18** regarding taking a wife to her sister, **Psalm 58:10-11** on the righteous seeing vengeance, **Psalm 76:10** that "the wrath of man shall praise God," and **Isaiah 63:3** describing divine judgment as treading enemies in fury.

The commentary presents Samson's story as both historical account and typological foreshadowing, emphasizing divine sovereignty working through human agency while maintaining moral accountability for individual choices.
