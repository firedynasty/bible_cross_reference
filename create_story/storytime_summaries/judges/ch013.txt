## Matthew Henry Commentary on Judges 13 Summary

### Main Theological Themes

Henry presents Samson as a **typological figure of Christ**, emphasizing that "the great Redeemer did in a particular manner concern himself about this typical redeemer." Key themes include:

- **Divine sovereignty in judgment and deliverance**: Israel's oppression follows the familiar cycle of sin leading to subjugation, demonstrating that "if there had been no sin, there would have needed no Saviour; but sin was suffered to abound, that grace might much more abound."

- **God's providential timing**: Henry notes that "mercies long waited for often prove signal mercies," as seen in barren mothers bearing deliverers (Isaac, Joseph, Samuel, John Baptist).

- **Progressive redemption**: Samson would only "begin to deliver Israel," illustrating how "God chooses to carry on his work gradually and by several hands."

### Key Practical Applications

**For expectant mothers**: Henry emphasizes moral responsibility, stating "women with child ought conscientiously to avoid whatever they have reason to think will be any way prejudicial to the health or good constitution of the fruit of their body."

**For families**: He advocates spiritual communication between spouses: "Thus should yoke-fellows communicate to each other their experiences of communion with God...that they may be helpful to each other in the way that is called holy."

**For ministry**: Those called to serve must demonstrate exceptional devotion: "Those that would save others must by singular piety distinguish themselves."

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 5** - The Nazarite calling represents complete consecration: Henry emphasizes that Samson "must appear as one, more than any of them, consecrated to God."

**Verses 8-9** - Henry praises Manoah's faith, contrasting it favorably with Zechariah's doubt: "There was not found so great faith, no, not in Zechariah, a priest...as was in this honest Danite."

**Verse 18** - Henry identifies the angel as "the Word of the Lord, who was to be the Messiah, for his name is called Wonderful."

### Cross-References and Connections

Henry draws extensive biblical parallels:
- **Genesis 49:16** - Connects to Jacob's prophecy about Dan judging his people
- **Hebrews 11:32** - Cites Samson as "an eminent believer"
- **Isaiah 54:1** - Links barren mothers to God's mercy
- **Romans 9:4-5** - Connects the Jewish church's role in bearing the Messiah

Henry particularly emphasizes how Samson prefigures Christ as both Nazarite and deliverer, though noting the crucial difference: "Samson did only begin to deliver Israel...but our Lord Jesus is both Samson and David too, both the author and finisher of our faith."
