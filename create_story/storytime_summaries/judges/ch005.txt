# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Judges Chapter 5: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry views this triumphal song as divinely inspired praise that demonstrates **God's sovereign justice and faithfulness**. The central theological theme is that God deserves glory for both "the avenging of Israel" against oppressors and "the grace he gave to Israel's friends" who willingly served. Henry emphasizes that **God's unchanging nature** connects past deliverances (like Sinai) with present victories, declaring "he is the same yesterday, to-day, and for ever."

The commentary highlights **divine retribution and reward**: God judges Israel's enemies while blessing those who serve Him willingly. Henry notes how Israel's idolatry ("They chose new gods") brought their oppression, demonstrating the consequences of abandoning covenant faithfulness.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers:

**Immediate gratitude**: "When we have received mercy from God, we ought to be speedy in our returns of praise, while the impressions of the mercy are fresh."

**The power of sacred music**: Songs serve as "a very proper expedient for spreading the knowledge and perpetuating the remembrance of great events" - allowing those without books to learn God's works through song.

**Leadership by example**: Deborah and the governors who "offered themselves willingly" demonstrate how leaders should serve sacrificially. Henry observes, "Those are worthy of double honour that recede voluntarily from the demands of their honour to serve God and his church."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 4-5**: Henry interprets God's march from Seir as either His manifestation of power against enemies or the giving of the law at Sinai, emphasizing how "the earth trembled, and the heavens dropped" before divine majesty.

**Verses 6-8**: The description of Israel's distressed condition - no trade, travel, tillage, justice, or arms - illustrates how sin brings comprehensive social breakdown.

**Verse 7**: Henry notes Deborah's role as "a mother in Israel, a nursing mother," showing pastoral care that restored order and enabled people to return to normal life.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage extensively with other biblical texts:
- **Psalm 145:4** on declaring God's mighty acts across generations  
- **Psalm 2:10-11** calling kings to serve the Lord with fear
- **Habakkuk 3:3-4** describing God's appearances against enemies
- **Daniel 5:4** contrasting praise of false gods with worship of the true God
- **Joshua 24:21** recalling Israel's original choice to serve the Lord

The commentary emphasizes how this victory song fits within the broader biblical pattern of God's faithfulness to His covenant people throughout redemptive history.
