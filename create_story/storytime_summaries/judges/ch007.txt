# Matthew Henry Commentary on Judges 7: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **God's sovereignty and the exclusion of human boasting** as the central theme. God deliberately reduces Gideon's army from 32,000 to 300 men because "the people that are with thee are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hands, lest Israel vaunt themselves against me, saying, Mine own hand hath saved me." Henry notes this demonstrates that God "would hereby silence and exclude boasting" and ensure "My own hand hath saved me is a word that must never come out of the mouth of such as shall be saved."

The commentary highlights **divine strength through human weakness**, showing how "God often [brings] great things to pass by a few hands." Henry interprets this as God proving He doesn't need human instruments but graciously employs them.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**Faith must produce action**: "Faith in God's promises must not slacken, but rather quicken, our endeavours." Gideon's early rising shows that divine assurance should increase, not decrease, our diligence.

**Character matters in God's service**: The water test reveals God chooses those who are "hardy" and "hasty" - people who "could endure long fatigue" and were "zealously affected in a good thing." Henry emphasizes that "fearful faint-hearted people are not fit to be employed for God."

**Divine encouragement comes seasonably**: When Gideon was "greatly agitated and full of care," God provided comfort, showing "Divine consolations are given in to believers not only strongly but seasonably."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 2-8**: Henry explains the two-stage army reduction as purposeful divine strategy. First, the fearful were dismissed because "cowards would be as likely as any, after the victory, to take the honour of it from God." The water test then selected men of zealous character over mere capability.

**Verses 9-15**: The dream sequence demonstrates God providing "recruits for his faith, instead of recruits for his forces." Henry notes the enemy's interpretation of the barley cake dream shows even Midian recognized God's hand in Israel's coming victory.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this account to broader biblical patterns:
- **2 Samuel 5:24** - "When we are sure God goes before us, then we must bestir ourselves"
- **Deuteronomy 20:8** - The law requiring dismissal of the fearful
- **Numbers 26:30** - Historical context of Mount Gilead
- **Judges 4:14** - Comparison to Barak's smaller army against Sisera

Henry concludes that this "story shines very brightly in the book of the wars of the Lord," demonstrating God's power to accomplish His purposes through the most unlikely means while ensuring all glory remains with Him alone.
