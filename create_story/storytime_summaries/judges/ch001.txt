# Matthew Henry Commentary on Judges Chapter 1: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **divine sovereignty in warfare and conquest**, noting that "God himself, as he was their King, so he was the Lord of their hosts." The central theme is God's appointment of leadership and the necessity of divine blessing for success. He stresses that "His service will not avail unless God give the success; but God will not give the success unless he vigorously apply himself to the service."

The commentary highlights **divine justice through providential retribution**, particularly in Adoni-bezek's punishment, where Henry observes how "the righteous God sometimes, in his providence, makes the punishment to answer the sin, and observes an equality in his judgments."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons about **Christian cooperation and mutual aid**. Regarding Judah's alliance with Simeon, he notes: "the strongest should not despise but desire the assistance even of those that are weaker" and "Those that crave assistance must be ready to give assistance." He applies this principle broadly: "It becomes Israelites to help one another against Canaanites; and all Christians, even those of different tribes, should strengthen one another's hands against the common interests of Satan's kingdom."

The commentary emphasizes **faithful service according to one's abilities**, noting that "God appoints service according to the strength he has given. Those that are most able, from them most work is expected."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry explains God's choice of Judah to lead (v. 2) through multiple factors: Judah's numerical strength, dignity as the praised tribe, priority in land allocation, and prophetic significance as "the tribe out of which our Lord was to spring." He connects this to Christ as "the Lion of the tribe of Judah" who "engaged the powers of darkness first."

Regarding Adoni-bezek's confession (v. 7), Henry highlights "the power of conscience, when God by his judgments awakens it," noting how the king "subscribes to the justice of God" in declaring "As I have done, so God has requited me."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects the narrative to broader biblical themes, referencing **1 Samuel 11:8** regarding Bezek, **Psalm 49:11** about naming lands after oneself, and **Isaiah 33:1** and **James 2:13** concerning divine retribution. He links Judah's leadership role to Christ's victory over "powers of darkness," drawing from Revelation's imagery of conquest.

The commentary presents this chapter as illustrating both faithful cooperation in God's work and the consequences of incomplete obedience, with Judah and Simeon as positive examples contrasted with other tribes' failures.
