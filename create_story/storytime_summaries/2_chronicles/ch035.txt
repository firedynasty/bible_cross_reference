# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Chronicles 35 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents the chapter as a study in contrasts between faithful religious observance and tragic human folly. He emphasizes that **"the passover was the chief"** among Jewish festivals, noting its profound significance from Israel's exodus to Christ's betrayal. The commentary treats Josiah's passover as both a high point of ceremonial faithfulness and a sobering reminder that external religious observance cannot substitute for spiritual discernment.

Henry draws a crucial connection between the Old Testament passover and the Lord's Supper, arguing that **"Religion cannot flourish where that passover is either wholly neglected or not duly observed."** This reflects his view that sacramental ordinances serve as both indicators and means of spiritual health in churches and individuals.

## Key Practical Applications

**For Ministers and Leaders:** Henry emphasizes that **"Ministers' work must begin at home"** - clergy must "sanctify themselves in the first place" before preparing others. He advocates for ministers helping one another **"as brethren, and fellow-servants of the same Master."** Magistrates should encourage ministers in their duties, following Josiah's example of speaking **"comfortably to them"** rather than merely commanding.

**For Generous Giving:** Josiah's provision of 30,000 lambs and 3,000 bulls demonstrates that **"Those who are serious in religion should...make it as cheap and easy to them as may be"** when encouraging others in righteousness. Henry notes how **"Providence sometimes raises up friends to bear them out in it"** for those genuinely seeking to do their duty.

**For Spiritual Discernment:** The commentary's most sobering application concerns Josiah's fatal battle. Henry warns against acting on presumption rather than divine guidance, showing how even the most faithful can fall through lack of spiritual discernment.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that unlike Hezekiah's passover, which displayed great popular enthusiasm, this celebration was **"more in compliance with the king"** than from genuine spiritual hunger among the people. They had **"some pride...in this form of godliness, but little pleasure in the power of it."**

Regarding the ark's restoration (v.3), Henry suggests it had been displaced either by wicked kings making **"room for their idols in the most holy place"** or during temple repairs, emphasizing the importance of proper order in worship.

The commentary praises the exceptional nature of this passover, noting that **"there was none like it since Samuel's time"** because of its strict adherence to the law, surpassing even Hezekiah's celebration in regulatory precision.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects the chapter to multiple biblical passages: Colossians 4:17 regarding ministerial faithfulness, 2 Corinthians 4:5 on serving both Christ and His church, and Titus 1:1 on being servants of God. He references 2 Kings 23:21 for the parallel account and draws connections to Samuel's era for historical comparison.

The commentary also implicitly references the New Testament's Lord's Supper as the Christian fulfillment of Passover symbolism, suggesting continuity between Old and New Testament sacramental worship.

Henry concludes with the tragedy that **"after all this"** faithful service, Josiah fell through his own rashness, demonstrating that past faithfulness cannot guarantee future protection apart from continued dependence on God's guidance.
