# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 14: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Asa's reign as a model of faithful kingship centered on **covenant relationship and divine dependence**. The primary theological theme is that **seeking God brings rest and prosperity**, while reformation of worship leads to national blessing. Henry emphasizes God's sovereignty in both peace and warfare, noting that "it is nothing with thee to help, whether with many, or with them that have no power."

The commentary highlights the **connection between personal piety and public policy**, showing how Asa's spiritual reformation (removing idols, commanding worship of "the LORD God of their fathers") directly resulted in political tranquility. Henry sees this as demonstrating that "the smiles of Providence should be taken notice of for our encouragement in that which is good."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for rulers and believers:

**For Leadership**: Those in authority should "use it vigorously for the suppression of profaneness and the reformation of manners" and will find that "Vice is a sneaking thing, and virtue has reason enough on its side." 

**For Times of Peace**: "Times of rest from war should be employed in work" and "In times of peace we must be getting ready for trouble." Henry advocates using peaceful periods for both building up and preparation, summarizing: "When the sword is sheathed take up the trowel."

**For Prayer**: Asa's prayer demonstrates proper dependence—acknowledging God's power while pleading covenant relationship and divine honor rather than trusting human strength.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry particularly emphasizes **verse 2** (Asa "did that which was good and right in the eyes of the LORD") as showing three principles: aiming to please God rather than man, recognizing God's constant observation, and receiving divine approval.

On **verses 6-7**, he notes how Asa wisely used rest as God's gift while preparing for future challenges, building fortifications "while the land is yet before us."

**Asa's prayer (verse 11)** receives extensive analysis, with Henry praising its brevity yet depth, especially the phrase "let not man prevail against thee," showing how Asa connected his cause with God's honor.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves in numerous scriptural connections: **Psalm 107:42** regarding virtue making "iniquity stop her mouth," **Job 34:29** on God's gift of quietness, **John 14:27** on Christ's peace, **Haggai 2:18-19** and **Malachi 3:10** on reformation bringing blessing, **1 Samuel 14:6** on God helping few or many, and **Acts 9:31** showing how "when the churches had rest they were built up."

The commentary presents Asa as exemplifying the principle that faithful seeking of God in reformation and worship leads to both temporal blessing and divine assistance in crisis, making his reign a paradigm for godly governance.
