# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles Chapter 2

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes that **true wisdom is for action, not mere speculation**. Solomon's wisdom was given "not merely for speculation, to entertain himself...but for action; and therefore to action he immediately applies himself." The primary theological theme centers on **God's transcendence and immanence** - while the temple must be great because "great is our God above all gods," Solomon humbly acknowledges that "the heaven of heavens cannot contain him."

Henry sees **divine humility** as central, noting Solomon's words: "Who am I that I should build him a house?" This reflects the proper attitude of "utter insufficiency" believers should have when undertaking work for God. The commentary also highlights **God's covenant faithfulness** through Huram's recognition that "Because the LORD hath loved his people, he hath made thee king over them."

## Key Practical Applications

**Priority in service**: Solomon builds "first a temple and then a palace" - demonstrating that God should be served first. Henry notes, "It is fit that he who is the first should be served."

**Proper treatment of workers**: Henry emphasizes Solomon's generous provision for laborers, stating that employers should ensure workers are "not only well paid, but well provided for with sufficient of that which is wholesome and fit for them." This reflects the principle: "Let the rich masters do for their poor workmen as they would be done by if the tables were turned."

**International cooperation**: Henry sees value in maintaining relationships with "fathers' friends" and shows how **faithful witness** can influence foreign leaders, as demonstrated by Huram's blessing of Israel's God.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1**: Henry emphasizes Solomon's **public-minded leadership** - building not just for personal convenience but "for the honour of it among its neighbours" and the kingdom's welfare.

**Verses 4-6**: The description of temple services shows how "mysteries of the true religion...coveted not concealment," contrasting with pagan religions. Solomon's humility despite his royal status demonstrates proper perspective on divine service.

**Verses 11-12**: Huram's response reveals how Israel's faithfulness made neighboring nations "willing to be instructed by them in the true religion," showing the evangelistic fruit of covenant obedience.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry connects this passage to **Acts 17:24** regarding God not dwelling in temples made with hands, and **1 Kings 7:14** concerning the craftsman's tribal heritage. He references **1 Chronicles 22:15** about David's preparation of skilled workers.

The commentary draws parallels to **Ezra 6** and the second temple, suggesting the mixed heritage of the chief craftsman (Jewish mother, Gentile father) foreshadows "uniting Jew and Gentile in the gospel temple." Henry also connects to **2 Samuel 12:24** regarding God's love for Solomon, while noting the focus remains on God's love for His people rather than personal favoritism.

The extensive workforce of 150,000 demonstrates both Canaan's fruitfulness and the temple's magnitude, with Henry suggesting this prefigures the gospel's inclusive reach.
