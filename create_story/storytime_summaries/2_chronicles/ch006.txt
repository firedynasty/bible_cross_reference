# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 6: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry identifies God's transcendent majesty as a central theme, emphasizing the divine paradox that while "the heaven of heavens cannot contain" God, He graciously chooses to dwell among His people. The commentary highlights God's faithfulness to His covenant promises, noting how "he will both keep covenant with them and show mercy to them" for those who "walk before him with all their hearts."

The temple serves as a type of Christ, functioning as "a figure of Christ, the great Mediator, through whom we are to offer all our prayers." Henry emphasizes God's omniscience, stating that "he, and he only, knows the hearts of the children of men," and addresses human sinfulness with the sobering declaration that "there is no man who sinneth not."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers. He emphasizes the importance of right intentions in religious acts, warning against Solomon building "in the pride of his heart" rather than for God's glory. He encourages believers to pursue "good designs which those that went before them have laid," recognizing that "temple-work is often thus done; one sows and another reaps."

The commentary stresses that affliction drives people to God, observing that "trouble will drive those to God who have said to him, Depart." Henry also emphasizes the universal scope of God's mercy, noting Solomon's prayer for strangers who would come "from afar to worship the God of Israel."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On verses 1-11, Henry highlights Solomon's four motivations: God's glory, compliance with God's choice of Jerusalem, pursuing David's intentions, and fulfilling God's promise. He notes God's approval of David's heart intention: "Thou didst well that it was in thy heart."

Regarding Solomon's posture in verse 13, Henry emphasizes the significance of the bronze platform, showing proper reverence. For the prayer section (verses 12-42), he outlines various scenarios Solomon anticipated, demonstrating wisdom in expecting both prosperity and adversity.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to 1 Kings 8:12-53, noting "very little variation" between the accounts. He references John 4:37-38 regarding collaborative ministry: "one sows and another reaps." The commentary cites Ecclesiastes 7:20 to support the universal nature of sin, and alludes to Psalm 18:11 regarding God making "darkness his pavilion."

Henry's treatment reveals deep theological reflection on God's transcendence and immanence, practical wisdom for religious service, and prophetic insight into the temple's typological significance pointing toward Christ as the ultimate mediator between God and humanity.
