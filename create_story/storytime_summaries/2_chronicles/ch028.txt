# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 28: Summary

## Main Theological Themes

Matthew Henry interprets Ahaz's reign as a stark illustration of divine justice and the consequences of apostasy. The central theological theme is that **God uses earthly instruments to chastise unfaithful rulers and nations**. Henry emphasizes that "it is just with God to make those our plagues whom we make our patterns or make ourselves partners with in sin." He demonstrates how God's sovereignty operates even through pagan nations - the Syrians and Israelites became "the rod of his indignation" against Judah's wickedness.

Henry also highlights God's mercy toward the oppressed, showing how divine providence moved the hearts of Israel's leaders to release their captives, demonstrating that "he regarded the affliction of this poor people."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several pointed applications:

**On Leadership:** Even good upbringing cannot guarantee godly character, as "parents cannot give grace to their children." Leaders bear enormous responsibility, as the people "suffered by these judgments" due to Ahaz's wickedness.

**On Pride and Victory:** Military success should not breed arrogance. Henry warns that "those understand not what victory is who think it gives them authority to do what they will." He emphasizes that "it ill becomes sinners to be proud" and "it ill becomes sinners to be cruel."

**On Mercy:** Those hoping to "find mercy with God must learn...with what tenderness to carry themselves towards those that lie at their mercy."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides vivid characterizations throughout. He describes Ahaz as "perfectly divested of all natural affection as well as religion" for sacrificing his children to Moloch. Regarding the massive casualties (120,000 in one day), Henry observes they "could not have been cut off in one day if they had not been strangely dispirited both by the consciousness of their own guilt and by the righteous hand of God upon them."

He praises the Israeli soldiers who released their captives, noting they "showed more truly heroic bravery than they did in taking them. It is a great honour for any man to yield to the authority of reason and religion against his interest."

## Cross-References

Henry weaves in multiple scriptural connections, including Romans 11:20-21 regarding pride after victory, Deuteronomy 9:5 on divine justice rather than human righteousness, and Psalm 106:44-46 concerning God's compassion for captives. These references reinforce his theological framework of divine sovereignty and mercy operating through human affairs.
