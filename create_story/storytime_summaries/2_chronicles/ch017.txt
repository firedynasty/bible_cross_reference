# Matthew Henry Commentary Summary: 2 Chronicles 17

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Jehoshaphat as exemplifying how **genuine piety leads to divine blessing and prosperity**. The central theme is that "his prosperity was the reward of his piety and his piety the brightest grace and ornament of his prosperity." 

Henry emphasizes the importance of **selective imitation of spiritual leaders**, noting that Jehoshaphat "walked in the first ways of his father David" - following David's best examples while avoiding his later failures. He explains this principle: "in every copy we propose to write after, as we must single out that only which is good, so that chiefly which is best."

The commentary highlights **the priority of spiritual education** in godly leadership, showing how Jehoshaphat combined political authority with religious instruction for lasting reform.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**For Leaders:** Jehoshaphat demonstrates that good governance includes both removing false teaching and providing sound instruction. His use of princes, priests, and Levites working together shows that "an abundance of good may be done when Moses and Aaron thus go hand in hand."

**For Spiritual Growth:** Henry notes that Jehoshaphat's "heart was lifted up in the ways of the Lord" - meaning he was "lively and affectionate in his religion, fervent in spirit." This describes wholehearted, joyful commitment rather than mere duty.

**For Ministry:** The emphasis on teachers carrying "the book of the law of the Lord with them" leads Henry to observe: "Ministers, when they go to teach the people, should have their Bibles with them."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2:** Henry commends Jehoshaphat's wisdom in strengthening defenses through "fair and regular methods" rather than ungodly alliances, showing he "trusted God."

**Verses 3-4:** The distinction between David's "first ways" and later ways teaches careful discipleship - "it is good to be cautious in following the best men, lest we step aside after them."

**Verse 6:** Henry interprets "his heart was lifted up in the ways of the Lord" as describing someone who "brought his heart to his work" with courage, joy, and spiritual enlargement.

**Verses 7-9:** Henry praises the systematic teaching program, noting how civil and religious authorities collaborated to ensure comprehensive instruction throughout the kingdom.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to multiple biblical texts:
- **1 Kings 15:3, 11** and **2 Kings 14:3, 16:2, 18:3** regarding David as the standard for royal evaluation
- **1 Corinthians 11:1** on selective imitation: "Follow me, as I follow Christ, and not otherwise"
- **Jeremiah 6:16** about seeking "ancient ways"
- **Psalm 119:32** on enlarged hearts in doing good
- **Genesis 29:1** and **35:5** showing God's protection during spiritual reformation
- **Deuteronomy 33:10** establishing teaching as the priests' proper office

Henry concludes by noting the remarkable outcome: God so influenced neighboring nations that "the fear of the Lord fell upon them," bringing peace and tribute rather than warfare - demonstrating how faithful leadership creates stability and respect among surrounding peoples.
