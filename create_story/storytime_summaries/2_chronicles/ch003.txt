# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 3: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes the **divine sovereignty in temple construction**, noting that Solomon "was neither at liberty to choose nor at a loss to fix the place" since God had predetermined the location. The temple serves as a **powerful type of Christ and the New Jerusalem**. Henry connects Mount Moriah to Abraham's near-sacrifice of Isaac, explaining that "That was typical of Christ's sacrifice of himself; therefore fitly was the temple, which was likewise a type of him, built there."

The lavish gold ornamentation points beyond material splendor to spiritual reality. Henry notes the temple "was set with precious stones, because it was a type of the new Jerusalem" described in Revelation 21, emphasizing how earthly worship prefigures heavenly realities.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers:

**Worship requires reverence**: The presence of cherubim should remind us "that we are worshipping him before whom the angels cover their faces" and "help to inspire us with reverence in all our approaches to God."

**Unity of worship with angels**: "We must not worship angels, but we must worship with angels; for we have come into communion with them, and must do the will of God as the angels do it."

**Spiritual elevation in devotion**: The ascending cherubim on the veil remind "worshippers to lift up their hearts, and to soar upwards in their devotions."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich detail on the temple's location, noting it was built "where God has met with me it is to hoped that he will still manifest himself." The timing in Solomon's fourth year wasn't delay but necessary preparation time.

Regarding the temple's dimensions, Henry emphasizes divine permanence: "the dimensions were given by divine wisdom, and what God does shall be for ever; nothing can be put to it, or taken from it."

The cherubim's positioning is significant - "their faces inward toward the ark...as themselves attendants on the invisible God" rather than objects of worship.

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry weaves extensive scriptural connections throughout: Genesis 22:2 (Abraham's sacrifice), 1 Chronicles 21:18-26 (David's encounter with God), Psalms 122:4-5 (Jerusalem as testimony place), Ecclesiastes 3:14 (God's eternal works), Isaiah 6:2 (seraphim covering faces), 1 Corinthians 11:10 (angels and worship), Hebrews 12:22 (communion with angels), and Revelation 21:18-21 (New Jerusalem's precious stones).

The commentary masterfully demonstrates how Old Testament temple worship anticipates New Testament spiritual realities, particularly through Christ's sacrificial work that rent the temple veil, granting believers direct access to God's presence.
