# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 16: Summary

## Main Theological Themes

Matthew Henry emphasizes the danger of **misplaced trust** as the central theme. Asa's gravest error was "relying on the king of Syria and not on the Lord his God." Henry argues that God is "much displeased when he is distrusted and when an arm of flesh is relied on more than his power and goodness." 

The commentary highlights God's **providential oversight**, noting that "the eyes of the Lord run to and fro through the earth" to help those "whose heart is perfect toward him." Henry explains this means God governs with infinite wisdom, acts for his people's good, and protects the upright—making human alliances unnecessary when walking in obedience.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several pointed lessons about **spiritual decline in good people**. Using Asa as an example, he warns: "let him that thinks he stands take heed lest he fall," showing how even godly leaders can compromise when they stop trusting God completely.

On **receiving correction**, Henry condemns Asa's rage against the prophet, asking: "Is this he whose heart was perfect with the Lord all his days?" He warns that "those that idolize their own conduct cannot bear contradiction."

Regarding **using earthly means**, Henry distinguishes between proper use and sinful trust: "His making use of physicians was his duty; but trusting to them, and expecting that from them which was to be had from God only, were his sin and folly."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry severely critiques Asa's alliance (vv. 1-6), calling it wrong to "plunder the temple to serve his carnal politics" and noting Asa became responsible for "all the mischief that the army of Benhadad did unjustly."

On verse 9's famous passage about God's eyes, Henry explains God's providence is "quick-sighted," "intent," and "reaches far—through the whole earth."

Regarding Asa's imprisonment of the prophet (v. 10), Henry observes the spiritual progression: persecution of God's messenger led to oppressing his own people, noting "Most persecutors have been tyrants."

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry connects Asa's victory over the Ethiopians (referencing ch. 14:11) to demonstrate his inconsistency in trusting God. He references 1 Kings passages for parallel accounts and cites Ephesians 1:22 regarding Christ's headship over all things for the church's benefit.

The commentary concludes graciously, referencing Ecclesiastes' teaching that "There is not a just man that doeth good and sinneth not," while encouraging that "The memory of the just is blessed"—suggesting we should remember leaders' faithful service despite their failures.
