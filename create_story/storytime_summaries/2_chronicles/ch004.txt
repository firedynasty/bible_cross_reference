# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Chronicles 4: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry interprets the temple furniture as rich typology pointing to gospel truths, with "the substance is Christ." He emphasizes the progression from brass (outdoor court) to gold (inner sanctuary), symbolizing increasing purity as one draws nearer to God: "The nearer we come to God the purer we must be, the purer we shall be."

The molten sea represents both gospel privilege (cleansing through Christ's blood) and gospel duty (personal sanctification). Henry sees the expansion from Moses' single candlestick to ten as signifying the growth of scripture and the multiplication of churches in the gospel age.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**Proportional Giving**: Since God enlarged Israel's borders, "it was therefore fit that they should enlarge his altars. Our returns should bear some proportion to our receivings."

**Preparation for Worship**: Just as priests and sacrifices required washing, believers must "purify ourselves in preparation for our religious performances" and cleanse "vain thoughts and corrupt aims which cleave to our performances."

**Devotional Focus**: In worship, "we must keep the eye of faith fixed upon Christ, the great propitiation," allowing the sacrificial imagery to point hearts toward the ultimate sacrifice.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**The Brazen Altar (v.1)**: Henry notes this altar was four times larger than Moses' tabernacle altar, reflecting Israel's growth in numbers, wealth, and expected devotion. Its ten-cubit height allowed worshippers to witness sacrifices, helping "their eye affect their heart with sorrow for sin."

**The Molten Sea (vv.2-6)**: Positioned "like the font at the church door," it signifies both our access to cleansing through Christ's blood and our duty of repentance, referencing John 13:10 - "he that is washed still needs to wash his feet."

**Golden Furnishings (vv.7-8)**: The ten candlesticks represent expanding biblical revelation and multiplied churches, while the ten tables with show-bread demonstrate God's abundant provision: "In my father's house there is bread enough for the whole family."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects the temple imagery to broader biblical themes: 1 John 1:7 (cleansing blood), Hebrews 9:14 (purified consciences), James 4:8 (drawing near to God), Revelation 1:20 (churches as candlesticks), and Psalm 107:16 (gates of brass). He particularly emphasizes how Old Testament shadows find their substance in Christ's atoning work and ongoing intercession.

The commentary concludes by praising both Huram's faithful craftsmanship and Solomon's generous abundance in temple service, modeling dedication and liberality in God's work.
