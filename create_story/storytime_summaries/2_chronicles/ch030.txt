# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Chronicles 30 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Hezekiah's Passover as a powerful example of **spiritual revival and covenant renewal**. The central theological theme is God's faithfulness to receive repentant hearts despite ceremonial imperfections. Henry emphasizes that **"the substance should not be lost upon a nicety about the time"** - demonstrating God's grace in prioritizing sincere worship over strict ritual compliance.

The commentary highlights **God's sovereignty in revival**, noting that when Judah responded with one heart, "the hand of God gave them that one heart; for it is in the day of power that Christ's subjects are made willing." Henry sees this as a principle that "it is God that works both to will and to do."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**Urgency in spiritual opportunities**: He warns that "delays are dangerous" and advocates "striking while the iron is hot" when people show spiritual receptiveness, as "by that time twelve-month the good affections of the people would cool."

**Inclusive evangelism**: Hezekiah's invitation to the northern tribes demonstrates "rendering good for evil" and prioritizing spiritual unity over political divisions. Henry notes Hezekiah essentially said, "Let them take whom they will for their king, so they will but take him for their God."

**Humble submission over pride**: The key requirement is to "yield yourselves unto the Lord" and overcome being "stiff-necked as your fathers were." Henry emphasizes that "pride keeps men from yielding themselves to the Lord; when that is brought down, the work is done."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On **verses 8-9**, Henry explains "yield yourselves" as "give the hand to the Lord" - consent to covenant relationship like confirming a bargain. This involves surrendering the will that has "a spirit of contradiction" to be "melted into the will of God."

Regarding **verse 10-11**, Henry observes how the same message produced opposite responses: "though to some it was a savour of death unto death, was to others a savour of life unto life." Even in rejection, God preserved "a remnant."

For **verses 18-20**, Henry emphasizes God's acceptance of sincere hearts over ceremonial purity, showing divine grace toward those who "prepareth his heart to seek God...though he be not cleansed according to the purification of the sanctuary."

## Cross-References

Henry connects this passage to **Numbers 9:11** (the provision for delayed Passover), **Isaiah 44:5** (subscribing with the hand to God), and **2 Kings 17:2** (regarding King Hoshea's relative tolerance). He also references **2 Corinthians 2:16** regarding the dual response to the gospel message.

The commentary presents this Passover as both historical revival and prophetic picture of how God receives sincere worship despite human imperfections, emphasizing grace over rigid ceremonialism while maintaining the importance of genuine heart preparation.
