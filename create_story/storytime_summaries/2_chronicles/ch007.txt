## Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles Chapter 7

### Main Theological Themes

Matthew Henry identifies God's divine approval and covenant faithfulness as the central themes. The **fire from heaven** that consumed the sacrifices demonstrates God's acceptance, connecting this moment to other biblical instances (Moses, Gideon, David, Elijah). Henry emphasizes that "our God is a consuming fire" - both "glorious in himself" and "gracious to Israel," as the fire consumed the sacrifice rather than the people, signifying God's mercy.

The **glory of the Lord filling the temple** represents God's manifest presence, with Henry noting that "the heart that is thus filled with a holy awe and reverence of the divine glory...is thereby owned as a living temple."

### Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from Israel's response to God's acceptance:

**Corporate Worship**: The people's immediate worship response teaches that "tokens of God's favour to us should enlarge our hearts in his service." Their praise - "He is good, for his mercy endureth for ever" - demonstrates "a song never out of season."

**Leadership by Example**: Solomon's sacrificial leadership stirred the people, showing that "good work is then likely to go on when the leaders of a people lead in it."

**Covenant Conditions**: Henry emphasizes the conditional nature of God's promises, particularly in verses 13-14 regarding national repentance: "They must humble themselves...pray...seek his face...and turn from their wicked ways."

### Key Verse-by-Verse Insights

On **verse 1** (fire from heaven), Henry applies this christologically to Christ's suffering: "His death was our life...That sacrifice was consumed that we might escape." He also sees it representing the Spirit's sanctification, "burning up our lusts and corruptions."

Regarding **verses 13-15** (God's covenant response), Henry outlines the progression: national judgments require "national repentance, prayer, and reformation," leading to God's promise that "pardoning mercy makes way for healing mercy."

On **verses 19-22** (warnings of judgment), Henry soberly notes God's knowledge of their "proneness to backslide" and warns that even this magnificent temple would become "an astonishment" if they forsook God.

### Cross-References

Henry extensively connects this passage to other Scripture, referencing Leviticus 9:24, Judges 6:21, 1 Chronicles 21:26, and 1 Kings 18:38 for divine fire acceptance. He cites Luke 24:32 ("Did not our hearts burn within us?") for spiritual application, Isaiah 56:7 regarding the temple as "a house of prayer for all people," and Psalm 103:3 and Matthew 9:2 connecting forgiveness with healing.

The commentary demonstrates Henry's characteristic blend of theological depth with practical application, emphasizing both God's sovereignty and human responsibility in covenant relationship.
