# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Chronicles 5 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**God's Dwelling with His People**: Henry emphasizes the profound spiritual truth that when humans open their hearts to God, "he will come in" (Rev. 3:20). The ark represents God's presence, which remains constant whether in "little assemblies" or large ones, in the church's "poor condition" or "prosperous estate."

**The Ark as Type of Christ**: Henry identifies the ark as "a type of Christ, and, as such, a token of the presence of God." He connects this to Christ's promise: "Lo, I am with you always, even unto the end of the world," noting that "the temple itself, if Christ leave it, is a desolate place" (Matt. 23:38).

**Transition from Old to New Covenant**: The priests being unable to minister due to God's glory foreshadows that "the Levitical priesthood should cease" when the Messiah comes, "in whom the fulness of the godhead should dwell bodily."

## Key Practical Applications

**Faithfulness to Parents' Spiritual Legacy**: Henry commends Solomon's dedication of David's gifts, noting that "children that would inherit their godly parents' blessing must religiously pursue their pious intentions and not defeat them."

**Proper Heart Preparation**: Just as priests "must come out, that the rightful owner may come in," believers must "leave room for God" in their hearts, letting "every thing else give way."

**Corporate Worship and Unity**: God's glory appeared when the musicians were "unanimous, when they were as one, to make one sound." Henry emphasizes that "where unity is the Lord commands the blessing."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-10**: Henry distinguishes between dedicated things (which "added to the wealth" but "could not add to the holiness") and the ark itself, noting that while other vessels were made new, "the ark, with the mercy-seat and the cherubim, was the same."

**Verse 13**: The timing of God's manifestation is crucial—"it was not when they offered sacrifices, but when they sang the praises of God, that God gave them this token of his favour; for the sacrifice of praise pleaseth the Lord better."

**Verse 14**: The priests' inability to minister becomes prophetic: when Christ comes to his temple "like a refiner's fire, who may abide the day of his coming?"

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry weaves extensive scriptural connections throughout: Revelation 3:20 (God entering open hearts), Matthew 23:17 (temple sanctifying gold), 2 Corinthians 3:8-10 (gospel glory exceeding temple glory), Psalm 69:31 (praise surpassing sacrifice), Acts 2:1-4 (unity bringing God's presence), and Malachi 3:1-2 (Christ's coming to his temple).

He particularly emphasizes the phrase "his mercy endureth for ever," noting it appears "twenty-six times in one psalm" and represents how "God's goodness is his glory, and he is pleased when we give him the glory of it."
