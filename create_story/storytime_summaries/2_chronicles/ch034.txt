# Matthew Henry Commentary Summary: 2 Chronicles 34

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Josiah's reign as God's final gracious attempt to reform Judah before judgment. He emphasizes that "by his pious endeavours for reformation God tried them yet once more" - if the people had properly responded to "the day of their visitation," their ruin could have been prevented. 

The discovery of the Law represents divine providence at work. Henry sees this as God's merciful revelation of His will at a crucial moment, noting how Josiah's "tender heart" exemplified the proper response to Scripture. The theological principle emerges that individual righteousness, while unable to prevent national judgment, can secure personal deliverance.

## Key Practical Applications

**Early Spiritual Commitment**: Henry emphasizes that Josiah "began to seek after God" at sixteen, writing: "It is the duty and interest of young people...to begin to seek God; for those that seek him early shall find him."

**Faithful Work as Worship**: The temple restoration demonstrates that "workmen are not completely faithful if they are not both careful and diligent." Henry notes how God distributes various gifts - some as "bearers of burdens," others as overseers with musical skills, teaching that "let each esteem and serve the other in love."

**Scripture's Transformative Power**: Henry urges readers to "affect our hearts" with God's word, noting that though Scripture truths may be familiar, "they are not the less weighty...for their being well known. Rend the heart therefore, not the garments."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that Josiah's reforms began gradually - personal seeking at age 16, active purging at age 20, complete reformation by age 26. This shows how spiritual maturity develops progressively.

Regarding the temple repair, Henry draws a parallel: "Thus we must do by the spiritual temple of the heart, get it cleansed from the pollutions of sin, and then renewed, so as to be transformed into the image of God."

On finding the Law, Henry marvels at God's providence in blessing our age: "Bibles are jewels, but, thanks be to God, they are not rarities."

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry frequently references 2 Kings 22-23 for parallel accounts. He connects Josiah's response to Acts 2:37 ("Men and brethren, what shall we do?") and Acts 16:30 regarding proper inquiry under conviction. 

He links the promise of peaceful death to Noah's ark during the flood, showing how death becomes refuge "when a deluge is coming." The commentary also references Isaiah 21:12's call to spiritual inquiry.

Henry concludes by warning that nations forsaking God "kindle a fire which shall not be quenched," while encouraging that humble seeking, like Josiah's, delivers individual souls even when national judgment cannot be averted.
