# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 31: Summary

## Main Theological Themes

Henry presents this chapter as the culmination of Hezekiah's reformation, emphasizing **genuine spiritual revival that produces lasting change**. The central theme is that true communion with God must result in holy living and zealous opposition to sin. Henry notes that "the comfort of communion with God should kindle in us a holy zeal and indignation against sin." 

A key theological principle emerges around **divine blessing following faithful stewardship**. Henry highlights how "those who conscientiously give God his dues out of their estates bring a blessing upon all they have," connecting material faithfulness to spiritual prosperity.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**Post-worship responsibility**: "When the religious exercises of a Lord's day or a communion are finished we must not think that then the work is done. No, then the hardest part of our work begins, which is to exemplify the impressions of the ordinance upon our minds."

**Comprehensive opposition to sin**: "Those that sincerely set themselves against sin will set themselves against all sin" - believers must not spare any cherished idols, regardless of their beauty, cost, or patronage.

**Supporting ministry**: Henry emphasizes that ministers should be "not only maintained, but encouraged" and should "live comfortably, that they may do it with cheerfulness," while being maintained "in the law of the Lord" rather than "in idleness, pride, and luxury."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: The people's zealous destruction of idols extended beyond their own territories into Ephraim and Manasseh, demonstrating how "we should not only reform ourselves, but do all we can to reform others too."

**Verses 5-10**: The abundance of offerings created "heaps" that lasted from the third to seventh months. Henry notes this wasn't covetousness but proof that faithful giving brings blessing: "Since they began to bring in the offerings the Lord has blessed his people."

**Verses 11-19**: The systematic organization of temple administration shows that "good order contributes much to the carrying on of a good work."

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry weaves numerous scriptural connections throughout:

- **Isaiah 2:20; 31:6-7** - linking the destruction of idols to prophetic fulfillment
- **Malachi 3:10-11** and **Haggai 2:19** - supporting the principle of blessing following faithful tithing
- **1 Chronicles 23:4-5** - connecting to David's original organization of temple service
- **Ezekiel 44:30** - reinforcing priestly maintenance provisions

## Hezekiah's Character

Henry concludes with high praise for Hezekiah's **wholehearted sincerity**: "in every work that he began in the service of the house of God, and in the law, and in the commandments, to seek his God, he did it with all his heart, and prospered." This becomes the model for faithful leadership that prioritizes both God's honor and people's welfare.

The commentary presents Hezekiah's reforms as a template for comprehensive spiritual renewal that encompasses worship, stewardship, and systematic organization of religious life.
