## Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 15: Summary

### Main Theological Themes

Henry presents the central theological principle: **"The LORD is with you, while ye be with him"** - emphasizing God's conditional presence based on human faithfulness. He explores the consequences of forsaking God, showing how abandoning divine ordinances leads to spiritual and societal chaos. The commentary highlights the importance of **active seeking of God** and demonstrates how reformation requires both divine enablement and human courage.

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from Asa's reforms:

1. **Bold Action in Reformation**: Victory should inspire greater boldness for God's work, not complacency. As Henry notes, Asa "took courage" after receiving God's message and "ventured to destroy all the abominable idols."

2. **Heart-work in Religion**: Emphasizing wholehearted devotion, Henry writes: "We make nothing of our religion if we do not make heart-work of it. God will have all the heart or none."

3. **Perseverance in Seeking**: "What is religion but seeking God, enquiring after him, applying to him, upon all occasions? We shall not enjoy him till we come to heaven; while we are here we must continue seeking."

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-7 (God's Message)**: Henry interprets Azariah's prophecy as addressing three possible time periods: the era of Judges, the contemporary ten tribes under Jeroboam, or future apostasy. He emphasizes that **"your work shall be rewarded"** - showing that faithful service to God never goes unrewarded.

**Verses 8-15 (Covenant Renewal)**: Henry notes the voluntary nature of the people's covenant, stating "Let every man be a volunteer that covenants with God." He explains their commitment required seeking God "with all their heart and with all their soul."

**Verse 16 (Maachah's Removal)**: Henry sees this as evidence of Asa's impartiality in reformation, not sparing even family members who promoted idolatry.

### Cross-References and Connections

Henry connects the passage to several scriptures:
- **Hebrews 11:6** regarding seeking God
- **Zechariah 8:23** about joining those who have God with them  
- **Psalm 110:3** on willing service
- **Hebrews 8:13** relating the altar's decay to ceremonial institutions that would "vanish away"

The commentary also references the broader biblical pattern of apostasy and restoration seen throughout Israel's history, particularly during the period of Judges.

Henry concludes by noting the **lasting peace** that resulted from Asa's reforms, demonstrating the practical benefits of wholehearted devotion to God while acknowledging the incomplete nature of the reformation (the high places remained).
