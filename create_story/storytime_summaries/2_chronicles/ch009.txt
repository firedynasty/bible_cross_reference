# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles Chapter 9: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes God's faithfulness in honoring those who honor Him, using Solomon as the prime example. **"Those who honour God he will honour"** serves as the central theological principle. Solomon's wisdom and wealth became instruments of divine glory because he had consecrated the temple to God. Henry presents Solomon as a **type of Christ**, noting that the queen of Sheba's visit foreshadows the wise men bringing gifts to Jesus, and that Solomon's throne represents "the spiritual glory of the kingdom of the Messiah."

The commentary highlights divine providence in Solomon's prosperity, tracing all blessings back to God's delight and love. Henry notes how the queen of Sheba recognized this, blessing God for Solomon's advancement **"because thy God delighted in thee and because he loved Israel."**

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**Pursuing Wisdom**: Like the queen of Sheba, believers should **"grudge no pains nor cost to obtain"** true wisdom, viewing it as **"that pearl of great price which is a good bargain to purchase by parting with all that we have."**

**Generous Sharing**: Christians should be **"communicative"** with their gifts, following Solomon's example of freely sharing wisdom. **"Being taught of God, freely he had received, and freely he gave."**

**Rejoicing in Others' Success**: Rather than envying others' prosperity, believers should **"rejoice and give God thanks for the gifts, graces, and usefulness, of others."** Henry warns that **"by envying the prosperity of others, we lose the comfort even of our own."**

**Good Order in Households**: The commentary emphasizes maintaining **"good order in a family... especially in the things of God"** as highly commendable.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that Chronicles omits Solomon's later defection mentioned in Kings, presenting him with **"an unstained reputation."** He suggests this demonstrates how **"we must not be forward to mention"** others' faults, preferring to **"speak largely of that in others which is praise-worthy."**

Regarding the queen of Sheba's amazement (v. 4), Henry notes she was overwhelmed by both Solomon's wisdom and the orderly worship in God's house. Her declaration about Solomon's servants being happy (v. 7) leads Henry to reflect on the blessing of **"constant converse with such as are knowing, wise, and good."**

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout:

- **Matthew 12:42**: Christ's reference to the queen of Sheba as an example for seeking divine wisdom
- **1 Samuel 2:30**: "Those who honour God he will honour"
- **Matthew 6:29**: Christ's comparison of lilies to Solomon's glory
- **1 Corinthians 2:8, 14**: The world's rejection of divine wisdom
- **Psalm 76:11; Romans 12:1**: Bringing presents/offerings to God
- **Matthew 2:11**: The wise men's gifts to Christ

Henry concludes with the sobering reminder that death equalizes all earthly glory: **"Mors sceptra ligonibus aequat--Death wrenches from the hand the sceptre as well as the spade,"** emphasizing the temporary nature of worldly magnificence compared to eternal spiritual realities.
