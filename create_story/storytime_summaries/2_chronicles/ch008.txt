# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Chronicles 8: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **divine providence and blessing** as central themes, noting how God "gratified even Solomon's humour" and granted success in all his building projects after Solomon prioritized God's house first. The commentary highlights the principle that **proper worship must follow proper preparation** - "The building of the temple was in order to the service of the temple" - showing that physical structures are meaningless without genuine devotion.

Henry also addresses the **balance between sacred and secular**, praising Solomon's wisdom in separating his Egyptian wife's court from David's holy residence, noting that "between things sacred and things common the ancient landmarks ought to be kept up."

## Key Practical Applications

**Industry and Purpose**: Henry commends Solomon for spending his days "not in contemplation, but in action" and keeping "abundance of people to work." He argues it's "the interest of a state by all means possible to promote and encourage industry, and to keep its subjects from idleness."

**Contentment and Boundaries**: Solomon exemplifies healthy desires, as he "knew how to set bounds to his desires" and "finished all he desired, and then he desired no more" - contrasting favorably with Alexander who "had not more worlds to conquer."

**Consistent Devotion**: Henry advocates for "settled method of devotion" through daily, weekly, monthly and yearly spiritual sacrifices, drawing from Solomon's faithful maintenance of temple worship.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-6**: Henry sees Solomon's building projects as wise preparation - "in a time of peace preparing for a time of war, which is as much a man's business as it is in summer to provide food for winter."

**Verse 11**: The separation of Pharaoh's daughter receives detailed treatment, with Henry suggesting her court contained "idols of Egypt, and a great deal of profaneness and debauchery" that would profane David's holy residence.

**Verses 12-16**: Henry emphasizes that the temple was only "perfected" when proper worship was established, not when construction ended.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry references **1 Kings 9:10-24** for parallel accounts, **Judges 18:7** regarding idle societies being vulnerable, **Habakkuk 2:5** about endless desires, and connects David's role as "man of God" to Moses' authority. He links temple worship patterns to current "spiritual sacrifices" required of Christians, showing continuity between Old and New Testament worship principles.

The commentary concludes by noting that despite Israel's wisdom and Canaan's richness, they still needed foreign expertise and gold, teaching that "grace, and not gold, is the best riches, and acquaintance with God and his law, not with arts and sciences, the best knowledge."
