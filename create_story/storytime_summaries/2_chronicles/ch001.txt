# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Chronicles Chapter 1: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Solomon's reign as beginning with divine blessing and proper priorities. The central theme is **God's faithfulness in magnifying those who honor Him first**. He emphasizes that "God's presence will be our strength" as demonstrated in Solomon's contested yet successful rise to power.

The commentary highlights the **principle of divine reciprocity**: "Where God sows plentifully he expects to reap accordingly." Henry shows how Solomon's generous sacrifices (1,000 burnt offerings) preceded God's generous response with wisdom and wealth.

A key theological insight involves **God's testing through choice**: "Men's characters appear in their choices and desires." Henry notes that God's invitation to "Ask what I shall give thee" was both gracious provision and divine examination, revealing whether Solomon was among "the children of this world" or "the children of light."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**Leadership and Influence**: "Authority and interest are well bestowed on those that will thus use them for the glory of God." Solomon's gathering of officials for worship demonstrates how leaders should use their position to promote religion.

**Prayer and God's Promises**: "God's promises are our best pleas in prayer." Henry emphasizes that believers can claim covenant promises made to previous generations, noting that "Children may take the comfort of the promises of that covenant which their parents...laid claim to."

**Proper Priorities**: Solomon exemplified right order by attending to worship before governance - "first the tabernacle, and then the throne." Henry warns against pursuing "present things most earnestly" while encouraging readers to "make the other world their end."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides nuanced interpretation of the divided worship locations (ark in Jerusalem, altar in Gibeon), noting that "it is the altar that sanctifieth every gift" and defending both David's and Solomon's differing approaches: "Both did well, and let neither be censured."

On Solomon's request for wisdom, Henry highlights two pleas unique to Chronicles: Solomon's appeal to succeed his father well and his invocation of God's promises to David. He notes that while wisdom wasn't explicitly promised, "it was necessary in order to the accomplishment of what was promised."

Regarding Solomon's wealth accumulation, Henry offers mixed assessment, criticizing the multiplication of horses as forbidden by Deuteronomy 17:16, while praising trade development as wise governance.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry extensively connects this passage to 1 Kings 3:5, Proverbs 2:6, James 1:5, and Deuteronomy 17:16. He references 1 Chronicles 27:29-31 regarding David's livestock legacy and 2 Samuel 7:13-15 concerning God's covenant promises. 

The commentary also draws connections to Proverbs 31:24, suggesting Solomon may have developed the linen trade based on his mother's teaching about the virtuous woman's industry.

Henry concludes with the principle "In all labour there is profit," emphasizing how godly wisdom should translate into practical benefit for one's people and kingdom.
