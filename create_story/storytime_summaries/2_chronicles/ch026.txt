# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 26 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes

Henry presents a classic narrative of pride's destructive power and the importance of staying within God-appointed boundaries. The central theme is that **prosperity can lead to spiritual downfall** when it breeds pride. As Henry notes, "When he was strong...his heart was lifted up to his destruction." The commentary emphasizes God's sovereignty in blessing and judgment, showing how "those only prosper whom God makes to prosper."

Another key theme is the **separation of offices** - kingly and priestly roles were distinct under Mosaic law, "not to be united again but in the person of the Messiah." This violation represents humanity's tendency to overstep divinely established boundaries.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

1. **The value of godly counselors**: "Happy are the great men who have such about them and are willing to be advised by them" - referring to Zechariah's influence on Uzziah's early reign.

2. **Religion supports prosperity**: "Religion and piety are very friendly to outward prosperity. Many have found...that as long as they sought the Lord...they prospered."

3. **The danger of success**: Henry warns that prosperity often "puffs up with pride" and destroys people, making Uzziah's story a cautionary tale about handling success humbly.

4. **Honest work is honorable**: Henry commends Uzziah's love of husbandry, noting "It is an honour to the husbandman's calling that one of the most illustrious princes...followed it."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 5** - Henry emphasizes the conditional nature of blessing: "as long as he sought the LORD, God made him to prosper," highlighting how Uzziah's prosperity was tied to his spiritual condition.

**Verses 16-19** - Henry describes Uzziah's sin as unique among kings - not idolatry or immorality, but **presumptuous invasion of priestly office**. He suggests this may have stemmed from thinking he could perform priestly duties better than the priests, or from imitating idolatrous kings who burned incense themselves.

## Cross-References

Henry connects this passage to several scriptures:
- **Exodus 30:7** and **1 Chronicles 23:13** regarding Aaron's sons being appointed to burn incense
- **Numbers 3:10; 18:7** concerning the penalty for non-priests approaching sacred duties
- References to **David's eating showbread** as legitimate emergency exception, contrasting with Uzziah's unnecessary presumption
- Connections to **1 Kings 13:1** regarding Jeroboam's unauthorized altar service

Henry concludes that Uzziah's tragedy demonstrates how "the good way is one; by-paths are many," showing how even well-intentioned people can fall through pride and presumption.
