# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 2 Chronicles 23: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry frames this chapter as God's faithfulness to His covenant promises despite human opposition. The **continuance of David's seed and throne was what God had sworn by his holiness**, and "an interruption was no defeasance; the stream of government here runs again in the right channel." Henry emphasizes that God's covenant with David remains unbreakable, even through six years of usurpation under Athaliah.

The restoration represents both civil and spiritual renewal, with Henry noting: **"They would not have done half their work if they had only destroyed the usurper of the king's right, and not the usurper of God's right."** True reformation must address both political and religious corruption.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from Jehoiada's character and actions:

**Leadership Qualities**: Jehoiada exemplified prudence (careful timing and planning), influence (commanding respect through wisdom and virtue), faith (dependence on God's promises), reverence (maintaining temple sanctity), and resolution (completing what he started). Henry observes: **"When God has work to do he will qualify and animate men for it."**

**Covenant Relationship**: The tripartite covenant between God, king, and people provides a model for Christian community. **"Let us look upon ourselves and one another as the Lord's people, and this will have a powerful influence upon us in the discharge of all our duty both to God and man."**

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 3**: Henry highlights Jehoiada's declaration that "the king's son shall reign, as the LORD hath said," emphasizing faith in God's promises over circumstances.

**Verses 6-7**: Despite urgent political necessity, Jehoiada maintained temple sanctity: **"Never let sacred things be profaned, no, not for the support of civil rights."**

**Verses 16-17**: The covenant preceded the destruction of Baal worship, showing proper priorities in reformation—establishing right relationship with God before addressing external corruption.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry references **Psalm 89:35** regarding God's oath concerning David's lineage, **Ecclesiastes 9:17** about wise words being "heard in quiet," and **Isaiah 11:1** describing "a rod sprung out of the stem of Jesse." He connects the temple restoration to **Romans 5:11** about rejoicing in atonement.

The commentary draws parallels to **2 Kings 11**, noting this as a nearly identical account, and references **Deuteronomy 13:5-6** regarding the execution of those who seduce others to idolatry.

Henry concludes with a spiritual application: **"When the Son of David is enthroned in the soul all is quiet and springs of joy are opened,"** connecting the historical restoration to Christ's reign in believers' hearts.
