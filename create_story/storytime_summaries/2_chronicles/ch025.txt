# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Chronicles 25: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Amaziah as a tragic example of incomplete devotion to God. The central theme is captured in verse 2: Amaziah "did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, but not with a perfect heart." Henry describes him as "a cool and indifferent friend" to religion, comparing such halfhearted faith to "too many in this Laodicean age."

The commentary emphasizes God's sovereignty over earthly alliances and military strength. When Amaziah hires Israelite mercenaries, God commands their dismissal because "the Lord is not with the children of Ephraim" due to their idol worship. Henry stresses that human alliances without divine blessing are worthless.

A major theological point concerns the destructive power of idolatry. Amaziah's worship of Edomite gods—the very deities of his defeated enemies—represents "egregious folly" and "the greatest absurdity that could be." Henry notes the irony: why worship gods "which could not deliver their own people?"

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers:

**On obedience despite financial cost**: When Amaziah worries about losing 100 talents by dismissing the Israelite army, the prophet responds, "The LORD is able to give thee much more than this." Henry applies this: "A firm belief of God's all-sufficiency to bear us out in our duty...will make his yoke very easy and his burden very light."

**On trusting God's provision**: Henry emphasizes that "whatever we part with for his sake shall be made up to us in kind or kindness," encouraging believers to trust God's faithfulness when obedience requires sacrifice.

**On dangerous associations**: The commentary warns against partnering with those "from whom the Lord has departed," noting it's "comfortable to employ those who...have an interest in heaven, and dangerous to associate with those" who lack God's blessing.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 3-4**: Henry praises Amaziah's justice in executing his father's murderers while sparing their children, calling this proper adherence to biblical law.

**Verses 7-10**: The prophet's command reveals that God's presence matters more than military numbers. Henry notes how "rashness makes work for repentance" regarding Amaziah's hasty hiring of foreign troops.

**Verses 14-16**: Henry highlights the absurdity of Amaziah's idolatry, suggesting his "barbarous inhumanity" in executing prisoners led to being "given up to this ridiculous idolatry." When Amaziah silences the prophet with threats, Henry observes the tragic irony that he had listened to counsel about military matters but rejected spiritual rebuke.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry references the parallel account in 2 Kings 14 and connects Amaziah's story to broader biblical themes. He cites Hosea 4:17 ("He is joined to idols; let him alone") regarding God's eventual withdrawal from the obstinate. 

The commentary links to Isaiah 30:10 about people who tell seers "See not," illustrating how sinners suppress prophetic truth. Henry also notes how the 100 talents Amaziah lost were later repaid to his grandson Jotham (2 Chronicles 27:5), demonstrating God's faithfulness to those who obey.

Henry concludes that Amaziah's rejection of prophetic correction led to divine judgment, warning that when "the blessed Spirit...forbears to strive," the condition becomes "miserable."
