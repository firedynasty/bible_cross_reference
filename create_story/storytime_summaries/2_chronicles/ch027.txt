# Matthew Henry Commentary on 2 Chronicles 27: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry presents King Jotham as an exemplary ruler who demonstrates the principles of godly leadership and consistent faithfulness. The central theological theme is that **steadfast obedience to God leads to divine blessing and prosperity**. Henry emphasizes that Jotham "prepared his ways before the LORD his God," meaning he "walked steadily and constantly in the way of his duty, was uniform and resolute in it."

The commentary highlights the contrast between Jotham's consistency and his predecessors who "had some good in them, lost their credit by their inconstancy and inconsistency with themselves." This underscores the theological importance of perseverance in faith rather than sporadic righteousness.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from Jotham's reign:

**Learning from Others' Mistakes**: "We must not imitate the best men...any further than they did well; but, on the contrary, their falls...must be warnings to us to walk the more circumspectly." Jotham wisely avoided his father Uzziah's presumptuous act of entering the temple to burn incense.

**Limitations of Personal Godliness**: Even godly leaders cannot force reformation on unwilling people. Henry notes that "the people did yet corruptly," suggesting that "Men may be very good themselves, and yet not have courage and zeal to do what they might do towards the reforming of others."

**Steadfastness Brings Strength**: "The more stedfast we are in religion the more mighty we are both for the resistance of that which is evil and for the performance of that which is good."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 2**: Henry emphasizes Jotham's selective imitation of his father—following Uzziah's good examples while learning from his failures.

**Verse 6**: The phrase "prepared his ways before the LORD" receives detailed attention as meaning he "walked circumspectly" and "established or fixed his ways," showing deliberate spiritual planning and consistency.

**Verses 3-5**: Jotham's building projects and military victories demonstrate how spiritual faithfulness translates into practical success in governance and defense.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry references **2 Kings 15:32** for parallel accounts and **2 Chronicles 20:1** regarding previous Ammonite invasions during Jehoshaphat's reign, showing historical continuity in regional conflicts.

The commentary concludes with a sobering note about succession, as "Ahaz his son, whose character, in all respects, was the reverse of his, reigned in his stead," illustrating how "wealth and power with which wise men have done good" can "devolve upon fools, that will do hurt with them."

Henry presents Jotham as a model of consistent godliness whose brief but exemplary reign demonstrates that spiritual faithfulness yields both divine favor and earthly prosperity.
