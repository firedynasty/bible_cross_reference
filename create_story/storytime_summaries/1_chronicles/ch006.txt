# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 6 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes that the Levitical priesthood represented "an honour more valuable than either the precedency or the double portion" - the spiritual privilege of serving God directly. He notes the theological significance that "the Levitical priesthood did not make anything perfect, but this was to be done by the bringing in of a better hope," pointing to Christ's superior priesthood.

The commentary highlights God's sovereign grace in how "the blessing entailed on the seed of the upright sometimes passes over one generation and fastens upon the next," using Heman (Samuel's grandson) as an example of divine redemption across generations.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from the Levites' service:

**Humble Service**: Even David's descendants "did not think it below him to be a precentor in the house of God. David himself was willing to be a door-keeper." This teaches that no service for God is beneath anyone's dignity.

**Spiritual Gifts and Diversity**: "As every one has received the gift, so let him minister. Those that could not sing must not therefore be laid aside as good for nothing." Henry emphasizes that different abilities serve different purposes in God's kingdom.

**Faithfulness in Office**: Azariah's opposition to King Uzziah's presumption exemplifies how religious leaders must "boldly maintained and defended the priest's office" even at personal risk.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides detailed genealogical analysis, noting how priests "were more concerned than any other Israelites to preserve their pedigree clear" because "all the honours and privileges of their office depended upon their descent."

Regarding the musical ministry (verses 31-48), Henry explains that David instituted singing "after that the ark had rest" because worship should reflect joy in God's established presence: "we should rejoice as much in the prolonging of our spiritual privileges as in the restoring of them."

The three choir leaders (Heman, Asaph, and Ethan) represent "an admirable order" with "equality in the distribution of this work and honour" among the Levitical houses.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this chapter to broader biblical themes:
- Links to **Ezra 2:62-63** regarding genealogical purity requirements
- References **2 Chronicles 26:17-18** for Azariah's confrontation with Uzziah
- Connects to **2 Samuel 23:1** calling David "the sweet psalmist of Israel"
- Points forward to Christ as the ultimate fulfillment of the priesthood
- Alludes to future heavenly worship where believers "shall take leave of all their burdens and be employed in everlasting songs"

Henry masterfully weaves these genealogical records into a tapestry showing God's faithful preservation of proper worship order while pointing toward the greater spiritual realities fulfilled in Christ.
