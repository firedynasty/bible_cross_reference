# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles 28: Summary

## Main Theological Themes

**Divine Sovereignty in Leadership Selection**: Henry emphasizes that both David's and Solomon's kingship were "jure divino" (divine right), chosen by God through prophecy rather than providence or primogeniture. He notes that "God takes whom he likes, and likes whom he makes like himself, as he did David, a man after his own heart."

**The Messianic Typology**: Henry sees Solomon as a clear type of Christ, particularly in God's declaration "I have chosen him to be my son." He connects this to Psalm 2:7 and Christ as God's beloved Son, noting that Solomon's temple-building points to Christ as "both the founder and the foundation of the gospel temple."

**Conditional vs. Eternal Promises**: Henry distinguishes between Solomon's conditional kingdom ("If he be constant to do my commandments") and the eternal nature of the Messianic kingdom, which "must have its accomplishment in the kingdom of the Messiah."

## Key Practical Applications

**Leadership with Humility**: Henry praises David's respectful address to his assembly, calling them "my brethren, and my people" rather than servants. He notes: "It becomes superiors thus to speak with affection and condescension even to their inferiors; they will not be the less honoured for it, but the more beloved."

**Wholehearted Service**: The commentary emphasizes serving God "with a perfect heart and with a willing mind," explaining that "we make nothing of religion if we do not mind it, and make heart-work of it."

**Perseverance in Faith**: Henry warns that "perseverance wears the crown, though it wins it not," emphasizing that God's favor continues only with constancy in duty.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 2**: Henry notes the temple is called "a house of rest for the footstool of our God," explaining that "heaven is his throne of glory; the earth, and the most magnificent temples that can be built upon it, are but his footstool."

**Verses 9-10**: On God searching hearts, Henry observes that God "searches all hearts, even the hearts of kings, which to men are unsearchable" (referencing Proverbs 25:3).

**Verse 19**: Henry emphasizes that the temple pattern came by divine revelation: "the LORD made me understand in writing by his hand upon me."

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry draws extensive scriptural connections including:
- Acts 13:36 (David serving his generation)
- Deuteronomy 31:28 and Joshua 23-24 (leadership transitions)
- Genesis 49:10 (Judah's scepter)
- Psalm 99:5 and 132:7 (worshipping at God's footstool)
- Isaiah 9:7 and Luke 1:33 (eternal kingdom)
- Hebrews 11:6 (seeking God diligently)

The commentary concludes by emphasizing two essential means for spiritual success: **"Caution: Take heed"** against evil, and **"Courage: Be strong, and do it"** - drawing strength from divine grace to accomplish God's work.
