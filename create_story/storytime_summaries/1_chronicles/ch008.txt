# Matthew Henry Commentary Summary: 1 Chronicles 8

## Main Theological Themes

Matthew Henry emphasizes God's sovereign preservation of His people throughout this genealogical record. He notes that "it is the honour of God to help the weakest and raise up those that are most diminished and abased," specifically referencing Benjamin's remarkable recovery from near extinction during the judges period. The preservation of these names serves as "a figure of the writing of the names of all God's spiritual Israel in the Lamb's book of life," connecting earthly genealogies to eternal spiritual realities.

Henry observes a significant theological distinction: while "all the kings of Israel after the defection from the house of David" are omitted because "they were all idolaters," Saul's family receives detailed treatment, and Jonathan's line is particularly honored due to his covenant faithfulness with David.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers. He encourages those "whose godly parents had their conversation in the new Jerusalem" to "set their faces thitherward and pursue the way thither, whatever it cost them." The commentary emphasizes that true honor comes not from "pomp or wealth" but from being "qualified to serve their country," as demonstrated by the 150 valiant archers from Ulam's lineage.

The genealogies served practical purposes for the returning exiles, helping them "settle as nearly as they could with those of their own family, and in the places of their former residence."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry identifies Ehud (verse 6) as likely the judge who delivered Israel from Moabite oppression, noting his descendants' settlement in Moab. He explains the genealogical discrepancy regarding Kish's parentage (verse 33) by clarifying that Hebrew commonly uses "sons for grandsons and other descendants."

The mention of Benjamites dwelling in Jerusalem receives special attention, as Henry notes this encouraged post-exile resettlement in the dangerous but important city.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry extensively cross-references this chapter with 1 Samuel 9:1, 14:51, and 20:15-42 regarding Saul's lineage and David's covenant with Jonathan. He connects to Judges 3:15 for Ehud's identity and references Nehemiah 11:2 regarding Jerusalem's resettlement challenges.

The commentary links to chapter 7:21 concerning conflicts with Gath inhabitants and connects the broader genealogical project to the historical records mentioned in chapter 9:1.

## Interpretive Approach

Henry acknowledges textual difficulties pragmatically, suggesting Ezra "copied them out as they came into his hand" without divine inspiration correcting every genealogical inconsistency. He emphasizes that while "many great and mighty nations" are forgotten, God preserves the memory of His people, as "the memory of the just is blessed."
