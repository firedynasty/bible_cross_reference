# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 14: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry emphasizes **divine providence and confirmation** as the central theme. David's recognition that "the LORD had confirmed him king over Israel" demonstrates that true authority comes from God, not human appointment. Henry notes David's relief: "Who shall unfix me if God hath fixed me?" 

The commentary highlights **purposeful blessing**, teaching that "David's kingdom was lifted up on high, not for his own sake, that he might look great, but because of his people Israel." Henry emphasizes that "we are blessed in order that we may be blessings," connecting this to Genesis 12:2.

**Divine guidance in warfare** forms another key theme, as David consistently "enquired of God" before battle, demonstrating proper dependence on divine direction rather than human wisdom.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

1. **Humility and interdependence**: Even mighty David needed Hiram's help, teaching us to "despise none, but, as we have opportunity, be obliging to all."

2. **Seeking God's counsel**: David's repeated inquiries of God should "direct us in all our ways to acknowledge God—in distress to fly to him."

3. **Spiritual warfare**: "Resist the devil, and he shall flee as the Philistines did before David."

4. **Attentiveness to God's timing**: The "sound of going in the tops of the mulberry trees" teaches us to "attend God's motions both in his providence and in the influences of his Spirit."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry acknowledges David's polygamy as an "infirmity" while noting that his children were still "a heritage of the Lord." He emphasizes the danger that "it is difficult to thrive without growing secure and indulgent to the flesh."

Regarding the Philistine conflicts, Henry stresses that each battle required fresh consultation with God, as divine strategy varied between encounters.

## Cross-References and Typology

Henry connects this passage to 2 Samuel 5:11 and references Genesis 12:2 regarding purposeful blessing. Most significantly, he views David's widespread fame (verse 17) as "a type and figure of the exalted honour of the Son of David," connecting to Christ's ultimate exaltation and the name "above every name."

The commentary concludes with the doxology: "Not unto us, O Lord! not unto us, but to thy name give glory," emphasizing proper attribution of success to divine intervention rather than human achievement.
