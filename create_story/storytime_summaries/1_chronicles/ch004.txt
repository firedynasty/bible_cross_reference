# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles 4: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes God's providential care for the tribe of Judah, noting that Ezra was particularly detailed with this genealogy because Judah "not only long survived the other tribes in Canaan, but in process of time...returned out of captivity." This demonstrates God's faithfulness to His covenant promises through specific lineages.

The commentary highlights the transformative power of prayer and God's responsiveness to genuine petition. Henry sees Jabez as exemplifying how "the way to be truly great is to be truly good and to pray much," emphasizing that spiritual devotion leads to divine honor and blessing.

## Key Practical Applications

**The Value of Honest Labor**: Henry commends the craftsmen families, noting that "those that are craftsmen are not therefore to be looked upon as mean men." He encourages viewing manual trades as honorable when pursued with diligence and skill.

**Prayer as Life Practice**: Using Jabez's example, Henry encourages believers to make prayer their constant practice, suggesting these may have been "the heads on which he enlarged in his daily prayers."

**Contentment vs. Ambition**: Henry criticizes those who chose comfortable exile over returning to God's promised land, calling them "unworthy the name of Israelites that would dwell among plants and hedges rather than be at the pains to return to Canaan."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 9-10 (Jabez's Prayer)**: Henry provides extensive analysis of this prayer, noting four specific requests: divine blessing, enlarged territory, God's presence, and protection from evil. He observes that "God granted him that which he requested," demonstrating God's readiness to answer sincere prayer.

**Verse 14**: The "valley of craftsmen" represents how skilled workers "chose to live together, for the improving of arts by comparing notes, and that they might support one another's reputation."

**Verses 21-23**: Henry notes the honor in generational craftsmanship, where families specialized in fine linen work and pottery, "not aiming to make them gentlemen" but excelling in their trades.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects Jabez's prayer to **Genesis 22:17** ("In blessing, I will bless thee"), suggesting Jabez may have sought Abraham's covenant blessings. He references **1 Timothy 5:4** regarding children honoring parents who bore them with sorrow.

The commentary links to **Psalm 90:15** about being "made glad in proportion to the affliction," and draws parallels to **Deuteronomy 33:7** regarding Moses' prayer for Judah's hands being sufficient.

## Memorable Quotes

"**God's blessings are real things and produce real effects. We can but wish a blessing: he commands it. Those whom he blesses are blessed indeed.**"

"**The sorrow he carried in his name might help to put a seriousness upon his spirit**" - regarding how our trials can shape godly character.

"**God was of old always ready to hear prayer, and his ear is not yet heavy**" - emphasizing God's unchanging nature in responding to prayer.

Henry masterfully weaves together genealogical details with profound spiritual applications, showing how even administrative records reveal God's faithfulness and provide models for faithful living.
