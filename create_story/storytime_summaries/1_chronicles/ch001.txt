## Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles Chapter 1: Summary

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents the genealogies as divinely purposeful, arguing that **"All scripture is profitable, though not all alike profitable."** He identifies two primary theological functions: First, these records were essential for post-exilic Jews to restore tribal distinctions after Babylon's captivity "had put all into confusion." Second, and more significantly, they establish the Messianic line, proving that "our blessed Saviour was, according to the prophecies which went before of him, the son of David, the son of Judah, the son of Abraham, the son of Adam."

Henry emphasizes God's sovereign election, noting how the genealogy progressively narrows from all humanity to Abraham's line, then specifically to Isaac and Jacob. He observes that **"when the promised Seed has come the line that was to lead to him is broken off"** - explaining why Jewish genealogies were lost after Christ's advent, as their purpose was fulfilled.

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from these genealogies. He contrasts Adam and Abraham as representative figures: **"Adam was the common father of our flesh, Abraham the common father of the faithful."** This leads to his central application - believers must ensure they become Abraham's spiritual children through faith, being "grafted into the good olive."

He also emphasizes human unity, declaring **"God has created of one blood all nations of men"** and asking **"Have we not all one father? Has not one God created us?"** This universal brotherhood serves as a foundation for understanding God's particular election of Israel.

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry highlights verse 19's mention of Peleg, "because in his days the earth was divided," connecting this to the Tower of Babel dispersion. He notes verse 10's reference to Nimrod as one who "began to be mighty upon the earth," identifying him as "probably an oppressor" of God's people.

Regarding the genealogical structure, Henry observes that **"This paragraph has Adam for its first word and Abraham for its last"** - spanning 2000 years and representing the transition from the covenant of works to the covenant of grace.

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively cross-references Genesis chapters 5, 10, 11, 25, and 36 as the sources for Chronicles' genealogies. He connects the genealogy to Luke 3:34-38, noting that Luke's order ascends while Chronicles descends. He references Romans 4:11-12 regarding Abraham's spiritual fatherhood and Galatians 4:22 concerning Ishmael representing unbelieving Jews.

He also cites Malachi 2:10 on human unity, Isaiah 43:3 on God valuing Israel above other nations, and Revelation 7:9 regarding the salvation of people from all nations, while maintaining that **"Israel was a chosen nation, elect in type."**

Henry concludes that while God's covenant focused on Abraham's line, this doesn't exclude individuals from other nations who "feared God and wrought righteousness" from finding divine favor, demonstrating God's particular election alongside His universal mercy.
