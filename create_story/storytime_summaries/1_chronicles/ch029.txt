# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 29 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes several key theological themes throughout this chapter. **Divine sovereignty** dominates David's prayer, where God is recognized as the source of all greatness, power, and glory. Henry notes that God "has them in and of himself" - highlighting God's self-existence and supremacy over creation. 

The theme of **human stewardship** runs throughout, with Henry observing that David's generous giving flows from recognizing that "all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee." This reflects the theological principle that humans are merely stewards of God's resources.

**Willing service** emerges as crucial - Henry emphasizes that "what is done in works of piety and charity should be done willingly and not by constraint; for God loves a cheerful giver." The commentary stresses that true service must flow from love rather than compulsion.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers. Leaders must **lead by example**, noting "Those who would draw others to do that which is good must themselves lead." David's personal generosity demonstrates this principle effectively.

The commentary emphasizes **wholehearted service**: "Work for God must be done with all our might, or we shall bring nothing to pass in it." Henry advocates for complete dedication rather than half-hearted effort.

**Encouraging young believers** receives attention, as Henry notes David's support for Solomon who was "young and tender, and needed help." This demonstrates the importance of mentoring in spiritual work.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On verse 5's question "who then is willing to consecrate his service," Henry explains this means we must "fill our hands to the Lord" - engaging completely in God's service. The Hebrew phrase suggests total commitment.

Regarding verse 14's humble acknowledgment, Henry highlights David's recognition that human generosity is really God's grace: all giving to God is simply returning what He first provided.

The prayer in verses 10-13 receives extensive treatment, with Henry noting how David's praise focuses on God's "infinite perfections" and "sovereign dominion" as the foundation for all worship.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry connects David's doxology to the Lord's Prayer, noting how both end with similar affirmations: "for thine is the kingdom, the power, and the glory." This demonstrates consistent biblical theology across testaments.

He references Psalm 26:8 to explain David's motivation: giving flowed from love for "the habitation of God's house." The commentary also alludes to Psalm 68:35 regarding God as Israel's strength-giver.

Henry concludes with the memorable phrase about this transition: "it is hard to say which shines brighter here, the setting sun or the rising sun" - referring to David's faithful conclusion and Solomon's promising beginning, demonstrating how God's work continues across generations through faithful stewardship and willing hearts.
