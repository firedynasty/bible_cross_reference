# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles 20: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents the defeat of giants as a spiritual metaphor for the gradual victory of grace over corruption. He notes that "in the conflicts between grace and corruption there are some sins which, like these giants, keep their ground a great while and are not mastered without much difficulty and a long struggle: but judgment will be brought forth unto victory at last." 

The commentary emphasizes God's pattern of humbling pride and power, connecting David's victories to Christ's ultimate triumph: "The victories of the Son of David, like those of David himself, are gradual. We see not yet all things put under him; but it will be seen shortly: and death itself, the last enemy, like these giants, will be triumphed over."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from the narrative:

**On Avoiding Temptation**: Regarding David's absence from battle (which led to the Bathsheba incident), Henry warns that "if he had been abroad with his army, he would have been out of the way of that temptation; but, indulging his ease, he fell into uncleanness."

**On Discussing Others' Failures**: Henry advocates discretion when addressing sin, noting that while Scripture records David's sin faithfully, the Chronicler's avoidance of repeating it "is designed to teach us that, though there may be a just occasion to speak of the faults and miscarriages of others, yet we should not take delight in the repetition of them."

**On Facing Powerful Enemies**: Henry encourages believers not to be intimidated by seemingly overwhelming opposition: "Never let the church's friends be disheartened by the power and pride of the church's enemies. We need not fear great men against us while we have the great God for us."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that God's people grow differently than worldly powers: "We never read of giants among the Israelites as we do of the giants among the Philistines... The growth of God's plants is in usefulness, not in bulk." He notes that "in the balance of the sanctuary David far outweighs Goliath."

Regarding the giants' defeat, Henry emphasizes that ordinary men prevailed because "they had God on their side, who takes pleasure in abasing lofty looks."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to Joshua 11:21 (the destruction of the Anakim), 2 Samuel 11-12 (David's sin with Bathsheba), and Deuteronomy 32:27 (God's intervention against proud enemies). He also alludes to 1 Corinthians 15:25-26 regarding Christ's ultimate victory over death as the "last enemy."

The commentary skillfully weaves together historical narrative with timeless spiritual principles, demonstrating Henry's characteristic blend of practical wisdom and theological insight.
