# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 27 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Order in Governance**: Henry emphasizes that David's organizational structure reflects God's wisdom in establishing order. The detailed military and civil arrangements demonstrate how godly leadership creates systems that balance public safety with minimal burden on the people.

**Spiritual Warfare Application**: Henry draws a consistent parallel between David's military organization and Christian spiritual warfare, noting that "Those that are Israelites indeed must learn war; for we have enemies to grapple with, whom we are concerned constantly to stand upon our guard against."

**Divine Displeasure with Pride**: The commentary highlights God's judgment on David's census as a warning against pride and self-reliance, showing how "a good man cannot, in the reflection, please himself with that which he knows God is displeased with."

## Key Practical Applications

**Wise Leadership Principles**: Henry praises David's militia system as exemplifying how leaders should "provide for the public safety" while making it "as little as possible burdensome to the people." The monthly rotation system allowed for national defense without creating an oppressive standing army.

**Merit-Based Advancement**: Henry commends how "honour thus attends merit," noting that all twelve commanders "had first signalized themselves by their great actions and then they were advanced to those great preferments."

**Simplicity in Administration**: Regarding David's court officers, Henry observes approvingly that "here are no officers for state, none for sport... but all for service, agreeable to the simplicity and plainness of those times."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-15 (Military Organization)**: Henry explains this wasn't a permanent standing army but a rotating system where citizens served one month yearly, calling it "very prudent" governance that kept the nation prepared while minimizing hardship.

**Verse 5 (Benaiah as "chief priest")**: Henry clarifies this translation issue, noting that "cohen signifying both a priest and a prince, it might better be translated here a chief ruler."

**Verses 23-24 (The Census)**: Henry notes David's attempt to limit the census to those over twenty was "but a poor salvo" since this was already customary practice, and the incomplete census remained unrecorded due to divine displeasure.

**Verses 25-31 (Court Officers)**: Henry emphasizes these were "all for service" rather than ceremony, showing David as "a great husband of his estate" who exemplified industry.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this chapter to several other passages:
- **2 Samuel 23 and 1 Chronicles 11** for the military commanders' backgrounds
- **1 Peter 2:13-14** regarding submission to governors under the king
- **Ecclesiastes 5:9** on kings being "served of the field"
- **Psalm 119:24** where David declares "Thy testimonies are my delight and my counsellors"
- **2 Kings 25:12** contrasting later appointments of "poor of the land" to agricultural oversight

Henry concludes with David's ultimate reliance on Scripture over human counsel, demonstrating that even the best earthly organization must submit to divine wisdom.
