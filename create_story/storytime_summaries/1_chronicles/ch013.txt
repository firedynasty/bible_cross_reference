# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles 13 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes the **primacy of religion in good governance**. He notes that after David's civil establishment (chapter 12), "care is taken about religion" in chapter 13. The commentary highlights God's jealousy and holiness, particularly through Uzza's death, declaring that "God will be sanctified in those that come nigh unto him" and warning against presumption in holy matters.

The **balance between divine sovereignty and human responsibility** emerges as David seeks God's will ("if it be of the Lord our God") while taking practical action. Henry stresses that good intentions cannot justify improper methods, as David's error in transporting the ark on a cart demonstrates.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**For leaders**: David models consultation and humility rather than autocratic rule, showing "respect to the great men of the kingdom." Henry notes David "would not rule with a high hand," advocating for collaborative leadership.

**For worship and reverence**: The commentary warns against "familiarity breeding contempt" in approaching God, while encouraging believers to "rejoice with trembling, and to serve the Lord with fear."

**For handling setbacks**: Henry cautions against being "driven off from our duty by those providences which are only intended to drive us from our sins," urging perseverance despite divine corrections.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3**: Henry praises David's immediate concern for the ark after his coronation, noting David asked "What pious thing?" rather than "What pompous thing?" This demonstrates that the wise "take God's ark with them" when beginning new endeavors.

**Verse 7**: The cart incident reveals that "we must walk by rule, not by example when it varies from the rule," even when Providence seems to bless irregular methods.

**Verses 9-11**: Uzza's death teaches that "a good intention will justify not a bad action" and demonstrates God's jealousy in holy matters.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry frequently references **2 Samuel 6** for parallel accounts, **Leviticus 10:3** (Nadab and Abihu) for similar divine judgments, **Hebrews 2:11** regarding Christ calling believers "brethren," and **Daniel 9:5** about taking personal responsibility for national guilt.

The commentary concludes by comparing the gospel's dual nature—like the ark, it can be "a savour of death unto death" to some, yet "a saviour of life unto life" to those who receive it properly.
