# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 16: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes the restoration of proper worship after a period of neglect. He notes that while the ark's circumstances improved dramatically—from obscurity "in the fields of the wood" to the royal city where "all might resort to it"—this tent was still temporary, awaiting Solomon's temple. Significantly, Henry observes: "The church's poorest times were its purest," suggesting that external grandeur doesn't guarantee spiritual vitality.

The commentary highlights God's covenant faithfulness as central to worship. Henry stresses that the everlasting covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob remains "sure to the spiritual seed" and should be "the great matter of our joy and praise." He emphasizes God's universal sovereignty, calling believers to recognize "God's universal monarchy" as both "the fear and joy of all people."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian worship and living:

**Generous Hospitality**: David's distribution of food demonstrates that "those whose hearts are enlarged with holy joy should show it by being open-handed."

**Comprehensive Praise**: Worship should glorify God through multiple means—"thanksgivings," "prayers," "songs," and "discourse" about God's works. Henry advocates making known God's deeds "among the people" and declaring "his glory among the heathen."

**Daily Devotion**: He emphasizes showing forth "from day to day his salvation," noting "we daily receive the benefits of it, and it is a subject that can never be exhausted."

**Intercessory Prayer**: Even in celebration, believers must "remember our afflicted brethren, and pray for their salvation and deliverance as our own."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich exposition of the psalm's structure, noting it combines portions from Psalms 105, 96, 136, and 106. He sees this as biblical precedent for creating hymns from various psalm portions.

On verse 29's "beauty of holiness," he explains: "Holiness is the beauty of the Lord, the beauty of all sanctified souls and all religious performances."

Regarding God's protection of the patriarchs (verses 19-22), Henry emphasizes their identity as "anointed of the Lord" and "prophets," warning that harming them means touching "the apple of God's eye."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects this passage to other biblical texts:

- Links David's psalm to Psalm 132:4-5 regarding David's determination to establish the ark
- References Hebrews 13:15 on offering "fruit of the lips" rather than agricultural offerings  
- Connects to 1 Samuel 10:5 regarding worship in "schools of the prophets"
- Cites Psalm 69:31-32 about praise pleasing God more than animal sacrifices
- References 1 Kings 19:16 and Isaiah 61:1 on prophetic anointing

Henry concludes by noting the people's corporate "Amen," emphasizing communal participation in worship as both biblical pattern and practical necessity for genuine congregational praise.
