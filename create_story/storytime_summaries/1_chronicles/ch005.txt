# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles 5: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Sovereignty and Justice**: Henry emphasizes how God orchestrates both victory and judgment. The successful conquest of the Hagarites demonstrates God's faithfulness to those who trust Him, while the eventual Assyrian captivity reveals His justice against apostasy. "The war was of God" when undertaken in faith, but God equally "stirred up the spirit of the kings of Assyria" as instruments of judgment.

**Sin's Lasting Consequences**: Reuben's forfeiture of his birthright due to moral sin illustrates how "seventh-commandment sins especially leave an indelible stain upon men's names and families." Yet Henry notes that "the sullying of the honour of an Israelite is not the losing of his happiness" - discipline doesn't equal rejection.

**Grace vs. Natural Order**: The birthright's division between Joseph (double portion) and Judah (dominion) shows how God's purposes follow "not the course of nature, but the choice of grace," ultimately pointing to Messiah coming through Judah.

## Key Practical Applications

**Prayer and Trust in Conflict**: Henry draws powerful lessons from the Hagarite victory, noting how the tribes "cried to God in the battle" and "put their trust in him." He emphasizes that "in our spiritual conflicts, we must look up to heaven for strength; and it is the believing prayer that will be the prevailing prayer."

**Dangers of Compromise**: The trans-Jordanian tribes' eventual apostasy serves as warning. Living "upon the borders" and conversing "most with the neighbouring nations," they "learned their idolatrous usages." Henry warns that proximity to worldly influences without spiritual vigilance leads to compromise.

**God's Readiness to Hear**: Despite being driven to prayer by necessity, "God was entreated of them" - demonstrating that "so ready is he to hear and answer prayer."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry explains the complex birthright transfer, showing how God divided its benefits rather than giving all to one successor, foreshadowing Christ's ultimate rule through Judah.

**Verses 18-22**: The detailed military account becomes a template for spiritual warfare - emphasizing both human preparation (skilled warriors) and divine dependence (prayer and trust).

**Verses 25-26**: The tragic conclusion shows how covenant privileges become greater condemnation when abused. Henry notes the irony that God used pagan kings as instruments of judgment against His own apostate people.

## Cross-References to Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout: Genesis 49:4 (Reuben's curse), Genesis 49:10 (Judah's scepter), Micah 5:2 (Messiah from Judah), Hebrews 11:21 (Jacob's blessing), Exodus 23:30 (gradual conquest), Deuteronomy 20:15 (warfare permissions), and 2 Chronicles 13:14 (crying to God in battle).

The commentary masterfully connects genealogical details to broader theological truths, showing how even mundane tribal records reveal God's sovereign grace, the consequences of moral compromise, and the vital importance of maintaining dependence on God through prayer and faithful obedience.
