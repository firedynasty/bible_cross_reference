# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles 22: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes God's sovereign grace emerging from judgment. The chapter begins with the powerful image: **"Out of the eater comes forth meat"** - showing how God brings blessing from the terrible judgment that preceded. The temple's location on a former threshing floor belonging to a Jebusite becomes **"a happy presage of the setting up of the gospel temple among the Gentiles,"** prefiguring the church's expansion beyond Israel.

Henry views the temple as deeply typological, representing Christ **"in whom all fulness dwells and in whom are hid all treasures."** The contrast between David (man of war) and Solomon (man of peace) points to Christ, who builds the gospel temple **"not by destroying men's lives, but by saving them."** The commentary highlights how **"grace here has recovered the upper hand"** in David's heart, showing spiritual restoration after his pride in numbering Israel.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living. He emphasizes intergenerational responsibility: **"Those that are aged and experienced should consider those that are young and tender, and provide them what help they can."** David's preparation for Solomon models how experienced believers should ease the path for younger ones.

The principle **"Where God gives rest he expects work"** teaches that divine blessings create obligations for service. Henry stresses that even great religious works don't excuse moral compromise - Solomon **"must not think by building the temple to purchase a dispensation to sin."** 

The commentary concludes with balanced Christian effort: **"Hope of God's presence must not slacken our endeavours. Though the Lord be with us, we must rise and be doing."**

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On the temple's location (v.1), Henry notes that **"all the earth is his"** yet God chose ground belonging to a Gentile, prophetically significant for gospel expansion. Regarding David's disqualification (v.8), Henry clarifies this refers to justified warfare, not just Uriah's murder, showing **"how precious human life is to him."**

Henry interprets the enormous wealth figures (v.14) as possibly using **"hundreds and thousands...to express that which is very much"** rather than literal amounts, or including all dedicated treasures accumulated over time.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout. He links the threshing floor to Isaiah 21:10 (**"the church of the living God is his floor"**), connects the temple's magnificence to Psalm 68:29 about kings bringing gifts, and references Acts 15:16-17 regarding Gentile inclusion.

The Gentile workers recall Israel's deliverance **"from the bondage of making bricks in Egypt."** David's prayer for Solomon's wisdom (v.12) connects to Solomon's later request for understanding. The charge to **"be strong and of good courage"** echoes Joshua's commissioning, while the final exhortation parallels Philippians 2:12-13 about working out salvation as God works within.
