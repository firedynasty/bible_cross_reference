# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 9: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Judgment and Restoration**: Henry emphasizes that the Babylonian exile was divine judgment "for their transgression," noting it should "never be forgotten, but ever be remembered, for warning to posterity." He sees the return as God's faithfulness in restoration, where "the pieces of metal that had been separated will run together again when melted in the same crucible."

**Unity of God's People**: The commentary highlights how the exile brought together remnants from all twelve tribes, fulfilling prophecies in Hosea and Ezekiel about Israel and Judah being "gathered together" and becoming "one nation again."

**Divine Order in Worship**: Henry stresses that proper worship requires organization, noting "God is the God of order" and that effective service happens "when every one knows the duty of his place and makes a business of it."

## Key Practical Applications

**Leadership in Spiritual Renewal**: Henry praises that priests "came with the first," asking "Who should lead in a good work if the priests, the Lord's ministers, do not?" This establishes the principle that spiritual leaders should pioneer restoration efforts.

**Contentment with Simple Provisions**: Regarding the temporary tabernacle, he advises: "Those that cannot yet reach to have a temple must not be without a tabernacle, but be thankful for that and make the best of it. Never let God's work be left undone for want of a place to do it in."

**Dedication to Ministry**: Henry commends how Levites "lodged round about the house of God" because "the charge was upon them," noting "It is good for ministers to be near their work, that they may give themselves wholly to it."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 10-13 (The Priests)**: Henry particularly highlights those described as "very able men for the work of the service of the house of God," noting this service "required at all times, especially in this critical juncture... great courage and vigour of mind, as well as strength of body."

**Verses 17-27 (The Porters)**: Though seemingly a "mean office," Henry notes David preferred being a doorkeeper to dwelling "in the tents of wickedness" (referencing Psalm 84:10). Their duties included opening doors, keeping out the unclean, and directing worshippers.

**Verses 33-34 (The Singers)**: Henry observes they served "day and night" as "chief fathers of the Levites," making worship their primary business. This created continuous praise, making "the temple a figure of the heavenly one, where they rest not day nor night from praising God."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous scriptural connections throughout: Hosea 1:11 and Ezekiel 37:22 regarding national reunification; Psalm 84:10 about preferring God's house; 2 Corinthians 3:6 concerning "able ministers of the New Testament"; and Revelation 4:8 comparing earthly temple worship to heavenly praise.

The commentary presents this chapter as a blueprint for spiritual restoration, emphasizing that proper worship requires qualified leadership, organized service, and wholehearted dedication to God's house.
