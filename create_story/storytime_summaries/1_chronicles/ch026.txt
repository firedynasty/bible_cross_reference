# Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles 26

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes that the **restoration of proper religious order under David** marks a dramatic revival of the Levites' role after centuries of diminished influence. He notes that "when David revived religion the Levites were, of all men, in the greatest reputation." The commentary presents the temple organization as a type of heavenly realities, with "treasures of the house of God" symbolizing "the plenty there is in our heavenly Father's house" and pointing to Christ, "the true temple," where "are hid treasures of wisdom and knowledge."

Henry also stresses the **divine blessing upon faithful service**, particularly through Obed-edom's family, who received preferential treatment because they "entertained the ark with reverence and cheerfulness."

## Key Practical Applications

The commentary draws several practical lessons for Christian ministry and stewardship:

**Faithful Service Leads to Greater Responsibility**: "He that is trusty in little shall be trusted with more. He that keeps God's ordinances in his own tent is fit to have the custody of them in God's tabernacle."

**Gratitude for Victory Should Motivate Giving**: Regarding dedicated spoils of war, Henry writes, "The more God bestows upon us the more he expects from us in works of piety and charity. Great successes call for proportionable returns."

**Balance Between Sacred and Civil Duties**: Henry praises how the Levites managed both religious and governmental affairs, noting "It is happy with a kingdom when its civil and sacred interests are thus interwoven and jointly minded and advanced."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich detail about the **porters' qualifications and duties** (vv. 1-19), noting they were "mighty men of valour" and "strong men" who not only guarded gates but also instructed worshippers and excluded the unclean. He draws an analogy to ministers having "the keys to the kingdom of heaven committed to them."

Regarding **merit over birthright** (v. 10), Henry explains that leadership sometimes bypassed firstborn sons when others showed superior qualifications, applying specifically to temple service rather than inheritance rights.

The **treasurers' responsibilities** (vv. 20-28) receive attention as Henry details their careful stewardship of both daily provisions and dedicated war spoils from leaders including "Samuel and David, but Saul, and Abner, and Joab."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects the porters to **Matthew 16:19** (keys of the kingdom), the principle of faithful service to **1 Timothy 3:4-5,13**, and Abraham's tithe to Melchizedek (**Hebrews 7:4**). He references **Revelation 21:12** regarding angels at New Jerusalem's gates, **Numbers 31:50** for military offerings, and **Deuteronomy 21:16-17** concerning inheritance laws.

The commentary concludes by noting this organization occurred "in the fortieth year of the reign of David," emphasizing the importance of establishing good systems before one's departure: "We should be so much the more industrious to do good as we can see the day approaching."
