# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 11: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **divine sovereignty and covenant fulfillment** as the chapter's central theme. He observes that "God's counsels will be fulfilled at last, whatever difficulties lie in the way. If God had said, David shall rule, it is in vain to oppose it." This demonstrates God's faithfulness to His promises made through Samuel.

The commentary also highlights the **principle of covenant relationship** between ruler and people. Even though David "might have claimed an absolute despotic power," he chose to "make a covenant with the people" and "rule by law," establishing a model of accountable leadership rooted in mutual obligation.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for leadership and service:

**For Leaders:** David's example teaches that true greatness comes through serving others and ruling under divine authority rather than claiming absolute power. Leaders should show "tenderness of his servants" and avoid being "prodigal of the blood of those they employ."

**For Followers:** The mighty men exemplify that "in strengthening him they strengthened themselves and their own interest." Henry notes that "the way to be great is to do good" and emphasizes that honor comes through faithful service despite "labour and the hazard of their lives."

**For All:** David's water-pouring incident (verses 17-19) reveals four virtues: repentance for weakness, self-denial, devotion to God, and care for others' welfare.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry particularly emphasizes **David's refusal to drink the water** (vv. 17-19) as demonstrating mature leadership. David's declaration "shall I drink the blood of these men that have put their lives in jeopardy?" shows his recognition that leadership demands putting followers' welfare above personal desires.

Regarding the **conquest of Zion** (vv. 4-9), Henry notes that opposition often "quickens and engages resolution in great undertakings," while the "prospect of preferment" motivates service, observing that "take away honour out of the soldier's eye and you cut off the spurs from his heels."

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry connects this chapter to **Psalm 2:6** ("I have set my king upon my holy hill of Zion") and **2 Samuel 23:8** for the parallel account of David's mighty men. He references **Joshua 23:10** to explain the supernatural victories, noting that these achievements required "the extraordinary presence of God."

Significantly, Henry observes that foreign warriors like the Ammonite and Moabite among David's men (despite Deuteronomy 23:3's restrictions) prefigure how "the Son of David would have worthies among the Gentiles: with him there is neither Greek nor Jew."

The commentary concludes that God works "by men and means and the use of second causes," showing that while David's success came from "the Lord of hosts," it manifested through human instruments whom God providentially provided.
