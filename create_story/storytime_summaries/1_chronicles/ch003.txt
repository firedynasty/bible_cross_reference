# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 3 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Christ's Royal Lineage**: Henry emphasizes the supreme theological significance of David's genealogy as the family "from which, 'as concerning the flesh, Christ came.'" He distinguishes between two messianic lines: "The sons of Nathan were his fathers as man, the sons of Solomon his predecessors as king," highlighting both Christ's humanity and royal authority.

**Divine Providence in Succession**: Henry notes that Solomon's selection wasn't based on personal merit since "his wisdom was God's gift," but rather reflected divine will: "so, Father, because it seemed good unto thee." This demonstrates God's sovereign choice in establishing the messianic line.

**Messianic Expectation**: The commentary concludes with Jewish interpretations of Anani as a messianic figure, showing that "their minds were always full of the thoughts of the Messiah" and their expectation of his coming after Zerubbabel's era.

## Key Practical Applications

**Value Faithful Rebuke**: Henry draws a powerful lesson from David naming his son Nathan after the prophet who confronted his sin: "It seems he loved him the better for it as long as he lived. It is wisdom to esteem those our best friends that deal faithfully with us."

**Honor Through Relationship to Christ**: While David had nineteen sons who "raised many noble families," only Nathan and Solomon's lines receive detailed treatment because "these only had the honour to be related to the Messiah."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**David's Family Challenges**: Henry observes that several sons "were a grief to him, as Amnon, Absalom, and Adonijah; and we do not read of any of them that imitated his piety or devotion except Solomon."

**Naming Patterns**: The presence of duplicate names (two Elishamas, two Eliphelets) suggests "the two former were dead, and therefore David called two more by their names," contradicting superstitions about reusing names.

**Royal Succession Stability**: Henry marvels that "seldom has a crown gone in a direct line from father to son for seventeen descents together," attributing this to "the recompence of David's piety."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this genealogy to **Luke 3:31** for Christ's lineage through Nathan, **2 Samuel 3:2** and **5:14** for parallel accounts of David's sons, **Psalm 127:5** regarding children as arrows in a quiver, **Jeremiah 22:24** about Jeconiah as God's signet ring, **Haggai 2:23** concerning Zerubbabel's restoration, and **Daniel 7:13** regarding messianic interpretation.

The commentary transforms a simple genealogical list into a profound meditation on God's faithfulness in preserving the messianic line through both triumph and exile.
