# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles 18 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry presents David's military victories as the direct result of his spiritual devotion in the previous chapter, establishing a clear **cause-and-effect relationship between piety and prosperity**. He writes that "those that seek first the kingdom of God and the righteousness thereof, as David did, shall have other things added to them as far as God sees good for them."

The commentary emphasizes **God's sovereignty and preservation** throughout David's campaigns, noting twice that "the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went." Henry interprets these victories as **typological foreshadowing** of Christ's ultimate triumph, stating that "all opposing rule, principality, and power, be, at the end of time, put down by the Son of David."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws four primary applications for believers:

1. **Strategic relationships**: "It is our interest to make those our friends who have the presence of God with them." Like the king of Hamath seeking David's favor, we should align with those blessed by God.

2. **Stewardship and dedication**: "What God blesses us with we must honour him with." David's dedication of spoils to the temple demonstrates that "that is most truly and most comfortably our own which we have consecrated unto the Lord."

3. **Divine protection through faithfulness**: "Those who take God along with them whithersoever they go may expect to prosper."

4. **Purposeful leadership**: "God gives men power, not that they may look great with it, but that they may do good with it."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that David's reluctance to use captured horses (v. 4) demonstrated practical faith: "Being resolved not to trust to them, he would not use them," referencing Psalm 20:7.

Regarding Hadarezer's defeat while trying to establish his dominion (v. 3), Henry notes the irony: "frequently men lose their wealth and power when they think to confirm it."

On David's administration (v. 14), Henry commends that David "was not so intent on his conquests abroad as to neglect the administration of justice at home," making him "an eminent type of the Messiah."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout: Jacob's renewed vigor after his vision (Genesis 29:1), the vanity of horses for safety (Psalm 33:17), enemies gathering like sheaves (Micah 4:11-12), the tabernacle built from Egyptian spoils, and Isaiah 23:18 on consecrating wealth to the Lord. He concludes by connecting David's righteous rule to Christ, whose "sceptre of whose kingdom is a right sceptre."

The commentary effectively demonstrates how David's external victories flowed from internal spiritual commitment, providing timeless principles for faithful stewardship and leadership.
