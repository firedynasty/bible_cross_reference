# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Chronicles Chapter 15: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Proper Worship and Divine Order**: Henry emphasizes that "it is not enough that we do that which is good, but we must do it well—not enough that we seek God in a due ordinance, but we must seek after him, in a due order." The chapter demonstrates God's requirement for reverent adherence to His prescribed methods of worship.

**Learning from Correction**: Henry notes that "when we have suffered for our irregularities we must learn thereby to be more regular; then we answer the end of chastisement." David's acknowledgment that they previously "sought him not after the due order" (v. 13) exemplifies proper response to divine correction.

**Divine Assistance in Service**: The commentary highlights that God's help is essential even in duties within our natural capacity, particularly emphasizing that "in all our religious exercises we must particularly derive help from heaven."

## Key Practical Applications

**Preparation and Planning**: Henry observes David's methodical approach: preparing a proper place for the ark before moving it, demonstrating that "wherever we build for ourselves, we must be sure to make room for God's ark, for a church in the house."

**Leadership Responsibility**: To the Levites, Henry notes the principle that "those who are advanced above others in dignity should go before others in duty," emphasizing that greater position demands greater service and influence.

**Using Gifts for God's Glory**: Regarding skilled musicians, Henry applies the principle: "As every man has received the gift, so he ought to minister the same" (1 Pet. 4:10), encouraging believers to use their abilities for common good and teach others.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 12-13**: Henry emphasizes David's acknowledgment of error without excuse-making, showing that "wise and good men may be guilty of an oversight, which, as soon as they are aware of, they will correct."

**Verse 26**: The divine help given to the Levites demonstrates that "if we perform any religious duties so as to escape a breach, and come off with our lives, we must own it is God that helps us."

**Verse 29**: Regarding Michal's contempt, Henry notes that "her despising him did not make him at all despicable," teaching that godly zeal shouldn't be diminished by others' criticism.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry connects this passage to **Numbers 7:9** regarding the Kohathites carrying the ark on shoulders, **Hebrews 9:10** concerning Old Testament ceremonial ordinances, and **Acts 26:22** about divine assistance in service. He also references **Malachi 1:9** regarding consequences of priestly neglect and **Psalm 81:3** about trumpets at solemn feasts.

The commentary presents this chapter as demonstrating that "we serve a good master, who delights to have his servants sing at their work," emphasizing that "times of public reformation are, and should be, times of public rejoicing."
