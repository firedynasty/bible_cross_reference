# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Chronicles 25: Summary

## Main Theological Themes

Henry emphasizes that **worship music is prophetic ministry**. He notes that "singing the praises of God is here called prophesying," explaining that while not all musicians received visions, they sang prophetical psalms composed by prophets with the church's edification in mind. This elevates musical worship beyond mere performance to spiritual proclamation.

The commentary stresses the **divine ordering of worship**. Henry observes that casting lots prevented "strife for precedency, a sin which most easily besets many that otherwise are good people," demonstrating God's sovereignty in organizing His house where "small and great, teachers and scholars, stand alike before God."

## Key Practical Applications

**Worship as dedicated service**: Henry calls praise "a service" and musicians "workmen," noting that while praising God brings "greatest liberty and pleasure," it requires intentional effort. "We must take pains with our hearts to bring them, and keep them, to this work."

**Parental responsibility in spiritual training**: The commentary emphasizes how "children were under the hands of their father," providing "a good example to parents to train up their children...in the service of God, and particularly in praising him." 

**Authority's role in promoting religion**: Henry commends how David's royal orders supported worship, encouraging all leaders to "use their power for the promoting of religion, and the enforcing of the laws of Christ."

## Notable Insights

Henry provides a detailed breakdown of the lot-casting system, showing how God's providence worked through seemingly random selection to honor merit over seniority. He traces each musician's family lineage and position, demonstrating how "God increased some and preferred the younger before the elder."

The commentary addresses the role of musical instruments, noting they were appropriate for that dispensation but cautioning that "those who use such concerts for their own entertainment should...preserve them always free from any thing that savours of immorality or profaneness."

## Cross-References

Henry connects temple music to **1 Samuel 10:5 and 19:20** regarding prophesying through praise, and **1 Corinthians 11:4 and 14:24** for New Testament prophetic worship. He references **Ephesians 5:19** to show continuity with gospel-church psalmody, emphasizing making "melody with the heart...unto the Lord."

The commentary also cites **Amos 6:5** as a warning against trivializing what was once sacred, and **1 Timothy 2:8** to suggest that while sacrifices were centralized, "the psalms penned by David might be sung every where."

Henry concludes by emphasizing generational faithfulness: "Let all, in their day, do what they can for God and his church," as Samuel began what David perfected and Solomon completed.
