# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel Chapter 10 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes that the establishment of monarchy in Israel represents "a great work" where "much of God is seen." The anointing ceremony, though conducted privately "under a hedge," remains fully valid because "God's institutions are great and honourable, though the circumstances of their administration be ever so mean and despicable."

The commentary highlights divine sovereignty in leadership selection, noting that Saul is anointed not merely as king, but as "captain over his inheritance" - emphasizing his role as steward rather than absolute ruler. Henry sees the sacred unction as pointing toward "the great Messiah, or anointed one," who was "anointed with the oil of the Spirit...without measure."

The transformation of Saul's heart demonstrates God's enabling grace: "whom God calls he qualifies." Henry notes that God gave Saul "another heart" but questions whether he became "a new man," foreshadowing future spiritual failures.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for leadership and Christian living:

**Humility in Leadership**: Saul receiving bread from worshippers serves as "an admonition to him not to spend the wealth of his crown in luxury, but still to be content with plain food." Leaders must remember humble beginnings and "be humble and charitable to the poor."

**Submission to Divine Authority**: Even as king, Saul "must act under the direction of Samuel...The greatest of men must own themselves in subjection to God and his word." Henry emphasizes that Saul was "upon his good behaviour" and must follow divine guidance.

**Providence Recognition**: Henry encourages believers to "eye Providence in favourable conjunctures (though the matter be minute) and be encouraged to trust it in greater matters."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1**: The anointing with a "vial of oil" rather than a horn signifies that Saul's "kingdom would soon be cracked and broken," while the small quantity indicates "he had but little of the Spirit conferred upon him" compared to David.

**Verses 2-4**: The encounter at Rachel's sepulchre serves as "a lecture of his own mortality" - reminding the newly anointed king that "now that he had a crown in his eye must think of his grave."

**Verses 5-6**: The phrase "turned into another man" represents genuine transformation through God's Spirit, though Henry distinguishes between being "another man" versus "a new man."

**Verse 11**: The proverb "Is Saul also among the prophets?" demonstrates public amazement at spiritual transformation, showing how God's work in individuals becomes evident to their communities.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to several biblical texts:

- **Psalm 2:12** ("Kiss the Son") - relating to Samuel's kiss of allegiance
- **2 Kings 3:15** - regarding music's role in preparing hearts for the Spirit
- **Psalm 138:5** - about returning from worship with enlarged hearts
- **Chapter 13:11** - foreshadowing Saul's future failure at Gilgal

The commentary also references the broader typological significance, connecting the anointing to Christ as the ultimate "anointed one" and linking the schools of prophets to the later development of synagogues.

Henry concludes by emphasizing that spiritual gifts serve "much more for the confirming of faith than any thing wrought for us by his providence," highlighting the priority of internal transformation over external circumstances.
