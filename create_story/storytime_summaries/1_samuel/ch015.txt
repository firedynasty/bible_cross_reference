# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Samuel 15 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a definitive test of obedience, emphasizing that **God's justice, though slow, is certain**. He notes that "though divine justice strikes slowly it strikes surely," highlighting how God remembered Amalek's ancient sins against Israel during the Exodus. The commentary underscores that **partial obedience equals disobedience**—Saul's selective execution of God's command revealed his fundamental rebellion.

A central theme is the **superiority of obedience over religious ritual**. Henry emphasizes Samuel's famous declaration: "to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams" (v. 22). He argues that Saul essentially honored himself above God by keeping the best livestock while destroying only "the refuse."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**Divine accountability for leadership**: "Men's preferment, instead of releasing them from their obedience to God, obliges them so much the more to it." Leaders bear greater responsibility due to their position.

**Separation from God's enemies**: Commenting on the Kenites' departure, Henry warns "it is dangerous being found in the company of God's enemies, and it is our duty and interest to come out from among them."

**The danger of self-deception**: Henry highlights how Saul claimed "I have performed the commandment of the LORD" while evidence of his disobedience was literally audible in "the bleating of the sheep."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3**: Henry notes that making this "expressly the trial of his obedience did very much aggravate his disobedience." God's clear command eliminated any excuse.

**Verse 22**: This receives Henry's strongest emphasis as demonstrating that external religious acts cannot substitute for genuine obedience to God's will.

**Verse 23**: Henry explains that "rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft" because both reject God's authority, calling stubbornness equivalent to idolatry.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this narrative to multiple biblical passages: Exodus 17:8 (original Amalek conflict), Deuteronomy 25:18 (aggravated nature of their sin), Psalm 116:12 (obligation from God's favors), and Revelation 18:4 (coming out from among the wicked).

The commentary concludes that this chapter demonstrates how "the deceitfulness of the heart of man" meets "the strictness of the justice of God," serving as a warning about the consequences of selective obedience to divine commands.
