# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Samuel 29: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Providence and Deliverance**: Henry's central theme emphasizes how God graciously delivers David from an impossible moral dilemma without David's own scheming. He contrasts this with Saul's increasing perplexity when forsaken by God, noting that "David, who kept close to God, when he was in a strait was extricated and brought off by the providence of God, without any contrivance of his own."

**God's Faithfulness to the Upright**: Henry highlights God's faithfulness to those whose hearts remain upright despite poor decisions, referencing 1 Corinthians 10:13 that God "would not suffer him to be tempted above what he was able, but with the temptation made a way for him to escape."

## Key Practical Applications

**Consequences of Unwise Associations**: Henry warns against intimate relationships with the ungodly, noting David brought this crisis upon himself "by his own unadvisedness, in quitting the land of Judah, and going among the uncircumcised. It is strange if those that associate themselves with wicked people, and grow intimate with them, come off without guilt, or grief, or both."

**Maintaining Integrity Under Pressure**: David's situation illustrates the difficulty of maintaining integrity when caught between competing loyalties. Henry observes how David "conducted himself with a great deal of caution" and emphasizes believers' need to "walk in wisdom towards those that are without."

**Inoffensive Christian Conduct**: Christians should "behave themselves always so inoffensively as if possible to get the good word of all they have dealings with," as demonstrated by Achish's testimonial of David's character.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry describes David's "great strait" - unable to fight against Israel without becoming a traitor, yet unable to abandon his post without appearing cowardly and ungrateful.

**Verse 3**: The princes' question "What do these Hebrews here?" prompts Henry's observation that "A Hebrew is out of his place...when he is in the camp of the Philistines."

**Verse 9**: Achish's praise of David as "good in my sight, as an angel of God" demonstrates how "wise and good men will gain respect, wherever they go."

## Cross-References to Scripture

Henry connects David's deliverance to **1 Corinthians 10:13** regarding God providing escape from temptation. He references **Psalm 26:5** where David declares hatred for "the congregation of evil doers." The commentary also alludes to **Zechariah 12:8** regarding the house of David being "as the angel of the Lord."

## Conclusion

Henry concludes that despite the apparent disgrace, God's providence worked "wisely and graciously" for David's benefit, hastening his return to help his own people at Ziklag. He emphasizes that "the steps of a good man are ordered by the Lord," reminding readers that divine purposes often remain hidden until later revelation.
