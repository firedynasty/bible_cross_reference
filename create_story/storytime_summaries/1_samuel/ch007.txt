# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Samuel 7: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a pivotal moment of national spiritual revival under Samuel's leadership. The **eclipse and restoration of God's glory** forms the central theological framework—the ark's 20-year obscurity at Kirjath-jearim represents Israel's spiritual lethargy, while their eventual "lamenting after the Lord" signals genuine repentance.

Henry emphasizes that **true revival requires complete abandonment of idolatry**. Samuel's demand to "put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth" and "serve him only" reflects God's intolerance of divided loyalty. The commentary stresses that authentic repentance "strikes at the darling sin" and demands wholehearted devotion.

The **primacy of prayer in ministry** emerges as another key theme. Henry notes that "Ministers would profit their people more if they did but pray more for them," highlighting Samuel's intercessory role as crucial to Israel's deliverance.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers and ministers:

1. **Don't fear God's ordinances because of judgment on others**: The judgment at Beth-shemesh "should not make us afraid of the ordinances, but of profaning them."

2. **Value spiritual privileges**: "Those that know how to value God's ordinances cannot but reckon it a very lamentable thing to want them."

3. **Genuine conversion begins with longing for God**: "True repentance and conversion begin in lamenting after the Lord."

4. **Ministers must combine preaching with prayer**: Samuel's effectiveness came through both proclamation and intercession.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry finds it "very strange" that Samuel never restored the ark to its proper place, attributing this to "decay of holy zeal." The 20-year neglect demonstrates how "formal professors rest satisfied in a round of external performances, without any tokens of God's presence."

**Verse 3**: Samuel's reformation message required total commitment—Henry emphasizes that "you must be wholly for God, for him and no other, serve him only, else you do not serve him at all."

**Verse 6**: The water-pouring ceremony receives detailed interpretation as signifying: humiliation ("as water spilt upon the ground"), earnest prayer ("the soul is, in prayer, poured out before God"), and complete reformation (expressing "willingness to part with all their sins").

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves extensive scriptural connections throughout:

- **Jeremiah 25:6** - "Provoke me not, and I will do you no hurt" (regarding God's judgment)
- **Zechariah 12:10** - Looking to "him whom they had slighted" and mourning
- **Hebrews 9:23; 12:27** - The ark as "typical of Christ, and those good things to come which cannot be moved"
- **Acts 2-3** - Dr. Lightfoot's comparison of this revival to the early church conversion
- **Hosea 14:8; Isaiah 30:22** - Complete abandonment of idols

Henry describes this revival as "a matter and time as remarkable as almost any we read of in scripture," with "the great conversion, Acts 2 and 3" as "the only parallel to it." The commentary masterfully demonstrates how Old Testament events prefigure New Testament spiritual realities while providing timeless principles for genuine revival and reformation.
