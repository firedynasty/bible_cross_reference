# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel 27: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a study of spiritual failure in a godly man. David is described as "a man after God's own heart, and yet he had his faults, which are recorded, not for our imitation, but for our admonition." The central theological theme is the weakness of faith under pressure. Henry identifies **"the prevalency of David's fear, which was the effect of the weakness of his faith"** as the root problem.

When David declares "I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul," Henry responds: **"But, O thou of little faith! wherefore dost thou doubt? Was he not anointed to be king?"** This highlights the tension between divine promises and human doubt. Henry emphasizes that **"unbelief is a sin that easily besets even good men"** and that **"Long trials are in danger of tiring the faith and patience even of very good men."**

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from David's choices:

**On seeking counsel:** David made his decision "consulting his own heart only, and not the ephod or the prophet" - a warning against relying solely on human reasoning rather than seeking divine guidance.

**On humility:** David's modest request to Achish demonstrates that **"Those that would stand fast must not stand high; and humble souls aim not to dwell in royal cities."** Henry notes that **"There is nothing lost by humility and modesty"** and that **"Real advantages follow those that flee from imaginary honours."**

**On family responsibility:** Henry praises David for bringing his household, noting that **"Masters of families ought to take care of those that are committed to them, to protect and provide for those of their own house."**

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1:** Henry sees David's despair as forgetting God's promises despite having "all the reason in the world to trust the promises of God" and his "experience of the particular care Providence took of him."

**Verses 5-7:** David's request for Ziklag is praised as both prudent (seeking privacy for religious practice away from Philistine idolatries) and modest in its presentation to Achish.

**Verses 8-12:** Henry presents a nuanced view of David's campaign against the Geshurites, Gezrites, and Amalekites. He **"acquit[s] him of injustice and cruelty"** since these were nations "heaven had long since doomed to destruction," but **"cannot acquit him of dissimulation with Achish"** regarding his deceptive reporting.

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry references several scriptural connections:
- **1 Samuel 22:5** - God's previous command for David to remain in Judah
- **Joshua 15:31 and 19:5** - Ziklag's original assignment to Judah and Simeon
- **1 Chronicles 12:1-22** - Future gathering of supporters at Ziklag
- **Psalm references** - David's later penitential reflection: "Remove from me the way of lying"

Henry also draws a sobering contrast: **"We may blush to think that the word of a Philistine should go further than the word of an Israelite"** and that **"the city of Gath should be a place of refuge for a good man when the cities of Israel refuse him a safe abode."**

The commentary concludes with the prayer **"Lord, lead us not into temptation"** and **"Lord, increase our faith!"** - recognizing both human weakness and the need for divine strength in times of trial.
