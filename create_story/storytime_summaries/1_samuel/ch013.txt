# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel 13: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a cautionary tale about rejecting God's government. The central theme is the **consequences of disobedience and self-sufficiency**. He argues that Israel's desire for "a king like all the nations" has led to their downfall, noting that "never was the state of Israel further gone in a consumption than in this chapter."

The commentary emphasizes how **God withdraws His presence when people abandon Him**: "doubtless they left God, else he would not have left them." Henry sees Saul's failure as both a result and cause of Israel's spiritual decline, demonstrating that human leadership without divine guidance leads to disaster.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons about **leadership and obedience**:

1. **The danger of partial obedience**: Saul waited seven days but lacked patience to wait until the end of the seventh day, showing how "it is not sinning that ruins men, but sinning and not repenting."

2. **The folly of self-sufficiency**: Saul's presumption in offering sacrifice reveals how "he thought it not worth while to stay for a prophet of the Lord, either to pray for him or to advise him."

3. **The importance of integrity**: Henry warns that when "professors of religion cheat and over-reach...religion suffers by it, and is had in abomination with the Philistines."

4. **The weakness that comes from guilt**: "Guilt makes men cowards" - explaining why thousands of Israelites fled in fear.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides unique interpretations throughout. On verse 1, he suggests Saul was "ignorant and imprudent, and as unfit for business as a child of a year old." Regarding Jonathan's attack on the Philistine garrison (v. 3), he suspects it may have been treacherous, making Israel "stink with the Philistines."

He emphasizes the irony of Saul's excuses for his disobedience, noting how Saul tried to make his sin appear as "an instance of his piety," covering "his disobedience to God's command with a pretence of concern for God's favour."

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry connects this passage to several other scriptures, including:
- **2 Chronicles 26:16** (King Uzziah's presumptuous offering)
- **Psalm 3:6** (contrasting faith versus fear)
- **Chapter 12:15** (Samuel's earlier warnings)

He draws parallels between Saul's impatience and "the scoffers of the latter days" who think "the promise of Christ's coming is broken, because he does not come in their time."

## Conclusion

Henry concludes that this chapter demonstrates how "all their glory departed from them" when Israel cast off God's government. The commentary serves as a warning about the inevitable consequences of choosing human wisdom over divine direction, and the danger of justifying disobedience through religious pretense.
