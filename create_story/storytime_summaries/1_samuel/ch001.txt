# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel Chapter 1: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Samuel as "a child of prayer" in contrast to Samson as "a child of promise," emphasizing that **"what wonders are produced by the word and prayer."** He introduces God's title "Lord of hosts" (Jehovah Sabaoth) as particularly meaningful when Israel's armies were weak, showing God's sovereign command over all heavenly and earthly forces.

The commentary explores divine sovereignty in affliction, noting that **"the LORD had shut up her womb"** while simultaneously working through Hannah's prayers. Henry sees God's providence in bringing great servants into the world through various means - some appearing suddenly in full growth, others "accounted for from the birth."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for family life and devotion:

**Family Religion**: Ministers should be "patterns of family religion," with Elkanah exemplifying consistent worship despite family difficulties. Henry notes that **"if the devotions of a family prevail not to put an end to its divisions, yet let not the divisions put a stop to the devotions."**

**Marriage and Unity**: The polygamous arrangement serves as a cautionary tale about departing from God's original design. Henry observes that **"how much better does the law of God provide for our comfort and ease in this world than we should, if we were left to ourselves!"**

**Bearing Others' Burdens**: Criticizing Peninnah's cruelty, Henry emphasizes that **"we ought to bear one another's burdens, not add to them,"** particularly condemning those who take pleasure in grieving the sorrowful.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 3**: Henry highlights Elkanah's faithfulness despite corrupt priests (Hophni and Phinehas), demonstrating that personal duty to God shouldn't depend on others' failures.

**Verse 5**: The "worthy portion" given to Hannah represents Elkanah's deliberate show of love to compensate for her affliction, teaching that **"we should testify our affection to our friends and relations, by abounding in prayer for them."**

**Verse 6-7**: Peninnah's timing of provocations during worship visits reveals spiritual warfare, as **"the great adversary to our purity and peace is then most industrious to ruffle us when we should be most composed."**

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects Samuel's pre-birth narrative to Jeremiah 1:5 (**"Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee"**) and parallels with John the Baptist and Christ's births. He references the marriage conflicts of Abraham and Jacob, Malachi 2:13 regarding marital discord affecting worship, and Ephesians 4:11-12 about gospel ministry freedom from corrupt leaders.

The genealogical details connect to 1 Chronicles 6:33-34 and Ruth 1:2, establishing Samuel's Levitical heritage and possible connection to David's lineage.
