# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Samuel 28: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Saul's consultation with the witch of Endor as the culmination of his spiritual downfall, emphasizing **divine judgment and abandonment**. When "the LORD answered him not," Saul demonstrates complete apostasy by seeking counsel from hell after being rejected by heaven. Henry views this as God's final rejection: "there is a time when he will not be found" - Saul had passed beyond his "days of probation."

The commentary emphasizes the **consequences of disobedience** - Henry notes that "troubles are terrors to the children of disobedience" and traces Saul's terror to his guilty conscience over "the innocent blood of the priests which he had spilt." The theological principle that **sin leads to spiritual blindness** is evident in Saul's self-contradiction: he had executed witches but now consults one.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several sobering applications about **spiritual decline**. He warns against **halfhearted seeking of God**, noting Saul "enquired in such a manner that it was as if he had not enquired at all" - doing so "faintly and coldly, and with a secret design, if God did not answer him, to consult the devil."

The commentary warns against **hypocrisy in opposing sin**: "Many seem enemies to sin in others, while they indulge it in themselves. Saul will drive the devil out of his kingdom, and yet harbour him in his heart." Henry also emphasizes the danger of **procrastination in spiritual matters**: "Seek the Lord while he may be found."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry notes David's "ambiguous answer" to Achish showed wisdom in avoiding commitment while maintaining his position - "he keeps himself free from a promise to serve him and yet keeps up his expectation."

**Verse 5**: Saul's fear demonstrates how "a guilty conscience made him tremble at the shaking of a leaf." Henry emphasizes that had Saul "kept close to God, he needed not have been afraid at the sight of an army of Philistines."

**Verses 7-8**: Henry highlights the depths of Saul's degradation: "Never did Saul look so mean as when he went sneaking to a sorry witch to know his fortune," showing how "those that are led captive by Satan are forced to disparage themselves."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout his commentary:

- **Isaiah 26:16** - "Lord, in trouble have they visited thee" (regarding seeking God only in distress)
- **Ezekiel 14:3** - questioning whether God should answer those who harbor idols
- **1 Chronicles 10:14** - noting that Saul's inquiry was so halfhearted "it was as if he had not enquired at all"
- **Exodus 22:18** - the divine command that witches "must not be suffered to live"
- **Deuteronomy 18:11** and **Isaiah 8:19** - prohibitions against necromancy and "seeking for the living to the dead"

Henry's commentary presents this chapter as a cautionary tale about the inevitable progression of spiritual rebellion, emphasizing that "Be not deceived, God is not mocked" - those who persistently reject divine guidance will ultimately find themselves abandoned to their own destructive choices.
