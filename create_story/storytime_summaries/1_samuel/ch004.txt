# Matthew Henry Commentary Summary: 1 Samuel Chapter 4

## Main Theological Themes

Matthew Henry presents this chapter as the beginning fulfillment of prophecies against Eli's house, emphasizing that "such sinners God often makes quick work with." The central theological theme warns against **idolatrous reliance on religious externals**. Henry argues that Israel treated the ark superstitiously, "making an idol of the ark" rather than seeking God himself. He observes that "to worship the true God, and not to worship him as God, is in effect not to worship him at all."

Henry highlights the danger of trusting in **religious forms without spiritual substance**: "Carnal people triumph much in the external privileges and performances of religion...as if the ark, God's throne, in the camp, would bring them to heaven, though the world and the flesh should be upon the throne in the heart."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons. First, he emphasizes that **proper spiritual preparation must precede action**. Israel's attempt to fight the Philistines "would have succeeded better if they had first repented and reformed, and so begun their work at the right end."

Second, he warns against **quarreling with God's providence**. When Israel questioned "Wherefore has the Lord smitten us?" Henry notes this represents how "the foolishness of man perverts his way, and then his heart frets against the Lord" (Proverbs 19:3).

Third, Henry criticizes those who show "profound veneration" for religious symbols while ignoring spiritual realities, noting how people often display "fiery concern for the name of Christianity" while maintaining "profane contempt of the thing."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry suggests Israel should have consulted Samuel, whose counsel "might have stood them in more stead than the presence of the ark did," but they "despised his youth."

**Verses 3-4**: The elders' decision to fetch the ark demonstrates their fundamental misunderstanding. Henry notes they expected the ark to save them despite having "no proper means used to engage God to favour them" and lacking "any warrant to remove it from its place."

**Verses 10-11**: The devastating defeat—despite having "the better cause," "greater confidence," and "the ark of God with them"—proves that "external privileges will secure none that abuse them."

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry references several scriptural connections. He links Israel's defeat to the principle from Judges 13:1 regarding the Philistines' forty-year dominion. He connects their misuse of the ark to Deuteronomy 12:5,11, noting they violated God's intention that the ark remain in its chosen place.

The commentary emphasizes how this event fulfills earlier prophecies against Eli's house while foreshadowing Samuel's later ministry (chapter 7:3). Henry also draws parallels to the "Achan in the camp" principle, showing how sin gives enemies advantage.

**Memorable conclusion**: Henry notes these events were designed to make "the ears of those that heard them to tingle," serving as a sobering reminder that God's presence cannot be manipulated through religious ritualism divorced from genuine faith and obedience.
