# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Samuel 20: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a striking contrast between "supernatural love" (Jonathan's friendship) and the "unnatural hatred" of Saul from the previous chapter. The central theological theme is **covenant friendship as a reflection of divine love**. Jonathan's loyalty to David exemplifies Christ-like love, with Henry noting that "Thus Christ testifies his love to us: Ask, and it shall be done for you."

The commentary emphasizes **God's providence in relationships**, observing that "God hath set the one over against the other" - showing how God provides faithful friends to counter enemies. Henry also highlights the **permanence of godly covenants**, as Jonathan seeks to extend their friendship "from generation to generation," calling it "the kindness of the Lord" because it mirrors God's covenant faithfulness.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**On friendship**: Jonathan exemplifies true friendship as "a brother that was born for adversity" (Proverbs 17:17), showing that genuine friends "loved at all times" regardless of circumstances.

**On integrity in relationships**: David's willingness to release Jonathan from their covenant if he had done wrong demonstrates that "No honest man will urge his friend to do a dishonest thing for his sake."

**On family devotion**: Henry notes approvingly that David's family maintained "a yearly sacrifice...for all the family," showing they were "a very religious family, a house that had a church in it."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides memorable analysis of key verses:

On **verse 3** ("there is but a step between me and death"): Henry emphasizes David's use of double certainty - "As the Lord liveth...and as thy soul liveth" - showing both absolute divine truth and personal conviction.

On **verses 12-13**: Jonathan's oath-making demonstrates how "God has confirmed his promises to us, that we might have strong consolation" (Hebrews 6:17-18).

On **verse 14**: The phrase "kindness of the Lord" refers to covenant loyalty that mirrors divine faithfulness to "their seed" across generations.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves multiple biblical connections throughout:

- **Proverbs 17:17** on friendship that "loved at all times"
- **1 Corinthians 13:5** regarding charitable thinking toward parents
- **Hebrews 6:17-18** on God's confirmed promises
- **Judges 9:5** (Abimelech's violence) as what Jonathan feared for his descendants

The commentary also references Christ's promise that our requests "shall be done for you," connecting Jonathan's loyalty to divine love, and draws parallels to God's covenant faithfulness across generations.

Henry masterfully presents this chapter as both a model of human friendship and a picture of divine covenant love, showing how earthly relationships can reflect heavenly realities.
