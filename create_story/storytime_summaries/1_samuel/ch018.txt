# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel 18: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry identifies the central tension between God's providence and human envy. David experiences both triumph and trial, illustrating that "for it a man is envied" (Ecclesiastes 4:4) even when doing righteous work. Henry emphasizes divine sovereignty orchestrating events: God arranges for Saul to employ David despite being his eventual replacement, and for Jonathan to befriend David even though David would inherit his throne. This demonstrates how "David's way might be the clearer when his rival was his friend."

The commentary highlights the contrast between those led by God's Spirit versus evil spirits. David's wisdom and success flow from the Lord being "with him," while Saul's jealousy opens him to demonic influence, showing that "those that indulge themselves in envy and uncharitableness give place to the devil."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**Leadership Development**: "Those that hope to rule must first learn to obey." David's faithful service under Saul prepared him for future kingship, demonstrating that character formation precedes position.

**Handling Success**: Henry notes David's remarkable ability to bear sudden honor "without being lifted up above measure," observing that "it is more difficult to know how to abound than how to be abased."

**Response to Persecution**: David's reaction to Saul's murderous attempts exemplifies proper Christian response - he fled rather than retaliated, showing "the meekness and usefulness of God's persecuted people."

**True Friendship**: Jonathan's selfless love, stripping himself to clothe David, provides a picture of Christ's love: "Our Lord Jesus has thus shown his love to us, that he stripped himself to clothe us."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-4**: Henry emphasizes the supernatural quality of Jonathan and David's friendship, describing their souls as "knit" together because of shared faith and character rather than worldly interests.

**Verses 6-11**: The women's song praising David above Saul reveals how "proud men cannot endure to hear any praised but themselves." Saul's javelin attack while David played harp demonstrates the "brutishness and barbarity of their persecutors" versus the service of God's people.

**Verses 12-16**: David's consistent wise behavior despite opposition shows divine blessing, while Saul's increasing fear reveals his spiritual bankruptcy.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to multiple biblical texts:
- Ecclesiastes 4:4 on envy accompanying righteous work
- Deuteronomy 33:17 regarding numerical comparisons
- Psalm 25:19 reflecting David's experience of cruel hatred  
- Proverbs 29:10 contrasting how "the bloodthirsty hate the upright"

The commentary particularly emphasizes how Jonathan's generous gift to David prefigures Christ's self-emptying love for believers, making this historical narrative a type of gospel truth.

Henry concludes that this chapter shows how "all that aim at the crown of life must expect" opposition and difficulty, yet God's providence works through human hatred to accomplish His purposes.
