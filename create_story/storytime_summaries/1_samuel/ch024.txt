# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel 24: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a powerful demonstration of **divine grace versus human malice**. The central theological contrast shows "Saul seeking an opportunity to destroy David" while "David had a fair opportunity to destroy Saul, and, to his honour, he did not make use of it." Henry emphasizes that "David's sparing Saul's life was as great an instance of God's grace in him as the preserving of his own life was of God's providence over him."

A key theme is **respect for God's anointed authority**. David's restraint stems from recognizing Saul as "the LORD's anointed" - representing God's appointed order that must be respected regardless of personal suffering. Henry also highlights **God's sovereignty in providential circumstances**, noting how easily we misinterpret both God's promises and providences to justify wrongful actions.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian conduct:

**Tender Conscience**: When David's "heart smote him" for merely cutting Saul's robe, Henry notes: "It is a good thing to have a heart within us smiting us for sins that seem little; it is a sign that conscience is awake and tender, and will be the means of preventing greater sins."

**Loyalty Despite Mistreatment**: David's example teaches "servants and subjects to be dutiful and loyal, whatever hardships are put upon them" (referencing 1 Peter 2:18).

**Overcoming Evil with Good**: David "rendered good for evil" and was "herein both a type of Christ, who saved his persecutors, and an example to all Christians not to be overcome of evil, but to overcome evil with good."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that Saul's relentless pursuit despite needing David against the Philistines shows how "it is not enough for Saul that David is thus cooped up; he cannot be easy while he is alive."

On David's servants urging him to kill Saul, Henry notes how we misunderstand God's promises and providences: "God had assured David that he would deliver him from Saul, and his men interpret this as a warrant to destroy Saul."

David's respectful behavior even toward his persecutor demonstrates proper conduct: "giving honour to whom honour was due, and teaching us to order ourselves lowly and reverently to all our superiors, even to those that have been most injurious to us."

## Cross-References

Henry connects this passage to **Jeremiah 7:10** regarding Saul's persistent wickedness, **Canticles 1:7** about sheep sheltering, **Psalm 7:3-5** which David may have written during this period, and **1 Peter 2:18** on loyal service despite hardship. The ancient proverb "Wickedness proceedeth from the wicked" illustrates how character determines conduct and how evil ultimately destroys itself.
