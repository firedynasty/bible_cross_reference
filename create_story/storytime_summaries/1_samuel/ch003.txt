# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Samuel Chapter 3: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Samuel's calling as marking the revival of prophecy in Israel during a spiritually dark period. He emphasizes the theological significance that "the word of the LORD was precious in those days; there was no open vision," suggesting God had withdrawn prophetic revelation due to the nation's corruption. The commentary frames Samuel's calling as divine preparation for establishing "a more faithful priest" and prophet to replace Eli's corrupted lineage.

Henry stresses the sovereignty of God's calling, noting that "the call which divine grace designs to make effectual shall be repeated till it is so." This demonstrates God's persistent grace in pursuing those He chooses for service, despite human confusion or resistance.

## Key Practical Applications

**For Young People**: Henry draws extensive practical lessons about youth ministry and character development. Samuel "though but a child, ministered unto the Lord," providing an example that "shamed" Eli's rebellious sons. Henry advocates that young people should be "humble and diligent, which they will find the surest way to preferment. Those are fittest to rule who have learnt to obey."

**For Spiritual Leaders**: Eli's response models proper mentorship - despite being passed over for divine revelation, he "honestly" instructed Samuel in receiving God's message rather than acting enviously. Henry notes, "the elder should, without grudging, do their utmost to assist and improve the younger that are rising up."

**For Spiritual Receptiveness**: The commentary emphasizes proper posture for hearing God: "Then we may expect that God will speak to us, when we set ourselves to hearken to what he says." Henry applies this to Scripture reading and sermon hearing.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich detail on the calling sequence, noting Samuel's "industry and readiness" in repeatedly responding to what he thought was Eli's call. He observes that "Samuel did not yet know the LORD" experientially, though he knew Scripture, highlighting the distinction between intellectual and revelatory knowledge.

The progressive nature of the calling receives careful attention - Henry notes the fourth call came with visual manifestation ("stood and called") and was doubled ("Samuel, Samuel"), showing God's delight and ensuring recognition.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout: Job 33:14 on God speaking repeatedly though man doesn't perceive; Psalm 85:8 and Habakkuk 2:1 on preparing to hear God; Exodus 33:12 on God knowing by name; Genesis 22:1 on God calling Abraham; and Revelation 1:12 on seeing the voice that spoke.

The commentary connects Samuel's preparation through faithful service to Jesus's teaching about faithfulness in little things, and references David's night meditations (Psalm 63:6) as context for Samuel's wakeful state when called.
