# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel 14: Summary

## Main Theological Themes

**Divine Sovereignty and Providence**: Henry emphasizes God's absolute control over circumstances, noting how "infinite power which works without means, and that infinite goodness which gives without merit" transforms Israel's desperate situation. He highlights that "there is no restraint to the Lord to save by many or by few," demonstrating God's unlimited power regardless of human resources.

**Faith vs. Human Weakness**: The chapter contrasts Jonathan's bold faith with Saul's spiritual decline. Henry observes that Saul, "now that he had fallen under God's displeasure," exhibits weakness and fear, while Jonathan displays courageous trust in divine guidance.

**God's Covenant Faithfulness**: Despite Israel's failures, Henry notes that God remains faithful "for his great name's sake," referencing Samuel's earlier promise that "The Lord will not forsake his people."

## Key Practical Applications

**Trusting Providence in Decision-Making**: Henry commends Jonathan's method of seeking divine guidance through circumstances, showing how believers should "acknowledge him in all their ways, and seek unto him for direction, with full purpose of heart to follow it."

**Faith Acting on Uncertainty**: Jonathan's declaration "It may be the Lord will work for us" exemplifies how "an active faith will venture far in God's cause upon an it may be." Henry encourages believers that we may "encourage ourselves with hope that God will appear for us, though we have not ground on which to build an assurance."

**Avoiding False Religious Security**: Henry critiques Saul's superficial religious observances, noting how "it is common for those that have lost the substance of religion to be most fond of the shadows of it."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry particularly emphasizes Jonathan's covenant confidence (v. 6), explaining that the Philistines "are uncircumcised, and have not the seal of the covenant in their flesh, as we have." He sees this as Jonathan's assurance of God's protection.

Regarding Jonathan's strategic approach (vv. 8-10), Henry praises how he "resolved to follow Providence in the execution" while noting that "God has the governing of the hearts and tongues of all men" to accomplish His purposes.

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry connects Jonathan's faith to **Joshua 23:10** ("One man of you shall chase a thousand") and **Deuteronomy 32:30**, showing scriptural precedent for God's power through few against many.

He references **2 Chronicles 14:11** to reinforce the theme: "It is nothing with God to help, whether with many or with those that have no power."

The commentary also alludes to **Psalm 32:8** regarding God guiding "with his eye," and connects Saul's religious formalism to broader biblical warnings about empty ritualism versus genuine faith.

Henry concludes by calling Jonathan "a sweet imp out of a crab-stock," highlighting how God can raise up faithful servants even from compromised backgrounds, ultimately demonstrating divine grace working through human courage and faith.
