# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel 26: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes the corruption of unsanctified hearts and the sovereignty of God's providence. He notes how "unsanctified hearts lose the good impressions which their convictions have made upon them and return with the dog to their vomit!" regarding Saul's renewed pursuit of David despite previous reconciliation.

The central theological theme revolves around God's absolute control over circumstances. Henry describes the supernatural sleep as "a deep sleep from the LORD," demonstrating that "how easily can God weaken the strongest, befool the wisest, and baffle the most watchful!" This divine intervention protects David while creating a test of his character.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**On Temptation and Corruption**: He warns that "we have so much of the tinder of corruption in our own hearts" that we must pray "the sparks of temptation may be kept far from us, lest, if they come together, we be set on fire of hell."

**On Respect for Authority**: David's refusal to harm Saul demonstrates proper submission to divinely appointed authority. Henry emphasizes that "to resist him was to resist the ordinance of God" and that David "prefer[red] his conscience to his interest."

**On Trusting God's Timing**: Rather than taking matters into his own hands, David chose to "wait till God shall think fit to avenge him," trusting that "The Lord shall smite him" in due time.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich exposition on key passages:

**Verses 9-11**: David's declaration "The LORD forbid that I should stretch forth mine hand against the LORD's anointed" becomes a cornerstone principle. Henry notes David's concern was that "his innocence more than his safety."

**Verse 12**: The miraculous sleep demonstrates God's protection - "Sleep, when God gives it to his beloved, is their rest and refreshment; but he can, when he pleases, make it to his enemies their imprisonment."

**Verses 19-20**: David's appeal reveals his desire for reconciliation while maintaining his innocence, comparing himself to "a flea" and "a partridge in the mountains."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to several biblical texts:

- **Psalm 76:5-6** regarding God's power over the mighty
- **Romans 11:8** on the "spirit of slumber" 
- **Romans 13:2** concerning resistance to God's ordained authority
- **Chapter 24** for parallel circumstances of David sparing Saul

Henry draws parallels to Gideon's bold venture and references the Nabal incident as precedent for trusting God's timing in judgment.

The commentary concludes with Saul's temporary conviction and renewed acknowledgment of David's righteousness, though Henry implies this repentance, like the previous occasion, would prove temporary. The overarching message emphasizes patient endurance under persecution while maintaining moral integrity and trust in divine providence.
