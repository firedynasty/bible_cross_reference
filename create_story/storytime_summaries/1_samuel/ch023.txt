# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Samuel 23: Summary

## Main Theological Themes

Matthew Henry interprets this chapter through the lens of **divine providence and protection**. David serves as a **typological figure of Christ**, who "not only ventured his life, but laid down his life, for those that were his enemies." The commentary emphasizes God's sovereignty over human affairs, showing how He "knows not only what will be, but what would be if it were not prevented." Henry presents the central theme that "Many are the troubles of the righteous, but the Lord delivereth them out of them all."

The **contrast between Saul's rejection and David's divine favor** runs throughout, with Henry noting how "the departure both of God and David from Saul" encouraged enemy attacks on Israel.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**Seeking Divine Direction**: David's repeated inquiries of the Lord demonstrate that "it is our duty, and will be our case and comfort, whatever happens, to acknowledge God in all our ways and to seek direction from him." Henry applies this by saying, "We have the scriptures, those lively oracles, in our hands; let us take advice from them in doubtful cases. 'Bring hither the Bible.'"

**Public Service Despite Personal Persecution**: David's willingness to defend Keilah while fleeing from Saul exemplifies that believers should "render good for evil." Henry criticizes those "unlike to David who sullenly decline to do good because they have not been so well considered as they deserved."

**Trusting God's Providence**: When human allies prove unreliable (as Keilah's inhabitants would have), believers must remember that God "delivered him out of his hand, as a bird out of the snare of the fowler."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry highlights David's **generosity and public-spiritedness** (vv. 1-6), noting his concern for national security despite personal danger. Regarding Saul's presumption (vv. 7-8), Henry warns against thinking "that one smiling providence either justifies an unrighteous cause or secures its success."

The **Jonathan-David encounter** (vv. 16-18) demonstrates true friendship, with Jonathan strengthening "his hand in God" rather than merely offering human comfort.

## Cross-References

Henry connects this passage to **Psalms 35:12 and 109:4** regarding David's treatment, and **John 10:32** showing how Christ similarly received evil for good. He references **Psalm 140:8** as David's prayer against Saul's schemes. The commentary also links back to **1 Samuel 22:5** explaining why David was directed to Judah.

Henry concludes by emphasizing God's perfect knowledge of human hearts and circumstances, providing both comfort for the faithful and warning for the wicked about divine justice.
