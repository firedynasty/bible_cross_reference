## Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Samuel 25: Summary

**Main Theological Themes and Interpretations**

Henry emphasizes God's providence in orchestrating events during David's wilderness period. The death of Samuel removes David's greatest earthly supporter, yet God provides through unexpected means. The narrative contrasts divine wisdom with human folly, showing how God uses even difficult circumstances to accomplish His purposes. Henry notes the irony that "so great a churl as Nabal abounds and so great a saint as David suffers want," demonstrating that earthly prosperity doesn't reflect spiritual worth.

The commentary highlights themes of restraint and divine justice. David's initial anger represents human nature, while his acceptance of Abigail's counsel shows wisdom and submission to God's timing. Henry sees God's hand in Nabal's natural death, sparing David from bloodshed and maintaining his integrity as God's anointed.

**Key Practical Applications**

Henry draws several practical lessons:
- **On faithful ministers**: "Those have hard hearts who can bury their faithful ministers with dry eyes, who are not sensible of the loss of those who have prayed for them and taught them the way of the Lord."
- **On wealth without wisdom**: "Riches are common blessings, which God often gives to Nabals, to whom he gives neither wisdom nor grace."
- **On marriage**: Many wise women are "tied to a Nabal," requiring great understanding to navigate such relationships.
- **On humility in need**: David's willingness to humble himself shows that even anointed leaders must sometimes accept difficult circumstances.

**Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights**

Henry provides rich character analysis, particularly of Nabal, whose name means "fool." He notes the tragic irony that Nabal inherited Caleb's estate but not his virtues: "He was a disgrace to his family, and then it was no honour to him." 

Regarding Abigail, Henry observes that "Many an Abigail is tied to a Nabal; and if it be so, be her understanding, like Abigail's, ever so great, it will be little enough for her exercises."

On David's request, Henry notes the courtesy and modesty, though he critiques David's address to "him that liveth in prosperity" as perhaps too complimentary for such a "muck-worm."

**Cross-References and Biblical Connections**

Henry connects this narrative to broader scriptural themes:
- References Psalm 120:5 regarding David dwelling "in the tents of Kedar"
- Links to Israel's wilderness wanderings in Paran for encouragement
- Connects to 1 Timothy 5:6 about those who "live in pleasure" being "dead while they live"
- References Joshua 14:14 and 15:54-55 regarding Caleb's inheritance
- Alludes to Psalm 37:25 about not seeing the righteous forsaken

The commentary presents this chapter as demonstrating God's faithful provision and protection of His anointed, even through human instruments and difficult circumstances.
