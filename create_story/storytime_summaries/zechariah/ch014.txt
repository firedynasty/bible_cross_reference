# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Zechariah 14: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry views Zechariah 14 as a prophetic revelation of "the day of the Lord" that encompasses both immediate and eschatological fulfillment. He interprets this day as bringing "both judgment and mercy, mercy to his church, judgment to her enemies and persecutors." The commentary emphasizes God's sovereignty in using enemies as instruments of refinement before ultimately judging them.

A central theme is the **breaking down of the Jewish-Gentile divide**. Henry interprets the Mount of Olives cleaving (v. 4) as symbolic of the "partition-wall between Jews and Gentiles" being removed, creating "a broad way of communication opened between Jerusalem and the Gentile world" through the gospel.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers:

**Trust in God's Providence**: He encourages faith during mixed circumstances, noting that believers live "between hope and fear" in a world that is "neither clear nor dark." The promise that "at evening time it shall be light" offers hope when "things are at the worst."

**Refuge in Christ**: The imagery of fleeing to "the valley of the mountains" represents fleeing "to Christ, in whom alone we can stand fast and be easy." Believers should flee "from the wrath to come" as urgently as "from an earthquake."

**Expectation of Christ's Return**: Henry emphasizes that "every particular believer" should "triumph in the expectation of his coming" when "all the saints shall be with thee."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry sees this as God gathering nations against Jerusalem for refinement, comparing it to putting "gold into the furnace" where the refiner "stands by it, and has his eye upon it."

**Verse 4**: The Mount of Olives reference connects literally to Christ's ascension - "the last place on which his feet stood on this earth."

**Verses 6-7**: These describe God's "strangely mixed" providences - neither perfect peace nor constant trouble, but Henry assures that it is "one day which shall be known to the Lord" who "orders and disposes of all for the best."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects Zechariah's prophecy to other biblical passages:

- **Isaiah 40:4** - mountains made low to prepare the Lord's way
- **Ephesians 2:14, 18** - breaking down the "middle wall of partition" between Jews and Gentiles
- **Acts 1:12** - literal fulfillment through Christ's ascension from the Mount of Olives
- **Matthew 24:37** - the "coming of the Son of man"
- **Romans 1:18** - fleeing from "the wrath of God from heaven"

## Conclusion

Henry presents Zechariah 14 as ultimately hopeful despite describing severe judgments. He emphasizes that while God's people may experience refinement through suffering, "judgment shall not make a full end there." The chapter culminates in the promise of gospel light breaking forth globally, uniting all believers in Christ's eternal kingdom.
