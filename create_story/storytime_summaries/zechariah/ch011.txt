# Matthew Henry Commentary on Zechariah Chapter 11 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a prophetic shift from promise to judgment. The central theme is **God's abandonment of the Jewish nation** following their rejection of the Messiah. Henry interprets the passage as Christ serving as both judge and shepherd, making "a trial yet made whether their ruin might be prevented by sending Christ among them as a shepherd."

The prophet serves as **a type of Christ**, demonstrating how "for judgment Christ came into this world" (John 9:39). The broken shepherd's staffs symbolize the dissolution of God's covenant relationship - "Beauty" representing God's covenant with all people, and "Bands" representing the unity between Judah and Israel.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several sobering applications:

**On Leadership Responsibility**: "It is sad with a people when those who should be as shepherds to them are as young lions to them." He warns that corrupt leaders who "abuse their power" will face divine judgment.

**On Self-Justification in Sin**: Those with "minds woefully blinded" who "do ill and justify themselves in doing it" face particular condemnation. Henry notes: "God will not hold those guiltless who hold themselves so."

**On False Gratitude**: Leaders who thank God for gains from oppression "mock him by making the gains of sin the gift of God, who hates robbery for burnt-offerings."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3**: The destruction imagery of Lebanon's cedars represents Jerusalem's fall. Henry notes a Jewish tradition that the temple gates opened spontaneously forty years before its destruction, fulfilling "Open thy doors, O Lebanon!"

**Verses 4-6**: The "flock of slaughter" describes Israel's desperate condition under corrupt rulers who "justified themselves" while showing no pity for God's people.

**Verses 12-13**: The thirty pieces of silver directly prefigures Judas's betrayal. Henry emphasizes the contempt shown: "a goodly price that I was prised at of them."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects this prophecy to its New Testament fulfillment, particularly **Matthew 21:37** ("They will reverence my Son") and **John 10:1-11** (the Good Shepherd discourse). He references **Jeremiah 5:31** regarding people loving corrupt leadership, and **Matthew 15:9** on teaching human commandments as doctrine.

The commentary demonstrates Henry's conviction that Old Testament prophecy finds its ultimate fulfillment in Christ's ministry and the subsequent destruction of Jerusalem in 70 AD, serving as divine vindication of the rejected Messiah.
