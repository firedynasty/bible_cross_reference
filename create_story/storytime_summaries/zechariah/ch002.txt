# Matthew Henry Commentary on Zechariah Chapter 2: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a vision of Jerusalem's restoration with profound **Christological significance**. The "man with a measuring line" is interpreted as "the man Christ Jesus," who serves as the "master builder of his church" (Heb. 3:3). The vision symbolizes both the literal restoration of Jerusalem and the spiritual expansion of the gospel church through "the admission of the Gentiles."

The central theological promise is **divine protection and glory**: "I, saith the LORD, will be unto her a wall of fire round about, and will be the glory in the midst of her" (v.5). Henry emphasizes that God himself becomes both protector and the source of true greatness for his people.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws three primary applications:

**1. Exhortation to Action**: Just as Jews in Babylon were called to return despite comfort in captivity, Christians must "deliver ourselves" from spiritual bondage. When "Christ has proclaimed deliverance to the captives," we must "loose ourselves from the bands of our necks" and resolve "that sin shall not have dominion over us."

**2. Separation from the World**: "What communion can light have with darkness?" Those remaining in Babylon risk "partaking with the daughter of Babylon both in her sins and in her plagues." Christians must "save themselves from the untoward generation of this world."

**3. Trust in Divine Provision**: God's promises should "engage us, whatever it cost us, to join ourselves to them and cast in our lot among them." Material comfort should never keep believers from spiritual duty.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry sees the measuring as preparation for expansion, noting that "when multitudes flock to Jerusalem it is time for her to enlarge the place of her tent." God "will take care that, when ever so many guests are brought in to the wedding supper, still there shall be room."

**Verse 4**: Jerusalem "shall be inhabited as towns without walls" - expanding "as freely as if it had no walls at all, and yet shall be as safe as if it had the strongest walls."

**Verse 8**: The phrase "apple of his eye" demonstrates that "he that toucheth you toucheth the apple of his eye" - showing God's intimate protection of his people.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves extensive biblical connections throughout: Isaiah 54:2 and 60:4 (church expansion), Luke 14:22 (room at the feast), Psalm 125:2 (mountains round about), Revelation 18:4 ("Come out of her, my people"), Acts 2:40 (separating from untoward generation), and Hebrews 3:3 (Christ as builder).

He particularly connects this vision to the "New Jerusalem" and "my Father's house above" with "many mansions," showing the eternal dimension of God's promises.

**Conclusion**: Henry presents this chapter as both historical promise and spiritual pattern - God expands, protects, and glorifies his people while calling them to active participation in their own deliverance from worldly entanglement.
