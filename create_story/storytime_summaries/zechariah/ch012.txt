# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Zechariah 12: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry interprets Zechariah 12 as primarily addressing the "gospel-Jerusalem"—the Christian church rather than literal Jerusalem. Drawing from Galatians 4:25-26, he distinguishes between the earthly Jerusalem "in bondage" and the spiritual Jerusalem "from above, that is free." The chapter presents God's covenant protection of His people through divine sovereignty, emphasizing that the Creator who "stretches out the heavens" and "forms the spirit of man within him" has both authority and ability to fulfill these promises.

The overarching theme centers on divine reversal: enemies who attack God's people will find their efforts result in their own destruction, while the church receives supernatural protection and strengthening.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers:

1. **Holy Security**: Christians can enjoy confident peace knowing that attacks on the church ultimately backfire on the attackers—"Jerusalem is in safety, and those are in all the danger who fight against it."

2. **Divine Strength for the Weak**: God will make "he that is feeble among them at that day shall be as David; and the house of David shall be as God" (v. 8), encouraging believers that weakness becomes strength through divine empowerment.

3. **Contemporary Relevance**: These promises "are still to be improved in every age for the directing of our prayers and the encouraging of our hopes with reference to the gospel-church."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1**: The "burden of the word of the Lord" is "heavy burden to all the church's enemies" but comfort "for Israel." Like the pillar of cloud and fire, God's word presents "a bright side towards the Israelites" but "a black side towards the Egyptians."

**Verses 2-3**: Henry uses vivid metaphors—Jerusalem becomes a "cup of trembling" that enemies cannot drink and a "burdensome stone" that crushes those who try to move it. He references historical examples like Alexander the Great's encounter with the high priest.

**Verse 6**: The "hearth of fire among the wood" imagery shows how God's justice makes His people instruments of judgment, though "it is God's wrath, and not theirs, that is the fire which devours."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects this passage to broader biblical themes:

- **Galatians 4:25-26** for the Jerusalem typology
- **Psalm 48:3-6** regarding enemies trembling before Jerusalem
- **Daniel 2:35, 45** for the stone imagery and God's kingdom
- **Matthew 21:44** linking to Christ as the cornerstone
- **Revelation 11:5** for fire consuming enemies of God's witnesses
- **Isaiah 25:4, 27:4, 31:9** for fire and protection imagery

Henry's interpretation transforms ancient promises into enduring spiritual realities, showing how Old Testament prophecies find fulfillment in the church age while maintaining practical relevance for contemporary believers facing opposition.
