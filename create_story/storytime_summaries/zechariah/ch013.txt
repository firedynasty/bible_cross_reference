# Matthew Henry Commentary on Zechariah 13: Summary

## Main Theological Themes

Henry centers this chapter on **Christ as the Lamb of God who takes away sin**. He identifies two primary works of Christ: removing sin's **guilt through his blood** (justification) and breaking sin's **dominion through his grace** (sanctification). The commentary emphasizes that "where he gives the one no doubt he gives the other" - justification and sanctification are inseparable.

The **fountain opened** (v.1) represents "the pierced side of Jesus Christ" from which "came there out blood and water, and both for cleansing." Henry interprets this as God's inexhaustible mercy - "a fountain; for there is in them an inexhaustible fulness."

## Purification and False Prophecy

Henry sees the eradication of **idolatry and false prophecy** as gospel-era reforms. He notes this was fulfilled in the Jews' post-exile aversion to idols and spiritually when "souls are brought off from the world and the flesh, those two great idols."

Regarding false prophets, Henry emphasizes the **severity of spiritual deception**. He warns that "the unclean spirit" works through false prophets, and true reformation requires banishing both together. The extreme measure of parents prosecuting their prophetic children demonstrates that "we ought to conceive...a very great detestation and dread of every thing that would draw us out of the way of our duty."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

- **True repentance requires confession**: Those who deceive others "will be forward to confess their sin, and will be so just as to rectify the mistakes which they have been the cause of"
- **Return to proper calling**: When convicted of error, "we must evince the truth of our repentance by returning to it again"
- **Value corrective discipline**: "Faithful are the wounds of a friend" - harsh correction from loved ones can prevent greater judgment
- **Prioritize spiritual loyalties**: "Natural affections, even the strongest, must be over-ruled by gracious affections"

## Notable Christological Interpretation

Henry offers a striking alternative interpretation of verses 6's "wounds in thine hands." While acknowledging the false prophet reading, he suggests these may be "the words of that great prophet" - **Christ himself**. Jesus "received them in the house of his friends--the Jews, who should have been his friends" but crucified him as a deceiver.

## Cross-References

Henry extensively connects this passage to: **1 John 3:5** (Christ manifested to take away sin), **1 John 1:7** (blood cleanses from all sin), **Revelation 1:5-6** (washed from sins in his blood), **Hosea 2:17** (idols not remembered), **Deuteronomy 13:6-11** (prosecuting false prophets), **Proverbs 27:6** (faithful wounds of a friend), and **Acts 19:18** (converted magicians confessing deeds).

The commentary masterfully weaves together themes of **cleansing, reformation, and the cost of spiritual truth**, culminating in Christ's redemptive suffering.
