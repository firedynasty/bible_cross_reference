## Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on Zechariah Chapter 6

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry divides this chapter into two kingdoms: **providence and grace**. He emphasizes that "all our temporal affairs being in a necessary subjection to divine Providence, and all our spiritual and eternal concerns in a necessary dependence upon divine grace." The vision of four chariots represents God as "King of nations," while Joshua's coronation symbolizes God as "King of saints."

Henry interprets the four chariots as representing **God's administration of divine Providence** rather than specific monarchies. The colored horses symbolize different aspects of God's providential work: red signifying war and bloodshed, black representing "dismal melancholy consequences," white indicating "the return of comfort, and peace, and prosperity," and grizzled/bay horses showing "events of different complexions interwoven."

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

1. **Submission to God's Providence**: "We should see them all coming from between the mountains of brass, and therefore see it as much our folly to quarrel with them as it is our duty to acquiesce in them."

2. **Balanced Perspective on Life's Circumstances**: He encourages believers to "labour to accommodate ourselves to God's will and design in the mixtures of Providence, rejoicing in our comforts as though we rejoiced not...and weeping for our afflictions as though we wept not."

3. **Trust in God's Timing**: After dark providences (black horses), comfort follows (white horses), "for as affliction abounds consolation much more abounds."

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

- **Verses 1-5**: The mountains of brass represent God's immovable counsels and decrees, "for he is in one mind, and who can turn him?"

- **Verse 5**: Angels are "four spirits of heaven" who "go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth" as ministers of God's providence.

- **Verse 8**: God's spirit is "quieted" when His providence accomplishes His purposes, showing divine satisfaction in completed justice and mercy.

- **Verses 12-13**: Joshua's coronation prefigures Christ as "The BRANCH" who uniquely combines priestly and kingly roles, with "the counsel of peace...between them both."

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects this passage to other biblical texts:
- **Psalm 68:17, 18:10**: Angels as God's chariots
- **Revelation 6:2, 4, 5, 8**: Colored horses representing divine judgments
- **Daniel 7:2**: Four winds and monarchies
- **Acts 7:45**: Joshua/Jesus name connection
- **Psalm 75:8**: Providence as a "cup...full of mixture"

Henry emphasizes that Joshua the high priest serves as a "type of Christ," noting the significance that both major Joshuas in scripture (the military leader and the priest) shared the same name as Jesus, prefiguring Christ as both "captain of our salvation" and "high priest of our profession."

The commentary concludes with the promise that distant peoples will participate in temple building, contingent upon obedience to God's voice, showing the universal scope of God's redemptive plan.
