# Matthew Henry Commentary on Joshua 18: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Providence and Sacred Order**: Henry emphasizes God's sovereign direction in establishing worship and distributing inheritance. The placement of the tabernacle at Shiloh was divinely guided, likely through "the judgment of Urim," demonstrating that God chooses where His name will dwell. The casting of lots represents sacred reliance on divine will rather than human preference.

**Typological Significance**: Henry sees profound symbolism in Shiloh's name, connecting it to Jacob's Messianic prophecy (Genesis 49:10). He suggests this placement "gave them a hint that in that Shiloh whom Jacob spoke of all the ordinances of this worldly sanctuary should have their accomplishment in a greater and more perfect tabernacle." The earthly tabernacle points forward to Christ's eternal priesthood.

**Spiritual Lethargy as Universal Problem**: The seven tribes' delay becomes a mirror for Christian complacency. Henry draws the parallel: "God by his grace has given us a title to a good land, the heavenly Canaan, but we are slack to take possession; we enter not into that rest, as we might by faith, and hope, and holy joy."

## Key Practical Applications

**Priority of Worship**: Henry notes approvingly that establishing the tabernacle was Israel's "first care" once they had security. This demonstrates proper priorities: "It was a good presage of a comfortable settlement to themselves in Canaan, when their first care was to see the ark well settled."

**Danger of Spiritual Procrastination**: Joshua's rebuke "How long are ye slack?" applies to believers who delay spiritual progress. Henry warns that "many are diverted from real duties, and debarred from real comforts, by seeming difficulties."

**Active Faith Required**: The surveying mission required courage and faith, as the men risked their lives among hostile Canaanites. Henry observes: "When we are in the way of our duty we are under the special protection of the Almighty."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1** - The tabernacle's establishment at Shiloh marked a permanent transition from wilderness wandering to settled worship, with "not one of the stakes thereof must any more be removed" (Isaiah 33:20).

**Verses 2-3** - The seven tribes' reluctance stemmed from comfort with the status quo and fear of hardship. They were "too well pleased with their present condition" and "slothful and dilatory."

**Verses 4-9** - The surveying process demonstrates systematic planning under divine guidance. The surveyors showed remarkable "faith and courage" in dangerous territory, protected by "good providence of God."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves extensive biblical connections throughout:
- **Genesis 49:10** - Jacob's prophecy of Shiloh as Messianic reference
- **Deuteronomy 12:11** - God's promise to choose where His name dwells
- **Psalm 46:5** - God in the midst of His people
- **Hebrews 9:1,11** - Earthly sanctuary pointing to heavenly reality
- **Ecclesiastes 11:4** - Paralysis from observing obstacles
- **John 17:2-3** - Christ as our Joshua dividing heavenly inheritance
- **Jeremiah 7:12; Psalm 78:60** - Shiloh's eventual destruction as warning

Henry's commentary transforms this administrative chapter into rich theological reflection, showing how Israel's inheritance distribution prefigures Christian spiritual inheritance and warning against the universal tendency toward spiritual complacency that delays our full possession of gospel promises.
