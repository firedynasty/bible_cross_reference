# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua 23: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Faithfulness and Providence**: Henry emphasizes God's unwavering faithfulness in fulfilling His promises to Israel. The commentary highlights that "the LORD your God is he that hath fought for you," demonstrating God as the true source of Israel's victories rather than their own strength or Joshua's leadership.

**Covenant Relationship**: The central theme revolves around Israel's covenant obligations to God. Henry frames Joshua's farewell as designed "to entail on posterity a sense of religion and their duty to God," emphasizing that spiritual fidelity takes precedence over political or economic concerns.

**Warning Against Apostasy**: Henry interprets the chapter as a solemn warning against the dangers of compromise with paganism, viewing idolatry as the primary threat to Israel's covenant relationship with God.

## Key Practical Applications

**Leadership Responsibility**: Henry draws from Joshua's example that aging leaders should use their remaining time wisely: "When we see death hastening towards us, this should quicken us to do the work of life with all our might."

**Influence of Leadership**: He emphasizes that "if great men be good men, they will help to make many good." Leaders bear special responsibility for maintaining spiritual standards in their communities.

**Separation from Corrupting Influences**: Henry advocates complete separation from idolatrous practices, stating that Israelites "could not contract any intimacy nor keep up any conversation with them, without danger of infection."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry notes the strategic timing - long enough for Israel to "feel the comforts of their rest" but also for Joshua to "observe which ways their danger lay of being corrupted."

**Verse 10**: On "One man of you shall chase a thousand," Henry explains this as demonstrating God's supernatural empowerment: "how many do you reckon him for?" when God fights for His people.

**Verse 7**: Henry provides detailed exposition on avoiding idolatry, including not even mentioning pagan gods' names, noting that "among Christians the names of the heathen gods are so commonly used" as problematic.

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry connects Joshua's role as aging leader to **2 Peter 1:13**, comparing Joshua's urgency to Peter's desire to remind believers of their duties before death.

He references **Exodus 15:3** to establish continuity of God as "a man of war" from the Exodus through the conquest.

The commentary links to **1 Samuel 14:13** regarding Jonathan's victory to illustrate how God empowers individuals against overwhelming odds.

Henry's interpretation emphasizes that Joshua's farewell serves as a timeless model for spiritual leadership transitions and the perpetual need for covenant faithfulness across generations.
