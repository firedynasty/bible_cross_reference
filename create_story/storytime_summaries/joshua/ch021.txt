# Matthew Henry Commentary on Joshua 21: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry emphasizes **God's faithful provision and wisdom** in dispersing the Levites throughout Israel. Rather than viewing their lack of a territorial inheritance as disadvantage, Henry argues "they were no losers, but the rest of the tribes were very much gainers, by their being dispersed." The theological centerpiece is God's covenant faithfulness—the chapter concludes with what Henry calls "a receipt entered in full of all that God had promised to his people Israel."

Henry draws **typological connections** between the Levitical priesthood and Christian ministers, noting that "there is an inheritance provided for all the saints, that royal priesthood." The distribution by lot demonstrates divine sovereignty: "when the Israelites had surrendered the cities into the hand of God, he would himself have the distributing of them among his servants."

## Key Practical Applications

**Ministerial Support**: Henry strongly emphasizes that "the maintenance of ministers is not an arbitrary thing, left purely to the good-will of the people." He connects this to 1 Corinthians 9:14, asserting that Christ "ordained, and a perpetual ordinance it is that those who preach the gospel should live of the gospel."

**Virtuous Conduct for Ministers**: The Levites' dispersal meant "they were made to see the eyes of all Israel upon them," requiring circumspect behavior. Henry notes they demonstrated "humility, modesty, and patience" by waiting until other tribes were settled, encouraging ministers not to "complain if at any time they find themselves postponed in men's thoughts and cares."

**Divine Providence**: Henry sees "particular providence directing and attending the removals and settlements of ministers," since ministers "must neither be idle nor live to themselves or to one another only."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that **the Levites had to petition** for their inheritance (v.1-2), drawing the parallel: "Ask, and it shall be given you." He praises their timing—waiting until others were settled showed proper humility.

Regarding **Aaron's descendants receiving cities near Jerusalem** (v.4), Henry notes God's wisdom since "that was to be, in process of time, the holy city, where their business would chiefly lie."

The **generous allocation** impressed Henry—"the cities they gave to the Levites were generally some of the best and most considerable in each tribe," demonstrating that "God tried their generosity, and it was found to praise and honour."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry frequently references **Numbers 35** regarding the original Levitical city commands, and **Leviticus 25:32-33** concerning property rights. He connects ministerial support to **1 Corinthians 9:14** and **Deuteronomy 12:19; 14** regarding kindness to Levites. The dispersion principle is linked to **Christ's disciples** being sent to "preach the gospel to every creature."

Henry concludes that this systematic provision demonstrates God's perfect faithfulness in fulfilling His promises to Israel, serving as both historical record and spiritual encouragement for believers regarding God's reliable provision.
