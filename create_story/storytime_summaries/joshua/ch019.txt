## Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua Chapter 19

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry emphasizes **divine sovereignty working through human prudence** in the land distribution. When Judah's portion proved "too much for them," God allowed human wisdom to correct the initial surveying error rather than supernatural intervention. Henry sees this as demonstrating how **God's providence fulfills prophecy**—Simeon's scattered inheritance within Judah fulfilled Jacob's prophecy "I will divide them in Jacob," showing how apparent disadvantages serve divine purposes.

The commentary highlights **Christ-centered geography**, noting that several tribal lots gained New Testament significance. Zebulun fulfilled Jacob's prophecy about being "a haven of ships" and became illustrious through **Nazareth, Mount Tabor, and Galilee's shores** where Jesus ministered. Similarly, Naphtali contained Capernaum and Bethsaida, sites of Christ's mighty works.

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws powerful lessons about **sacrificial generosity**: "We must look on the things of others, and not on our own only." When Judah willingly surrendered excess territory to Simeon, they modeled how "abundance of some must supply the wants of others, that there may be somewhat of an equality." This principle transcends legal rights in favor of equitable sharing.

The commentary emphasizes **good neighborhood influence**, noting how Simeon's placement within Judah led to beneficial confederacy and later loyalty to David's house. "It is good being in a good neighbourhood" demonstrates how geographical proximity can shape spiritual and political allegiances.

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Simeon's lot** (vv. 1-9): Despite Jacob's disgrace upon this tribe, their scattered inheritance became providentially beneficial, producing no notable judges or prophets but serving God's larger purposes.

**Zebulun's borders** (vv. 10-16): Henry notes the fulfillment of patriarchal blessings, with access to both the Mediterranean and Sea of Galilee enabling trade and fishing.

**Dan's expansion** (v. 47): When their initial territory proved insufficient, Dan's conquest of Leshem showed legitimate means of addressing inadequate provisions through "industry and valour."

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves extensive biblical connections throughout. He references **Genesis 49:13** for Zebulun's maritime destiny, **Judges 1:3** for Judah-Simeon cooperation, and **2 Chronicles 15:9** for Simeon's later loyalty to Asa. New Testament connections include **Luke 2:36** (Anna the prophetess from Asher) and **Matthew 5:1** (Christ's mountain sermon in Naphtali).

### Joshua's Final Inheritance

The commentary concludes by highlighting Joshua's exemplary leadership: though "the eldest and greatest man of all Israel," he **"was content to be unfixed till he saw them all settled."** This demonstrates how true leaders prioritize others' welfare over personal advantage, making Joshua a type of Christ who served before being glorified.
