# Matthew Henry Commentary on Joshua 24 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **covenant faithfulness** as the central theme, presenting Joshua's final address as a masterful exercise in covenant renewal. The commentary highlights God's **sovereign grace** throughout Israel's history, noting that Abraham "was bred up in idolatry" until "God by his grace snatched him as a brand out of that burning." Henry stresses that **divine initiative** drives salvation history - repeatedly noting God's "I took," "I brought," "I delivered" throughout the historical recounting.

The **tension between human responsibility and divine sovereignty** emerges prominently when Joshua declares "Ye cannot serve the LORD" - which Henry interprets as emphasizing God's holiness and the serious commitment required, not impossibility.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers. He emphasizes **persistent ministry**, noting that Joshua "did not say, 'I have taken my leave of them once, and let that serve'" but continued serving: "We must never think our work for God done till our life is done." 

The commentary stresses **authentic worship**, highlighting the call to serve God "in sincerity and truth, with a single eye and an upright heart, and inward impressions answerable to outward expressions." Henry warns against spiritual compromise, urging believers to "put away the strange gods" - even family heirlooms or cultural attachments that might lead to temptation.

**Corporate covenant commitment** receives strong emphasis, with Henry noting how the ceremony was designed to make religion both their "deliberate choice" and "determinate choice."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1**: Henry explains Shechem's significance as Abraham's first settlement and the location of previous covenant renewals near mounts Gerizim and Ebal.

**Verses 2-13**: The historical recounting demonstrates God's initiative. Henry particularly notes the "hornets" (v.12) as literal divine intervention: "These hornets...annoyed the enemy more than the artillery of Israel."

**Verse 15**: Joshua's famous declaration receives extended treatment. Henry sees this as Joshua setting a godly example: "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" represents leadership by personal commitment.

**Verses 19-20**: Joshua's warning "Ye cannot serve the LORD" is interpreted as emphasizing God's holiness and jealousy, not impossibility of service.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage extensively to other biblical texts. He links Abraham's calling to Romans 4:5 regarding "justifying the ungodly." The hornets reference connects to Exodus 23:27-28 and Deuteronomy 7:20 as fulfilled promises. 

The call for sincere worship references Psalm 51:6 about "truth in the inward part." Jacob's earlier removal of idols at Shechem (Genesis 35:2-4) provides historical parallel. The fear of the Lord connects to Hosea 3:5 - "fear the Lord and his goodness."

Henry also draws parallels to New Testament principles, noting that while they lacked visible tokens like the ark, "where two or three are gathered together in Christ's name he is as really in the midst of them."

The commentary concludes by emphasizing the covenant's binding nature and the importance of witnesses, creating accountability structures that transcend individual commitment to become corporate faithfulness.
