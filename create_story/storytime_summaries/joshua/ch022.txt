# Matthew Henry Commentary on Joshua 22: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as a remarkable example of godly conflict resolution, noting "whereas in most differences that happen there is a fault on both sides, on this there was fault on no side; none were to be blamed, but all to be praised." The central theological themes include **faithfulness to covenant obligations**, **unity among God's people**, and **proper worship practices**. Henry emphasizes how the eastern tribes fulfilled their military commitment while maintaining spiritual devotion, and how misunderstandings about worship can be resolved through honest communication rather than hasty judgment.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from Joshua's dismissal of the eastern tribes. He commends their **"readiness of obedience to their commanders"** and **"constancy of their affection and adherence to their brethren."** When Joshua sends them home with wealth to "divide the spoil with your brethren," Henry applies this to Christian generosity and shared blessings. 

Most significantly, Henry emphasizes Joshua's parting counsel to **"take diligent heed to do the commandment and the law"** - noting that having God's commandments is useless unless we actively obey them. He stresses that spiritual vigilance is essential because **"so apt are we to turn aside, and so industrious are our spiritual enemies to turn us aside."**

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that these tribes **"were first in the assignment of their lot were last in the enjoyment of it"** - connecting this to Jesus' teaching that "the last shall be first, and the first last." He notes Joshua calls their homes "tents" because **"the strongest and stateliest houses in this world are to be looked upon but as tents, mean and movable in comparison with our house above."**

Regarding the altar controversy, Henry highlights the western tribes' measured response - they first sent a diplomatic delegation before declaring war, demonstrating proper biblical conflict resolution.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects the eastern tribes' faithful service to Matthew 8:9 regarding obedience to authority. He references Numbers 32:32 and Joshua 1:16 concerning their original covenant promises. The wealth-sharing principle links to Numbers 31:27 about dividing spoils from the Midianite war. Henry also draws parallels to the principle that those "who goes a warfare at his own charge" should receive fair compensation, and connects the "tents" imagery to our temporary earthly dwelling versus our eternal home above.
