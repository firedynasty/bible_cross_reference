## Matthew Henry Commentary on Joshua 5: Summary

### Main Theological Themes

**Covenant Renewal and Divine Protection**: Henry emphasizes how God's covenant with Israel is dramatically renewed through circumcision after 40 years of suspension. The restoration of this "seal of the covenant" signifies that God's promise to give them Canaan is now being fulfilled. Henry notes this shows "divine Wisdom" in timing—done precisely when entering the Promised Land to "confirm his covenant with them."

**God's Sovereignty Over Enemies**: A central theme is how "God impressed these fears upon them, and dispirited them" regarding the Canaanite kings. Henry explains that God "can make the wicked to fear where no fear is," demonstrating divine control over both Israel's protection and their enemies' defeat.

**Transition from Wilderness to Promise**: The chapter marks a pivotal transition. Henry observes that "the church in the wilderness has now come up from the wilderness" and emphasizes how the manna ceasing represents moving from miraculous provision to natural abundance in the Promised Land.

### Key Practical Applications

**Beginning with God**: Henry draws the principle that we should "in all great undertakings begin with God, to make sure of his favour, by offering ourselves to him a living sacrifice...and then we may expect to prosper in all we do."

**Trusting Divine Timing**: Despite human wisdom suggesting circumcision was dangerous at this moment, Henry emphasizes that "when God commanded him to do it, he must not consult with flesh and blood." This teaches believers to trust God's timing over conventional wisdom.

**Confidence in Divine Care**: The Israelites' willingness to disable themselves militarily through circumcision "proclaimed such confidence in the divine care for their safety" and actually increased their enemies' fears.

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-9 (Circumcision)**: Henry provides an extensive analysis of why circumcision was suspended for 38 years, offering four explanations but favoring that it was "a continued token of God's displeasure" against the unbelieving generation. The restoration shows the curse is lifted.

**Verse 9**: The phrase "rolled away the reproach of Egypt" indicates complete separation from their former bondage and full acceptance as God's covenant people.

**Verses 13-15 (Captain of the Lord's Host)**: Henry sees this as Christ's pre-incarnate appearance, coming to "animate and direct" Joshua for the coming battles.

### Cross-References

Henry connects this chapter to multiple scriptures:
- **Exodus 23:27** - God's promise to send fear before Israel
- **Jeremiah 51:31** - Comparing news spreading like when Babylon fell  
- **Genesis 17** - Original circumcision covenant with Abraham
- **Isaiah 40:2** - "That warfare is accomplished, that iniquity is pardoned"
- **Numbers 14:33** - Children bearing consequences of parents' rebellion

### Memorable Phrases

Henry describes Israel as appearing "fair as the moon, clear as the sun, and terrible as an army with banners." He notes how enemies' hearts "melted like wax before the fire" and emphasizes that this moment shows "the camp of Israel was not governed by the ordinary rules and measures of war, but by immediate direction from God."

The commentary masterfully weaves together themes of divine faithfulness, strategic spiritual preparation, and God's sovereignty over both His people and their enemies during this crucial transition into the Promised Land.
