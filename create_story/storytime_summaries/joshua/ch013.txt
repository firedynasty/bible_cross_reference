## Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua Chapter 13

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry emphasizes God's faithfulness in fulfilling His promises to the patriarchs regarding the land inheritance. The detailed geographical account serves to "illustrate the performance of the promise made to the fathers, that this land should be given to the seed of Jacob." Henry interprets the division of land as demonstrating divine sovereignty in determining "the bounds of our habitation" through the casting of lots, which "referred the matter wholly to God."

A key theological principle emerges regarding divine-human cooperation: "We must work out our salvation, and then God will work in us and work with us; we must resist our spiritual enemies, and then God will tread them under our feet." God's promise to drive out remaining enemies requires Israel's active participation in conquest.

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from Joshua's aging leadership. He notes that God "considers the frame of his people, and would not have them burdened with work above their strength," teaching that God accommodates human limitations. For older believers specifically, "All people, but especially old people, should set themselves to do that quickly which must be done before they die, lest death prevent them."

The incomplete conquest serves as a warning against complacency: "Nor must we lay aside our spiritual armour, nor be off our watch, till our victory be completed in the kingdom of glory." Henry emphasizes that believers must maintain readiness for spiritual warfare until final victory.

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On verse 1's mention of Joshua's age, Henry observes it's "good for those who are old and stricken in years to be put in remembrance of their being so," as some "have gray hairs here and there upon them, and perceive it not." God mentions Joshua's age both to release him from further military campaigns and to urgently complete the land distribution.

Regarding the unconquered territories (verses 2-6), Henry explains these details help Israel appreciate God's goodness, maintain proper distance from pagan neighbors, and "keep themselves in a posture for war" rather than premature peace.

The emphasis on inheritance (verse 7) highlights both the ancestral promise fulfillment and future generational security, showing "God kept this mercy for thousands."

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to Numbers 36:9 regarding maintaining tribal inheritances, and references Ecclesiastes 9:10 about urgency in completing life's work. He cites Hosea 7:9 about unrecognized aging, 2 Samuel 12:8 about God's abundant provision, and Proverbs 18:18 about lots ending contentions. The commentary also references Exodus 17:10 regarding Joshua's military background and draws spiritual parallels throughout about Christian warfare and divine promises.
