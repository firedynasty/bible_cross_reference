# Matthew Henry Commentary on Joshua Chapter 10: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Timing and Providence**: Henry emphasizes that "Canaan was not to be conquered in a day," referencing Exodus 23:30 that God would drive out the Canaanites "by little and little." He notes that "He that believeth will not make haste" - trusting God's timing rather than human impatience. God orchestrated the enemy confederation to "gather them as sheaves into the floor, to fall together under the flail" (Micah 4:12).

**God's Faithfulness in Covenant**: The commentary highlights God's protection of those who align with His people, even the Gibeonites who obtained their covenant through deception. Henry sees this as demonstrating that God honors commitments made in His name and protects those who "come to trust under the shadow of his wings" (Ruth 2:12).

**Divine Warfare**: The miraculous elements - the sun standing still and hailstones from heaven - demonstrate that "the LORD fought for Israel." Henry presents this as God's direct intervention in human affairs when His purposes are at stake.

## Key Practical Applications

**Duty of Protection**: Henry draws parallels between Joshua's obligation to protect the Gibeonites and believers' responsibilities: "it is the duty of masters to take care of the poorest and meanest of their servants." Those who "pay allegiance may reasonably expect protection."

**Diligent Action with Faith**: While God promised victory, Joshua still marched all night, showing that "God's promises are intended, not to slacken and supersede, but to quicken and encourage our endeavours." Henry emphasizes balancing trust in God with vigorous action.

**Spiritual Application**: Henry connects the Gibeonites' appeal to Joshua with believers' appeals to Christ: "When our spiritual enemies set themselves in array against us...let us, by faith and prayer, apply to Christ, our Joshua, for strength and succour."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-6**: Henry notes the irony that Adoni-zedec had "a good name (it signifies lord of righteousness)" yet was "an implacable enemy" to Abraham's posterity, contrasting him with his predecessor Melchizedek. He observes that "Satan and his instruments make war upon those that make peace with God."

**Verses 12-14**: On the sun standing still, Henry emphasizes the uniqueness of this miracle: "there was no day like that before it or after it, that the LORD hearkened unto the voice of a man."

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry weaves numerous scriptural connections throughout:
- Links to **Exodus 23:30** on gradual conquest
- References **Micah 4:12** about gathering enemies like sheaves
- Connects to **Psalm 119:94** ("I am thine, save me") regarding protection
- Cites **2 Corinthians 12:8-9** about God's sufficient grace
- References **Proverbs 24:11-12** on the obligation to help those in danger

The commentary demonstrates Henry's characteristic approach of finding practical spiritual lessons in historical narratives while maintaining respect for the literal events described.
