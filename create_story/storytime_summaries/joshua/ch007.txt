# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua Chapter 7

## Main Theological Themes

Henry emphasizes the **corporate nature of sin and its consequences**. Though only Achan sinned, "the children of Israel are said to commit the trespass, because one of their body did it." This demonstrates how individual sin affects entire communities, as "guilt was brought upon the whole society of which he was a member." 

The commentary highlights **God's sovereignty in judgment and mercy**. Henry notes how "the affairs of Israel, even when they were in the happiest posture" can be "perplexed and embarrassed by sin." He observes that divine blessing depends on obedience: "If we lose our God, we lose our friends, who cannot help us unless God be for us."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical warnings:

- **Vigilance against covetousness**: "The love of the world is that root of bitterness which of all others is most hardly rooted up."
- **Community responsibility**: Christians should "take heed of having fellowship with sinners" since "others' sins may redound to our damage. Many a careful tradesman has been broken by a careless partner."
- **Humility in leadership**: Joshua models proper response to defeat by looking "up to God" rather than blaming subordinates.
- **Avoiding overconfidence**: The Israelites' attitude that "they are but few" led to complacency, teaching that we should not "make too light of an enemy."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1**: Henry emphasizes that despite only one person sinning, God held the entire nation accountable, illustrating collective responsibility.

**Verses 2-5**: The decision to send only 3000 men reflected "culpable assurance" and "indulgence of the people in the love of ease." Henry warns against overconfidence: "as few as they were, they were too many for them."

**Verses 6-9**: Joshua's response demonstrates proper leadership during crisis - he "rent his clothes" and prostrated himself before God rather than blaming others. Henry notes Joshua's prayer, while emotionally charged, came from a different spirit than the murmurers of Moses' time.

## Cross-References and Connections

Henry connects this passage to several scriptural themes:
- References **Hebrews 12:15** regarding how one person's sin can "defile or disquiet many"
- Compares Joshua's intercession to **Psalm 72:14**, portraying him as a type of Christ who values his subjects' lives
- Links the corporate guilt principle to Israel's previous failures: "the golden calf, the murmuring at Kadesh, and the iniquity of Peor"
- Connects to **Psalm 119:120** regarding proper fear of God even among the brave

Henry concludes that this episode demonstrates how "the laws, so Canaan itself, made nothing perfect," pointing toward the ultimate fulfillment in "the heavenly Canaan only."
