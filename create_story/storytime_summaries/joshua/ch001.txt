# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua Chapter 1 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Succession and Continuity of God's Work**: Henry emphasizes that while "Moses the servant is dead, God the Master is not: he lives for ever." The commentary highlights God's sovereignty in changing instruments while maintaining His eternal purposes. The transition from Moses to Joshua demonstrates that God's work continues despite the loss of even the most essential leaders.

**Typological Significance**: Joshua serves as a clear type of Christ throughout Henry's analysis. Just as "Moses's minister" succeeded where the law could not complete the work, "Christ, our Joshua, does that for us which could never be done by the law of Moses—justifies and sanctifies." The crossing of Jordan represents the believer's transition from law to grace.

**Divine Promise and Faithfulness**: God's promise "I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee" receives special attention as a foundational assurance that extends to all believers. Henry notes this promise's application in Hebrews 13:5 as an argument against covetousness and for contentment.

## Key Practical Applications

**Leadership Preparation**: Henry draws extensive lessons about godly leadership, noting that "those are fittest to rule that have learnt to obey." Joshua's long service as Moses's minister equipped him for greater responsibility, demonstrating that "he that was here called to honour had been long bred to business."

**Response to Loss**: "The removal of useful men should quicken survivors to be so much the more diligent in doing good." Rather than becoming paralyzed by grief, believers should recognize that God raises up new instruments to continue His work.

**Courage through God's Word**: Verses 7-8 receive emphasis as showing true courage comes from meditation on Scripture: "This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth; but thou shalt meditate therein day and night." Success depends on faithful adherence to God's revealed will.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 2**: The phrase "Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise" demonstrates both honor for the departed leader and urgency for continuing the mission. Henry notes this prevents harmful "interregnum" while showing proper respect.

**Verses 3-4**: The land grant is viewed as already accomplished—"I have given it; though it be yet unconquered, it is as sure to you as if it were in your hands." This demonstrates faith's confidence in God's promises before their visible fulfillment.

**Verse 5**: The promise of God's presence paralleling Moses's experience provides crucial encouragement. Henry observes that while Joshua lacked Moses's wisdom, "if he had always the same presence of God, he would do well enough."

**Verse 9**: The repeated command for courage emphasizes that fear contradicts faith in God's promised presence "whithersoever thou goest."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects Joshua's calling to broader biblical themes. He references **Hebrews 3:5** regarding Moses's faithful service, **Romans 7:4** concerning death to the law, and **Acts 13:39** about justification through Christ rather than Moses's law.

The commentary links Joshua's experience to **Numbers 27:18-21** regarding his original appointment, **Deuteronomy 31:8** for Moses's prior assurance, and **Hebrews 2:10** concerning Christ as the true captain of salvation who brings "many sons to glory."

**Psalm 116:15** is cited regarding God's notice of His servants' deaths, while **Hebrews 13:5** demonstrates the enduring application of God's faithfulness promises to all believers.

The opening concludes with Moses's blessing from **Deuteronomy 33:29**: "Happy art thou, O Israel! Who is like unto thee, O people?"—showing how proper divine leadership brings blessing to God's people.
