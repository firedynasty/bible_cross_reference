# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua Chapter 4: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes that **God's miraculous works must be remembered and memorialized for future generations**. The commentary highlights God's sovereignty in commanding the memorial while the people were still busy with practical concerns, teaching that "we must not neglect nor omit what we have to do for the glory of God...for that is our best business."

A key theological insight involves the **typological connection to Christ**: "when the Lord Jesus, our Joshua, having overcome the sharpness of death and dried up that Jordan, had opened the kingdom of heaven to all believers, he appointed his twelve apostles according to the number of the tribes of Israel." The twelve stones and twelve apostles both serve as memorials of God's redemptive work.

Henry also emphasizes **divine authority and human obedience**, noting how Joshua "did nothing without divine direction, finishing all that the Lord had commanded him."

## Key Practical Applications

**Parental responsibility in spiritual education** stands central: the stones would prompt children to ask "What mean ye by these stones?" creating opportunities for parents to teach God's faithfulness. Henry stresses that spiritual instruction cannot be postponed for more convenient times.

**Corporate remembrance** matters - each tribe contributed to the memorial, showing that "every Israelite must, in his place, help to make known to the sons of men God's mighty acts." The commentary advocates for community-wide participation in preserving spiritual heritage.

**Proper priorities** emerge when Henry notes that despite being "busy" with practical concerns during the crossing, care for God's memorial "might not be adjourned to a time of greater leisure."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-9**: Henry explains the **dual monument system** - twelve stones taken from Jordan to Gilgal, and twelve stones placed in Jordan's midst. The underwater stones would be "discernible" to confirm "the very place where the ark stood."

**Verse 10**: The phrase "hasted and passed over" receives detailed analysis. Henry suggests four reasons for hasting: distrust of God's protection, reluctance to test God's patience, eagerness for Canaan, and following others' example.

**Verses 12-13**: The two-and-a-half tribes leading demonstrates **covenant faithfulness** and strategic wisdom, as "those who had the first settlement should be the first in the encounter of difficulties."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects the memorial stones to **Psalm 145:12** regarding making known God's mighty acts. He references **Numbers 27:21** about Eleazar's role in seeking divine counsel through the Urim.

The **Isaiah 28:16** passage about believing and not making haste illuminates proper response to God's works. Most significantly, Henry draws parallels between Joshua's twelve representatives and **Christ's twelve apostles**, both serving as channels for transmitting God's redemptive work to future generations.

The commentary emphasizes continuity between **Moses' original instructions** and Joshua's current obedience, showing how faithful leaders build upon previous spiritual foundations while remaining responsive to immediate divine direction.
