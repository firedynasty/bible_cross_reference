## Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua Chapter 15

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes **divine sovereignty in the distribution of the Promised Land**, noting that while the land wasn't completely conquered, God provided "rest from war" to begin the division process. He highlights the **significance of Judah receiving precedence** among the tribes, connecting this to the forfeited birthright of Reuben that "devolved" upon Judah (dominion) and Joseph (double portion). Henry sees prophetic significance in Judah's southern position guarding against Edom, noting that "Our Lord therefore, who sprang out of Judah, and whose the kingdom is, shall judge the mount of Esau."

The commentary emphasizes **God's faithfulness in fulfilling promises**, particularly through Caleb's inheritance, demonstrating that "the confidence he had expressed of success...did not deceive him, but the event answered his expectation."

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from the text:

**Balanced Self-Interest and Community Concern**: Using Caleb's example of conquering Debir for the general good beyond his personal inheritance, Henry writes: "Let us learn hence not to seek and mind our own things only, but to concern and engage ourselves for the welfare of the community we are members of; we are not born for ourselves, nor must we live to ourselves."

**Proper Boundaries for the Powerful**: Regarding Judah's defined borders, he notes that "Though it was a powerful warlike tribe, and had a great interest in the other tribes, yet they must not therefore be left to their own choice, to enlarge their possessions at pleasure, but must live so as that their neighbours might live by them."

**Trusting Divine Guidance**: Henry emphasizes seeking God's direction in doubtful matters, noting Joshua's consultation with God regarding Caleb's inheritance: "In every doubtful case it is very desirable to know the mind of God, and to see the way of our duty plain."

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Geographical Significance**: Henry explains the Salt Sea as having "extraordinary and more than natural saltness, the effects of that fire and brimstone with which Sodom and Gomorrah were destroyed," calling it "dead water, which never either was moved itself or had any living thing in it."

**Historical Markers**: He interprets the "stone of Bohan" as likely commemorating a Reubenite commander, and notes that the "valley of Achor" served "to remind the men of Judah of the trouble which Achan...gave to the congregation of Israel, that they might not be too much lifted up."

**Caleb's Victory Strategy**: Regarding the defeat of Anak's sons, Henry observes they weren't necessarily killed but fled: "with the countenances of lions they had the hearts of trembling hares." He draws a spiritual parallel: "if we resist the devil, that roaring lion, though he fall not, yet he will flee."

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects Joshua 15 to multiple biblical passages:
- **Numbers 33:54** regarding inheritance distribution principles
- **Numbers 34:3-5** for comparison of tribal boundaries  
- **Obadiah 21** linking Judah's position to messianic prophecy
- **Psalm 76:12** and **Job 12:24** regarding God's power over human pride
- **1 Samuel 17:25** comparing Caleb's daughter-offering to Saul's promise regarding Goliath

The commentary particularly emphasizes typological connections, seeing in Judah's geographical position and tribal precedence a foreshadowing of Christ's ultimate reign and victory over enemies.
