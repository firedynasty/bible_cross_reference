## Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua 14: Summary

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Sovereignty in Providence**: Henry emphasizes God's sovereign control over the distribution of the Promised Land. The use of lots demonstrates that "the disposal of that is of the Lord" (Prov. 16:33), showing believers should trust God's wisdom over their own preferences. He draws a parallel to spiritual inheritance: "In allusion to this we are said to obtain an inheritance in Christ...we have obtained it by lot...for it is obtained by a divine designation."

**Faithful Obedience Rewarded**: Caleb's story exemplifies how wholehearted following of God brings both temporal and eternal rewards. Henry notes that "those that follow God fully when they are young shall have both the credit and comfort of it when they are old, and the reward of it for ever in the heavenly Canaan."

**God's Faithfulness**: The preservation of Caleb for 45 years demonstrates God's covenant faithfulness. Henry sees this as proof that divine promises not only come to pass but often exceed expectations: "God not only gives what he promises, but he gives more."

### Key Practical Applications

**Trust in Divine Providence**: Henry encourages believers to submit to God's will as the Israelites did with the lot system, "believing him fitter to determine for them than they for themselves" and maintaining "a willingness to abide by the determination of it."

**Perseverance in Faith**: Caleb's unwavering faith despite peer pressure offers a model for believers. Henry emphasizes that "it adds much to the praise of following God if we adhere to him when others desert and decline from him."

**Gratitude for Preservation**: Henry urges readers to recognize God's hand in preserving their lives: "The longer we live the more sensible we should be of God's goodness to us in keeping us alive...Let the life thus kept by the providence of God be devoted to his praise."

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On the organized distribution (vv. 1-5), Henry emphasizes that Canaan wasn't conquered "to be left desert" but to fulfill God's purpose that the earth be inhabited. The careful administration involving Joshua, Eleazar, and tribal representatives ensured fairness and prevented disputes.

Regarding Caleb's petition (vv. 6-12), Henry highlights Caleb's strategic approach: bringing tribal representatives, appealing to Joshua's knowledge, and honoring Moses. He sees Caleb's strength at 85 as miraculous evidence of God's blessing.

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous scriptural connections throughout: Isaiah 34:13 and 45:18 regarding God's purpose for inhabited land; Psalm 47:4 on trusting God's choice; Ephesians 1:11 on spiritual inheritance by divine lot; Numbers passages detailing the original promises to Caleb; and Psalm 90:10 on the typical limits of human strength, which Caleb's vitality transcends through divine blessing.

The commentary presents Joshua 14 as both historical narrative and spiritual instruction, demonstrating how God's faithfulness to His people serves as a model for Christian trust and perseverance.
