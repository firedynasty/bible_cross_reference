# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua Chapter 3: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents Israel's Jordan crossing as a profound demonstration of divine providence and covenant faithfulness. **The central theme is God's presence leading His people through impossible circumstances.** He emphasizes that "the Lord was their God, a God in covenant with them" and that the ark of the covenant served as the visible guarantee of this relationship. 

Henry draws a parallel between the Red Sea crossing (marking Israel's exodus from Egypt) and Jordan's division (marking entrance into the Promised Land), showing how **God magnifies His chosen leaders through miraculous works.** He notes that God "began to magnify Joshua" at Jordan's banks, just as He later magnified Jesus at the same location during His baptism.

The commentary emphasizes **God's sovereignty over creation** - Jordan's waters obeying their Creator demonstrates that He is "Lord of all the earth," giving assurance that He will "without fail drive out" the Canaanites.

## Key Practical Applications

**Following Divine Guidance**: Henry stresses that believers must "follow the ark" - representing God's Word and ordinances - with "implicit faith." He writes: "We must go on in the way of our duty though we foresee difficulties, trusting God to help us through them when we come to them."

**Preparation for God's Work**: The command to "sanctify yourselves" teaches that **spiritual preparation is essential** before witnessing God's mighty acts. Henry explains: "we must separate ourselves from all other cares, devote ourselves to God's honour, and cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of flesh and spirit."

**Leadership Example**: Joshua's early rising demonstrates that "those that would bring great things to pass must rise early" and that leaders must be diligent in their calling.

**Maintaining Holy Distance**: The required space between people and the ark teaches proper reverence while ensuring everyone could see God's guidance clearly.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 4**: Henry explains the 2,000-cubit distance served three purposes: expressing reverent regard, demonstrating the ark's self-sufficiency for protection, and ensuring all could see their divine guide "for you have not passed this way heretofore."

**Verse 5**: The call to sanctification prepares people for God's wonders, as "to-morrow the Lord will do wonders among you."

**Verse 7-8**: God's magnification of Joshua establishes his authority parallel to Moses, with Henry noting how divine endorsement legitimizes human leadership.

**Verse 11**: The title "Lord of all the earth" emphasizes God's universal sovereignty over both the natural world and nations.

## Cross-References and Biblical Connections

Henry connects this passage to **Micah 6:5**, urging remembrance of God's works "between Shittim and Gilgal." He references **Isaiah 64:3** about God doing unexpected things, and **Psalm 114:5,7** describing Jordan being "driven back" at God's presence.

The **Red Sea crossing (Exodus 14:31)** provides the foundational parallel, with Henry noting both events served to authenticate God's chosen leaders. He draws a **New Testament parallel** to **1 Corinthians 10:2** regarding being "baptized unto Moses in the sea."

Most significantly, Henry connects Joshua's magnification at Jordan to **Christ's baptism at the same location**, where the Father declared "This is my beloved Son," suggesting typological significance between Joshua and Jesus as deliverer-leaders.

The commentary concludes with the principle that "those that honour God he will honour," demonstrating how faithful service leads to divine endorsement and effectiveness in ministry.
