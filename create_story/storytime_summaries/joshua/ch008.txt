# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Joshua Chapter 8: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes the restoration of divine favor after Achan's sin was dealt with, noting that "when we have faithfully put away sin, that accursed thing, which separates between us and God, then, and not till then, we may expect to hear from God to our comfort." He highlights God's sovereignty in military success, stating that Israel was "very happy in having such a commander as Joshua, but Joshua was more happy in having such a director as God himself."

The commentary stresses the principle of divine justice and reward: "How Achan who caught at forbidden spoil lost that, and life, and all, but the rest of the people who had conscientiously refrained from the accursed thing were quickly recompensed for their obedience with the spoil of Ai."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian warfare and conduct. He notes that "corruptions within the church weaken the hands, and damp the spirits, of her guides and helpers, more than oppositions from without; treacherous Israelites are to be dreaded more than malicious Canaanites."

Regarding self-denial and obedience, he teaches: "The way to have the comfort of what God allows us is to forbear what he forbids us. No man shall lose by his self-denial; let God have his dues first, and then all will be clean to us and sure."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

On God's encouragement to Joshua (v. 1), Henry observes that Joshua needed reassurance after Achan's failure, showing how internal sin affects leadership more than external opposition. The permission to take spoil from Ai (v. 2) demonstrates God's generosity after obedience is restored.

Henry defends the legitimacy of the military stratagem, noting "here was no untruth told; nothing was concealed but their own counsels" and emphasizing that "truth, when once it is plighted, becomes a debt even to the enemy."

## Structure and Key Elements

The chapter divides into two main sections: the conquest of Ai through divine strategy and human effort, and the covenant renewal ceremony at Mounts Gerizim and Ebal. Henry notes they "make the business of their religion to keep pace with their secular business," showing the integration of spiritual and practical life.

The commentary emphasizes Joshua's exemplary leadership qualities: his prudence in following divine direction, his diligence in rising early, and his courage in personally leading the renewed attack despite previous failure.
