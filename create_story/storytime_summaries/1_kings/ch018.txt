# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Kings 18: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents 1 Kings 18 as a pivotal chapter demonstrating God's sovereignty over false gods and His faithfulness to preserve His remnant. The commentary emphasizes God's perfect timing—Elijah emerges from obscurity precisely when God ordains rain to return. Henry interprets Jezebel's persecution of God's prophets as divine judgment, noting that "when Jezebel cut off God's prophets God cut off the necessary provisions by the extremity of the drought." He sees the drought as both punishment for idolatry and a means to demonstrate Baal's powerlessness compared to the Lord's control over nature.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons, particularly through Obadiah's character. He commends Obadiah as proof that "God has his remnant among all sorts, high and low; there were saints in Nero's household, and in Ahab's." The commentary argues that faithful believers need not "go out of the world, bad as it is" but can serve God even in corrupt environments. Henry emphasizes that "those who profess religion should study to recommend themselves to the esteem even of those that are without by their integrity, fidelity, and application to business."

He also highlights Obadiah's active protection of God's prophets, noting that he "did not think it enough to fear God himself" but used his position to shelter 100 prophets, demonstrating that privilege should be leveraged for God's service.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry observes that Obadiah "feared the LORD greatly" (v.3) and "from his youth" (v.12), commenting that "Early piety, it is to be hoped, will be eminent piety; those that are good betimes are likely to be very good." He notes the irony that Ahab searched desperately for grass to save his livestock while taking "no care about his soul, not to lose that."

Regarding Elijah's humility with Obadiah, Henry notes the prophet transferred honor back to Ahab, saying prophets "should be called seers, and shepherds, and watchmen, and ministers, rather than lords, as those that mind duty more than dominion."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry references multiple passages to support his interpretations: 2 Chronicles 11:13-14 (priests fleeing to Judah), Hosea 11:12 (Judah's faithfulness), Amos 4:7-8 (selective judgment), Luke 1:16-17 (Elijah's reforming mission), Luke 4:25 and James 5:17 (duration of drought), and Isaiah 30:20-21 (teachers emerging as a sign of favor). These references reinforce his theological framework of judgment, remnant preservation, and divine timing.

Henry concludes that God's calling ministers "out of their corners" signals impending blessing: "It bodes well to any people when God calls his ministers out of their corners, and bids them show themselves—a sign that he will give rain on the earth."
