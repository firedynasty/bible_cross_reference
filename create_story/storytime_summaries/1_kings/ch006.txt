# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Kings Chapter 6: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes the temple's divine origin and purpose, calling it "the house of the Lord" because it was "directed and modelled by him" with "Infinite Wisdom as the architect." He connects the temple to God's progressive revelation, noting it represented "a shadow of good things to come" under the old covenant's "carnal ordinances" and "worldly sanctuary" (Hebrews 9:1,10). 

The commentary stresses that external magnificence means nothing without obedience. Henry warns that "all this charge...in erecting this temple, would neither excuse them from obedience to the law of God nor shelter them from his judgments." He emphasizes that "keeping God's commandments is better, and more pleasing to him, than building churches."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from the construction process. The silent building (no hammers or axes heard) teaches that "God's work should be done with as much care and as little noise as may be. Quietness and silence both become and befriend religious exercises." He notes that "clamour and violence often hinder the work of God, but never further it."

The temple windows, "broad within, and narrow without," symbolize proper spiritual perspective: "Such should the eyes of our mind be, reflecting nearer on ourselves than on other people, looking much within, to judge ourselves, but little without, to censure our brethren."

Solomon's careful preparation teaches that "we are truly serving God when we are preparing for his service and furnishing ourselves for it."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry meticulously calculates the 480-year timeline from Egypt to temple construction, showing God's patient timing despite Israel's unworthiness. He notes the temple's doubled dimensions compared to the tabernacle reflected Israel's growth in numbers and wealth: "Where God sows plentifully he expects to reap so."

The oracle's perfect cube shape (20x20x20 cubits) and gold overlay represent the Holy of Holies' supreme sanctity. The cherubim's protective positioning and the decorative motifs throughout speak to heavenly realities made manifest.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects the temple to Moses' tabernacle pattern (1 Chronicles 28:11-12), the Hebrews 9 discussion of old covenant worship, Isaiah 54:1-2 on expansion, and Psalm 74:4,6 contrasting the temple's silent construction with its violent destruction. He references Proverbs 24:27 on proper preparation and Ezra 4:24 regarding building obstacles.

**Central Message**: The temple's true glory lay not in its material splendor but in being God's dwelling place, contingent upon continued covenant faithfulness. External religious works, however magnificent, cannot substitute for obedience to God's commands.
