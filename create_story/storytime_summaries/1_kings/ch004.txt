## Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Kings Chapter 4

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry views Solomon's prosperity as the fulfillment of God's promise, showcasing both divine wisdom and wealth. He consistently presents Solomon's kingdom as **"a glorious type of the kingdom of the Messiah,"** with explicit connections to Psalm 72 and prophecies about Christ's reign. However, Henry maintains proper perspective, concluding that **"our Lord Jesus was greater than he, though he took upon him the form of a servant; for divinity, in its lowest humiliation, infinitely transcends royalty in its highest elevation."**

The commentary emphasizes how God's promises to Abraham regarding numerous descendants and territorial extent were fulfilled during Solomon's reign, demonstrating divine faithfulness across generations.

### Key Practical Applications

**Leadership Principles:** Henry commends Solomon's wisdom in retaining his father David's trusted officials, noting that wise leaders don't automatically change what works well. He observes that **"Those preserve the name and honour of their place that mind the business of it and make conscience of it."**

**Administrative Wisdom:** The twelve-officer provision system demonstrates practical governance—preventing any individual from becoming "continually burdened" or "exorbitantly rich," while ensuring **"all the parts of the kingdom might be equally benefited."**

**Stewardship and Hospitality:** Henry draws lessons about generous yet prudent household management, encouraging **"good house-keeping, to be generous in spending according to their ability, but prudent in providing."**

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-6:** Henry highlights Solomon's deliberate retention of David's officers as respectful wisdom, particularly noting the preference given to priests' sons, showing **"grateful respect he had for their good father."**

**Verses 20-25:** The description of Israel dwelling **"every man under his vine and under his fig tree"** represents perfect security and property protection, where Solomon **"invaded no man's property"** but protected everyone's possessions.

**Verses 22-23:** The enormous daily provisions (enough for 3000-4800 men) demonstrate the constant intellectual feast Solomon provided, as people came **"to hear his wisdom."**

### Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects this passage to **Psalm 72** as messianic prophecy fulfilled in Solomon's reign. He references **Genesis 15:18 and 22:17** regarding Abrahamic covenant fulfillment, **Isaiah 49:6-7 and 53:12** concerning Christ's universal kingdom, and **Revelation 7:9** about the multitude of God's spiritual Israel.

He contrasts Solomon's provisions with Christ feeding the 5000, noting that **"all believers have in him a continual feast,"** and connects Israel's joy to **"the kingdom of God...righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost"** rather than mere material abundance.
