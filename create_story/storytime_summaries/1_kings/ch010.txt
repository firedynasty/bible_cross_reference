# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Kings 10: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes that Solomon's greatness serves as a type of Christ, repeatedly noting that "where Christ is, by his word and Spirit, 'Behold, a greater than Solomon is there.'" The queen of Sheba's visit represents the Gentiles seeking divine wisdom. Her journey demonstrates genuine spiritual hunger - she came not merely for political purposes but "concerning the name of the LORD," seeking instruction about God himself. Henry sees this as prefiguring how people would seek Christ, who "having lain in his bosom, was much better able to instruct them" than Solomon.

## Key Practical Applications

**For Leaders and Ministers**: Henry emphasizes that "Those whom God has called to any public employment, particularly in the magistracy and ministry, should, by all means possible, be still improving themselves in that knowledge which will more and more qualify them for it." The queen's reminder to Solomon that he was made king "to do judgment and justice" shows that leaders "must be more solicitous to do the duty of their places than to secure the honours and profits of them."

**For Believers**: Henry draws parallels between the queen's experience and Christian life. Those who experience "communion with God will say that the one-half was not told them of the pleasures of Wisdom's ways." He applies her declaration about Solomon's servants to Christians: "With much more reason may we say this of Christ's servants, Blessed are those that dwell in his house."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3**: Henry notes the queen came primarily for spiritual instruction, "to be instructed in the things of God," making her journey an act of genuine seeking rather than mere curiosity.

**Verse 8**: The queen's declaration "Happy are thy men, happy are these thy servants" leads Henry to observe: "It is a great advantage to be in good families, and to have opportunity of frequent converse with those that are wise, and good, and communicative."

**Verse 9**: Her blessing of God demonstrates that Solomon's advancement came "not for thy merit's sake, but because it so seemed good unto him," emphasizing divine grace over human merit.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects this passage to the New Testament, particularly **Matthew 12:42** where Christ references the queen of the south. He also references:
- **Acts 8:27** (Candace, connecting to Ethiopian tradition)
- **Psalm 72:15** (David's prophecy about gold of Sheba)
- **Matthew 2:11** (wise men's gifts to Christ)
- **1 Corinthians 2:9** (regarding heavenly glory exceeding expectation)
- **Ecclesiastes 9:11** and **Jeremiah 22:15** (regarding prosperity through justice)

Henry concludes that Christ surpasses Solomon in both wisdom and kindness: "whatsoever we ask, it shall be done for us; nay, he will, out of his divine bounty, which infinitely exceeds royal bounty, even Solomon's, do for us more than we are able to ask or think."
