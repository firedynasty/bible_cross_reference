# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Kings Chapter 1: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry's commentary centers on **God's sovereignty in establishing proper succession** despite human ambition and family dysfunction. The chapter illustrates how "our deliverances from or through diseases and dangers are but reprieves" - even great kings like David must face mortality. Henry emphasizes divine providence working through earthly events to fulfill God's predetermined plan for Solomon's kingship, despite Adonijah's rebellion.

The commentary presents **parental discipline as a sacred responsibility**. David's failure to correct Adonijah becomes a cautionary tale about honoring sons "more than God" by avoiding proper discipline. Henry notes the irony that indulged children often become the source of their parents' greatest grief.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws sharp lessons about **child-rearing**: "The way to keep them humble is to keep them under." He warns that children who are never corrected learn pride and ambition, which "is the ruin of a great many young people." Parents who fail to displease their children with necessary correction will ultimately face greater sorrow.

For **young people**, Henry counsels: "Let young people remember their Creator in the days of their youth, before these evil days come." He urges using strength well while it lasts, since "the night of old age" will inevitably come when "no man can work."

The commentary warns against **false religious exercises**, noting how Adonijah may have used sacrificial feasts to legitimize his usurpation, making "all religious exercises...patronise all religious practices."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Regarding **David's decline** (vv. 1-4), Henry observes that David was "now chastised for his former sins, especially that in the matter of Uriah." He criticizes the physicians for "foolishly prescrib[ing] nuptials to one that should rather have been preparing for his funeral."

On **Adonijah's character** (v. 6), Henry notes the father "had not displeased him at any time" - meaning David never corrected him by "calling him to an account" or "reproving him for what he saw...amiss."

Concerning **Adonijah's supporters** (vv. 7-8), Henry marvels that experienced men like Joab and Abiathar were "drawn to follow him," while noting that faithful servants like Zadok and Nathan weren't even approached because their integrity was well-known.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects David's physical decline to **Job 37:17** and **Haggai 1:6** regarding garments that provide no warmth without God's blessing. He references **Matthew 19:5** and **1 Corinthians 7:1** to critique the marriage advice given to the dying king.

The commentary links Adonijah's rebellion to **Christ's kingdom**, noting how "there are those that say, 'We will not have him to reign over us.'" He compares Adonijah's religious feast to **Absalom's deception** (2 Samuel 15:7) and warns against those who "serve their own belly" (Romans 16:18).

Henry's memorable phrase captures the tragedy: **"In nomine Domini incipit omne malum"** - "In the name of the Lord begins all evil" - when religious observance masks ungodly ambition.
