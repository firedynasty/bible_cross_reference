# Matthew Henry's Commentary on 1 Kings 15: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Divine Covenant Faithfulness**: Henry emphasizes God's unwavering commitment to His covenant with David, even through unfaithful descendants. He notes that Abijam's reign continued "for David's sake," highlighting how God preserves His promises despite human failure. The commentary stresses that "David might have a lamp" and Jerusalem be established according to divine purpose.

**The Contrast of Faithful vs. Unfaithful Leadership**: Henry draws stark contrasts between kings whose "hearts were perfect with the Lord" (like Asa and David) versus those who walked in sin (like Abijam). He observes that good kings generally enjoyed longer reigns, suggesting divine blessing follows faithfulness.

**Reformation and Spiritual Renewal**: The commentary presents Asa's reign as a model of comprehensive reformation, showing how true revival requires both removing evil and re-establishing good practices.

## Key Practical Applications

**Family Loyalty vs. Divine Obedience**: Henry praises Asa's difficult decision to remove his idolatrous grandmother from power, noting "he loves her well, but he loves God better." This demonstrates that "reformation must begin at home" and earthly relationships cannot supersede divine duty.

**Comprehensive Reform**: Henry emphasizes that true reformation requires both negative and positive action - "We must not only cease to do evil, but learn to do well, not only cast away the idols of our iniquity, but dedicate ourselves and our all to God's honour and glory."

**Warning Against Spiritual Pride**: The commentary warns that divine favor can lead to dangerous complacency, noting how Abijam became "proud and secure by his great victory" which led to his downfall.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**On Abijam's Character (v. 3)**: Henry notes the significance of the name change from "Abijah" (My father is the Lord) to "Abijam," suggesting that "Jah, the name of God, is, in disgrace to him, taken away from his name" when discussing his sins.

**On David's Legacy (v. 5)**: Henry provides nuanced commentary on David's character, acknowledging that while David "was guilty of other faults," the matter of Uriah was uniquely serious, yet "being repented of...did not prevail to throw him out of the covenant."

**On Asa's Incomplete Reform (v. 14)**: Though praising Asa's heart as "perfect with the Lord," Henry notes the irony that "the high places were not removed," showing that even faithful leaders have limitations.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to multiple scriptures:
- **Psalm 132:17** regarding God's promise of "a lamp for his anointed"
- **2 Chronicles 13-16** for expanded accounts of these reigns
- **Deuteronomy 33:9** comparing Asa's prioritizing God over family to the Levites' faithfulness
- **Exodus 32:20** linking Asa's destruction of idols to Moses' actions with the golden calf
- **Isaiah 26:10** warning that "Let favour be shown to the wicked, yet will he not learn righteousness"

The commentary demonstrates how these historical accounts provide timeless principles for spiritual leadership, family relationships, and the ongoing need for reformation in every generation.
