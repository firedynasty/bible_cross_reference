# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Kings 19: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry presents this chapter as divine judgment on Israel's "insincerity and inconstancy of their reformation." The central theme is God's grace toward His servants during spiritual depression. Henry emphasizes that "when people will not learn it is just with God to remove their teachers into corners."

The commentary highlights God's sovereignty in sustaining His servants through various means - ravens, angels, and supernatural provision - demonstrating that "man lives not by bread alone." Henry sees Elijah's journey to Horeb as providential, connecting him to Moses' experience and the revival of the law.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons from Elijah's experience:

**On Human Frailty:** "Great faith is not always alike strong" and "Lord, what is man!" Henry notes how Elijah, who courageously faced hundreds of Baal's prophets, "trembles at the impotent menaces of a proud passionate woman."

**On Discouragement:** Henry addresses Elijah's death wish as "not the deliberate desire of grace, as Paul's, but the passionate wish of his corruption." He warns that "those that are, in this manner, forward to die are not in the fittest frame for dying."

**On God's Care:** "How much better does God deal with his froward children than they deserve!" Henry emphasizes that believers remain "under their Father's eye and care" even in wilderness experiences.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3:** Henry criticizes Ahab for telling Jezebel "what Elijah had done" rather than "what God had done," showing his spiritual blindness. He suggests Jezebel's threat was primarily meant "to frighten him and get him out of the way" rather than actually kill him.

**Verses 4-8:** Henry sees God's angelic provision as remarkable patience: "Our case would be bad sometimes if God should take us at our word and grant us our foolish passionate requests."

**Verses 9-18:** The "still small voice" represents God's gentle approach versus dramatic displays. Henry emphasizes God's encouragement that 7,000 remained faithful, showing Elijah's isolation was more perceived than real.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry makes several biblical connections:
- **Exodus 15:9** - comparing persecutors' confidence
- **Genesis 16:13** - Hagar seeing God in wilderness
- **1 Kings 17:3** - God's previous command for Elijah to hide
- **1 Kings 18:4** - Jezebel's earlier killing of prophets

He also draws parallels between Elijah's 40-day journey and Israel's wilderness wandering, and between Elijah's experience at Horeb and Moses' encounters with God at the same location.

Henry concludes that this chapter demonstrates both human weakness and divine faithfulness, showing how God sustains His servants through discouragement and prepares them for future service, ultimately strengthening Elijah through Elisha's companionship.
