# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Kings 14: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry emphasizes the **inevitable consequences of apostasy and divine judgment**. The chapter illustrates how "when God judges he will overcome, and sinners shall either bend or break before him." A central theme is **God's omniscience versus human deception** - Jeroboam's attempt to disguise his wife demonstrates the folly of thinking one can hide from God. Henry notes: "Those who think by their disguises to hide themselves from God will be wretchedly confounded when they find themselves disappointed."

The commentary highlights **God's faithfulness despite human unfaithfulness** - though Israel had forsaken God, "God had not cast them off." Henry also emphasizes **divine accountability**, showing how God keeps careful record of His mercies to highlight human ingratitude.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons:

**On sickness and family affliction**: "When sickness comes into our families we should enquire whether there be not some particular sin harboured in our houses, which the affliction is sent to convince us of and reclaim us from."

**On seeking spiritual counsel**: Many people "would rather be told their fortune than their faults or their duty." Henry critiques those who "love to prescribe to their ministers, limit them to smooth things" rather than hearing "the whole counsel of God."

**On proper response to trouble**: Instead of merely wanting to know outcomes, we should ask "wherefore God contended with him, had begged the prophet's prayers, and cast away his idols."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-6**: Henry sees profound irony in Jeroboam consulting the very prophet whose God he had rejected through idolatry. The disguise attempt shows "what a strange notion had Jeroboam of God's prophet when he believed that he could...tell what would become of the child, and yet...not discover who was the mother!"

**Verses 7-9**: Henry emphasizes how Jeroboam's calves were "other gods" even though supposedly representing Israel's God, because they "changed the truth of God into a lie."

**Verse 13**: The child Abijah's unique honor of proper burial because "in him there is found some good thing toward the LORD" demonstrates that even in judgment, God recognizes and rewards faithfulness.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this passage to several other scriptures:
- **Ezekiel 14:4** regarding God answering idolaters "according to the multitude of their idols"
- **2 Kings 1:2** and **2 Kings 8:8** showing similar patterns of consulting prophets about outcomes rather than seeking repentance
- References to **David's faithfulness** as the standard against which other kings are measured
- **John 3** (Nicodemus coming by night) as parallel to shameful disguise

The commentary presents both kingdoms - Israel and Judah - as cautionary tales demonstrating "the mischievous consequences of sin and the calamities it brings on kingdoms and families."
