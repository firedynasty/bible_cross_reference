# Matthew Henry Commentary on 1 Kings Chapter 5 - Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry centers this chapter on Solomon as a **type of Christ**, emphasizing that "he shall build the temple of the Lord" (Zech. 6:12). The temple construction represents the building of God's spiritual house through the Messiah. Henry interprets the cooperation between Solomon and Hiram as prophetic of Gospel times, when "the sons of strangers shall build up the wall of the gospel temple" and Gentile ministers would serve Christ's kingdom (referencing Isaiah 60:10-13).

The commentary highlights **divine providence in establishing peace** for sacred work. Henry notes that when "Satan does all he can to hinder temple work," God's provision of rest creates opportunities for spiritual building. He draws parallels to New Testament passages, stating "when the churches have rest let them be edified" (Acts 9:31).

## Key Practical Applications

Henry emphasizes several practical lessons:

**Humility in relationships**: "Let us never look with disdain on those below us, because we know not how soon we may need them." Solomon, despite his superior position, needed Hiram's expertise.

**Seizing opportunities**: "Days of peace and prosperity present us with a fair gale, which we must account for if we improve not." Believers should use peaceful times for spiritual advancement.

**Fair dealing in business**: Henry stresses "justice on both sides, in every bargain," commending both parties for honoring their agreements completely.

**Wisdom in specialization**: "Better be an Israelite skilful in the law than a Sidonian skilful to hew timber," yet acknowledging that "true piety is a much more valuable gift of heaven than the highest degree of ingenuity."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich commentary on Solomon's diplomatic correspondence. On verse 4's mention of "no adversary," he notes the Hebrew word is "Satan," emphasizing that spiritual opposition must be bound before temple work prospers.

Regarding Hiram's response (v. 7), Henry observes Hiram's generous spirit: "Blessed be the Lord, who has given to David a wise son." This shows how "David's character will win the affections even of those that are without."

On the practical arrangements (v. 8-9), Henry commends Hiram's prudence: "I have considered the things" - noting that "those do not lose time who take time to consider."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves extensive biblical connections throughout:
- **Zechariah 6:12** - Christ as temple builder
- **Isaiah 60:10-13** - Gentile participation in Gospel temple
- **1 Thessalonians 2:18; Zechariah 3:1** - Satan hindering God's work
- **Revelation 20:2** - Satan's binding enabling progress
- **Deuteronomy 8:8** - Canaan's agricultural focus explaining Israel's limited craftsmanship
- **Proverbs 31:16** - Wise deliberation before decisions

The commentary demonstrates Henry's characteristic approach of finding Gospel applications in Old Testament narratives, viewing Solomon's temple project as a foreshadowing of Christ's spiritual kingdom building through diverse, international cooperation.
