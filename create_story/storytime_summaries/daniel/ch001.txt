# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Daniel Chapter 1: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry emphasizes **God's sovereignty over earthly kingdoms** as the central theme. He notes that "the kingdoms of the earth" are "short-lived things" while "the kingdom of heaven is everlasting." The commentary highlights God's righteous judgment in allowing Jerusalem's vessels to be carried to pagan temples, stating: "When men profane the vessels of the sanctuary with their sins it is just with God to profane them by his judgments."

Henry also stresses **divine providence in adversity**, showing how God protected and favored Daniel even in captivity. He observes that God's varied methods of preparing servants - Daniel through human learning before divine visions, unlike Isaiah or Ezekiel who began with immediate divine revelations.

## Key Practical Applications

The commentary draws several practical lessons:

**Education and Character**: "Those that would do good in the world when they grow up must learn when they are young. That is the learning age; if that time be lost, it will hardly be redeemed." Henry emphasizes combining liberal education with proper maintenance and moral character.

**Faithfulness in Compromise**: Daniel's dietary restrictions demonstrate principled living under pressure. Henry notes Daniel's wisdom in proposing a practical test rather than outright defiance, showing "ingenuity and sweetness of temper."

**Public Service**: Henry commends Nebuchadnezzar's policy of seeking qualified leaders, stating: "It is the interest of princes to have wise men employed under them; it is therefore their wisdom to take care for the finding out and training up of such."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-2**: Henry dates the seventy-year captivity from this first deportation, noting Daniel "continued the whole seventy years" and witnessed the complete rise and fall of Babylon within one lifetime.

**Verses 6-7**: The name changes represent attempted cultural assimilation. Hebrew names containing references to God (Daniel = "God is my Judge") were replaced with Babylonian names honoring pagan deities, designed to "insensibly wean them from the former and instil the latter into them."

**Verse 9**: Henry emphasizes that "it was God that brought him into favour" with the prince of eunuchs, demonstrating divine providence working through human relationships.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects Daniel's story to broader biblical patterns:
- Links to **Isaiah 39:6-7** regarding Hezekiah's judgment
- References **Jeremiah 25:11** on the seventy-year servitude
- Cites **Job 5:3** and **Proverbs 29:16** on witnessing the fall of the wicked
- Connects to Joseph's favor with the prison keeper as a parallel example of God's providence

The commentary presents Daniel 1 as demonstrating how God prepares and protects His servants even through national judgment, emphasizing faithful character development and divine sovereignty over human affairs.
