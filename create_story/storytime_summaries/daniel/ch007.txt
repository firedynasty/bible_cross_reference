# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Daniel Chapter 7: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Henry begins by noting the shift from historical to prophetical content, emphasizing the tension between "many things dark and hard to be understood" alongside "many things plain and profitable." The chapter centers on God's sovereign control over earthly kingdoms through three primary theological themes:

**Divine Sovereignty Over Nations**: The four beasts represent successive world empires (Babylonian, Persian, Grecian, and either Syrian or Roman) that oppress God's people. Henry interprets these as driven by "brutish rage and tyranny," showing how earthly powers rise and fall under God's ultimate authority.

**The Coming Judgment**: The vision of the Ancient of Days establishes that "there is a judgment to come, and God is the Judge." Henry emphasizes that while "men have their day," God's day is coming when all earthly thrones will be "cast down."

**The Eternal Kingdom**: The "Son of man" receiving everlasting dominion represents Christ's kingdom, which "shall not pass away" - contrasting sharply with the temporary, brutal earthly empires.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers facing persecution and political upheaval:

**Preparation for Tribulation**: Rather than expecting "complete and uninterrupted tranquility," believers should understand that spiritual prosperity comes through "the kingdom of grace" while earthly trials continue.

**Comfort in Persecution**: The visions provide "comfort and support of the people of God in reference to the persecutions they were likely to sustain," written "for their learning on whom the ends of the world have come."

**Trust in God's Timing**: Believers can find "unspeakable satisfaction" knowing that despite worldly "convulsions and revolutions," God's throne remains established and "his kingdom rules over all."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry provides rich symbolic interpretations: The **lion with eagle's wings** (Babylon) shows how conquering powers can have "the wings plucked" and lose their courage, becoming "feeble and faint." The **bear with three ribs** (Persia) represents voracious appetite for conquest but inability to "devour all." The **leopard with four wings and heads** (Greece) depicts Alexander's swift conquests and subsequent division among four generals.

The mysterious **fourth beast** receives extensive treatment, with Henry acknowledging scholarly disagreement while emphasizing its unprecedented cruelty and opposition to God's people. The **"little horn" with "eyes like the eyes of man" and "mouth speaking great things"** represents either Antiochus Epiphanes or a future antichrist figure.

The **Ancient of Days** vision emphasizes God's eternal nature through imagery of white garments, pure wool hair, and a fiery throne with countless servants, establishing divine judgment over all earthly powers.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous biblical connections throughout: He references **Job 33:15** regarding divine revelation through dreams, **Psalm 37:13** about God laughing at his enemies, **1 Corinthians 15:24** concerning the abolishment of opposing authorities, and **Psalm 103:19** affirming God's sovereign rule.

The commentary connects Daniel's prophecies to Christ's teachings about expecting persecution, while linking the eternal promises to "the spiritual blessings of the kingdom of grace" and "eternal blessings of the kingdom of glory." Henry particularly emphasizes how these prophecies serve those "on whom the ends of the world have come," providing "patience and comfort" through scripture for maintaining hope amid trials.
