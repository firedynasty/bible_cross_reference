# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Daniel Chapter 9 Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry identifies this chapter as containing "the clearest, brightest, prophecy of the Messiah, in all the Old Testament." He presents two major theological movements: the immediate restoration of the Jews from Babylonian captivity serving as a type of the greater redemption through Jesus Christ.

Henry emphasizes that **God's promises are intended not to supersede, but to excite and encourage our prayers**. Daniel's discovery through Jeremiah's prophecy that the seventy years were ending prompted intensive prayer rather than passive waiting. This demonstrates that prophetic fulfillment requires human participation through intercession.

The commentary highlights God's covenant faithfulness—He is described as "keeping the covenant and mercy to them that love him" while remaining "the great and dreadful God." Henry notes the importance of viewing "both God's greatness and his goodness, his majesty and mercy in conjunction."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for Christian living:

**Scripture Study for All Believers**: Though Daniel was "a great prophet" and "prime-minister of state," he remained "a diligent student in the scripture." Henry observes that "the greatest and best men in the world must not think themselves above their Bibles."

**Extraordinary Prayer in Pivotal Moments**: When prophetic timing indicates God is moving, believers should engage in special intercession beyond daily prayer. Henry notes that "when the day of deliverance dawns it is time for God's praying people to bestir themselves."

**Genuine Confession**: Daniel's prayer demonstrates that "in every prayer we must make confession," including both acknowledgment of sin and "confession of our faith in God and dependence upon him."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3**: Henry emphasizes the historical timing—this prayer occurred in Darius's first year when the seventy years ended but before Cyrus's decree, possibly before Daniel's experience in the lions' den.

**Verse 2**: Daniel's study of Jeremiah's prophecies shows that even prophets must be students of Scripture. Henry notes the exact Hebrew word connections between Jeremiah's "desolation" (chorbath) and Daniel's usage.

**Verses 5-6**: The confession uses multiple Hebrew terms for sin, demonstrating that "it becomes penitents to lay load upon themselves." Henry identifies violation of express laws and rejection of prophetic warnings as the two primary aggravating factors.

**Verses 16-19**: The urgent, repetitive petitions ("O Lord, hear; O Lord, forgive; O Lord, hearken and do") reflect proper intercession that appeals to God's character rather than human merit.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry weaves numerous scriptural connections throughout:

- **Jeremiah 29:10 and 25:11** for the seventy-year prophecy
- **Ezekiel 14:14** regarding Daniel's intercession
- **Isaiah 40:2** on warfare being accomplished
- **Romans 7:13** on sin's true nature
- **2 Chronicles 36:16** on despising God's messengers

He also references the broader theological framework connecting the return from Babylon to the future Messianic redemption, treating the immediate deliverance as typological of the greater salvation to come.

Henry's commentary presents Daniel 9 as a masterpiece of intercessory prayer that models how believers should respond when God's timing and human responsibility intersect, combining deep scriptural knowledge with urgent, humble petition based on God's covenant character rather than human merit.
