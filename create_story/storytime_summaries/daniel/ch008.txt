# Summary of Matthew Henry's Commentary on Daniel Chapter 8

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry interprets this chapter as a prophetic vision focused specifically on the Persian and Grecian monarchies, with no reference to the Chaldean empire which was ending. The **ram with two horns** represents the Persian empire (Media and Persia), while the **he-goat** symbolizes Alexander the Great and the Grecian empire. The **little horn** represents Antiochus Epiphanes, who would persecute the Jewish people.

Henry emphasizes God's sovereignty over earthly kingdoms, noting that "those kingdoms which, when they had power, abused it...may expect to have their power at length taken from them." The vision demonstrates the transient nature of worldly power and the ultimate protection God provides His people during persecution.

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers:

**On worldly power and vanity**: Alexander's death "left a lasting monument of the vanity of worldly pomp and power, and their insufficiency to make a man happy." This serves as a warning against placing trust in temporal achievements.

**On God's providence**: The detailed prophecy shows that "even then God might not leave himself without witness, nor them without a guide." God provides His people with foreknowledge of coming trials to help them prepare spiritually.

**On spiritual freedom**: "The soul may be a liberty when the body is in captivity; for, when we are bound, the Spirit of the Lord is not bound." This offers comfort to those facing persecution or hardship.

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry notes the vision's setting at **Shushan palace** (v.2), explaining that Daniel was there "in vision" while physically in Babylon, demonstrating God's ability to transcend physical limitations.

Regarding the **ram's horns** (v.3), he observes that "the last shall be first, and the first last," as Persia eventually surpassed Media in prominence.

The **he-goat's speed** (v.5) represents Alexander's incredible military swiftness: "he touched not the ground, so lightly did he move" - describing how Alexander conquered much of the known world in just six years.

The **2,300 days** (v.14) refers to the specific duration of persecution under Antiochus before the sanctuary would be cleansed.

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects this vision to **Daniel 7:6** (the leopard with four heads), showing consistency in prophetic symbolism. He references **Ecclesiastes 1:9** to illustrate the cyclical nature of earthly kingdoms, and **Isaiah 33:1** regarding divine justice for abusive rulers.

The commentary also alludes to **Psalm 48:2**, describing the "pleasant land" of Israel as "beautiful for situation, the joy of the whole earth."

**Language significance**: Henry notes that this chapter returns to Hebrew (from Chaldean) because it concerns Jewish interests specifically, "that they might know what troubles were before them and what the issue of them would be."

This vision ultimately provides both warning and comfort - demonstrating that while God's people may face severe persecution, His purposes will prevail and deliverance will come.
