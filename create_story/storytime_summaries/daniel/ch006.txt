# Matthew Henry Commentary on Daniel Chapter 6: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry interprets Daniel 6 as a divinely-selected story that "serves for the confirming of our faith in God and the encouraging of our obedience to him." He draws a theological parallel between Daniel's trial and that of the three Hebrew children, noting that "the three children were cast into the fiery furnace for not committing a known sin, Daniel was cast into the lions' den for not omitting a known duty." This establishes the central theme of unwavering faithfulness to God regardless of earthly consequences.

Henry emphasizes God's sovereignty in preserving His faithful servants, viewing Daniel's miraculous deliverance as encouragement "for his servants in all ages to be resolute and constant both in their abhorrence of that which is evil and in their adherence to that which is good, whatever it cost them."

## Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers:

**For Public Servants**: Daniel serves as an exemplar for those in positions of trust, showing they should "approve themselves both careful and conscientious" and be "free, not only from fault, but from error."

**For Facing Opposition**: Henry notes that "it is an excellent thing, and much for the glory of God, when those who profess religion conduct themselves so inoffensively in their whole conversation that their most watchful spiteful enemies may find no occasion of blaming them, save only in the matters of their God."

**For Maintaining Integrity**: Daniel's example shows that "such honesty is the best policy, for it secures a reputation; and those who thus honour God he will honour." Henry emphasizes that Daniel "kept in by being an oak, not by being a willow, by a constancy in virtue, not by a pliableness to vice."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verses 1-3**: Henry praises Darius for preferring Daniel "purely for his personal merit," despite Daniel being elderly (over 70 years old) and from a conquered foreign kingdom. He notes that Daniel possessed "an excellent spirit" and remained "as able as ever for business both in body and mind."

**Verses 4-5**: Henry observes that Daniel's enemies "could find no occasion against him except concerning the law of his God," highlighting that Daniel maintained his religious profession without it hindering his governmental service. He notes the irony that "when Daniel's enemies could find no occasion against him concerning the kingdom, they had so much sense of justice left that they did not suborn witnesses."

**Verses 6-9**: Henry criticizes the deceptive nature of the conspiracy, noting that while the plotters claimed "all the presidents" agreed to the decree, "Daniel, the chief of the three presidents, did not agree to it." He warns about how "that which few approve of is sometimes confidently said to be that which all agree to."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry connects Daniel's faith to **Hebrews 11:33**, noting how Daniel "by faith stopped the mouths of lions, and so obtained a good report." He references **1 Peter 2:13-14** regarding submission to governmental authorities, **Ecclesiastes 4:4** on envy, **Proverbs 27:4** on the destructiveness of envy, and **Psalm 27:11** regarding walking carefully under observation.

The commentary concludes with the encouraging reminder that "this God is our God for ever and ever," emphasizing the eternal relevance of God's faithfulness demonstrated in Daniel's deliverance.
