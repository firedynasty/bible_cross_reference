## Matthew Henry's Commentary on Daniel 11: Summary

### Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

Matthew Henry interprets Daniel 11 as Gabriel's detailed fulfillment of his promise to reveal "what should befall his people in the latter days." The chapter primarily focuses on the succession of Persian and Greek kingdoms, with particular attention to Antiochus Epiphanes as a type of future persecution against God's people. Henry emphasizes God's sovereignty over world empires, noting how "the golden head was broken, and the axe laid to the root of the tree" through divine intervention. A key theological insight emerges in Alexander's story, which Henry sees as demonstrating that "Never was the vanity of the world and its greatest things shown more evidently than in the story of Alexander. All is vanity and vexation of spirit."

### Key Practical Applications

Henry draws several practical lessons for believers. He emphasizes acknowledging "the hand of God in the strengthening of those that are friends to the church" and recognizing that God "uses the ministry of angels more than we are aware of." The commentary encourages Christians that "the many instances we have known of God's care of his church formerly encourage us to depend upon him in further straits and difficulties." Henry also warns against the dangers of pride, noting how Ptolemy's heart "was lifted up" after victory, leading to his downfall.

### Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

Henry identifies the four Persian kings (v. 2) as Cyrus, Cambyses, and Darius, noting the Persian attributes: "Cyrus was a father, Cambyses a master, and Darius a hoarder up." He interprets the "mighty king" (v. 3) as Alexander, whose kingdom was "plucked up for others besides those of his own family," with his dynasty destroyed by internal violence. The detailed prophecies about the kings of the north (Syria) and south (Egypt) are seen as historically fulfilled through the conflicts between the Ptolemies and Seleucids, particularly focusing on failed marriage alliances and subsequent wars.

### Cross-References and Connections

Henry connects this chapter to earlier visions in Daniel 8:11-12 regarding Antiochus Epiphanes, and references the dating to Daniel 9:1. He links the prophecy to historical fulfillment found in Ezra 6:7, where Darius revived temple building after recognizing "the hand of God against his predecessors for hindering it." The commentary also connects to Nebuchadnezzar's image vision, interpreting the failed alliance between northern and southern kingdoms as the iron and clay that cannot unite.

Henry concludes that these detailed prophecies serve to strengthen faith in God's sovereign control over history and His faithful protection of His people through changing worldly powers.
