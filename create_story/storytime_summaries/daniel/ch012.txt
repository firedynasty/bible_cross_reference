# Matthew Henry's Commentary on Daniel Chapter 12: Summary

## Main Theological Themes and Interpretations

**Christ as Michael the Great Prince**: Henry identifies Michael as Jesus Christ, "the great prince" who stands as the church's patron and protector. This standing occurs both during persecution and ultimately through Christ's incarnation to "destroy the works of the devil." Christ stands as substitute, sacrifice, and intercessor for God's people.

**Multiple Fulfillment of Prophecy**: Henry sees the prophecies applying to three distinct periods: the persecution under Antiochus (immediate), the destruction of Jerusalem in 70 AD (near fulfillment), and the final judgment (ultimate fulfillment). The "time of trouble such as never was" references both Christ's prediction about Jerusalem's destruction and the final day of judgment.

**Resurrection and Eternal Rewards**: The resurrection in verse 2 operates on multiple levels—spiritual awakening through the gospel, restoration from political oppression, and the literal resurrection at the last day. Henry emphasizes how this doctrine provided crucial comfort to those suffering under persecution.

## Key Practical Applications

**Comfort for Suffering Saints**: Henry notes that prophets always provide "proper antidotes" alongside predictions of trouble, with "no relief so sovereign" as that drawn from "Christ and the future state."

**Ministerial Encouragement**: Those who "turn many to righteousness" will "shine as the stars for ever and ever." Henry emphasizes that "the more good any do in this world, especially to the souls of men, the greater will be their glory and reward in the other world."

**Active Pursuit of Knowledge**: Regarding the prophecy being sealed until the time of fulfillment, Henry advocates diligent study: "Those that would have their knowledge increased must take pains, must not sit still in slothfulness...but run to and fro, must make use of all the means of knowledge."

## Notable Verse-by-Verse Insights

**Verse 1**: Michael's standing occurs when "the persecution is at the hottest," demonstrating Christ's perfect timing in delivering his people.

**Verse 2**: The resurrection encompasses both "good and bad" since "the net of the gospel encloses" hypocrites alongside true believers.

**Verse 4**: The sealing serves protective purposes—the prophecy "would not be understood, and therefore would not be regarded, till the things contained in it were accomplished." Henry sees this as preserving "a treasure of great value, laid up for the ages to come."

**Verses 11-12**: The specific time periods (1,290 and 1,335 days) point to defined seasons of trial with promised blessing for those who persevere: "Blessed is he that waiteth."

## Cross-References to Other Scripture

Henry extensively connects Daniel's prophecies to other biblical texts: Matthew 24:21 (the great tribulation), John 5:29 (resurrection of life and damnation), Acts 24:15 (Jewish expectation of resurrection), Hebrews 11:35 (martyrs seeking "better resurrection"), and Revelation 20:15 (the book of life).

He also references Ezekiel 37 (resurrection vision), Isaiah 4:3 (those "written among the living"), and James 5:20 (saving souls from death) to demonstrate the interconnected nature of biblical prophecy.

**Memorable Conclusion**: Henry closes with Daniel's personal comfort—though he wouldn't live to see these events fulfilled, he would "rest, and stand in thy lot at the end of the days," emphasizing that God's faithful servants have secure hope beyond their earthly circumstances.
