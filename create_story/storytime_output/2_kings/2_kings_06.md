# Story Time — 2 Kings Chapter 6

## Setting the Scene

In the land of Israel, the air is tense with the whispers of conflict. Elisha, the man of God, is at the heart of it all, quietly wielding a power that cannot be seen but is undeniably felt. The school of the prophets is bustling, having swelled with eager learners. Yet with growth comes the need for space, and so the young prophets seek a larger place to dwell, a humbler haven by the Jordan River.

In this ordinary setting, extraordinary events are about to unfold, reminding us that God is not constrained by the limits of our world.

## The Floating Axehead

The scene opens by the Jordan, where the prophets are busy chopping down trees, their minds fixed on the task. An axe swings, and then—a gasp. The iron head detaches, splashing into the murky depths.

Despair clouds the young prophet's face as he turns to Elisha. "Alas, master! It was borrowed!" he cries out, his distress a testament to the integrity he holds dear. Even in their need, these sons of the prophets know the weight of responsibility.

Elisha, calm and composed, asks only, "Where did it fall?" As the spot is pointed out, he cuts a stick and throws it in. Miraculously, the iron rises to the surface, defying nature's laws.

It's a small moment, yet profound. **God's sovereignty over nature shines through.** He is not bound by the laws He created; rather, they submit to His will.

## The Invisible Armies

The narrative shifts. The king of Syria, frustrated by foiled plans, seeks the counsel of his servants. "Who among us is for the king of Israel?" he demands, suspecting betrayal.

But the answer is simpler: none betray. This is the hand of God at work through Elisha, revealing Syria's plans before they hatch.

In anger, the king of Syria sends a mighty army to Dothan to seize Elisha. Imagine the echo of marching feet, the gleam of armor in the dawn. Elisha's servant steps outside and is paralyzed by fear at the sight of chariots surrounding the town.

"Fear not," Elisha assures him, a quiet strength in his voice. "For they that be with us are more than they that be with them."

He prays for the servant's eyes to be opened, and **suddenly the hills are alive with fiery horses and chariots**—God's unseen armies standing ready.

## Mercy Overcomes

The Syrians advance, but Elisha prays, and they are struck blind. With gentle leadership, he guides them to Samaria and, once their vision is restored, reveals their precarious position.

Should the king of Israel strike them down? No. Elisha advises mercy. A feast is prepared, and the enemies are sent home, unharmed. **Mercy triumphs, achieving what might could not.**

## A Final Reflection

This chapter of 2 Kings speaks softly yet powerfully of God's presence in our world. Through the floating axehead, we learn of His control over creation. Elisha's calm in the face of danger teaches us trust in divine protection. And in mercy shown to enemies, we glimpse a higher path.

In all, this is a story of **faith, integrity, and the unseen forces of good that surround us** each day. Invisible yet invincible, God's plans unfold, reminding us that spiritual realities are far richer than what meets the eye.
