# Story Time — 2 Kings Chapter 14

---

### Setting the Scene

The kingdom of Judah is shrouded in tension, with the shadow of past betrayals and the lingering hope of a new dawn. Amaziah, the young king, stands at the helm. He has stepped into the shoes of his father, Joash, whose life ended in treachery, murdered by his own servants. Now, the eyes of Judah rest on Amaziah, waiting to see what kind of king he will become.

Amaziah has taken his first bold steps as a ruler. He administers justice for his father's murder, but with notable restraint, sparing the murderers' children in accordance with the law of Moses. This act of mercy sets the tone—a king who respects the sacred words of Deuteronomy: "The fathers shall not be put to death for the children’s sins."

### Walking Through the Chapter

Amaziah begins his reign with a heart inclined toward the Lord, but like many before him, he falters at the high places. He does right, but not with the fervent devotion of David. There’s a hint of incompleteness—his obedience stops where tradition begins, unable to uproot the deeply ingrained practices of idol worship.

With the kingdom relatively stable, Amaziah’s ambition grows. He musters his courage and his army, and marches against Edom, claiming victory and capturing Sela. Encouraged by his success, Amaziah’s heart swells with pride, a dangerous swelling that can blind even the righteous.

He turns his gaze toward Israel. Emboldened, Amaziah sends a challenge to Joash, king of Israel. It’s a call to arms—an invitation to battle. But Joash, a seasoned warrior and ruler, responds with a parable—a story of a thistle and a cedar, a tale of pride and presumption. Joash warns Amaziah, cautioning him against overreaching: "The thistle that was in Lebanon sent to the cedar that was in Lebanon, saying, Give your daughter to my son for a wife. And a wild beast of Lebanon passed by and trampled down the thistle."

Despite Joash's warning, Amaziah’s pride blinds him. He presses on, and Judah meets Israel in battle at Beth Shemesh. There, reality crashes down upon him. Judah is defeated, and Amaziah learns the bitter lesson that pride brings low even the mighty.

### Slowing Down on the Key Moment

The thistle and the cedar—what a vivid illustration Joash paints! It’s a moment rich with meaning, one that slows the narrative, demanding reflection. Joash’s parable is more than just a story; it’s a mirror held up to Amaziah’s heart, a challenge to see himself as he truly is.

But Amaziah, in his pride, cannot see his reflection. The warning goes unheeded, the narrative driving toward its inevitable conclusion. Amaziah discovers that the **root of all sin lies in the heart**—a lesson taught in the heat of battle.

### Weaving in Matthew Henry's Insights

Matthew Henry reminds us that Amaziah’s story is one of **incomplete righteousness**. He does what is right, but not with the complete devotion of a king after God’s own heart. The high places remain, a testament to partial obedience, a symbol of the corruptions that linger in the heart.

Amaziah’s decision to spare the murderers' children speaks of **prudence and adherence to God’s law**. Yet, his prideful confrontation with Joash illustrates the **danger of ambition** unchecked by wisdom. "Justice strikes surely by striking slowly," Henry notes, and Amaziah’s rashness in battle serves as a cautionary tale.

### Closing Reflection

This chapter teaches us that true righteousness requires more than mere tradition; it demands the heart of David, seeking God’s will with wholehearted devotion. It warns of the perils of pride, showing how it can creep in after success and blind us to wisdom. Amaziah’s story is a tapestry of ambition, mercy, and the eternal struggle against the **corruptions that endure**. It invites us to examine our own hearts and ask, are we truly seeking God’s will, or are we merely following the footsteps of those before us without the faith that fueled them? 

In the end, Amaziah's reign stands as a reflection of the enduring struggle between human ambition and divine justice—a reminder that the heart truly matters.
