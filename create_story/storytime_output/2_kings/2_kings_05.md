# Story Time — 2 Kings Chapter 5

## Setting the Scene

The land of Aram, a place of war-hardened men. Here stands Naaman, a commander cloaked in valor, draped with accolades. His victories echo through the kingdom, yet beneath his polished armor lurks a shadow—**leprosy**. A great man blemished by a relentless affliction.

Life swirls around him, teeming with whispers of a prophet in Israel, a man of God who could wrestle this disease from his skin. Among the murmurs is a voice, small yet resolute—the voice of a captive Jewish girl, a servant within Naaman's house. Her faith dares to imagine healing for the mighty warrior.

## Key Moments

Curiosity piqued, Naaman takes his case to the king of Aram, who sends him forth with royal letters and treasures, a peace offering to Israel's king. The journey is long, and Naaman's retinue is laden with silver, gold, and garments—a display of wealth and desperation.

The king of Israel reads Naaman's letter and tears his robes. "Am I God, to kill and to make alive?" he laments, fearing a trap. But Elisha, the prophet, hears of the commotion and sends word: "Let him come to me, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel."

Naaman arrives at Elisha's doorstep, expecting grandeur. Yet, the prophet does not come out. Instead, a messenger delivers the simple, humbling instruction: **"Go and wash in the Jordan seven times."**

Naaman's pride flares. "Are not the rivers of Damascus better than all the waters of Israel?" he fumes, ready to storm off. But his servants—their voices steady, persuasive—whisper wisdom: "If the prophet had told you to do some great thing, would you not have done it?"

## The Cleansing Waters

Reluctantly, Naaman descends into the Jordan. **Once. Twice. Seven times.** As water cascades over him, the transformation is miraculous. His skin, once marred, becomes like that of a young boy, pure and new. The river has washed away more than leprosy; it has cleansed his spirit, too.

Naaman returns to Elisha, his heart swelling with gratitude. "Now I know that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel," he proclaims, offering gifts. Yet Elisha, refusing all, stands firm, a silent testament to the grace of God, unattached to wealth.

## A Lesson in Integrity

But not all hearts remain pure. Gehazi, Elisha's servant, sees opportunity in Naaman's gratitude. He runs after him, seeking personal gain. Deception leads to judgment, and Gehazi inherits Naaman's leprosy—a solemn reminder of integrity lost.

## Reflection

This chapter speaks of **God's grace that knows no borders**, reaching even a Syrian commander. Naaman's story is a tapestry woven with humility, faith, and the unexpected channels through which God works. It challenges us to lay aside pride and embrace the simple, profound acts of obedience. 

In the end, it's a story of transformation—of flesh, of heart, and of the deep, unyielding power of divine grace that beckons all, Gentile and Jew alike, to the source of healing and hope.
