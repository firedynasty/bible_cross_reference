# Story Time — 2 Kings Chapter 3

---

The dust of recent events still lingers in the air. In the northern kingdom of Israel, power has shifted hands. **Jehoram**, son of the infamous Ahab and Jezebel, has taken the throne. The kingdom is restless, teetering between old sins and a facade of reform. Jehoram, in a bid to distance himself from his tyrannical parents, removes the idols of Baal. Yet, the golden calves remain—a symbol of compromise that ties his people to the sins of Jeroboam.

Meanwhile, in the kingdom of Moab, rebellion brews. **Mesha**, the king of Moab, refuses to pay the bountiful tribute of lambs and wool that once flowed to Israel. Jehoram cannot let this act of defiance stand. War drums echo as he seeks the help of **Jehoshaphat**, the righteous king of Judah, and the king of Edom, forging a fragile and uneasy alliance.

Together, these kings and their armies march through the arid wilderness of Edom. But trouble looms. The scorching sun beats down mercilessly, and soon they find themselves ensnared in a **drought**, their water supplies depleted. The alliance teeters on the brink of disaster. Desperation sets in, and Jehoram, blaming God, despairs: "Is this why we came, to fall at the hands of Moab?"

But Jehoshaphat, unlike his companion, turns his heart toward God. "Is there no prophet of the Lord here, that we might enquire?" he asks, ever seeking divine counsel. It is here we learn of the **prophet Elisha**, who once served the great Elijah, known only by a lowly servant and not by kings.

Elisha stands before them, resolute and uncompromising. His steely gaze meets Jehoram's. "What have I to do with you?" he challenges, recognizing the hypocrisy of those who seek divine aid only when their plans crumble.

Yet for Jehoshaphat's sake, Elisha agrees to intercede. The prophet calls for a musician, and as the soothing strains fill the air, the Spirit of the Lord moves. **"Make this valley full of ditches,"** he commands, an order as puzzling as it is profound. Without visible clouds, without a hint of rain, the promise of water seems like a dream. But God’s ways often defy human understanding.

The next morning, a miracle unfolds. Water flows from Edom, filling the ditches with life-giving sustenance. The Moabites, seeing the water tinged red by the rising sun, mistake it for blood and assume a massacre among the allied kings. Their false confidence leads them into the trap. Israel's army rises, now rejuvenated, and the Moabite forces fall into chaos and defeat.

But the victory is bittersweet. In a last act of defiance, Mesha sacrifices his own son on the walls of Kir-hareseth, a grim reminder of the darker powers at play.

---

**What does this chapter teach us?** It speaks of God's providence, weaving His purposes even through human missteps and alliances. It warns against incomplete repentance, as seen in Jehoram's half-hearted reforms. Above all, it reminds us of the importance of seeking God earnestly and early, not just in crisis. The true miracle lies not in the water but in the faith to dig ditches in a dry desert, trusting in a promise unseen.
