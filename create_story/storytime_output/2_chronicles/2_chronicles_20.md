# Story Time — 2 Chronicles Chapter 20

## A Crisis Unfolds

The air is heavy with tension in Jerusalem. Rumors swirl through the streets like a bitter wind, carrying news of approaching armies—a great multitude of Moabites, Ammonites, and others. It's a confederacy against Judah, threatening devastation.

King Jehoshaphat stands with a grave face. Once, peace reigned, but now, an ominous cloud looms. Fear dances at the edges of his heart, but in times like these, he knows where true strength lies.

## Jehoshaphat's Choice

Rather than panic, Jehoshaphat chooses a path less traveled by kings of his time. **He sets himself to seek the Lord**. He calls all of Judah to fast and pray, a nation bowing its heart to God. The people gather in Jerusalem, a vast assembly of hope and desperation.

Jehoshaphat stands before them in the temple courtyard. His voice echoes as he acknowledges **God's sovereign dominion**, reminding all present that God rules over the kingdoms of the nations. He speaks not as a king commanding armies, but as a servant depending entirely on divine power. This is a moment of extreme humility—"We have no might against this great company," he prays, "neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee."

## Heaven's Response

The atmosphere shifts when Jahaziel, a Levite, rises with the Spirit of the Lord upon him. "The battle is not yours, but God's," he declares, breathing life into weary hearts. There's no need for swords and shields; no need to tremble or fret.

The people of Judah will not fight this battle, Jahaziel assures. Instead, they will witness God's salvation firsthand. **"Stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord,"** he proclaims—a promise echoing the faith of Moses at the Red Sea.

## The March of Faith

At dawn, they march. But this is no ordinary army. **Singers lead the way, lifting voices instead of arms**, praising the beauty of holiness. "Give thanks to the Lord, for His love endures forever," they sing, each note a testament of faith.

As they sing, confusion grips the enemy camp. Allies turn against each other, and by the time Judah reaches the battlefield, they find only silence where hostility once reigned. The enemies are defeated, not by human hands, but by divine intervention.

## The Spoils of Victory

Judah collects the spoils for three days—riches, jewels, more than they can carry. But the true treasure was unearthed in their hearts. They had witnessed **God's marvellous loving-kindness**, His power made perfect in their weakness.

## The Reflection

This chapter is about faith in the face of fear. It's about knowing when to fight and when to let go, acknowledging that sometimes, the best action is to stand still and trust. Jehoshaphat's story reminds us that when we're at the end of ourselves, God is only beginning. In crises, look up—our eyes are upon thee.

In the tapestry of Scripture, 2 Chronicles 20 weaves a vivid picture of divine grace. It's a call to trust, to worship, and to witness God's deliverance when we choose to depend on Him completely.
