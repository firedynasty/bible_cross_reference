# Story Time — 2 Chronicles Chapter 6

### Setting the Scene

A majestic silence envelops the newly built temple, its grandeur both humbling and awe-inspiring. Solomon, king and son of David, stands before a multitude of Israel. The temple is complete, a gleaming testimony of faith and promise — the culmination of a vision first kindled in his father's heart. The air is thick with anticipation, reverence, and divine presence. The Ark of the Covenant, symbol of God's promise and presence, has been placed within the inner sanctuary, and now Solomon, poised and prayerful, rises to address his people and his God.

### Walking Through Key Moments

Solomon steps forward onto a bronze platform, a subtle yet significant act of humility, as if to elevate not himself but the glory of God. He stands, then kneels with hands outstretched toward heaven, an image of surrender and intercession. His voice rings out, echoing through the sacred halls of the temple, a blend of personal devotion and kingly authority.

Solomon begins by acknowledging the paradox of the divine: **"The heaven of heavens cannot contain You, how much less this temple I have built!"** Yet, in His immeasurable mercy, God chooses to dwell among His people. It’s a profound mystery — the Almighty God, so grand, yet so near.

He recalls God's faithfulness to David, his father, and the fulfillment of the promise to establish David's line. “You have kept what You promised,” Solomon says with gratitude, honoring the covenant that ties generations together.

As his prayer unfurls, Solomon anticipates the many paths his people might walk — the paths of prosperity and adversity, obedience and sin. He prays for forgiveness, for guidance, for a listening ear from heaven when Israel turns back to God. In these petitions, Solomon recognizes the truth of the human heart: **"There is no one who does not sin."**

Solomon’s prayer stretches beyond Israel’s borders, envisioning a day when even foreigners will come to this temple, drawn by the God of all nations. A prayer not just for his people, but for all who seek truth.

### Slowing Down on a Key Moment

Pause here, at the heart of Solomon's plea. The moment crystallizes when Solomon asks God to hear the prayers of even the foreigner. **"When a stranger comes and prays toward this temple," he prays, "hear from heaven.”** It’s a radical inclusion, a glimpse into the scope of divine mercy — a vision that sees beyond ethnic boundaries, foreshadowing a time when all peoples are invited into God's grace.

### Weaving in Matthew Henry's Insights

Matthew Henry reminds us that Solomon's actions are layered with purpose. The temple is not just a building; it’s a portrait of Christ, the Great Mediator. Through Solomon, we see the call to carry forward the "good designs" begun by others, a reminder that in God’s kingdom, one sows and another reaps. 

Henry observes that affliction, evident in Solomon’s words, often leads people back to God. Trouble, he says, will drive the wayward to seek the One they once dismissed.

### Closing Reflection

At its core, 2 Chronicles 6 is a testament to God's unwavering faithfulness and extravagant mercy. Here, Solomon’s prayer is not just a dedication but a declaration. It’s about a God who is both near and far, transcending time and space, yet ever present. It's about a love that knows no boundaries, drawing all people to His heart. The temple, like the cross, calls us to worship, to repent, and to seek a God who hears and heals. Solomon's words echo through time: God keeps His promises.
