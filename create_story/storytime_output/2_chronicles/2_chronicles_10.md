# Story Time — 2 Chronicles 10

## Scene Setting

The sun rises over ancient Shechem, casting long shadows across the city where history is about to hinge on the decisions of a king. Solomon, the great and wise ruler, is gone. In his place is his son, Rehoboam, untested and standing on the precipice of a monumental moment. Whispers of unrest ripple through the crowd gathered to see the new king crowned. Among them are elders and young men alike, each carrying the weight of their own expectations.

## The Threads of Sovereignty

Rehoboam is met by a group of people led by **Jeroboam**, a man who knows something of leadership and rebellion. They come with a simple request: lighten the heavy burden Solomon placed on them. The crowd holds its breath, each person waiting for the king's response. Will the new ruler listen? Will he lead with the humility and grace that could unite a kingdom?

Rehoboam, seeking counsel, first turns to the wise elders who served his father. They advise kindness, urging him to **speak good words** to his people. "Serve them now," they say, "and they will serve you forever." Here, Matthew Henry would nod approvingly, seeing the wisdom of humility and gentleness.

But the echo of wisdom is drowned by the voices of the young men Rehoboam grew up with. "Tell them," they urge, "my little finger is thicker than my father's waist! I will add to your yoke." It's a moment tangled with bravado and short-sightedness, a rejection of the gentle leadership that could save him.

## The Turning Point

And so, the decision is made. Rehoboam speaks with arrogance, doubling down on the burdens of the past. He declares, "My father disciplined you with whips, but I will discipline you with scorpions."

The crowd erupts. Their hopes dashed, their hearts turned cold. They cry out, "What share do we have in David?" and with those words, they sever their ties to the house of David, ten tribes breaking away to form their own kingdom under Jeroboam. 

But hold on, let's slow down on *verse 15*. Here is where the divine dance unfolds. **The cause was of God**, allowing the rift to fulfill the word He had spoken. Yet, Rehoboam is not absolved. His pride and folly leave a scar on history—a reminder of the cost of haughtiness and the power of a gentle answer left unspoken.

## Reflection

Rehoboam returns to Jerusalem, now a king of a diminished kingdom. His choices, his pride, have left their mark. Yet, through it all, God remains sovereign, weaving His purposes through even the broken threads of human folly.

In this chapter, we witness the **symmetry of God's sovereignty and human responsibility**. It's a tale of leadership's weight and the consequences of ignoring wise counsel. Through human folly, God's purposes unfold—a sobering reminder that **gentleness and humility can avert disaster**, while pride and harshness often accelerate it.

Ultimately, 2 Chronicles 10 is a canvas painting the tension between divine plans and human actions, urging us to seek wisdom and lead with grace.
