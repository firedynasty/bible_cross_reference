# Story Time — 2 Chronicles Chapter 27

## Setting the Scene

A new chapter begins in the story of Judah. After King Uzziah’s long reign, where pride tainted his successes, his son Jotham now ascends to the throne. The air is thick with expectation. The people have witnessed the highs and lows of his father's rule and now look to Jotham with hope, wondering if he will follow in the faithful steps of his early years or fall into the pitfalls of pride like his father.

## Walking Through the Key Moments

Jotham rises with a clear vision. He has learned from the shadow of his father’s mistakes, choosing a path of obedience and humility. **He reigns for sixteen years in Jerusalem**, and during this time, he is a beacon of godly leadership. Jotham carefully chooses which of his father’s steps to follow. He builds on Uzziah’s foundations but avoids his missteps, particularly steering clear of the temple where Uzziah foolishly overstepped his bounds before God.

His reign is marked by military success and strategic wisdom. **Jotham builds the high gate of the house of the Lord** and fortifies the walls of Ophel, an area vulnerable to attack. He knows that a kingdom's strength is not only in its spirituality but also in its preparedness and defense. His victories over the Ammonites show that faithfulness and practical wisdom can coexist powerfully.

## The Heart of the Story

But what shines most in Jotham’s story **is his heart**. He "prepared his ways before the LORD his God." This pivotal moment is a quiet yet profound testament to his steady devotion and deliberate spiritual planning. Jotham walks circumspectly, not swayed by the temporal applause or tempted by shortcuts to glory. Here lies the secret of his success: steadfastness.

## Weaving in Matthew Henry's Insights

Matthew Henry paints Jotham as a man of unwavering faith, showing that **steadfast obedience to God leads to blessing and prosperity**. Unlike his predecessors, Jotham does not falter in his commitment. His life is a living lesson that good intentions are not enough; what matters is consistency.

Yet, there's a bittersweet note—a sobering reminder that even a godly king cannot enforce righteousness on a people who are unwilling. The **people continue to act corruptly**, reminding us of the limitations of personal godliness in catalyzing collective transformation. Jotham can build walls and win battles, but he cannot change hearts.

## Closing Reflection

In the chronicles of kings, Jotham stands out as a model of consistent godliness. His reign, though brief, shows us that faithfulness is not about sporadic acts of righteousness but a steady, constant walk with God. It's a powerful reminder that **true strength lies in steadfastness**, and sometimes, the greatest victories are won in the quiet, deliberate choices of obedience to God.
