# Story Time — 2 Chronicles Chapter 26

---

## The Rise of Uzziah

The kingdom of Judah is once more in a delicate dance between prosperity and peril. Uzziah, a young but promising ruler, ascends to the throne after his father Amaziah. The air is thick with expectation. The people watch, hopeful yet wary, as this new king begins his reign. 

Uzziah is sixteen—barely a man, yet burdened with the weight of a nation. But he is not alone. **Zechariah, a wise and godly advisor, stands at his side**, guiding him with counsel rooted in reverence for the Lord. Under his influence, Uzziah is diligent in seeking God's favor. The people watch as the young king grows in wisdom and strength, and the kingdom flourishes. The fields yield abundant harvests, and Uzziah's love for husbandry brings honor to the soil. Cities are fortified, armies are strengthened, enemies are pushed back. Judah is prosperous, and it seems that **"as long as he sought the LORD, God made him to prosper."**

## The Tipping Point

But prosperity is a double-edged sword. As the years pass and Uzziah's successes mount, a shadow begins to creep over his heart. Confidence turns into **pride**, the subtle thief that robs the soul of its humility. He feels invincible, his heart lifted high by the weight of his accomplishments. The king, who once sought divine wisdom with eagerness, begins to forget the source of his strength.

One day, Uzziah makes a bold and fateful decision. He enters the temple of the Lord, his royal robes sweeping behind him. But he is not there to worship in humility. No, Uzziah has come to burn incense upon the altar, a duty reserved exclusively for the priests, descendants of Aaron. The sacred space is filled with tension as his intentions unfold.

## The Moment of Presumption

Azariah the priest, accompanied by eighty courageous priests, confronts Uzziah. Their voices shake with a mix of reverence and fear. "This is not for you, Uzziah," they declare. **"This is the office of the priests."** But Uzziah, wrapped in his royal pride, dismisses their warnings. He stands poised, censer in hand, ready to defy the sacred boundary.

The air grows heavy. Then, in an instant as swift as it is devastating, leprosy breaks out on Uzziah's forehead. Panic ripples through the temple. The king, once full of arrogance, is now a fearful man, driven from the sanctuary by the priests. He leaves in shame, marked by his trespass.

## Reflection and Reality

Uzziah's kingdom, once flourishing, is marred by his downfall. He lives the rest of his days isolated, a leper in a house apart, while his son Jotham oversees the kingdom. It's a sobering reminder of how **"those only prosper whom God makes to prosper."** Prosperity had swelled into presumption, and the cost was tragic.

---

2 Chronicles 26 is a cautionary tale about staying within God-appointed boundaries. It warns us of the dangers of pride and presumption, showing us that prosperity must be handled with humility. Uzziah's story reminds us that in God's kingdom, success is sustainable only through continued faithfulness and humility.
