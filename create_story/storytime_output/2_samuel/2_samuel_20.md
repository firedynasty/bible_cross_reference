# Story Time — 2 Samuel Chapter 20

---

The air is heavy with tension in the aftermath of Absalom's rebellion. The bruised kingdom is trying to mend, but the seams are still fragile. David, weary yet resolute, attempts to piece together his fractured rule from Jerusalem. However, peace is elusive. Just as the rain finally ceases, another cloud of trouble looms — **Sheba, a man of Belial**, is about to stir a storm.

Amidst this fragile truce, a voice rises. Sheba, the son of Bichri from the tribe of Benjamin, calls out defiantly, “We have no share in David, no part in Jesse’s son!” Like wildfire, his cry grabs the attention of the restless Israelites, who are quick to follow. This man is no stranger to dissent — he embodies the age-old enmity against God's chosen leader. As Israel deserts David, the sounds of their departure echo across the hills.

The hastiness of their rebellion is a chilling reminder of human fickleness. One day, they shout "Hosanna!" and the next, "Crucify!" Where, one wonders, is true loyalty in the tumult of shifting allegiances?

David, however, is not naive to these plots. He sees through Sheba’s act — this is no mere rebellion; it is the fulfillment of the ancient prophecy that the sword will never leave his house. **The seeds of David's own choices, his polygamy, continue to bear bitter fruit.** Now, decisive action must be taken. 

David's first task is unsettling. He returns to his palace and finds his concubines, the ones left behind during Absalom's reign. David’s past mistakes hang heavy in the air. He knows what must be done — seclusion, care, but emotional distance. It's a duty mixed with judgment, a necessity that speaks volumes about sin's lasting scars.

The kingdom needs a leader to chase Sheba. David assigns Amasa to gather Judah’s forces, but Amasa dawdles. And in the space created by his delay, Joab steps in like a shadow. His dagger — hidden, deceitful — strikes with lethal precision. **Joab’s treachery is a stark portrayal of ambition without restraint.**

As Amasa’s blood stains the ground, Joab steps over him, unflinching. Joab calls the troops to follow him instead, and such is his influence that they rally, leaving Amasa’s body in the dirt. In this moment, **we see the destructive power of unresolved conflict and unchecked ambition.**

The chase after Sheba leads to Abel of Beth-maacah, a city with strong walls and proud history. As Joab's men lay siege, a wise woman appears at the ramparts. Her voice, strong and clear, echoes with wisdom born of necessity. "Why would you swallow up the heritage of the Lord?" she asks, challenging the very impulse to destroy.

In a twist of diplomacy, warfare is set aside. Sheba’s head is cast over the wall, and with it, the rebellion ends. This is not victory through might, but through wisdom and concession.

---

In 2 Samuel 20, we witness the **shadow of past sins shaping the present**, the fleeting nature of human loyalty, and the insidiousness of ambition. It reminds us that earthly power is slippery, prone to mutability, yet God’s purposes endure. Through rebellion and betrayal, His justice and grace weave a larger story, one that points forward to a Kingdom unshakeable and eternal.
