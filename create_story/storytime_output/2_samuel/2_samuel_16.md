# Story Time — 2 Samuel Chapter 16

## Setting the Scene

The sun rises over a landscape trembling with tension. David, once the mightiest king, now finds himself on the run from his own son, Absalom. The air carries whispers of betrayal and the heartache of a father at odds with his flesh and blood. As David and his loyal followers cross the rugged terrain, uncertainty clings to their footsteps. The mood is somber, yet resolute; a king without a throne, a man with God as his only refuge.

## Key Moments

**Ziba's Approach**

David is deep in thought when Ziba, the servant of Mephibosheth, approaches with donkeys laden with food and wine. The gesture seems generous, a welcome relief for these weary travelers. But beneath the surface, deception waits. Ziba claims that his master has stayed behind to claim the kingdom, throwing Mephibosheth under the bus. David, perhaps too quick to judge, grants Ziba all that belonged to his master, not knowing the full truth.

Is Ziba a friend or a foe? The line is blurred. Here, Matthew Henry would remind us: "Great men ought always to be jealous of flatterers."

**Shimei's Outburst**

As David moves on, a new voice rises above the sound of trudging feet and beating hearts. It's Shimei, a man bitter with resentment against David, hurling curses and stones from a hillside. His words are venomous, accusing David of being a man of blood, a usurper of Saul's house. Yet, with all his hate, Shimei keeps a safe distance, revealing the impotence of his malice.

David's response? Silence and submission. "Let him curse," he tells his men. Perhaps, he reasons, this is God's doing, a chastisement he must endure. He chooses patience, reflecting a deeper understanding of divine discipline.

**Absalom's Arrival**

Meanwhile, Absalom enters Jerusalem, claiming the throne. All eyes are on him, the city a stage for his ambition. He is young, he is rash, and seeking to solidify his power, he listens to the sinister counsel of Ahithophel, who advises actions that would alienate his father and spur the people to his side.

## Slow Down — The Important Moment

David's restraint in the face of Shimei's curses is a profound moment. As rocks fly and insults pierce the air, David's heart remains anchored. He sees beyond the immediate pain and humiliation to the sovereignty of God. This is not mere passivity; it is a deep, abiding trust in God's purposes. Here lies a lesson in endurance: "It may be that the LORD will look on mine affliction, and that the LORD will requite me good for his cursing this day."

## Weaving in Henry's Insights

Henry would highlight this scene as a powerful example of God using even the wickedness of men for His good purposes. Just as He fed Elijah through ravens, God can turn the curses of Shimei into blessings for David. David’s ability to submit to God’s will, to discern the divine hand even in his enemies' taunts, marks the true character of a king after God's own heart.

## Closing Reflection

This chapter is a tapestry of human folly and divine wisdom, where deception and truth intertwine. It’s about seeing God’s hand in the trials that come our way, understanding that even in curses, there can be blessings. It reminds us of the importance of patience, discernment, and trust in the sovereign plan of God. In a world where flatterers abound and stones are hurled, the quiet strength of faith endures.

In the end, it’s not about avoiding adversity but allowing it to refine us into vessels of grace and wisdom.
