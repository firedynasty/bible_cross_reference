# Story Time — 2 Samuel Chapter 17

### Setting the Scene

The tension is palpable. The air in the room feels heavy, charged with ambition and betrayal. Absalom sits with his new court, the usurper of his father's throne, fueled by the fervor of youth and the intoxication of power. Beside him, the trusted counselor Ahithophel, once David's advisor, now whispers deadly plans—plans to seal David's fate and secure Absalom's reign.

Outside the city, David is on the run. But despite the desperation of his situation, there's a quiet resolve about him. He clings to the assurance of a promise made by God, a promise that sees beyond his current trials.

### Key Moments Unfold

Ahithophel speaks first, his words calculated and sharp. He proposes a swift and lethal strike: a surprise attack on David alone, "Smite the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered," he advises. It's a strategy with chilling logic, aimed at cutting the head off the resistance.

Absalom, eager for quick victory, finds this counsel pleasing. The prospect of ending this rivalry in one decisive blow appeals to his impatience. But there's another voice in the room, hushed yet authoritative—Hushai the Archite, David's covert ally.

Hushai understands the art of persuasion. He paints a picture of caution, appealing to Absalom's pride and vanity. "Your father is a man of war," he begins, sowing seeds of doubt about Ahithophel’s plan. Instead, he suggests a grander strategy: gather all Israel and lead them yourself, Absalom. Be the hero at the head of a mighty host.

It's a masterstroke. Absalom, flattered and blinded by his ambition, chooses Hushai's counsel. Unbeknownst to him, this delay buys David precious time—a divine thread weaving through human decisions.

### The Most Important Moment

Verse 14 is the fulcrum of this chapter: "For the LORD had appointed to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel, to the intent that the LORD might bring evil upon Absalom." The divine hand is at work, unseen yet active, turning the schemes of men into instruments for His purpose.

It's here that we pause, breathing in the profound truth that **God's sovereignty reigns over human machinations**. Even when the world feels like it's spinning into chaos, there's a divine order at play.

### Illuminated Insights

Matthew Henry's reflections shine brightly here. He sees in Hushai's success a lesson against the corruption of pride. Absalom, ensnared by his own vanity, becomes his own worst enemy. The narrative echoes with Henry’s insight that "it is easy to betray proud men, by applauding them."

Furthermore, there's a typological echo—a whisper of future betrayal. Ahithophel's plot to strike down David reflects Caiaphas's plot against Jesus, the Son of David. Both schemes, seemingly wise, unravel under the weight of divine intervention.

### Closing Reflection

This chapter is about the unseen hand of divine providence, guiding the course of history even through flawed human agents. It's about the futility of schemes born from pride and the ultimate victory of God's promises. As the day of David's deliverance begins to dawn, we're reminded that while human plans may falter, the purposes of the Lord stand firm, unshakable and true.
