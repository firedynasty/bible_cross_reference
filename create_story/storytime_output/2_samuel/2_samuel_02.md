# Story Time — 2 Samuel Chapter 2

---

David stands at a crossroads, in both mind and spirit. Saul is gone, the king's shadow no longer looms over Israel. But with the end of one era comes the uncertainty of the next. David knows he is anointed. Yet, he waits. He waits not on man, but on God.

In the aftermath of Saul's death, the question hangs heavily: where does David go from here? 

The answer comes not from his own ambition or haste, but from the quiet stillness of prayer. David seeks out the Lord's guidance, asking not just if he should move forward, but precisely where to go. The response is clear and direct: **Hebron**. A city of refuge, a city of priests. A sanctuary. God is leading him to build his kingdom on a foundation of divine protection and spiritual grounding.

---

With trust in God's timing, David takes his first steps in establishing his rule. He goes to Hebron, and there, he is anointed king of Judah. Yet, his kingdom is but a fraction of what it could be. **One tribe, not twelve.** A humble beginning, but one that echoes the unfolding of Christ's own kingdom, built gradually and often quietly amidst opposition.

Meanwhile, somewhere else, another plot is unfolding. Abner, Saul's loyal general, sees a different future. He raises Ishbosheth, Saul's surviving son, as king over Israel. In doing so, Abner pits human ambition against divine will. The tension mounts as two kingdoms, one led by God’s chosen, the other by man’s design, stand at odds.

---

Amidst the political chess game, David extends a hand of grace. He sends a message to the men of Jabesh-Gilead, honoring them for their loyalty to Saul in his final moments. It is a gesture of sincerity, showing that David’s heart is not embittered by rivalry but is magnanimous, seeking peace and unity over conflict.

But not all is peaceful. The clashing ambitions soon escalate into violence. In a field not far from the river Gibeon, young men face off in combat. Each representing their respective kings, they engage in a brutal struggle. **The land is soaked not just in blood, but in the tragedy of civil strife.**

Through this conflict, the narrative reminds us of the futility of opposing God's anointed. It is a sad spectacle, a reminder of how far human ambition can stray from divine purpose.

---

What is this chapter really about? It's about the patient unfolding of God's promises and the need for divine guidance every step of the way. David's journey from an anointed shepherd to king mirrors the gradual establishment of Christ's kingdom. It's a testament that God’s purposes unfold not in the swiftness of human desire, but in the steadiness of divine timing. 

Ultimately, God calls us to **seek Him first**, to move when He says "go," and to wait when He whispers "wait."
