# Story Time — 2 Samuel Chapter 14

## Setting the Scene

The air in the royal court is thick with unspoken tension. King David sits on his throne, but his heart is far away, torn between duty and paternal love. His beloved son, Absalom, is in exile—a self-imposed punishment for the murder of his brother. The kingdom is at peace, but the king's heart is not. **Joab**, David's loyal commander, perceives the struggle within David. He knows that the king yearns for reconciliation with his son, though pride and justice hold him back.

## A Woman with a Parable

Joab, ever the strategist, devises a plan. He enlists the help of a wise woman from Tekoa, a master storyteller who can weave a narrative with the power to melt even a king's resolve. She approaches David with a fictitious tale of family strife—two sons, one slain, the other condemned. Her plight mirrors David's own, and as she speaks, the echoes of his own troubles ring in his ears.

"**Remember the LORD thy God**," she says, reminding David of the mercy he has known, the mercy God shows to his own children. King David, moved by her plea, declares that her surviving son shall be spared. The woman seizes this moment to reveal the parallel to David's own situation. **Why should Absalom remain banished**, she asks, when God himself devises means to bring back the estranged? Her words are a mirror, reflecting David's heart back to him.

## The Moment of Decision

David listens, the weight of her words pressing on him. **Verse 14** stands out like a beacon: "God devises means, that his banished be not expelled from him." This is a story about more than a wayward son. It's about divine mercy, a reminder that justice tempered with mercy is the hallmark of true kingship.

For a moment, the room holds its breath. David, caught between his role as king and his heart as a father, sends for Joab. Absalom's return is ordered, yet not without a condition—he may return to Jerusalem, but he shall not see his father's face. David's decree is both a compromise and a postponement of inevitable confrontation.

## The Tension of Mercy and Justice

Matthew Henry notes that mercy should sometimes "rejoice against judgment." But in this case, David's mercy is incomplete. It's an act of love, yes, but one that lacks full reconciliation. His decision, though merciful, leaves room for future tragedy.

## The Return of the Prodigal

Absalom returns, his presence in Jerusalem a silent testament to David's lingering affection. Yet, he remains banished from the warmth of his father's embrace—a prince in name, but not in heart. The seeds of future rebellion lie hidden beneath these layers of unresolved tension.

## Reflection

This chapter tugs at the complexities of human relationships and divine attributes—justice, mercy, and the difficult dance between them. It reminds us that **God's mercy knows no bounds and that He devises ways to restore the estranged**. For David, the lesson is bittersweet; for us, it's a call to mirror divine compassion in our own lives. True reconciliation requires more than proximity; it needs the courage to fully forgive.
