# Story Time — 1 Chronicles Chapter 28

---

### Setting the Scene

King David stands before the assembled dignitaries of Israel. The air is thick with anticipation. Elders, warriors, and captains fill the room, their eyes fixed on their aging king. David's hair is gray, but his eyes still spark with the fervor that drove a young shepherd to a giant's court. The people expect an announcement, and David prepares to speak with the heart of a father and the authority of a divinely chosen king.

### The Address

David's voice rings clear as he greets the assembly: "My brethren, and my people." His words are a balm, signifying respect and kinship. Although he is their ruler, he does not stand above them but among them. This humility is not mere politeness; it is a reflection of his heart, a lesson in leadership by example.

David recalls his deep desire to build a house for God's Ark. But God, in His sovereign will, chose otherwise. "You shall not build a house for my name," God had told him. Instead, it will be Solomon, David's son, who will have this honor. Solomon, the chosen, points toward a greater reality—a typology of Christ Himself, the ultimate Son who builds an everlasting kingdom.

### The Charge to Solomon

David turns to Solomon, his successor, who stands by with youthful anticipation. "Know the God of your father," David councils. **"Serve Him with a perfect heart and a willing mind."** These words are charged with gravity. God, who searches all hearts, will know if Solomon is constant in obedience. David reminds him that it is not merely the act of building a physical temple that matters but the devotion of his heart.

The task is monumental, not just in scope but in spiritual significance. David unveils the plans for the temple, a divine blueprint given by God’s hand upon him. Each detail is not merely architectural but theological, pointing to a heavenly reality where God’s presence will dwell among His people.

### Focused Moment: Verse 10

The room grows still as David commands, "Be strong and do it." Strength is not merely of the body, but of the spirit. It is an encouragement and a warning. There are promises, yes, but they are coupled with conditions. Solomon must persevere in faith and duty, knowing that **perseverance wears the crown**.

### David's Final Exhortation

David concludes with an exhortation to the leaders and the people. "Take heed now," he cautions. His warning is not just for Solomon but for all who listen. Divine favor rests upon those who are constant in their calling. They are to seek God with diligence, their efforts undergirded by divine grace.

### Reflection

1 Chronicles 28 is not just a historical record; it is a profound narrative of divine sovereignty, humility in leadership, and the enduring call to wholehearted service. It heralds the coming King, who will fulfill these types perfectly. At its heart, this chapter is about fidelity to a divine mission, echoed in each call to "be strong" and "do it" by faith. In God's kingdom, leadership is not about who is first or strongest but about who is chosen and who serves with an undivided heart.
