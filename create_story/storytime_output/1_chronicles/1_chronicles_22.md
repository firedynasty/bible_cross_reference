# Story Time — 1 Chronicles Chapter 22

### Setting the Scene

The air is thick with history and hope. David stands on holy ground, contemplating the future. The Lord has turned former judgments into a site for dreams. This is the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite—once a place of divine judgment, now to become the sacred ground for God's dwelling among His people.

The decision is clear in David's heart: the temple must rise. But it's not for him to build. His hands, though blessed in battle, have shed blood. This is a task for Solomon, his son—a man destined for peace, not war. David sees his own limitations and, in them, God's sovereign grace.

### Walking through the Chapter

David gathers the people of Israel. The dust of the threshing floor still clings to the hems of their garments. He begins preparations with zeal, organizing materials—iron for nails, bronze in abundance, cedar logs in great supply. The king shows an urgency and foresight that only comes from understanding the weight of his calling. **"Where God gives rest, He expects work."**

Addressing Solomon, David's voice is both a father's gentle guidance and a king's solemn charge. This temple will not just be a building; it will be a testament, a beacon of God's presence. But Solomon must know: this is no simple inheritance. It carries a divine responsibility.

David is acutely aware of his son's youth. He remembers his own early days, the fear and the faith. Yet here, he is determined to give Solomon a head start, easing the path for this young king to fulfill a divine mandate. **"Those that are aged and experienced should consider those that are young and tender, and provide them what help they can."**

The vision expands beyond personal dynasties. This temple will stand as a symbol of hope—a promise of God's presence. And in an act of foreshadowing, this site chosen from a Gentile's land whispers of a future when God's family will swell beyond the borders of Israel, encompassing all nations.

### The Moment to Breathe

David's instructions to Solomon shine with clarity and purpose: "Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or discouraged." These words are not mere encouragements; they are a divine commission. Just as Joshua once stood on the precipice of promise, so now Solomon stands, called to build where others have fought. **"Grace here has recovered the upper hand."**

### Illuminating Insights

Matthew Henry observes that David's disqualification from building the temple does not stem only from his sin with Uriah but from the very nature of warfare. Yet, in this exclusion, there's a deeper grace—God's choice to establish a temple of peace through a man of peace. This points to Christ, who would build His church not through conquest, but through the saving of souls.

David's labor sets the foundation, but it is Solomon who will build. This intergenerational partnership reveals a divine order, where the past sacrifices lay the groundwork for future glory.

### Closing Reflection

1 Chronicles 22 isn't just about constructing a physical temple. It's a lesson in grace, legacy, and divine purpose. God turns judgment into blessing, preparing a place for His presence among His people. It teaches us that **divine rest calls for divine work**—that our lives, like David's, are part of a larger tapestry of God's redemptive plan. In our limitations, God’s grace prevails, guiding us to prepare for the peace and presence He promises to bring.
