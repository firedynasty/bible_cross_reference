# Story Time — Judges Chapter 1

---

The dust of Joshua's passing still lingers in the air. The Israelites, once united under Joshua's leadership, now find themselves standing at a crossroads. Each tribe is entrusted with the responsibility to claim the lands promised to their ancestors. The task is immense, and the path forward is uncertain. Yet, in this moment of transition, the question hangs in the air: **Who will lead us now?**

As the chapter begins, the Israelites seek divine guidance. "Who shall go up first for us against the Canaanites, to fight against them?" they inquire. The divine answer comes swiftly: "Judah shall go up." Judah, the tribe esteemed for its strength and honor, is appointed by God to take the lead. It's no random choice. This is the tribe out of which the King of Kings will eventually arise — the tribe that holds the scepter of destiny.

Judah, with the faithful Simeon at its side, marches into battle. **Christian cooperation and mutual aid** unfold before us, as the tribes band together to face a common foe. They capture Bezek, and there they encounter Adoni-bezek, a king whose reputation for cruelty precedes him. This monarch, who once delighted in the misery of others, meets justice in a form strikingly familiar. His thumbs and big toes are severed — the same fate he had callously inflicted on seventy kings. Adoni-bezek's own words echo the divine justice at play: "As I have done, so God has requited me." A poignant reminder that "what goes around, comes around," woven into a tapestry of providential retribution.

The city of Jerusalem falls into Judah's hands, and the conquests continue. Hebron, Debir — each city falls under the weight of divine blessing and Judah's mighty effort. But the chapter also tells of cities left unconquered, of Canaanites dwelling alongside Israelites. This incomplete obedience paints a picture of the challenges that persist when God's commands aren't fully embraced.

Amidst victories and setbacks, **divine sovereignty** remains the backbone of the narrative. The Israelites learn that success isn't just about their strength or strategy. It's about aligning with divine will, understanding that God is the true leader of their armies. Without His blessing, even the mightiest warrior cannot prevail.

But let's pause and breathe in verse 7 — Adoni-bezek's confession. It's a moment where conscience awakens. The scales of divine justice tip, and a king acknowledges his downfall as justly deserved. Here, even amid conquest, we are reminded of the **power of conscience** and the righteous judgments of God.

As this chapter draws to a close, we see a tapestry of humanity interwoven with divine threads. It's a story of tribes striving to fulfill a divine mandate, of alliances tempered by faith and need, of victories marred by incomplete obedience. But through it all, it is a story of God's faithfulness to His word.

**Judges Chapter 1** is about the necessity of aligning human effort with divine purpose. It's about cooperation, justice, and the consequences of incomplete obedience. Above all, it's a call to trust in God's sovereignty and seek His guidance in every endeavor.
