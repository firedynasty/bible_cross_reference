# Story Time — Judges 20

---

### Setting the Scene

The land is heavy with a sense of unease. Not long ago, the people of Israel celebrated their settlement in the land promised to them. Now, an unfathomable darkness shadows the land. A Levite's concubine has been brutally violated and murdered, a crime so heinous it echoes the sin of Sodom. Tension simmers as tribes prepare to gather—a storm of vengeance and judgment is on the horizon.

Mizpeh, the chosen meeting place, bustles with activity. **400,000 armed men** assemble, not as individuals but as one, unified by righteous indignation. They come from every tribe, bound by a common cause: justice for the atrocity in Gibeah, a city nestled within the tribe of Benjamin. Yet, beneath their righteous zeal lurks the potential for tragedy—an entire tribe teetering on the brink of annihilation.

### Key Moments

The Levite stands before the assembly, his countenance stoic yet tinged with grief. He recounts the nightmare, his words careful, his modesty evident. He speaks of lewdness without dwelling on the sordid details—a narrative both clear and damning. The assembly listens, hearts ablaze with anger.

**Unity for a Just Cause**

There's a palpable unity among the tribes, an agreement that transcends their usual disputes: the wicked must be purged from Israel. They resolve to confront Gibeah, seeking justice in accordance with God's laws. It's an extraordinary moment of national consensus—**"knit together as one man,"** as they prepare for war.

**The Benjamites Stand Alone**

When the messengers deliver the call for justice to Benjamin, the response is defiant. Instead of surrendering the guilty men of Gibeah, the Benjamites choose to stand with them, their hearts hardened. It's a decision that sets the stage for a tragic conflict; obstinacy against righteousness.

**A War of Brothers**

The clash is fierce, brother against brother, and the cost is great. The men of Israel, though zealous, face unexpected resistance from the Benjamites, who fight with desperate valor. The battlefield becomes a place of sorrow, soaked with the blood of kin—a grim reflection of how even a just cause can lead to devastation.

### The Heart-Stopping Moment

Israel suffers two defeats initially, questioning their resolve and purpose. They weep and fast before the Lord, seeking divine guidance. The Lord speaks through the priests, promising victory at last. The third day of battle dawns with hope. Using strategy and the lessons learned from prior failures, Israel lures the Benjamites out from their safe confines of Gibeah and strikes with a final, decisive blow. 

The smoke from Gibeah rises as a signal, a sign from heaven. The Israelites turn upon the Benjamites with renewed vigor. It's a harrowing moment, where justice meets mercy, where victory nearly erases an entire tribe from existence, fulfilling the irony of righteous zeal turning nearly catastrophic.

### Matthew Henry's Insights

Henry's insights underscore the complexity of this war—a just and holy battle marred by tragic outcomes. **Corporate responsibility** and justice demand unity, but they must also be tempered by wisdom and mercy. The story warns of the dangers when obstinacy meets righteous zeal.

**"So little do those that do iniquity consider what will be the end thereof."** This haunting truth reverberates through the ages.

### Reflection

This chapter is not just a historical account; it's a mirror reflecting the human condition. It asks us to consider the cost of justice and the peril of unchecked zeal. It challenges us to uphold righteousness while being wary of its potential to consume us. In the end, it's a reminder that even amidst the darkest tales, there's a call to seek God's guidance and mercy. 

Justice must be served, but it is mercy that must temper our hearts.
