# Story Time — 1 Samuel 26

---

## Setting the Scene

The tension is palpable. David, the future king of Israel, is once again on the run. Saul, the current king, who once swore peace with David, has had his heart twisted back to jealousy and rage. He's in pursuit, with three thousand of Israel's best soldiers at his command, scouring the wilderness for the one he views as a threat.

David, however, is not alone. His loyal band of followers stays close, and they find themselves near the hill of Hachilah, watching as Saul sets camp for the night. Darkness falls, and within it, a quiet opportunity arises.

---

## Key Moments Unfold

David stands at the edge of Saul’s camp, the flickering light of distant fires casting shadows across his face. With him is Abishai, one of his loyal men. Quietly, they make their way down into the camp, stepping carefully over sleeping soldiers. It's as if a divine hand has swept a deep sleep over them all — a slumber from the Lord, as if the very breath of God bids them to rest.

There, before them, lies Saul. His spear, emblematic of his authority and power, is thrust into the ground beside his sleeping form.

Abishai leans into David, whispering eagerly, "Today, God has delivered your enemy into your hands. Let me pin him to the ground with one thrust of my spear; I won't need to strike twice."

But David's heart beats with a different rhythm. He shakes his head firmly. "No. The LORD forbid that I should lay a hand on the LORD's anointed." His voice carries a weight of conviction, a refusal to seize power through violence. Instead, he opts for a less bloody proof of his presence — taking Saul's spear and water jug as evidence of this night’s encounter.

---

## Slowing Down — Key Verse and Themes

As David and Abishai slip back out of the camp, the scene breathes with tension and divine orchestration. Saul's spear in hand, David climbs to a safe distance and calls out to awaken the camp. The moment is ripe with dramatic irony — Saul, who sought David's life, now awakened to the proof of his vulnerability.

David's voice rings out, not with anger, but with a plea for understanding and justice: "Why does my lord pursue his servant? What have I done, and what wrong am I guilty of?" He does not boast of his intention, rather he shows a heart that prizes innocence over retribution, trust in God's timing over seizing the moment. **"The LORD rewards every man for his righteousness and faithfulness,"** David declares, hoping the message will not just waken Saul’s ears, but his heart too.

---

## Weaving in Henry's Insights

Matthew Henry sees in David a model of patient endurance and respect for God’s authority. Even when presented with the perfect opportunity to dispatch his adversary, David chooses the path of integrity. He recognizes that to act against Saul is to act against God’s chosen, demonstrating a profound submission to divine providence.

The deep sleep over Saul's camp serves as a reminder of God's power to protect and control — He can, in a moment, render the mighty powerless, the watchful blind. David's faith in God’s justice helps him to resist the temptation to take matters into his own hands.

---

## Reflection

1 Samuel 26 tells of heart's corruption and divine sovereignty. It’s about waiting for God’s perfect timing rather than forcing our hand. David shows us that even when surrounded by temptation and danger, we have choices. Will we trust in God’s providence or seize control ourselves? David’s decision teaches us that true power lies in patience and righteousness. Sometimes, the greatest strength is restraint.
