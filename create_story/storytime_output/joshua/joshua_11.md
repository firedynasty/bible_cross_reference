# Story Time: Joshua 11

---

## Setting the Scene

Joshua stands on the cusp of another battle. The land of Canaan, with its sprawling cities and formidable kings, seems a world unto itself, teeming with challenges. News of Israel's victories spreads like wildfire, and fear grips the hearts of many.

But now, a new alliance forms. Kings gather, driven by desperation, uniting under the banner of a shared threat — Israel. The air is thick with tension, the clamor of armies preparing for war. They're ready to reclaim their lands, or so they think.

## Key Moments in the Narrative

The kings of the north rally, like waves crashing against a steadfast shore. Jabin, king of Hazor, sends out the call. From every corner, warriors come: from the hills and valleys, the forests and the plains. Their numbers are vast, as numerous as the sand on the seashore.

Yet, in the face of this overwhelming force, Joshua stands undeterred. He's bolstered by a quiet assurance, **a promise whispered from Heaven**: “Do not be afraid. Tomorrow at this time, I will deliver them up slain before Israel.”

The march is relentless. The Israelites advance under the cloak of God's promise, hearts synchronized with divine timing. And when the moment comes, they descend upon their enemies with a thunderous resolve.

Swords clash, and the air rings with the cries of battle. The horses and chariots, symbols of the enemy's strength, are rendered useless. Joshua, in obedience to God's command, hamstrings the horses and burns the chariots. A bold act of defiance, **a declaration of trust in God over military might**.

## A Moment to Breathe

As the dust settles, the enormity of the victory sinks in. Joshua leaves nothing undone. Every instruction passed down from Moses, fulfilled with unwavering fidelity. It's not just a conquest of land but a testament to faithfulness — from promise to fulfillment.

Here, we see a profound truth: **Obedience to God is the true measure of strength.** Joshua's adherence to divine commands showcases a legacy of faith, passed from Moses, now firmly rooted in his leadership.

## Weaving in Henry's Insights

Matthew Henry speaks of divine sovereignty, depicted starkly in the hardening of hearts. The kings gathered, not by chance, but to fulfill divine justice. **Sinners bring ruin upon their own heads**, and God's hand is justified in the unfolding events.

Unity against a common enemy reflects the worldly wisdom often lacking among the faithful. Yet, amidst diversity, the church is called to unity, shaming discord as we face our own spiritual battles.

Joshua's reliance on God, over horses and chariots, reminds us of the true source of power — a theme echoed through **Psalm 20:7** and beyond.

## Closing Reflection

Joshua 11 is more than a tale of battles won. It's a story of divine orchestration, where human obedience and heavenly purpose intersect. This chapter teaches us that true victory lies in trusting God's word over the world's might. In life's battles, we are called to unite, to trust, and to obey, finding strength in promises kept and commands fulfilled. 

May we, like Joshua, walk in courage and faith, knowing that **with God, the impossible becomes possible**.
