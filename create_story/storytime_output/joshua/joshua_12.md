# Story Time — Joshua Chapter 12

---

As the dust of battle begins to settle across the plains of Canaan, a sense of stillness descends upon the camp of Israel. The warriors, weary yet triumphant, gather to recount their victories. Joshua stands amidst them, a steady leader holding the mantle first worn by Moses. The land lies conquered before them, a testament to **God's unfailing promises**.

The scene is both somber and celebratory. Behind them linger the remnants of past struggles, yet before them stretches the promise of home—a land to claim and to cultivate.

---

In this chapter, we encounter a list, a litany of conquests, stretching from the days of Moses to the recent triumphs under Joshua. Here are **thirty-one kings**—each a story, a battle, a triumph over formidable foes.

The first part recounts victories under Moses' leadership. There was Sihon, king of the Amorites, who fell before Israel's march. Then came Og, king of Bashan, with his great cities—Ashtaroth and Edrei. Mighty palaces stood as testaments to his rule, but now those stones quietly tell of God's might. Og, with his dual regal residencies, now lies under one earth, a single grave sufficing where once two palaces did not.

Yet it is under Joshua's leadership that the list truly swells. In rapid succession, God delivers king after king into their hands. From the ridges of Hermon to the lush plains by the Great Sea, Israel's victories weave a tapestry of divine faithfulness. 

These aren’t just battles, they are **the stories of God's goodness**. Each victory comes with a lesson, each conquered city a testament to God's enduring mercy. 

---

But one cannot merely pass over what these names represent. Let's pause on one of them. Perhaps it's the king of Jerusalem, Adoni-Zedek. Imagine his view from the fortress walls as the Israelite force approached. Did he see an invincible army or a wave of divine judgment? What thoughts crossed his mind in those final moments? This is more than history; these are echoes of choices made and fates sealed.

Henry reminds us that these victories are not just Israel's to celebrate. They are a foreshadowing, a glimpse of the work completed by **our Lord Jesus, the true Joshua**. Just as Joshua led Israel into the promised land, Christ leads us into spiritual blessings, into the heavenly Canaan. This isn't just ancient history; it's a timeless promise.

---

As we conclude, let us not forget the human element. The Israelites stand at the brink of a new chapter. Behind them, kings and kingdoms lie vanquished. Before them, the uncharted future beckons with hope.

This chapter is a reminder that **God is unwaveringly faithful**. He fulfills His promises, not just in ancient days but today. It’s a call to remember past mercies, to honor those who have led before, and to recognize that God's goodness continues in every blessing we receive.

In essence, Joshua 12 isn’t merely about victories won; it’s about **God's enduring faithfulness** and the unshakeable foundation that faith offers us all.
