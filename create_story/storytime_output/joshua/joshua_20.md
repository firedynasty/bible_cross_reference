# Story Time — Joshua 20

---

The dust has barely settled from the conquests. Joshua, the seasoned leader of Israel, stands amidst a people who have found a new land but are still learning to live within its borders. The Israelites, now scattered across the vast territories, are beginning to feel the weight of everyday life—the mundane, the routine, and yes, even the unexpected dangers.

God, ever watchful over His people, commands Joshua to establish something extraordinary: **cities of refuge**. These aren't just ordinary cities; they are appointed places saturated with divine purpose, echoing both justice and mercy.

### Setting the Scene

Imagine the bustle and noise of Israel's tribes settling into their new homes. There are tents being pitched, families establishing roots, and children playing, unaware of the deeper strains of life. Amidst this, a different urgency grips the elders as Joshua gathers them.

"You shall appoint cities of refuge," God instructs, a provision for an accidental killer to flee—a sanctuary from vengeance, a haven of safety. It's a law that whispers of both justice and grace, a shadow of greater things to come.

### Key Moments

Joshua, faithfully executing God’s command, establishes **six cities of refuge** throughout the land—three on each side of the Jordan. Kedesh, Shechem, Hebron on the west; Bezer, Ramoth, Golan on the east. Each city strategically placed, ensuring no child of Israel is more than a day’s journey from protection.

Picture a man fleeing, heart pounding, as he runs from a tragedy he never intended. The cities stand open, gates wide, ready to receive the weary and fearful. The elders at the gates listen, discerning intent, ensuring justice is not blind but compassionate.

Within these walls, **life changes**. The refugee is safe but not free, bound to the city until the high priest’s death. It's an odd kind of liberty, a "prisoner at large," but one that teaches the delicate dance between justice and mercy.

### The Most Important Moment

Focus in on one man, perhaps, standing at the city gate, his voice trembling as he recounts his story. The elders nod, their faces etched with understanding and wisdom. Here, a balance is struck—a human life valued, with provision for its protection and the promise of eventual release.

In this moment, we see a **glimpse of the Gospel**. These cities are a refuge just as Christ is our refuge. They are not merely places; they are symbols of God's enduring mercy and a foreshadowing of the ultimate sanctuary found in Jesus.

### Insights from Matthew Henry

Like the cities, our refuge in Christ is accessible to all—no one is too far to reach Him. The names of the cities themselves whisper His nature: Kedesh, holy as He is holy; Shechem, bearing our burdens as He shoulders ours; Hebron, inviting us into fellowship as He does. In each, we find a reflection of the safe harbor Jesus offers.

### Closing Reflection

This chapter is about **more than cities**; it's about a God who plans for our safety, who knows our need for refuge in a world that is unpredictable and harsh. It's about finding solace and redemption not just for the body, but for the soul. And most profoundly, it's about a promise that transcends time—a promise fulfilled in Christ, our ultimate city of refuge.
