# Story Time — Joshua 5

---

### Setting the Scene

The air is thick with anticipation. Joshua and the Israelites stand at the threshold of the Promised Land, having just crossed the Jordan River on dry ground. Before them lies Canaan, a land ripe with promise but shadowed by formidable foes. The mood is a tense mix of excitement and awe. Here, God has shown them the way with miraculous power, and yet, a solemn act of faith is required next.

### Key Moments

The story unfolds as the Israelite men, weary from their journey, face a command that seems almost reckless. **Circumcision.** As they camp at Gilgal, God instructs Joshua to renew this ancient covenant ritual, long suspended during their wilderness wandering. With trust, Joshua obeys, knowing such vulnerability could be perilous so close to enemy cities. Yet, this act is not just about physical compliance; it's a profound declaration of faith, a rolling away of the **"reproach of Egypt."** They are no longer slaves; they are heirs to God's promise.

Watching from their fortified walls, the kings of Canaan's cities can feel their courage melting. Unknown to them, it is God's sovereignty weaving this tapestry of fear and faith, a divine script where His people will not fight alone.

### A Moment to Breathe

Amid the healing and hope, the manna—their daily bread from heaven—ceases. It’s a subtle yet monumental shift. They taste the produce of Canaan for the first time, a rich abundance replacing the miraculous provision. God’s people are transitioning from wilderness wanderers to settlers of the promise. The end of manna marks a new chapter, where reliance on God is not diminished but transformed.

### The Mysterious Captain

As night falls, Joshua wanders near Jericho, perhaps lost in thought about the battles ahead. There, a man stands with a drawn sword. Joshua approaches cautiously, asking, "Are you for us, or for our enemies?" The reply is neither, but rather, “As commander of the army of the Lord, I have now come.” Joshua falls to the ground, recognizing this divine visitor. A pre-incarnate Christ, Henry suggests, who comes to guide and fortify Israel’s leader for what lies ahead.

God's armies are unseen, yet omnipresent. What comfort is there in knowing we are never truly alone?

### Reflection

Joshua 5 is a chapter of trust and transition. It shows Israel stepping into their identity as God’s covenant people, relying not on military might but on divine promises. It challenges us to begin our endeavors with God, trusting His timing and provision, even when it defies logic. True confidence comes from knowing the Captain of our salvation leads the charge. Here, the past is rolled away, and the promise unfolds—a true testament to faith over fear.
