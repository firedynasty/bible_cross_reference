# Story Time — Joshua Chapter 22

---

The journey has been long, and the dust of battle still lingers in the air, but now, as the sun begins to dip below the horizon, a new chapter unfolds. The eastern tribes—Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh—stand at the banks of the Jordan River. Their hearts are full with the joy of a mission completed. They have been faithful to their brothers, fighting alongside them to claim the Promised Land. Now, Joshua, their leader, their commander, stands ready to send them home.

**A palpable sense of accomplishment fills the air.** Joshua's words are warm, brimming with gratitude. He commends their readiness, their loyalty, their unwavering support in the battles that lay behind them. “Return to your tents,” he tells them with a smile, “and take with you the riches of victory: the spoils of war.” But he offers more than just gold and livestock; he leaves them with a charge—a spiritual charge to remain steadfast in obedience to God’s law.

As they depart, there is a warmth in the air, a feeling of unity among God’s people. The tribes begin their journey back, laden with wealth and a deeper treasure of shared experiences and covenant faithfulness.

But as they cross the river, something unexpected occurs. An altar rises on the horizon—a great, imposing structure. The other tribes, those who remain in Canaan, see it and their hearts stir with concern. **Could it be rebellion?** They had just left the land unified, and now this?

**Misunderstandings have a way of lighting fires in the heart.**

Thankfully, the western tribes choose wisdom over rash action. A delegation is sent—an act of peace, not war. Phinehas, a man of zeal and integrity, leads them. Their mission: to understand, not to accuse.

The eastern tribes welcome them and explain. The altar is not for sacrifices or offerings, not a place of worship apart from the tabernacle, but a symbol, a witness. It stands as a testament to their shared faith in the God of Israel, a reminder to future generations that they too are part of the covenant community. 

**"The Lord Himself knows,"** they plead, "it is not rebellion. It is remembrance."

As Phinehas listens, understanding dawns. The potential for division fades, and peace reigns. The delegation returns with good news, and the people rejoice. Instead of the spark of conflict, there is the glow of unity.

**What can we take away from this moment of misunderstanding resolved?**

Joshua’s parting counsel echoes: **"Take diligent heed to do the commandment and the law."** The strength of God’s people lies in their covenant faithfulness and unity. In our spiritual journeys, we must guard against division, seek understanding before judgment, and hold fast to obedience.

In these verses, a simple yet profound truth emerges: **True unity is anchored in shared faith and honest communication.** This story is not merely about ancient tribes and their alter, but about us—about the unity we strive for in our communities and the vigilance required to maintain it.
