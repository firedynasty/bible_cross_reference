# Story Time — 1 Kings Chapter 7

## Setting the Scene

The dust settles over Jerusalem as the laughter and shouts of workers fade with the evening sun. The scent of cedar wood and fresh stone lingers in the air, remnants of the tremendous labor that has just culminated in a house fit for the King of Kings. Solomon's temple stands proudly, a beacon of devotion and skill. 

Yet, Solomon's work is far from over. With the temple completed, he turns his attention to his own palace and other significant structures, each demanding precision and artistry. The city hums with anticipation as the next chapter of grand construction begins.

## Walking Through the Chapter

Solomon doesn't rush. Thirteen years he spends building his palace, a testament to patience and care. Unlike the haste and fervor seen in the temple's construction, his own house rises slowly, thoughtfully, as if to silently affirm that God's honor takes precedence over personal splendor.

His residence is no ordinary abode. The "house of the forest of Lebanon," with its columns of cedar and impressive courtyards, resembles a majestic forest. Imagine walking through halls where sunlight dances through "three tiers of windows," creating a tapestry of light and shadow. It's a retreat, a sanctuary, echoing the peace of country life and the elegance of royal living.

Among these edifices, the "porch of judgment" stands out. It's where King Solomon, known far and wide for his wisdom, personally hears appeals. Here, justice is not a distant concept but an accessible reality. It's a place where the king meets his people, reflecting divine justice in earthly form.

And then, there's Hiram of Tyre, a man of mixed heritage and extraordinary skill. His craftsmanship is unmatched, his hands guided by a wisdom that speaks of divine favor. Bronze pillars, basins, and intricate carvings come alive under his touch. One might wonder, how does such talent come to be? God, it seems, delights in weaving diverse gifts into His grand designs.

## Key Moment

Pause at the crafting of the temple furnishings. Such detail, such care in every piece. The notion that "God, who is the best, must be served and honoured with the best" permeates the work. Bronze seas, intricate capitals, and pomegranate motifs speak not just of artistic prowess but of a heart that offers nothing less than excellence to God.

Take a moment here. Breathe in the dedication, the devotion embedded in each artifact. These are not mere objects; they are expressions of worship, reflections of a heart intent on glorifying God.

## Weaving in Henry's Insights

Matthew Henry tells us that Solomon's priorities teach a profound lesson: **"Seek God first."** It's a timeless truth, echoed in the New Testament, that establishing a foundation in piety leads to lasting prosperity. Solomon's patience in building his house, after completing God's temple, embodies this principle.

Hiram's role, guided by God's providence, displays how divine wisdom works through human abilities. God's purposes unfold through the talents and skills of His people, sometimes in the most unexpected ways. 

## Reflection

This chapter is about priorities — divine and human intertwined. It's about offering our best to God, seeking Him first, and trusting that everything else will follow in its time. Solomon's buildings are architectural giants, but their true magnificence lies in the heart of worship they symbolize.

In God's kingdom, every detail matters. Every act of service, every offering of excellence, becomes a fragrant act of worship. This chapter invites us to consider: What are we building? Whose glory are we seeking first?

In the end, Solomon's wisdom and patience remind us to lay our foundations in early piety and let our lives become temples — each act a stone, each day a pillar in the dwelling place of the Almighty.

---

**"Seek first the kingdom of God."** Solomon's story calls us to align our priorities, to offer our best in every endeavor, and to trust in the gentle weave of divine providence through our humble hands.
