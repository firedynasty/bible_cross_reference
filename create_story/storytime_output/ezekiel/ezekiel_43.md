# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 43

---

### Setting the Scene

The air is thick with anticipation. Ezekiel stands in a vision, having been transported far from the chaos of his people's judgment into a realm that beckons with hope and renewal. The atmosphere is solemn yet charged, as if the very stones around him are holding their breath. This is a moment of divine revelation—a promise of restoration.

Ezekiel, the prophet, finds himself at the threshold of a new era. The remnants of past transgressions linger in the air, but there's a palpable shift. The scene unfolds with a sense of holiness, veiled in mystery. This is a place where man meets God, and where God, despite past separations, is ready to return in glory.

### Key Moments of the Chapter

The vision begins as Ezekiel is led to the eastern gate. **Out of the east comes the glory of God, like the rising sun that chases away the night.** The light is radiant, filling the temple with a presence that speaks of mercy, forgiveness, and new beginnings. God's glory arrives not merely to dwell in stone and mortar but to fill the hearts of His people once more.

The sound accompanying this divine arrival is like the roar of "many waters," a voice that speaks of His power and His purification. It is the voice of authority and grace, casting away shadows and illuminating truth.

Inside the temple, Ezekiel is awestruck. The temple, once bare and lifeless, now pulses with life as God's glory fills it. Yet, this isn't about architectural splendor but about divine presence—God has chosen to return to His people, to dwell among them in a new and profound way.

God speaks directly to Ezekiel, declaring, "Here will I dwell," reaffirming His commitment to His people. But there is a caveat—**the people must rid themselves of their idols, their false thresholds set beside His.** This is a call to purity and undivided worship.

Ezekiel is tasked with laying out the plans for the temple to the people. **It is not merely architectural but moral and spiritual—a blueprint for holy living.** This pattern is meant to inspire repentance and a deeper understanding of God’s desires for His people.

### A Moment to Breathe

Pause for a moment at the heart of the chapter when the glory of God fills the temple. Feel the weight of that glory—a glory not just meant to be seen, but to transform. This is a foretaste of the gospel's fullness, where Christ would shine as the ultimate revelation of God’s glory.

### Insights and Reflections

Matthew Henry reflects on this chapter as a turning point. God, once distant due to Israel's rebellion, is now returning with "everlasting loving-kindness." **Repentance leads to greater revelation, and knowledge demands obedience.** God's goodness should move us to reflect on our own shortcomings and spur us to change.

This chapter isn't merely about ancient rituals but about the heart—a heart open to God, ready to embrace His presence and live in His ways.

### Closing Reflection

Ezekiel 43 is about **God’s glory returning not to a building but to a people.** It challenges us to tear down our idols and prepare room for Him. When we do, we find that His light not only fills our spaces but also transforms our lives, leading us from shame to understanding, from estrangement to communion. This chapter is a call to be filled with the very presence of God, echoing His voice, and living as His temple in the world. 

In the end, it's about connection—God with His people, and His people with their God.
