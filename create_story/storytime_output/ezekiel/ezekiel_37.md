# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 37

---

## Setting the Scene

The air is thick with anticipation as Ezekiel, God's chosen prophet, stands in a place that is anything but alive. He finds himself in a valley littered with **dry, lifeless bones**. This isn't just any vision—it's a desolate landscape that speaks volumes of Israel's shattered hope. Captivity has left them in political and spiritual ruin, dispersed among foreign lands with no strength in sight. 

The mood is heavy. Yet, there’s an undercurrent of expectation. **Can these bones live again?** 

## The Valley of Dry Bones

Ezekiel hears the voice of the Lord, asking the seemingly impossible, "Son of man, can these bones live?" Ezekiel, wise and humble, responds not with certainty or doubt but with trust. "O Lord God, you know."

This is a moment pregnant with possibility. Is God about to demonstrate His unmatched power over what seems irrevocably lost? 

Ezekiel is commanded to prophesy over the bones, a task that feels like watering a dry stick. But what happens next is nothing short of miraculous. As Ezekiel speaks, he hears a rattling sound—the bones are **coming together**. What was once a scattered heap begins to take form, sinews and flesh appearing, a testament to God's creative power.

Yet, something crucial is missing. Despite the physical restoration, there is no life. God instructs Ezekiel to call to the breath, the very ruach, the spirit of life, to enter these bodies, transforming them into "an exceeding great army." They are not just living men now but effective, fit for service.

## Unity and Restoration

The vision doesn't end with the army. God goes further, illustrating His promise of unity through the symbol of two sticks. One represents Judah and the other Ephraim—once bitterly divided. God instructs Ezekiel to join them into one stick, uniting them as one in His hand. This act is a powerful sign of reconciliation, hinting at a future where divisions will be healed under the Messiah's reign.

Here, in this fusion, lies a prophecy of a greater union: the joining of Jews and Gentiles, all peoples brought into one covenant family through Christ.

## Breathing Life Into Despair

The valley is not just about bones; it's about **God's power to bring life into the darkest despair**. It's about revival—on three levels. Spiritually, as souls are resurrected from sin to righteousness. Corporately, as the church emerges from persecution to strength. And individually, as believers look forward to the resurrection at the end of time.

In a world where hope seems distant, Ezekiel’s vision is a clarion call: with God, *nothing is impossible*. Restoration begins with faithful proclamation, coupled with earnest prayer, inviting God's breath to animate what appears dead.

---

**Reflection**

Ezekiel 37 is a story of resurrection in the face of ruin, a reminder that no situation is beyond God's reach. The dry bones speak of our deepest despair, but also of the divine promise that life, unity, and restoration are always possible through God's Spirit. This chapter assures us that God makes the impossible possible, turning desolation into a **mighty army** of hope.
