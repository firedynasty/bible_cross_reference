# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 9

---

### **Scene Setting**

The air is heavy with the weight of divine revelation. Ezekiel stands amidst a vision, a vivid tapestry of celestial and earthly realities intertwining. The sins of Jerusalem have been laid bare, and now, the consequence looms. The glory of the Lord begins its gradual retreat, a hesitant departure that speaks louder than words. The Shekinah glory, once dwelling among the people as a sign of divine presence, shifts slowly, regretfully towards the threshold.

The heavens are about to act, and Ezekiel is caught right in the middle of this cosmic interplay.

### **A Stark Command**

In the depths of this vision, a call rings out with authority and urgency: "Draw near, O executioners of the city, each with a weapon of destruction in hand."

Once, only two angels were dispatched to the wicked Sodom, but here, in Jerusalem, six approach with solemn purpose, each representing a gate of entry into the holy city. A clear message: Jerusalem's transgressions demand a thorough and grievous reckoning.

Yet, amidst the grim assembly, one figure stands apart. A man clothed in linen, dignified and serene, bears a writer's inkhorn by his side. This is no ordinary scribe. Here, in the vision’s stark landscape, he embodies something profound—**mercy woven into judgment.**

### **A Mark of Distinction**

The Lord's voice pierces the tension—"Pass through the city, through Jerusalem, and put a mark on the foreheads of those who sigh and cry over the abominations within."

How beautifully selective is this act! A mark, unseen by human eyes, distinguishes between the righteous and the wicked. In a sea of complacency, it's the mourners over sin who are recognized by God. Their grief is their salvation. This mark is a signature of divine ownership and protection, a reflection of God's care for those who remain pure and compassionate amidst widespread corruption.

Does this not echo the ancient Passover night, where markings preserved life amidst death's shadow?

### **Judgment Begins**

The scene shifts with a chilling command: "Begin at my sanctuary." The judgment's commencement from the temple signifies a crucial truth—proximity to holy things does not shield from accountability. The religious garments and sacred spaces offer no immunity from God's righteous scrutiny.

The six angels move swiftly and methodically. Judgment is indiscriminate of status, reaching even those who once served in His house. A sobering reminder that divine justice is impartial.

### **The Reluctant Departure**

As judgment unfolds, the glory of the Lord, still lingering at the threshold, is reluctant to depart completely. It’s as though the very presence of God hesitates, yearning for the people to turn, to repent, to invite His return.

This gradual departure—a slow, sorrowful exodus—is a plea as much as it is a consequence. God departs only when driven away by the unrepentant hearts of His people.

### **Closing Reflection**

Ezekiel 9 is not just about wrath; it's about **God's faithfulness in judgment**. He marks His own, preserving them for His purposes, even while allowing sin's consequences to unfold. It is a poignant reminder that God’s love persists even amidst deserved judgment—a calling for us to remain tender-hearted, to hate sin, and to weep over the world’s brokenness. 

In the shadow of calamity, the divine mark assures us of something greater—**eternal life**—a comfort that sustains past the temporal and into the eternal.
