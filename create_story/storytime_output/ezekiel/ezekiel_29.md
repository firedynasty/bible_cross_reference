# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 29

## Setting the Scene

The world stands in tension. Israel, exiled and broken, watches for a savior. Eyes turn to Egypt, a juggernaut of old, a beacon of hope. Yet, amidst the chaos, the voice of God comes to Ezekiel, stirring with power and purpose. The mood is one of anticipation, a hushed waiting in the air as if the very earth holds its breath.

## The Dragon of the Nile

Ezekiel stands, eyes lifted, and God speaks to him with clarity and force. "Son of man," He begins, "set your face against Pharaoh, king of Egypt."

The scene shifts to the mighty Nile, its waters coursing with life and mystery. Here, Pharaoh likens himself to a great dragon, a creature of legend and might. "My river is mine, and I have made it for myself," he boasts, chest swelling with pride.

But God sees through the façade. He is not impressed by Pharaoh's self-deification, his bold claims to divine prerogatives. God whispers of hooks and jaws, a humbling extraction from the river—a reminder of divine sovereignty over even the most arrogant of rulers.

**Who truly holds power?** The answer is clear: God alone.

## Egypt: A Broken Reed

Ezekiel, with prophetic clarity, conveys a shattering message. Egypt, a nation trusted by Israel in desperate times, will prove a "staff of reed." When leaned upon, it will break, leaving splinters and wounds. 

Here lies the danger of creature-confidences. It's a stark reminder that earthly alliances are frail. Egypt, once a towering symbol of strength, will be laid waste, a desolation for forty years. A period of humbling, like a long, dark night.

**Could Israel learn to fully trust in God alone?**

## Humble Restoration

Yet, there is a flicker of grace in God’s words. Egypt will rise again, but as "the basest of the kingdoms." Not the head, but the tail. A future restored but stripped of past grandeur. It's a lesson in humility, a divine balancing act between judgment and mercy.

Ezekiel watches as the warnings unfold. He sees the consequences of misplaced trust and the folly of pride. Pharaoh’s river is not his own; it belongs to the Creator of all things.

## Breathing in the Moment

The heart of this chapter beats strongest at the realization that **all is God’s**. Whether rivers or kingdoms, the ownership is always His. Humanity, often so quick to claim and control, falls into the trap of self-worship, neglecting the truth of divine sovereignty.

## Reflection

Ezekiel 29 isn’t just a story of judgment. It’s a vivid tapestry of God’s power, woven with threads of humility and trust. It calls us to relinquish our tight grasp on earthly powers and possessions, recognizing that all we are and have is God’s. In a world obsessed with self, this chapter whispers about **true freedom found in surrender**. Let us learn to rest in that knowledge.
