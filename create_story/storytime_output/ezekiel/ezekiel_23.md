# Story Time — Ezekiel 23

## The Two Sisters

The scene opens with a mournful weight hanging in the air. Ezekiel, the prophet, stands amidst a people who have forgotten the sacredness of their bond with God. A story, as old as time, unfolds through his words — a tale of two sisters, Aholah and Aholibah, who have lost their way.

Aholah represents Samaria, and Aholibah embodies Jerusalem. These names whisper their meanings: **"her own tabernacle"** and **"my tabernacle is in her."** They are symbolic, revealing the deep-seated truths of their spiritual journeys.

## A Seductive Allure

From their youth, these sisters engaged in folly. Their hearts wandered first in the land of Egypt, where they tasted what it meant to stray from the true God. This early corruption clung to them, much like inherited sin, seeping into their bones.

Aholah, the elder, was drawn to the allure of Assyria. The power, the culture, the might — it dazzled her. She sought after what seemed strong and appealing, ignoring the One who had claimed her as His own. Israel's infatuation with Assyria was like a moth to a flame, leading her down a path that would soon demand a price.

## The Cost of Betrayal

Jealousy and judgment from God are swift. **The very nations Israel courted become her scourge.** What she desired was the very instrument of her downfall, as if the idols she worshipped turned on her. The Assyrians, once allies, now deliver judgment upon her, stripping her of honor and freedom.

Aholibah, witnessing her sister's plight, should have learned. But instead, she spiraled deeper into the embrace of Babylon. Judah, too, felt the pull of worldly strength, forgotten was the God whose presence was in their midst. The wisdom of lessons unheeded — hadn't they seen the ruin Israel faced?

## The Most Potent Moment

As the narrative unfolds, the gravity of their betrayal becomes evident. God's words pierce through like a blade: **"They were mine."** This is not just a statement of possession but an anguished cry of a lover scorned. Their infidelity is **the highest injustice** to the One who had laid claim to them with love and covenant.

This is where the story slows, drawing us in, forcing us to confront the reality of spiritual adultery. God's heart aches not just for their disobedience but for the ingratitude toward a Benefactor who had given them everything.

## Reflection and Warning

Ezekiel's tale is not just for ancient ears. We are invited to see our own reflections in these two sisters. What do we idolize? To what do we cling, thinking it will save us? The chapter warns against idolatry in all its forms, cautioning us against alliances that pull us away from God.

**Sin is a slippery slope.** It calls with a whisper but can consume with a roar. We are urged to learn from the judgments of others, to let their stories be our cautionary tales.

In the end, Ezekiel 23 is about fidelity — about recognizing that we are claimed by a God who desires our whole hearts. It's a story meant to stir the soul, urging us to remain steadfast in our covenant with Him.

A tale of two sisters, two paths, and the God who calls us back. Let us listen, and let us choose wisely.
