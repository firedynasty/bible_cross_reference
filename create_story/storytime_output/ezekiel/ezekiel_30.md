# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 30

---

The air is thick with tension. It's a cloudy day in the prophetic landscape, where the horizon is painted with the strokes of impending doom. Ezekiel stands as God's mouthpiece, his voice charged with the weight of divine justice. The stage is set for a dramatic unfolding of God's plans — not just for Israel, but for mighty Egypt and her allies.

**"Woe worth the day!"** Ezekiel cries out. It's a call to mourn, for a "day of the LORD" is dawning. This isn't just any day; it's a day when God reveals himself as a judge over the nations, including Egypt. The mood is somber, filled with an ominous anticipation. What is this "cloudy day" Ezekiel speaks of? A day darkened by the absence of comfort, where the harsh reality of God's judgment looms large.

As Ezekiel's prophecy unfolds, the imagery is vivid and unsettling. Egypt, with its great power, populous cities, and mighty warriors, seems invincible. Yet, in the face of God's decree, all these sources of strength are doomed to crumble. **The fire that consumes nations** is kindled by none other than God Himself.

Picture this: Egypt, with its royal splendor and ancient idols, stands proud, leaning on its false supports. But like a great tree, it will be uprooted, left to fall without the support it once trusted. It's a stark reminder of the **futility of false supports**. No military might, no geography, no alliances will stand when God decides to act.

Ezekiel speaks of a "broken arm" when he mentions Egypt's fate. It's an image of irreversible defeat. An arm that will not be bound up or healed signifies a permanent weakening. Pharaoh's power will be shattered, and the once-great nation will be brought to its knees. **What can protect Egypt now** when the righteous God has come forth to contend with them?

In contrast, Babylon's arm is strengthened. Here is God, not an indifferent observer, but an active force shaping the world's power dynamics. He strengthens Babylon, even though they are called "the wicked." It's a perplexing reality, but it shows how God uses even the wicked for His righteous purposes.

**And there lies the irony**: Egypt, who deceived and failed others, will herself be deceived. Those who stand as God's enemies will face inevitable consequences. The message is clear — **association with God's enemies brings disaster upon their heads**.

Ezekiel's voice echoes, reminding all who hear: "They shall know that I am the Lord." It’s the revelation of divine supremacy over all gods and nations, a declaration that none can resist His will or His justice.

As we reach the end of this chapter, we see that it's not just about historical events. It's a profound statement about **God's sovereignty**. In executing judgments, God reveals His character — just, sovereign, and supremely in control. The message is timeless: trust not in earthly powers, for they will all pass, but in the Lord, whose **justice is as certain as the dawn**.
