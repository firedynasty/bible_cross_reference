# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 7

---

## Setting the Scene

The air is heavy with impending doom. Ezekiel stands alone, a solitary figure amidst a world teetering on the brink of divine judgment. Jerusalem, once a city of glory and promise, is now shrouded in the weight of its own sins. The people cling to their lives, oblivious to the storm gathering on the horizon.

Ezekiel, the watchman, feels the burden of his message. The time for warnings is running out; the end is not just near—it's here.

## The Coming End

As Ezekiel lifts his voice, the words are sharp and clear, cutting through the noise of denial and distraction. "The end, the end has come upon the four corners of the land." These are not the idle threats of a forgotten prophet. This is a divine decree.

God's voice thunders through him, resonating with a finality that cannot be ignored. The judgment is not arbitrary; it is the culmination of years of rebellion and rejection of mercy. **"I will judge you according to your ways,"** the Lord declares, and in those words lies a profound truth: divine justice is perfectly calibrated.

**"An end, the end is come,"** echoes repeatedly, as if to drill into the hearts of the hearers the certainty of what's to come. This is not merely the end of a city or a nation—it's a harbinger of the ultimate judgment, the final reckoning.

## Wrath and Justice

God's wrath is not capricious; it is sovereign and controlled. The Lord is the master of His anger, unleashing it only when His patience is exhausted. **"My eye will not spare you, neither will I have pity,"** He says, and the chill of those words is palpable. It's a sobering reminder that rejecting divine mercy leads inevitably to judgment.

As the chapter unfolds, the sins of the people are laid bare. Pride and violence have sprouted, and now they blossom into judgment. **"The rod of wickedness"** has risen, and it will strike with unerring accuracy. Retributive justice is at work; the punishment mirrors the sin perfectly.

## Universal Destruction

Ezekiel paints a picture of inescapable destruction. **"The sword is outside, pestilence and famine are inside,"** he warns. There is nowhere to hide, no refuge from the coming storm. It's a scene of total devastation, where safety is a mirage and hope, a flickering candle in the wind.

Yet, even in this darkness, there is an implicit call to repentance. The urgency is tangible—there is still a sliver of time to turn back, to change course before the final curtain falls.

## Reflection

Ezekiel's message is a clarion call across the centuries. It's a reminder that divine justice is not arbitrary but perfectly just. The chapter challenges us to reflect on our own lives, to consider the inevitable end of days, both collectively and individually. 

Repentance is not just a one-time act; it's a posture of the heart. The end for Israel was near, but for each of us, the end of our own days could be closer than we think. In the face of divine judgment, are we prepared?

In Ezekiel 7, we see a God who is just and sovereign, a people called to accountability, and a world on the brink of ultimate judgment. The urgency of Ezekiel's message rings through time, calling us to repentance and reformation before it's too late.
