# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 6: The Mountains Shall Hear

---

### Setting the Scene

The air is thick with judgment in exile-bound Babylon. Ezekiel stands amidst his fellow captives, a prophet with a vision so intense, it's as if the very mountains themselves quiver at the sound of his voice. God has directed him, and now, with eyes set like flint, he must speak to the land of Israel through an unusual audience—the mountains.

**Why the mountains?** Because, in the eyes of God, they are as guilty as the people, standing as silent witnesses to the idolatrous deeds done upon them. This is the backdrop as Ezekiel draws breath, ready to deliver the weighty words of the Lord.

---

### The Judgment Against Idolatry

Ezekiel lifts his face, speaking not to men, but to the earth itself. "Mountains of Israel, hear the word of the Lord!" he cries, as if he were a judge declaring a sentence. It’s a dramatic scene; the mountains may not respond, but they must listen. For they have been places of false worship, altars built upon them for gods who are not gods.

**Idolatry, after all, is no small offense.** It's a betrayal, a spiritual whoredom, as if the very marriage covenant between God and His people has been broken. The idols will fall, Ezekiel declares, and the places of worship turned to ruin. What’s made of human hands will become dust, consumed by the sword of the Chaldeans—a potent instrument in the hand of God’s justice.

---

### The Gracious Preservation of a Remnant

In the midst of such dire pronouncements, a glimmer of mercy emerges. God will spare a remnant, those who remember Him among the nations to which they are scattered. **Why is this significant?** Because even in judgment, God’s grace continues to breathe life into His people. They will remember, they will repent, and they will return—not necessarily to a physical place, but to the heart of their Creator.

This remnant is a testament to God’s patience. A reminder that even in the face of rebellion, the door to repentance remains open. Affliction becomes a tool not just for punishment, but for awakening. Out of the ashes of destruction, faith will rise again.

---

### A Call to Lamentation

Ezekiel’s message is not just about judgment or mercy, but also about a call to deep lamentation. The people must grieve not just the loss of their land, but the loss of their relationship with God. It begins with remembering—a crucial turning point in true repentance. **Have they forgotten Him?** If so, their return begins now, in their memory and their tears.

---

### Slowing Down on Verse 9

"And those of you who escape will remember me among the nations where they are carried captive, how I have been broken over their whoring heart that has departed from me."

This verse breathes with divine emotion. God speaks of being "broken"—a rare, vulnerable expression of divine regret and longing. It is as if the heart of God itself is laid bare, wounded by His people's waywardness. Yet, simultaneously, it echoes hope: they will remember.

---

### Closing Reflection

Ezekiel 6 is a chapter of contrasts: judgment and mercy, idolatry and remembrance, rebellion and repentance. It reminds us that God is both just and merciful, a jealous God who will not share His glory with another. Yet, He is unfailingly loving, desiring His people to return to Him with all their heart.

In times of spiritual wandering, may we be quick to remember and return, allowing even our afflictions to draw us closer to the One who calls us His own.
