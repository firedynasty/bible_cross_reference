# Story Time — Ezekiel 28

---

The air in the bustling city of Tyre is thick with the salt of the sea and the clatter of commerce. This island fortress, seated at the confluence of sea routes, has grown fat with riches. Its king, perched upon a throne of opulence, surveys his domain with a heart swollen by self-glory. He stands at the pinnacle of human achievement, and whispers echo through the marble halls, chanting his name as if it were divine.

But behind this façade of invincibility, a storm brews. Ezekiel, the prophet, brings a message that cuts through the gold and glitter like a sword. He stands, his voice carrying the weight of divine authority, confronting the king who dares to claim, **"I am a god."**

The king of Tyre, in his silken robes, believes himself to be untouchable, wiser than any, even the revered Daniel. But Ezekiel’s words dismantle this illusion with the precision of a craftsman. **Pride is a blinder**, and this king, drunk on his own success, cannot see the precipice on which he stands.

Ezekiel paints a picture of judgment that is as vivid as it is inevitable. Strangers, fierce as lions, will descend upon Tyre. They are not traders or allies, but "the terrible of the nations." They will tear down the city's walls and leave its king to die not as a god, but as a mere mortal, exposed to the harsh truth of his own humanity.

In this moment of vulnerability, the king must confront the reality of his mortality. **"Wilt thou then say, I am God?"** Ezekiel's question pierces the king's guise, a reminder that all riches, all wisdom, and all power are fleeting shadows beneath the eternal sun of God's sovereignty.

Ezekiel pauses, letting the gravity of his message hang in the air. He speaks not only to the king but to all who would hear: **Pride is a thief of reason.** It steals the heart's balance, leading the proud toward a fall. The king’s fatal flaw is not his achievements but his belief that they made him equal to God.

This chapter is a wake-up call, a reminder that **man is but man**—regardless of wealth or wisdom. Each heart beats finite rhythms. We are dust, and to dust we shall return. The call here is to humility, a recognition that all we have is from the hand of God. Riches and achievements are gifts, not altars for self-worship.

Ezekiel 28 teaches us this truth: **Pride precedes the fall, but humility opens the door to grace.** In every age, before every throne, there is only one God, and His glory He will not share.
