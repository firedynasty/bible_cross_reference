# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 26

---

## Setting the Scene

The air is thick with anticipation. News travels fast, even to the island city of Tyre, which sits majestically over the azure sea. Tyre has watched the fall of Jerusalem with a calculated mix of scorn and delight. Jerusalem, once a rival, now lies in ruins — an opportunity for Tyre to expand its wealth and influence. The mood in Tyre is not one of mourning but of self-satisfied opportunity.

But Ezekiel, standing as God's mouthpiece, has a message for Tyre. It is not one of prosperity, but of impending doom.

## The Waves of Judgment

The word of the Lord comes to Ezekiel with the fury of a storm. **"Behold, I am against thee, O Tyre,"** God declares. Like waves relentless against the shore, God promises to bring many nations against Tyre. Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, will lead this charge — a "king of kings," a figure of overwhelming might used as an instrument of divine justice.

**What peace is left for those against whom God wages war?**

Tyre's pride, the joy over Jerusalem’s misfortune, becomes its folly. God's judgment is not light or momentary. The once prosperous city will be leveled, scraped bare like the top of a rock, left to be a place for the spreading of nets in the midst of the sea. The very stones and timber, symbols of its prosperity, will be cast into the waters, ensuring that Tyre will never rise again.

## A Moment of Total Devastation

As the prophecy unfolds, the imagery grows more vivid. **Nebuchadnezzar’s siege is meticulously described.** His armies will break through gates and batter down walls, bringing the city's bustling streets to an eerie silence. Not just a conquest — but a thorough erasure.

The destruction is total, and the implications are profound. Even the dust of Tyre will be swept away, leaving a barren rock exposed to the elements. What once was vibrant and arrogant will become a spectacle of desolation.

## Reflection on Worldly Security

Tyre's story is a stern warning against the **vanity of worldly prosperity**. The riches Tyre hoarded and valued above all else now serve as its downfall, a grim reminder that earthly wealth is fleeting and fragile. 

Ezekiel’s prophecy is a mirror reflecting Tyre’s heart: opportunistic and cold, rejoicing in the downfall of others. It is a caution against the secret pleasure found in others' misfortune, a selfishness that corrodes the soul.

## Closing Reflection

This chapter of Ezekiel is a narrative of divine justice, a reminder that no nation or person is beyond the reach of God’s judgment. Tyre's fall serves as a warning to cherish humility over pride, compassion over opportunism. In God’s kingdom, true security does not rest on wealth or power, but in righteousness and love for one’s neighbor. Tyre's story is a call to examine our hearts and align them with the values of our Creator.
