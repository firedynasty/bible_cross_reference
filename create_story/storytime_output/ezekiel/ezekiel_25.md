# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 25

---

## Scene Setting

The dust has barely settled over Jerusalem, a city now echoing with the silence of God's judgment. Ezekiel, the prophet with a heavy heart, continues to hear the voice of the Almighty. The mood is somber but filled with a glimmer of hope. Before him is a vision that extends beyond the borders of Israel, a divine proclamation against the nations that mocked and reviled God's people in their darkest hour.

The air crackles with the tension of divine justice. For now, the spotlight shifts from Israel to those who gloated over its downfall.

---

## The Judgment Unfolds

Ezekiel stands firm as the words of the Lord pour forth, aimed first at the **Ammonites**. Their sin? A malicious glee at the collapse of the temple, a holy site profaned and turned to ruin. Their laughter was a dance of mockery, a chorus of "Aha!" resounding in the heavens. But divine justice sees all, and God's patience has limits.

"Men of the east," says the Lord, will descend upon them. The once regal city of Rabbah will become a mere stable for camels, a testament to pride turned to dust. It's a lesson in the danger of **Schadenfreude**. Does not God delight in mercy rather than punishment? The gleeful heart provokes heaven. 

The vision moves to the **Moabites**, whose sneers cut deeper than any sword. They revel in declaring, "The house of Judah is just like all the heathens." Their error lies in their eyes - seeing nothing special, assuming Israel's God no different than the pagan pantheon. But behold, says the Lord, Moab shall face the same fate as the Ammonites. Their pretense of security is but a house of cards against divine wrath.

Then come the **Edomites**, their hearts hardened with vengeance instead of compassion. They are Israel's brother, yet they acted as an avenger, not ally. God’s message is clear: vengeance belongs to Him alone. The hand of Israel, empowered by divine justice, will be their undoing.

---

## The Most Important Moment

Watch the judgment against the Philistines. Amidst the litany of condemnations, it’s here the deep reasons for God's actions cascade forth — His **jealousy for His people's honor**. What a profound truth! His own name is intricately tied to the fate of Israel. When the Philistines sought old hatreds and vengeful hearts, God’s wrath was kindled. It is not His pleasure to punish, but His duty to defend His honor and those of His own.

Through His actions, He teaches a timeless truth: **"You shall know that I am the LORD."** Not only Israel, but all nations should recognize His sovereignty.

---

## Reflection

Ezekiel 25 isn't just a roll call of judgment but a revelation of God's character — just, sovereign, and fiercely protective of His people. It's a reminder that divine justice is meticulous, reaching beyond borders and generations. While Israel is punished, she is never forsaken. God’s love, unfailing and unyielding, shines brightest against the backdrop of judgment.

Here lies the chapter’s heart: despite appearances, God vindicates His own. The message resounds — **divine justice will prevail, and in doing so, all shall see that He is the LORD.**
