# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 15

---

## Setting the Scene

The air is tense. The prophet Ezekiel stands amid the exiled Israelites, with the weight of a nation heavy on his shoulders. These are people who have seen their homeland fall, their temple desecrated, their city burned. They long for answers, for hope, but Ezekiel must deliver a hard truth. 

God calls him once again. This time, with an image as vivid as the scorching sun hanging over the Babylonian landscape.

---

## An Unfruitful Vine

Ezekiel raises his voice, painting a picture with his words. "Consider," he begins, "the vine that grows in the forest." His audience envisions the twisted branches, its tendrils reaching for the sky. But what is its worth compared to the mighty oaks and cedars? 

"Is it of any use when it's barren, without fruit?" he asks. 

The people murmur, recognizing that an unfruitful vine is **useless**, not even good for timber. It is only **fit for the fire**, he declares, a metaphor that hits home with a searing clarity.

But Ezekiel isn’t speaking of botany. He speaks of them—of Jerusalem—once **a choice vine planted by God**, now barren, no longer fulfilling its holy purpose.

---

## The Fire Consumes

Ezekiel's words turn ominous. He describes the vine being consumed by flames, its **ends and middle burning**, until nothing is left. Here, his voice echoes with the authority of divine judgment. 

"Even when whole, it was meet for no work," he says. 

The warning is clear: their escape from one calamity is not deliverance but a mere **reprieve**; another fire awaits. The fire that devours is not just Babylon's wrath but the consuming fire of God's judgment.

The people listen, their hearts heavy with the realization that without repentance, they too will face a succession of judgments.

---

## A Moment to Breathe

Ezekiel pauses. The crowd shifts, the gravity of his message settling upon them. They are like that vine—chosen, cherished, yet now at risk of being discarded, of having failed their calling. 

This is more than a tale of woe; it's a call to renewal, a plea to return to God, lest they become utterly consumed—**good for nothing** but the fire.

---

## Weaving in Henry's Insights

Ezekiel's message isn't just for ancient Israel. Matthew Henry reminds us that this is a universal truth about God's sovereignty and the serious call for His people to live up to their divine purpose. If they fail, like the vine, they become as **salt that has lost its savor**, doomed to be cast out and trampled underfoot.

The solemn reminder: God will bring glory through obedience or judgment. He will not be a loser by any of His creatures.

---

## Closing Reflection

Ezekiel 15 isn't just about Israel's past. It's a poignant reminder for us today. Without fruit, we risk becoming unfit for God's work, mere kindling for the fire. It's a call to examine our lives, to seek holistic renewal, and to bear fruit worthy of our calling. The true essence of this chapter lies in the choice set before us: to be fruitful or to be consumed.
