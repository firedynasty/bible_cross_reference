# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 3

---

### Setting the Scene

Ezekiel stands in a surreal landscape, a space where heaven and earth seem to touch. He just experienced a vision of God's glory, a glory so overwhelming that it brought him to his knees. Now, God speaks directly to him, entrusting a mission that will define his life. The air is heavy with divine purpose. There's an urgency in God's voice, a weight in His words.

### Key Moments

With a great voice, the Lord instructs Ezekiel to receive His message in the most intimate way possible. **"Eat the scroll,"** God commands. It's not just any scroll but one filled with laments and woes. Yet, as Ezekiel takes it into his hands and consumes it, its bitterness gives way to sweetness. Here is a profound truth: God's words, truthfully digested, are always sweet to the soul, even when they speak of judgment.

But there's no time to savor the moment. God sends Ezekiel to a rebellious house—the house of Israel. Imagine the irony: had Ezekiel been sent to a foreign nation with unfamiliar speech, they might have listened. But to these, who should be most receptive, God assures Ezekiel they will not listen. **Why? Because of their hardened hearts.** A sobering truth about divine sovereignty is laid bare: God's people, despite their blessings, have become callous.

Yet, God doesn't leave Ezekiel to face this daunting task alone. **"I will make your face as hard as theirs,"** God promises. In the face of stubbornness, Ezekiel receives a divine fortification—courage like an adamant, harder than flint. It's a promise of resilience and determination, God equipping him for what lies ahead.

Ezekiel is picked up by the Spirit, carried away in bitterness and heat of spirit, yet the hand of the Lord is strong upon him. This mix of emotions—sweetness and bitterness—is the reality of his prophetic calling. It's a divine paradox: the joy of receiving God's word coupled with the sorrow of delivering it to a rebellious people.

### Slowing Down

Pause and picture Ezekiel as a watchman. This is more than just a title; it's a sacred duty. **A watchman's role is to see danger and sound the alarm.** But what if the people refuse to heed the warning? God articulates the seriousness of this role. Ezekiel must speak God's words with fidelity, for neglecting this duty places the burden of lost souls on him. Yet, if he warns and they do not turn, their fate is their own. Ezekiel, then, bears the mantle of profound responsibility in a world deafened by its own rebellion.

### Matthew Henry's Insights

Matthew Henry reminds us: ministers must internalize and faithfully proclaim God's word. They must carry the sweetness of truth, even when it's difficult. And when faced with opposition, the strength God imparts is not just for Ezekiel, but for anyone called to speak His truth. **When vice is bold, let righteousness be bolder.** 

### Closing Reflection

Ezekiel 3 is an invitation to see God's word as both sweet and serious. It is not enough to hear; we must internalize and respond. God equips those He calls, granting courage in the face of opposition. As bearers of truth, may we be watchful, resilient, and ever faithful to our divine assignment. 

What does it mean for us today? To be both sweetly nourished and soberly fortified by God's word, ready to share its life-changing message in a world that may not always want to listen.
