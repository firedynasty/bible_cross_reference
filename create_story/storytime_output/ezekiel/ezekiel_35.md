# Story Time — Ezekiel Chapter 35

---

### Setting the Scene

In the aftermath of hope rekindled, where the echoes of God's promise to restore Israel still linger, we turn our eyes to a desolate mountain range. It's a land that once thrived, now cloaked in silence and shadows: Mount Seir, the heart of Edom. The air is thick with an ancient tension, a story as old as the two brothers, Jacob and Esau. Here, Ezekiel stands, a voice in the wilderness, tasked with a message as heavy as the stones beneath his feet.

### Unfolding the Drama

Ezekiel's voice rings out, breaking the stillness like a prophet’s horn. "Set your face against Mount Seir," he commands. The words carry a divine decree. Mount Seir, a symbol of unyielding enmity, stands as the epitome of all who oppose God's people, just as Esau opposed Jacob.

This isn't just about land or lineage. It’s about **God's justice**—a reckoning for those who have harbored "perpetual hatred." Edom, with its bloodstained history and treacherous deeds, has stood against Israel time and again. But now, the scales of divine justice are being set right. Those who have thirsted for blood will find it pursuing them relentlessly. A graphic picture of justice, indeed. 

Amidst Ezekiel’s words, a critical moment hangs in the air. **“Whereas the LORD was there,”** he says. Let’s slow down here. Despite Israel's exile, despite the rubble of Jerusalem, the promise remains. God is present. His covenant stands firm, unbroken even by the vast wilderness. How comforting it must be to know that the Lord does not forget His people or His promises.

### Theological Insights Interwoven

Ezekiel’s message isn’t merely a warning to Edom; it’s a reminder to all who witness. It reflects the **perpetual conflict between good and evil**—between those who align with God's purposes and those who stand against them. The seed of Esau's hatred sprouts and spreads like a persistent weed through generations. Yet, God will vindicate His people, taking their cause as His own. 

Matthew Henry reminds us: "Marvel not if the world hate you." This ancient enmity has such a familiar ring, does it not? It's the spiritual warfare that believers face even today.

### Closing Reflection

This chapter isn't just about judgment; it's a portrait of **divine faithfulness**. It's about God’s unwavering commitment to His people, despite temporary judgments and trials. In Ezekiel’s words, we see a God who is both just and compassionate, who punishes evil yet remains faithful to His promises. As we read this chapter, we are reminded that though His people may be cast down, they are never cast away. God's presence, like the ember of a fire, remains even in the darkest night.
